## Five editorial dependencies to resolve before large-scale rewriting

### 1. Establish the chronology and master event ledger
Chapter 8 calls the Oracle silent for **three weeks**, then treats that as **three years** in the same chapter. The eclipse of Chapter 11 leads to Chapter 12's specific five-day recovery. Chapter 13 suddenly describes **late winter** after earlier summer weather without a clear elapsed period. Chapter 20 celebrates Thalia's belated sixteenth birthday. These details need a single dated calendar, including how many weeks Percy has been at camp, not a sequence of improvised local fixes.

### 2. Reconcile Jason's Camp Jupiter visits and communications
- **Chapter 12:** Jason is physically present for a **three-day approved visit** (L186).
- **Chapter 13:** that visit has ended (L267).
- **Chapter 14:** Jason is physically at the lake again (L41) with no intervening new authorization.
- **Chapter 19:** an Iris-message is portrayed as a long-awaited contact, including the implication they were never allowed to see him before (L41–85).
- **Chapter 22:** Jason is at camp and Thalia says she taught him 'last week' (L139, L475).

This is the highest-impact cross-chapter continuity problem. Separate **initial visit, repeat visits, remote calls and restricted Camp Jupiter communications** in a canonical event ledger. Do not invent a timeline rewrite to cover inconsistencies.

### 3. Resolve Poseidon's availability and letter chronology
Chapter 7 includes a cryptic letter telling Percy to 'Find me' while he believes Poseidon's whereabouts are unknown (L291–299). Chapter 14 has Percy talking to Poseidon by Iris-message, saying some letters were unanswered and later citing a letter received from Poseidon. Record the sender, receipt date, current location and purpose of each item. Keep the Poseidon/Clarisse/Silena unequal-parenting conflict intact.

### 4. Enforce remembered-life boundaries, especially for Atlantis
Percy and Annabeth retain their original future and lack complete memories of childhoods lived by their altered selves. Chapters 17, 21 and 26 sometimes treat Percy's Atlantean childhood as a complete autobiographical past, and Chapter 26 mentions an undocumented recent **physical** palace visit. Frame knowledge as an *observed present*, *trained reflex*, or *specific relative's testimony*. Do not transfer erased history to Thalia, Nico, Chiron or Zoe.

### 5. Clean the POV architecture without flattening the domestic scenes
Chapters 19 and 21–26 contain explicit `### POV` or character-label headings; Chapter 24 uses `### EVERYONE`, and Chapter 26 enters Percy's and Thalia's **private unspoken wishes** inside Annabeth's viewpoint (L407). The bible requires scene breaks and one clearly established focal consciousness per scene. Fix the form while protecting differentiated intimate scenes.

## Material worth preserving

The batch moves from prophecy and immediate danger into a fuller Camp Half-Blood community. The strongest work tends to give relationships **practical form**: repairing the Hall (Ch 12), Thalia's changing response to Zeus (Ch 13), Atlantean family correspondence and Beryl's contested letter (Ch 14–15), the lake (Ch 17), the forge (Ch 18), the birthday preparation (Ch 20), Sally's cookies (Ch 23), and the night-watch assignments (Ch 25). The domestic genre is intentional. Judge whether each scene changes the characters' understanding or daily life, not how many monsters appear.

## Recommended correction sequence

1. Resolve existing **Chapters 1–6** issues before changing their downstream consequences.
2. Create a master calendar, character/visitor presence ledger, letters/evidence ledger, and incident/injury tracker covering Chapters 1–26.
3. Fix hard contradictions and inaccessible autobiographical memory.
4. Tighten scene POV boundaries and remove prohibited POV labels.
5. Revise dialogue and repetitions while keeping all approved romantic, sibling and found-family priorities.
6. Preserve individual chapter files; make **one pull request per logically coherent revision**; update cross-chapter handoffs and rebuild reader editions after the selected canon sources are reconciled.

**Length note:** The 10,000-word minimum governs **newly drafted chapters** unless the author applies it retroactively. Existing early chapters should not be filled to that number with repetitive dialogue or synonyms.

**Audit limitation:** These source-grounded reports identify specific defects and candidate improvements, not a proof that every error has been found or that a future plot thread is abandoned. Later revisions may change what appears contradictory. The chapter files remain the authoritative evidence for each line-linked claim, and the author's story decisions override the reviews.
