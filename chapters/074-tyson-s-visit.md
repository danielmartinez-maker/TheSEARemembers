## Chapter 74: Tyson's Visit

The conch announced Tyson's return for a longer visit with three short blasts just after breakfast. Tyson had stayed for the anniversary festivities and watched the camp play, then gone back to his duties in the undersea forges after the performance. Percy had missed him even during that brief separation. Now Chiron had approved a longer stay, and the signal sounded less like a war alert than a welcome.

Percy was sitting on the steps of the Poseidon cabin with Annabeth beside him, her shoulder pressed to his, her fingers idly tracing the worn wood of the step while they waited. The morning air was warm and gold in a mild April spell, smelling of cut grass and salt off Long Island Sound and smoke from the forges already going. Down by Half-Blood Hill, the mist over the barrier shimmered.

"You're bouncing your knee," Annabeth said.

Percy stilled his leg. "Am not."

"You are. You're nervous."

"I'm not nervous. I'm excited. There's a difference."

Annabeth smiled, that soft, knowing smile that was only for him, and laced her fingers through his to hold his knee still. "It's cute. You've checked the time like six times."

"He said morning. It's morning. That's all morning," Percy said, gesturing at the sky like it was personally responsible.

Across the green, Thalia walked over with her hands in the pockets of her jeans jacket, her black hair pulled back, her blue eyes bright. She flopped down on Percy's other side without asking.

"Relax, Kelp Head," she said. "He's coming. Cyclopes are never late when there's food involved."

As if on cue, the barrier at the top of the hill rippled.

A huge shape ducked through the pine trees by the boundary pine, careful not to brush the lowest branches where the boundary wards glittered gold. Even from a distance, Percy would have known him anywhere — eight feet tall if he was an inch, shoulders wide as a doorway, shaggy brown hair sticking up in tufts, one big brown eye blinking in the sunlight, a battered duffel bag slung over one shoulder and a paper grocery bag clutched in his other hand like treasure.

Tyson stopped at the top of the hill and looked down at the whole camp spread out below him. His face split into a grin so wide it looked like it hurt.

"PERCY!" he bellowed, loud enough to startle the harpies out of the woods.

Percy was already on his feet and running before he realized he was moving. Annabeth laughed behind him and Thalia whooped, but he barely heard them over the pounding of his own sandals on the grass.

Tyson dropped both bags and opened his arms, and Percy hit him at full speed and got scooped up off the ground as easily as if Percy were still a little kid. Tyson spun him once, laughing, that deep happy rumble that shook his whole chest.

"Little brother!" Tyson said. "I came! I came for whole week!"

"You made it," Percy said, hugging him hard around the neck, not caring that his feet were dangling two feet off the ground. "You actually made it."

Tyson set him down carefully, then looked past him. His eye went wide. "Annabeth!" he shouted, just as loud, and Annabeth had just enough time to brace before Tyson picked her up in a hug too, lifting her clean off her feet while she laughed.

"Hi, Tyson," she managed, patting his shoulder. "Put me down before you crack my ribs."

"Sorry!" Tyson set her down instantly and patted her head gently to fix the hair he had mussed. "You look pretty. Your hair is yellow like sun."

Annabeth blushed pink. "Thank you. You look great too. Did you get taller?"

Tyson stood up straighter, proud. "One inch! Boss measured!"

Thalia came up last, hands on her hips, grinning up at him. "Hey, Big Guy."

"Thalia!" Tyson beamed and offered her a fist bump the size of a ham, which she returned with a solid knock. "You kept pine tree safe! Good tree!"

"Best tree on Long Island," Thalia agreed solemnly.

Tyson bent and retrieved his bags. He held out the paper grocery bag to Percy with both hands, formal as an offering. "For you. For cabin. I brought."

Percy took it and peeked inside. Six industrial-size jars of peanut butter, creamy and crunchy mixed, plus a box of plastic spoons.

Annabeth burst out laughing. "Tyson, you sweetheart."

Tyson nodded earnestly. "Peanut butter is best mortal food. Better than ambrosia. Boss says I cannot eat only peanut butter in forges under sea, but here —" He spread his arms wide to encompass all of Camp Half-Blood. "Here I can!"

"Here you can," Percy promised, slinging an arm around his brother's massive waist as best he could and steering him down the hill. "Come on. Let's get you settled. You're in my cabin, by the way. Whole week."

Tyson stopped walking. "Sleep in Poseidon cabin? With you?"

"Yeah, man. Just like old times. I already set up the guest bunk beside ours."

Tyson's single eye went shiny. He blinked fast. "Really? For whole week?"

"Really," Percy said quietly.

Tyson made a small happy sound and hugged the duffel bag to his chest. Annabeth reached up and squeezed his arm, and Thalia punched Percy's shoulder lightly as they walked, her eyes a little bright too.

Chiron trotted to meet them halfway, smiling broadly. "Tyson! Welcome home."

"Hello, Chiron!" Tyson said, bending deeply at the waist in a bow. "Thank you for having me. I will work hard. I will not break anything too big."

Chiron chuckled. "The forges have missed you. Nyssa has a list a mile long."

"I will make everything!" Tyson promised.

Campers were coming out of cabins and the mess hall to wave. Clarisse, passing with her spear, gave Tyson a curt nod that for her was practically a hug. Grover trotted up, bleating happily, and Tyson picked him up and spun him too while Grover yelled, "Tyson! Buddy! Put me down — okay, one more spin!"

It took twenty minutes to get from the hill to Cabin Three because everyone wanted to say hi, and Tyson wanted to say hi back to everyone, by name, with a compliment attached. By the time they reached the low gray walls of the Poseidon cabin with its seashell trim and fountain gurgling out front, Percy's face hurt from smiling.

He pushed open the door. "Ta-da."

---

Inside, the Poseidon cabin was cool and dim and smelled of salt. Sunlight fell in blue-green shafts through the shell windows. Percy's bunk on the left was rumpled, his book splayed open on the pillow.

On the right, beyond Clarisse's neatly stacked gear and Silena's shelf of shells, the guest bunk was made up fresh — and bigger. Percy and Beckendorf had spent yesterday reinforcing the frame with bronze struts and laying two mattresses together, and Annabeth had found extra blankets. A new pillow. A folded Camp Half-Blood T-shirt in XXXL.

Tyson stood in the doorway and had to duck to get in. He stared at the bed.

Percy rubbed the back of his neck, suddenly shy. "I know it's not as big as your room in Atlantis, but Beckendorf said the struts'll hold like five hundred pounds, so you should be good, and if it's too small we can —"

Tyson crossed the room in two steps and sat down on the bunk. It creaked but held. He bounced once, experimentally. Then he lay back, his feet hanging off the end by a good foot, his arms spread wide, and sighed a huge, happy sigh.

"Perfect," he declared to the ceiling. "Best bed."

"It's too short," Percy said.

"It is perfect because it is in brother's cabin," Tyson said firmly, sitting up. He opened his duffel and began unpacking with great ceremony: a lump of Celestial bronze wrapped in cloth, a small hammer, a conch shell polished to a shine, a photograph in a barnacle-crusted frame — Percy and Tyson at the bottom of the ocean in front of the palace, Percy in his ratty wetsuit grinning with his arm around Tyson's neck, both of them squinting against the blue.

Tyson set the photo on the windowsill facing Percy's bed.

"So you can see me when I am gone," he explained.

Percy's throat went tight. "Yeah," he said. "Yeah, that's — thanks, T."

Annabeth, leaning in the doorway with Thalia, caught Percy's eye and smiled at him in a way that made his ears go warm. She mouthed, *cute*, and he mouthed back, *shut up*, fondly.

Tyson pulled out the last thing in his bag — a small, lopsided clay peanut butter jar with PEANUT BUTTER stenciled on it in wobbly Greek letters. "For cabin altar," he said. "For Dad. To thank him for visit."

Percy laughed, a little wet around the edges. "Dad's gonna love that. He's more of a ... I don't know, sushi guy, but he'll appreciate the thought."

They set it together on the little altar in the corner, and Tyson poured a careful spoonful as an offering. The salt breeze through the window lifted the scent of peanuts and sea.

That night, Percy lay in his bunk listening to Tyson snore — a sound like a foghorn with a cold — and staring at the dark ceiling with a grin he couldn't wipe off. Across the narrow aisle, Tyson's huge hand dangled off the edge of his bunk. Percy reached out without getting up and bumped his knuckles against it.

Tyson snorted, half-asleep, and curled his fingers around Percy's hand. "Night, brother," he mumbled.

"Night, T," Percy whispered.

He fell asleep holding his brother's hand, and the sea murmured outside like it was happy too.

---

By the second morning, the forges had a new rhythm.

The forges were set back in the woods in a big stone building with a chimney that never went out, smelling of coal and hot metal.

Tyson loved it instantly.

Nyssa, the head counselor of Hephaestus cabin with her perpetually singed eyebrows and her bandana tied tight, took one look at Tyson ducking through the door and shouted, "Thank the gods! Grab that tongs, Big Guy, we've got three chariots to re-shoe and a pile of swords that won't hold an edge!"

Tyson beamed and rolled up his sleeves. "Yes, please!"

Percy had come to watch for a bit before sword training, Annabeth with him, her arm looped through his. They stood in the doorway out of the way while Tyson settled at the biggest anvil like he had been born there — which, in a way, as a Cyclops son of Poseidon, he kind of had.

Cyclopes were the first blacksmiths. They had made Zeus's lightning bolt.

Tyson worked bare-handed where other smiths needed gloves, turning the glowing metal with his fingers. His hammer falls were precise and musical — *ting, ting, TANG* — as he folded a bar of Celestial bronze until the impurities sweated out.

"Whoa," said Jake Mason, elbowing Percy. "He's good."

"He's the best," Percy said proudly. "He made my watch-shield. You know, the one that —"

"That turns into a shield? Yeah, everyone knows," Jake said. "Dude, can he stay forever?"

Annabeth squeezed Percy's arm.

Tyson, hearing his name, looked up and waved with a red-hot horseshoe in his hand. "Percy! Look! I made! Good, yes?"

"Great!" Percy called, giving him two thumbs up. "Don't wave the hot metal around, buddy!"

"Oh! Sorry!" Tyson quickly dunked it in the quench bucket with a furious hiss and a cloud of steam, while the Hephaestus cabin laughed fondly.

At lunch, Tyson ate at the Poseidon table with Percy. He had a mountain of barbecue and, beside it, a full jar of peanut butter with a spoon stuck in it like a flag.

"Want some?" he offered to Annabeth, holding out a heaping spoon.

Annabeth, who was sitting on the Poseidon bench with Percy — a privilege girlfriends of counselors were quietly allowed — leaned forward and took the bite. "Mmm. Crunchy. Good choice."

Tyson glowed. "Percy likes creamy," he confided loudly. "But crunchy is better. Do not tell Percy I said."

"I heard that," Percy said around a mouthful of fries.

"I meant you to hear," Tyson said, nodding wisely, and Annabeth laughed so hard she had to lean into Percy's side while he wrapped an arm around her to keep her from falling off the bench.

Thalia stole a fry off Percy's plate. "Hey, Tyson, you coming to the arena? Clarisse wants to see if island life made you soft."

Tyson frowned. "I am not soft. I am hard like rock. But I do not fight friends."

"It's not a fight," Thalia said, grinning wickedly. "It's an arm-wrestle."

---

Word spread fast. By four o'clock, half the camp was crowded around a picnic table outside the arena, sitting on the grass in the shade.

Clarisse La Rue stood on one side of the table with her arms crossed, her dark hair pulled back in a bandana, her sea-green eyes narrowed. She had her sparring war paint on — red streaks across her cheeks — and her biceps strained the sleeves of her shirt. Behind her, the Ares cabin chanted her name.

Tyson stood on the other side, blinking in the sunlight, looking confused but willing. He had a smear of soot on his cheek from the forge and his sleeves rolled up to show forearms thick as tree trunks.

Percy, Annabeth, Thalia, Grover, and Silena stood in the front row. Silena had her camera — one of those mortal disposable ones — ready.

"Rules," Clarisse barked. "Elbow on table. Shoulder to shoulder. First to pin wins. No crying."

"I will try not to cry," Tyson said earnestly.

The Ares cabin howled. Clarisse's mouth twitched — the closest she got to a smile before battle.

They gripped hands. Clarisse's hand disappeared entirely inside Tyson's. The size difference got a laugh from the crowd, but Clarisse didn't flinch. She had wrestled hellhounds. She wasn't scared.

Chris Rodriguez, acting as ref, put his hands over theirs. "Ready ... go!"

Clarisse's arm bunched. Her shoulder dropped. She pulled with her whole body, her boots digging into the dirt, her face going red.

Tyson's arm didn't move.

He looked down at their hands, curious. "Oh," he said. "We are starting?"

The crowd roared. Clarisse growled and pulled harder, veins standing out in her neck. Tyson, realizing the game had begun, frowned in concentration and began to push back — slowly, gently, like he was afraid of hurting her.

Clarisse held him for a full ten seconds. For a mortal girl against a Cyclops, that was insane. The Ares kids screamed themselves hoarse.

But inch by inch, Tyson's hand pressed hers toward the wood.

"Come on, Clarisse!" Silena shouted, bouncing on her toes, her blue eyes wide. "You got her, you got her — oh no —"

*Thunk.*

Tyson pinned her wrist to the table, soft as laying down a baby bird.

Silence. Then the whole hillside exploded.

Tyson jerked his hand back like he'd been burned. "Oh no! Did I hurt you? Are you broken? I am sorry! I did not mean —"

Clarisse sat back, staring at her hand, then at him. Her face was flushed, her hair coming out of her bandana. She flexed her fingers. Then, slowly, she stood up, walked around the table, and held out her hand again — this time for a shake.

"You," she said, grudging and loud, "are freakishly strong. Good match."

Tyson stared at her offered hand, then shook it very, very gently. "Good match," he repeated, delighted. "You are very strong for not-Cyclops! You almost beat me for ten seconds!"

"I know," Clarisse said, but there was no venom in it. She looked ... shocked, and pleased, and a little dazed. "Nobody lasts ten seconds. Except Percy, and he's cheating with water."

"Hey!" Percy said.

"It's true," Annabeth said, patting his chest.

Clarisse rolled her shoulder and nodded once at Tyson, sharp and respectful. "You can sit at my table any time, Cyclops. You fight fair."

Tyson's chest puffed up two inches. "Thank you, Clarisse! You can sit at my table any time too! I have peanut butter!"

That got a real laugh out of her — a short, surprised bark — before she remembered she was Clarisse and scowled at the Ares cheering section to shut up. As she walked off, Percy could have sworn she was walking a little taller.

Tyson turned to Percy with shining eyes. "Did you see? Clarisse laughed! We were already friends, but now she knows I can be a good opponent too. That is best winning!"

"I saw, Big Guy," Percy said, hugging him sideways, having to stand on tiptoe to reach around his shoulders. "You were awesome. And nice. Mostly nice."

"I was mostly nice," Tyson agreed solemnly.

Annabeth reached up and wiped the soot off his cheek with her thumb. "Come on, champion. Let's get you some dinner. You earned about four desserts."

"Four desserts?" Tyson gasped. "Is that allowed?"

"When you beat Clarisse? It's mandatory," Thalia said.

---

Silena Beauregard found Tyson the next afternoon in the meadow behind the cabins, sitting cross-legged in the grass with a jar of peanut butter and a book Percy had lent him — a picture book about whales — turning the pages very carefully with his big fingers.

"Hi, Tyson," she said softly, settling down beside him with a tote bag in her lap.

"Hello, Silena!" Tyson said happily. "You smell like strawberries. That is nice."

Silena blushed. His half-sister always smelled nice — shampoo and sunshine — but nobody said so quite like Tyson did, without any agenda at all. "Thank you. What are you reading?"

"Whales! Percy loves whales. Did you know blue whales hearts are as big as cars? My heart is not as big as car, but Percy says it is big anyway."

Silena laughed, then grew a little shy, twisting the strap of her tote. "Tyson ... I heard the nights get cold in the Poseidon cabin. By the water, you know? And your bunk is by the window. So I ... I made you something. If you want it."

She pulled a folded blanket out of her tote. It was hand-crocheted in thick, soft yarn — deep sea-blue with white wave stripes and a single big brown eye stitched in the center in honor of him, surrounded by little gold peanut shapes. The edges were a little uneven, but the stitches were tight and loving and it was clearly huge, made to fit an eight-foot Cyclops.

Tyson stared at it. His mouth opened. No sound came out.

"You ... made? For me?" he whispered.

Silena nodded, holding it out. "I started it when we heard you were coming. I wasn't sure I'd finish in time. It's not perfect —"

Tyson took it with both hands like it was made of glass. He pressed it to his face and inhaled. Then he wrapped it around his shoulders, and it actually covered him, and his eye went wet.

"Nobody made one for this bunk before," he said thickly. "At home in Atlantis, I have blankets from the forge. They are strong and practical. This one has my eye on it. And little peanuts. You thought about who I am, Silena."

Silena's own eyes filled instantly. She had wanted her brother to feel at home in Cabin Three, not to suggest his Atlantean life had been empty. "I wanted you to have something made just for you," she said.

"It is soft," Tyson said, rubbing his cheek against it. "It is warm like hug. I will keep it forever. I will never let it get dirty. I will —" He stopped, overwhelmed, and then he opened his arms. "Can I hug? Gentle hug?"

Silena nodded, already opening her arms. Tyson hugged her gently.

---

The slang lessons started as a joke and became a tradition by Wednesday.

It was raining that afternoon — a warm, pattering spring rain that turned the camp paths to mud and sent everyone crowding onto the covered porch of the Big House with mugs of hot chocolate. Tyson sat on the top step with his legs stretched out, his blanket around his shoulders, watching the rain hit the grass with fascination.

Annabeth and Thalia sat on either side of him, sharing a blanket, their hair damp. Percy lay on his back on the porch floor with his head in Annabeth's lap, half-dozing while she absently played with his hair — a privilege of officially-dating that still made his ears pink when Chiron walked by and pretended not to notice.

"Rain is nice," Tyson said. "Under sea, rain is just ... more water. Here it sings."

"That's poetic, Big Guy," Thalia said.

"What is poetic?" Tyson asked.

"It means pretty-words," Thalia said. "Like you're a poet. Like Shakespeare, but with one eye and better arms."

Tyson considered this. "I am poet," he decided. "I will make poem. *Rain goes pitter-patter. Peanut butter tastes better. Brother sleeps near me. Best week ever.*"

Annabeth clapped. Percy sat bolt upright. "That was actually good!"

"It rhymed!" Tyson said proudly.

"You need mortal slang," Thalia announced suddenly, sitting up. "If you're gonna visit the mortal world, you gotta sound cool. Right now you sound like a Hallmark card."

"What is Hallmark?" Tyson asked.

"Exactly my point," Thalia said. "Lesson one. Repeat after me: 'That's sus.'"

"That's suss?" Tyson tried.

"Sus. S-U-S. It means suspicious. Like, if Percy says he didn't eat your peanut butter but he has peanut butter on his face —"

"I did not!" Percy protested, wiping his mouth guiltily. Annabeth raised an eyebrow at him and he wilted. "Okay, one spoonful. It was on his nightstand! It was calling to me!"

"See?" Thalia said triumphantly. "That's sus, Tyson."

Tyson pointed at Percy. "You are sus, brother!"

Percy clutched his chest. "Betrayed by my own blood."

Annabeth laughed and kissed the top of his head without thinking, then went pink when Thalia wolf-whistled. Tyson watched the kiss with wide, curious eyes.

"Okay, my turn," Annabeth said, rallying. "Tyson, say 'no cap.' It means you're telling the truth. No lie."

"No cap!" Tyson boomed. "I love my brother no cap!"

"Perfect!" Annabeth said. "Now say 'bet.' It means okay, deal."

"Bet!" Tyson said.

"Now say it all together," Thalia coached.

Tyson took a deep breath. "That's sus, no cap, bet!"

The whole porch laughed.

Tyson beamed, rocking happily under his blanket. "I am cool now? Like mortal teenagers?"

"The coolest," Annabeth promised.

"Cooler than Percy?" Tyson asked hopefully.

"Way cooler than Percy," Thalia said instantly.

"Hey!" Percy said, sitting up and pulling Annabeth's blanket over his head. "I take back my poem praise."

Tyson patted his head comfortingly. "It is okay, brother. You are cool in old way. Like dad jeans."

Percy stared at him. "Where did you learn 'dad jeans'?"

"Thalia taught me yesterday," Tyson said proudly.

"I regret everything," Thalia said, but she was grinning.

Later, Tyson practiced his new words on Clarisse, who sighed and walked off muttering, "Bet."

Tyson gasped and turned to the girls. "She said it back! I taught Clarisse slang!"

"You did," Annabeth said, wiping tears of laughter from her eyes. "You're a natural teacher."

"I am teacher poet Blanket King," Tyson said with dignity, pulling his blanket tighter. "No cap."

---

Thursday night was the night swim, their old tradition — just the two of them, floating where no one could hear and the water held them up.

Percy waited until the cabin was dark, then whispered, "T. You awake?"

Tyson's eye snapped open. "Night swim? Bet!"

They changed in the dark and slipped out into the cool night.

Annabeth was waiting at the edge of the sand with a towel. "Have fun. Don't let him drown."

"I will protect brother!" Tyson promised.

Annabeth cupped Percy's face and kissed him briefly. "Be safe, Seaweed Brain. I love you."

"Love you too," Percy mumbled. "Back in an hour."

The moment the water hit their knees, Percy relaxed. They dove under together and surfaced fifty yards out, floating on their backs.

"Best week ever," Tyson said. "No cap."

Percy laughed, water sloshing into his mouth. He spat it out. "Yeah? Even with Clarisse crushing your hand?"

"She did not crush. She tried hard. I liked that she tried hard," Tyson said. "Everyone here tries hard. At home under sea, everyone is ... scared of me. Because I am Cyclops. Here, Clarisse arm-wrestles me. Silena makes me blanket. Thalia teaches me sus. Annabeth feeds me peanut butter and does not laugh when I talk wrong."

"Hey, we never laugh *at* you," Percy said, rolling onto his stomach to look at him. "Only with you. There's a difference."

"I know," Tyson said. "That is why it is best week. Here I am not monster. I am brother. I am friend. I am Blanket King poet."

"You are all those things everywhere," Percy said fiercely. "Anyone under the sea who says different is sus and I will fight them."

Tyson smiled, but then his expression turned shy. He fidgeted with the water, swirling it with his fingers. "Percy? Can I ask question? Brother question?"

"Always."

Tyson glanced toward the beach, where Annabeth's small figure sat wrapped in his blanket, waiting. "Is Annabeth ... your girlfriend?"

Percy blinked. Water dripped off his eyelashes. "What?"

"In Atlantis, mermaids asked me. They said, 'Is your brother dating the smart blonde girl?' I said I did not know. Is she?" Tyson looked at him earnestly. "Are you ... boyfriend-girlfriend? Like kissing?"

Percy's face went furnace-hot. He was deeply grateful for the dark. "Oh. Uh. Yeah. Yeah, she — she's my girlfriend. We're together. Since we found each other here last January. And longer in the life we remember."

Tyson's grin was sunrise-bright. "I knew! I told them yes! I said my brother has pretty smart girlfriend who loves him very much!"

"You — you already told them yes?" Percy sputtered, laughing despite himself. "Then why'd you ask?"

"To make sure I was right," Tyson said simply. "I am happy, Percy. You smile bigger when she is near. Like sun comes out. Before, you smiled, but tired. Now you smile awake."

Percy ducked his head, his ears burning. He trailed his hand in the water. "Yeah," he said quietly. "She ... she makes everything better. Even bad stuff feels ... survivable, with her. She believes in me even when I'm being an idiot. Especially then."

"She did," Percy said, shy and proud. "Do you kiss her back?" Tyson asked.

Percy snorted. "Yeah, T. I kiss her back."

"Good. Kiss her lots. Tell her she is pretty and smart every day. And bring her peanut butter."

Percy laughed so hard he snorted seawater. "That's ... actually great advice. I'll do that."

They floated in comfortable silence. A shooting star scratched across the sky.

"Tyson?" Percy said after a while. "Are *you* happy? Under the sea? Honestly?"

Tyson was quiet a moment. "Boss is kind. Work is good. I have friends. But I miss you every day. Then I remember photo, and I am okay."

Percy slung an arm around his shoulders. "I miss you every day too."

"You can visit!" Tyson said eagerly. "Anytime! I made you your own room with whale posters! And peanut butter drawer!"

"I want to," Percy said, his voice thick. "Gods, I want to. Let's ask Chiron and your forge boss about a month in summer. Until then, can we try an Iris-message every week? I can actually keep that promise if the signal cooperates."

"Bet," Tyson said solemnly, holding out his pinky — which was the size of Percy's wrist.

Percy hooked it with his own. "Bet. No cap."

Tyson giggled — actually giggled, high and happy — and then, with a roar, he flipped backward and cannonballed, drenching Percy in a wall of water. Percy yelped and tackled him, and they wrestled in the shallows like otters, splashing and shouting and laughing until Annabeth's laughter drifted out to them from the beach.

They swam back in finally, shivering and pruny. Annabeth met them at the waterline with the blanket and towels, and she wrapped Percy up first, fussing over him while he stood dripping and grinning.

"Have fun, boys?" she asked.

"Best night ever," Tyson declared, wringing out his hair onto the sand. "Percy was talking about Annabeth again! He loves his girlfriend so much!"

Annabeth's eyebrows shot up. She looked at Percy, who turned scarlet and stared at his feet.

"Tyson, she already knows!" he hissed.

"What? It is good thing!" Tyson said, confused. "I am happy!"

Annabeth's expression softened. She stepped closer to Percy, still holding his towel around him, and slipped her cold hands around his warm neck. "Is that right, Seaweed Brain?" she murmured, eyes dancing. "Still my boyfriend after all this time?"

Percy swallowed. The moon was in her hair. The sea was at his back. His brother was beaming at them both.

"Yeah," he said, leaning into her hands. "Unless you want to renegotiate after the part where I stole your fries yesterday. I love you, Wise Girl. You know that."

Annabeth smiled like he had handed her the sky. She kissed him right there in front of Tyson and the moon and the whole sleeping camp — quick and sweet and sure.

"I do," she whispered against his mouth. "And I still love you, Seaweed Brain. No cap."

Behind them, Tyson cheered. "BET! THAT'S SUS — no, wait. That's ... that's CUTE! Thalia said cute means —"

"Let's go with cute," Percy said, laughing into Annabeth's shoulder, his face on fire, his heart so full it hurt. He held out one arm and Tyson ducked in, and Annabeth leaned in, and the four of them — three people and one blanket — stood tangled together on the moonlit sand while the waves applauded.

---

The last day came too fast.

Friday morning dawned clear and gold. Tyson packed his duffel slowly, folding his new Camp T-shirt, tucking the empty peanut butter jars — souvenirs — around his hammer. The blanket Silena made he folded last, carefully, and held it for a moment before setting it gently on top.

"To keep safe on trip," he explained to Percy, who was sitting on his own bunk watching, trying not to look as miserable as he felt.

The whole camp turned out to say goodbye at the hill. Chiron shook Tyson's hand formally. Nyssa gave him a bronze gear pin from the forge — honorary Hephaestus — and Tyson pinned it to his blanket-cape with trembling fingers. Grover hugged his waist and made him promise to write. Clarisse ... Clarisse nodded at him, then, after a visible internal struggle, held out a small paper bag.

"Peanut brittle," she muttered. "Silena said you like peanut stuff. Don't make it weird."

Tyson clutched it to his chest. "Thank you, sister Clarisse! I will eat one piece every day and think of your strong arm!"

Clarisse's ears went red. "Yeah. Whatever. Come back and I'll beat you next time."

"Bet!" Tyson said, and Clarisse almost smiled.

Silena threw herself into his arms, crying happy-sad tears, and Tyson hugged her gently, patting her hair. "Do not cry, blanket-maker. I will keep it forever. I will send you pearl from Atlantis for thread."

"You better," Silena sniffled, laughing.

Thalia fist-bumped him. "Stay cool, Big Guy. Don't let the fish push you around."

"I am coolest," Tyson confirmed. "Thalia taught me."

Annabeth hugged him long and hard. "Message us when you get home so we know you're safe," she said into his shoulder. "And Tyson? Thank you. For ... for loving him so much when I couldn't be there. Under the sea, I mean. For being his brother."

Tyson hugged her tighter. "He is best brother. I share him with you because you love him too. That is okay. We both keep him safe."

Annabeth pulled back, eyes bright. "Deal."

Finally, Percy. He stood in front of his brother on the hilltop, hands in his pockets, trying to smile.

"So," he said.

"So," Tyson said.

"This was the best week," Percy said. "Forge loved you. Clarisse respects you, which is like getting struck by lightning and living. You've got your own slang now. You're basically a camper."

"I am camper in heart," Tyson said. "But I must go back to work. Boss needs me. The palace pipes leak."

"Yeah. Stupid pipes." Percy's voice cracked. He looked down fast.

Tyson set down his bags. "Percy? Brother hug?"

Percy stepped into his arms and held on tight, burying his face in his brother's shoulder. Tyson enveloped him completely, blanket-cape and all, warm and solid and smelling of coal smoke and peanuts.

"Miss you already," Percy mumbled.

"Miss you already too," Tyson rumbled. "But we are brothers. Distance does not matter. Like star and sea. Always connected."

Percy laughed wetly. "When did you get so wise?"

"Poet," Tyson reminded him.

They stood there while the camp politely pretended not to watch, Annabeth's hand on Percy's back, Thalia's hand on Tyson's arm.

At last Tyson picked up his bags, slung the duffel over his shoulder, and turned to the barrier. He stopped at the pines and looked back, his single eye shining.

"Bye, brother!" he called, waving huge. "Bye, Annabeth-girlfriend! Bye, Thalia! Bye, camp! I love you no cap!"

"EVERYONE?" Thalia shouted back, cupping her hands around her mouth.

"Bet!" the camp shouted back as one, laughing, waving — Percy loudest of all, Annabeth beside him with her fingers laced through his, Silena in her blanket-cape twin, Clarisse with her arms crossed but her hand raised, Grover bleating, Chiron smiling.

Tyson waved until the mist closed behind him and he was gone.

Percy stood staring at the empty hill for a long moment. Annabeth leaned her head on his shoulder. Thalia bumped his other shoulder with hers.

"He'll be back," Annabeth said quietly.

"Yeah," Percy said. He turned his hand over and looked at the faint peanut-butter stain on his palm from that morning's shared breakfast — Tyson insisting on one last spoonful together, clinking spoons like champagne glasses. He smiled. "He left a jar. Unopened. On my nightstand."

"That was for you," Annabeth said. "He told me. Emergency brother peanut butter. For when you miss him."

Percy swallowed hard and nodded, slipping his arm around her waist and pulling her close. Thalia slung an arm around both of them, and they stood there on Half-Blood Hill with the wind in their faces and the sea glittering beyond, the Grace sisters and Percy watching the road.

That night, Cabin Three was quiet again. Percy's bunk was rumpled; Clarisse and Silena were settling into theirs; Tyson's reinforced guest bunk was empty and neatly made. The blue blanket with the stitched brown eye had gone home in Tyson's bag, as he wanted. One loose strand of matching yarn remained caught on the bedframe, and Percy left it there.

Percy lay awake a while, listening to the absence of snoring. Then he reached across the aisle — out of habit — and his fingers found only air.

He smiled in the dark anyway, rolled over, and ate one spoonful of emergency brother peanut butter straight from the jar. Creamy. His favorite. Tyson had remembered.

*Best week ever,* he thought, and fell asleep with the taste of peanuts and salt on his tongue and his girlfriend's goodnight kiss still warm on his forehead and his brother's laughter echoing somewhere under the sea.

No cap.
