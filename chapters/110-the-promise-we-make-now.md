## Chapter 110: The Promise We Make Now

The camp had a way of turning small agreements into traditions.

It started with a conversation at the shrine. Annabeth had been measuring the drainage channel again, and Percy had been holding the end of the cord. Clarisse and Silena came by to return a hammer. Thalia followed with a box of spare tiles. Grover arrived carrying lemonade. Ella came last, with a notebook already open.

"We should make a promise," Annabeth said.

Clarisse eyed her. "About the shrine?"

"About the people here. About how we treat each other."

"That's a big promise," Percy said.

"We don't have to make a big one." Annabeth sat on the stone ledge. "Just something small enough to keep. Something we choose, not something a prophecy tells us to do."

Thalia looked at her sister. "What kind of promise?"

Annabeth glanced around the group. "That we ask before we assume. That we let people say no. That we don't keep secrets just because we're scared someone might be upset."

Clarisse leaned against the wall. "That last one sounds difficult."

"It is."

"Then it should be included."

Silena set the hammer down. "And that we show up for each other. Even when we don't know the right thing to say."

Grover lifted his lemonade. "And that nobody gets left alone at a campfire unless they ask to be."

Ella wrote each line in her notebook. "Should we make a copy?"

"No," Annabeth said. "Let's just say it now."

They formed a loose circle around the shrine. It wasn't ceremonial. Nobody lit the fire or called for the gods. The sky was ordinary blue, and the worksite smelled of dust and wet stone.

Annabeth went first. "I promise to ask what people want instead of deciding for them."

Percy followed. "I promise to tell the people I care about when I'm scared, instead of pretending I'm fine."

Thalia looked at him with surprise, then said, "I promise to protect my sister without treating her like she belongs to me."

Annabeth's face softened.

Clarisse shifted her weight. "I promise to let people help me before I'm bleeding on the floor."

"That's very specific," Silena said.

"I have examples."

Silena's turn came next. "I promise to say what I want, not only what everyone else needs."

Grover promised to keep trying for his Searcher's license even if he failed again. Ella promised to keep writing things down, but to put her notebook away when someone needed her attention. Thalia promised to ask for help before she was exhausted. Percy promised to stop making terrible jokes about seaweed when Annabeth was trying to be serious.

"That's impossible," Annabeth said.

"Then I'll try."

She smiled. "I promise to tell Percy when I want him close, and when I need space."

The circle quieted. Nobody teased. Nobody made it into a grand thing.

They just stood there, listening.

---

Afterward, the others went to lunch. Percy and Annabeth stayed behind to stack the tools.

"Do you think we can keep those promises?" Percy asked.

"Not perfectly."

"That's not very reassuring."

"It's honest. We'll forget sometimes. We'll make mistakes. Then we'll apologize and try again. That's how promises work when they're real."

Percy set a trowel in the box. "In the older life, I think we made big promises. Forever ones."

"We did."

"Do you remember them?"

"Yes." Annabeth picked up the pencil she had left on the ledge. "I remember making you promise we'd always come back to each other. I remember you saying we would."

Percy's chest tightened. "I still want that."

"So do I. But I don't want us to say it because we're afraid the other person will leave. I want us to say it because we keep choosing to come back."

He took her hand. "Then that's what I'll promise. I'll keep choosing you. Today. Tomorrow. And when I get it wrong, I'll tell you and try again."

Annabeth's fingers closed around his. "I promise the same."

"Good."

She leaned forward and kissed him. Percy kissed her back, briefly, warmly, then rested his forehead against hers.

"That's a good promise," she whispered.

"Yeah."

They stayed close for another few seconds, then went to lunch.

---

The promise became a tradition by accident.

Every month, they met at the shrine and repeated it. Sometimes they added a line. Sometimes someone crossed one out and replaced it with something they could actually keep. Ella recorded the changes in her notebook. Clarisse complained about the wording. Silena made sure everyone had eaten first.

The promise changed as they did.

In spring, Thalia added: *I will let people see when I'm tired.*

Clarisse crossed it out, then wrote: *I will stop calling exhaustion a strategy.*

Silena added: *I will take up space even when it makes someone uncomfortable.*

Percy added: *I will let myself miss the people I can't bring here.*

Annabeth added: *I will not make my memories into instructions for my future.*

No one asked her to explain. Percy squeezed her hand, and she squeezed back.

---

That evening, they all gathered at the campfire. Grover played his pipes. Ella passed around the notebook. The flames burned blue and green, reflecting off the shrine's small mosaic tiles.

Thalia sat between Annabeth and Clarisse. Percy sat beside Annabeth, their shoulders together. Silena sat on Clarisse's other side, her hand resting lightly on her sister's wrist.

They were not a perfect family. They were not even the same kind of family. Some were siblings by blood, some by choice, some by histories that existed only in two people's memories. They argued. They forgot promises. They hurt each other and apologized.

But they showed up.

When the song ended, Percy looked at Annabeth. "Want to go for a walk?"

"Yes."

They slipped away from the fire and walked to the lake. The stars were clear, scattered across the sky like someone had spilled salt over dark cloth.

"Do you think we'll remember this later?" Annabeth asked.

"I will."

"Even if everything changes again?"

"Especially then."

She took his hand. "Then let's make it worth remembering."

They stood at the dock, listening to the water. Percy kissed her once, softly. Annabeth leaned into him, and he put an arm around her shoulders.

Behind them, their friends were laughing around the fire. Ahead, the lake carried the stars in its dark surface.

For tonight, the world was simple enough: her hand in his, the sound of the camp behind them, and the promise they had made because they wanted to.

That was enough to start with.
