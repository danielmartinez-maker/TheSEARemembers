## Chapter 109: No Borrowed Promises

Annabeth found the photograph in the bottom of Percy's bag.

It was old, softened at the corners, and bent once down the middle. The image showed Percy and Annabeth as teenagers, older than they were now, standing in front of a stone building with a banner that read NEW ROME UNIVERSITY. They were smiling at whoever held the camera. Percy had his arm around her shoulders. Annabeth's hand rested over his heart.

She stared at it for a long time.

The image belonged to a life she remembered and a future she could no longer take for granted. She knew the day. She remembered the sunlight, the smell of the city, Percy's stupid joke about the banner being misspelled. She remembered being happy. She also knew that the girl in the photo was not exactly her—not the girl who had grown up with Thalia, not the girl whose body had learned to summon lightning before she understood how.

She was still holding the photograph when Percy came into the Poseidon cabin.

"I was looking for my pencil," he said. Then he saw her face. "Oh."

"You brought this with you?"

"It was in my jacket pocket. I didn't even know it was there until we got back from the quest."

Annabeth turned the picture over. Nothing was written on the back. "You look happy."

"We were."

"I know." She set it on the table. "That's the problem. I remember being happy with you. I remember loving you. And then I look at this life, and I worry we're trying to keep every old plan because we're afraid of losing anything else."

Percy didn't move closer. He didn't reach for her hand. He waited.

"I need you to tell me if you want to slow down," he said. "Or stop. You don't owe me anything because of this picture. Or because of what you remember."

"I know that."

"Do you?"

The question wasn't accusing. It was gentle, and that made it harder.

Annabeth folded her arms. "Sometimes I feel like I'm borrowing our older promises. I still love you. But if I need a different plan now, I feel like I'm betraying everything we worked for. Like choosing you means following every instruction we left ourselves."

Percy nodded slowly. "I don't want you to borrow any promises."

"Then what do you want?"

"You. As you are now. Not a copy of the person I remember. Not a promise from somebody else." He paused. "I want to be with you if you want to be with me. And if you need room to decide, I'll give you room."

Annabeth looked down at the photograph. "The older me would have kissed you right now."

"Maybe."

"I think I want to. But I need us to be here together, not trying to repeat a photograph."

"Then take your time."

She studied the picture again. The older girl had one hand against Percy's chest. The pose looked natural, unplanned. The kind of thing you did when you trusted someone enough to stand close without thinking about it.

Annabeth put the photograph down.

"I want to be with you," she said. "I want the hand-holding. I want the letters. I want to kiss you. But I need us to keep asking each other. I need to know I can change my mind without losing you."

"You can."

"And if I do change my mind?"

"I'll be sad. But I'll still care about you."

She watched him, waiting for a catch. There wasn't one.

"Okay," she said.

Percy smiled a little. "Okay?"

"Okay, I'm choosing you. Today. Not forever because a picture says so. Today, because I want you here."

"Today is good."

She stepped closer and took his hand. "And I want to kiss you."

"Then you can."

Annabeth leaned in. She kissed him, slow and sure, then drew back before the moment could become too much. Percy didn't chase her. He only kept holding her hand.

"Thank you," she said.

"For what?"

"For letting our plans be new, even when we aren't."

He pressed his forehead to hers. "We're both new at this. This time."

---

They spent the afternoon on the hill, sorting through their memories.

Not all of them. Just the easy ones. The first time they met. The first time Percy made Annabeth laugh. The first time Annabeth trusted him with a plan. They laid the memories out between them like cards, noticing which still made them laugh and which hurt more now that the future around them had changed.

"This one feels like mine," Annabeth said. "The first time you brought me blue candy from the dining pavilion because you heard I liked it."

"I thought we agreed never to speak of that."

"You got the wrong kind, though."

"That sounds like me."

"You got the wrong kind three times."

"That's less like me."

"You were trying very hard."

"That's more like me."

They laughed. Annabeth found that the memories didn't vanish when they laughed at them. They didn't take over either. They simply sat there, part of the person she was becoming.

Percy showed her the photograph again. This time she looked at it without feeling as though it was a command.

"I like her," Annabeth said.

"The older you?"

"She looks like she knows what she wants. And like she worked hard to get it." Annabeth handed the picture back. "I don't have to become exactly her. But I don't want to pretend she wasn't me either."

"You don't."

"And you don't have to pretend all those years disappeared because we're younger now. We remember them, even when different parts hurt. You get to be sad about that."

Percy looked toward the Sound. "Sometimes I worry that the older me is going to crowd you out. That I keep expecting us to fit plans we made before any of this happened."

"Then tell me when you notice. And I'll tell you when I feel like I'm being compared."

"Deal."

They shook on it, solemnly, and Annabeth laughed at how serious the handshake was.

---

At dinner, Percy sat beside her at the Zeus table. Thalia watched them for a few seconds, then passed Annabeth the bread basket without comment.

Clarisse leaned across from the Poseidon table. "You two having a council meeting over there?"

"We're negotiating terms," Annabeth said.

"About what?"

"Memory, choice, and whether Percy is allowed to steal the last roll."

"He is not," Clarisse said. "That's Poseidon cabin law."

"It's not an actual law," Percy said.

"It is now."

Silena put the last roll on Annabeth's plate. "There. Problem solved."

Thalia caught Annabeth's eye. "You okay?"

Annabeth nodded. "We talked."

"Good."

Thalia didn't ask what they'd talked about. She didn't demand to see the photograph. She didn't tell Annabeth what she should feel. She simply stayed there, beside her, eating dinner.

Annabeth reached under the table and took Percy's hand. It was a small choice, made in the middle of ordinary noise, and it felt more like hers than any memory ever could.

---

That night, Annabeth returned the photograph to Percy's bag. She tucked it in the inner pocket, smoothing the fold but not trying to erase it.

Beside it, she slipped a new sketch: two kids at the lake, one dark-haired and one blond, with their shoulders touching. No caption. No prophecy. No promise of what they would become.

Just what they were that day.

When Percy found it the next morning, he didn't ask whether she was sure. He only found her at breakfast, sat beside her, and offered his hand.

She took it.
