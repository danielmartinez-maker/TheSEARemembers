## Chapter 72: New Kids

A cold morning in April came to Camp Half-Blood with fog in the strawberry fields and the smell of woodsmoke from the forge, and Percy Jackson woke up thinking for half a second that he was new again.

It was the light that did it. Pale and thin through the seashell window of Cabin Three, the same gray-gold he remembered over the harbor more than a year ago. That arrival still tangled sometimes with his original infirmary memory: a blonde girl staring at him like he had personally offended her. His bunk was warm. Clarisse's and Silena's blankets were folded beside their pillows. His visiting brother Tyson snored in the guest hammock by the window. Somewhere out on the Sound a foghorn sounded, low and kind.

Percy sat up, scrubbed his hands through his black hair, and grinned at nothing. More than a year. He knew where the mess hall was. He knew which steps on the Big House porch creaked. He knew that the harpies took the breakfast plates at exactly eight and that you did not get between Ella and a new book.

Down the hill, in Cabin One, Annabeth Grace woke up thinking almost the same thing.

Her cabin was all high windows and storm-light, blankets folded with military corners because Thalia insisted. Thalia's bunk was already made — she had been up since dawn — with a note on Annabeth's pillow in jagged handwriting.

*NEW KIDS TODAY. DON'T SCARE THEM LIKE YOU SCARED PERCY. — T*

Annabeth snorted and folded the note into her notebook. As if she had scared Percy. Percy had scared himself. He had walked into a sword rack on his second day.

She dressed fast in jeans and her camp shirt and her necklace with four beads, pulled her curls back, and met Percy on the Big House porch at the same time as always, like their feet had the same clock.

"Hey, Seaweed Brain," she said.

"Hey, Wise Girl," Percy said, and bumped her shoulder with his. His hand found hers for a second and held on. It still made his ears go warm through summer, winter, and now spring, and Annabeth's ears went pink too, and they both pretended very hard that this was normal and casual.

It was not casual. It had never been casual.

Chiron was already waiting for them on the porch with his clipboard, his wheelchair ramp damp with dew, his kind brown eyes bright.

"Ah, my guides," he said. "Good morning, Percy. Annabeth. Did you sleep well?"

"Yes," they said together, then looked at each other and laughed.

"Good. Because today you are needed." Chiron turned his chair toward the road that wound down from Half-Blood Hill. A black camp van was crawling up it through the fog, headlights on. "Three new year-rounders arriving midterm. Two eleven-year-olds and one nine-year-old. All recently claimed or brought by satyrs. Grover brought one of them in himself last night. They are frightened, tired, and one of them threw up in the van. Which reminds me of someone."

Percy pointed at himself. "Hey. I only threw up a little."

"You threw up a great deal," Chiron said mildly. "Annabeth held your head and told you the names of all the cabins while you did it. I remember. I was there."

Annabeth's cheeks went red. "I was being practical. He needed distraction."

"You told me Cabin Eleven was where thieves slept and I was going to get robbed," Percy said. "Very comforting."

"It was accurate," Annabeth said primly, but she was smiling.

Chiron smiled too, then grew serious in the way adults did when they were about to trust kids with something real. "I asked for you two specifically. You remember what it is to be new. Clarisse and Thalia will help, of course, and Grover and Ella will do the tour, but you two will be their first friends. Will you do it?"

Percy looked at Annabeth. Annabeth looked at Percy. More than a year of memories passed between them without words — the harbor path, finding each other by the lake, getting punched into a lake, getting lost on the way to the bathrooms, crying in bunk beds the first week and pretending it was allergies.

"Yeah," Percy said. "Of course."

"We've got them," Annabeth said.

The van door opened by the Big House and three small figures climbed out, blinking in the fog.

---

The new kids stood in a damp cluster by the van with their duffel bags too big for their shoulders, and everything about them shouted *first day* so loudly that Percy felt his chest ache.

The tallest was a boy about eleven with dark skin and close-cropped hair and a brave expression that kept slipping. He held his bag strap with both hands like a shield. The patch on his backpack said MILO in stitched letters that were coming loose.

Next to him was a girl about the same age with copper-red hair in two braids and freckles and sneakers that lit up when she stepped, which she kept trying to hide by standing very still. Her tag said LUCY.

The smallest was a girl no more than eight or nine with straight black hair cut blunt and huge dark eyes and a stuffed seal clutched so tight its flipper was damp. She did not look around. She looked at the ground. Her tag said BEA in careful marker.

Argus, with his hundred eyes blinking sleepily, unloaded their bags and rumbled something kind and went back to the van.

Percy remembered standing in that exact spot with Grover's hand on his elbow and his knees shaking and thinking *I want my mom.* Sally in the life he remembered; Amphitrite waiting below the water in this one. He glanced at Annabeth. She had heard Thalia's account of their childhood arrival often enough to picture it: seven-year-old Annabeth with her sister's hand clamped around hers, Thalia fierce and telling anyone who stared, *that's my sister, got a problem?* Picturing it still wasn't remembering it.

Thalia herself came down the porch steps like a thunderstorm in sneakers, her black hair spiked, her silver hairband catching light, her bow slung over her back even though she was on guide duty. She went straight to the smallest girl and crouched down so they were eye-level, which was something Thalia never did for anyone unless she meant it.

"Hey," Thalia said. Her voice, usually loud enough to startle pigeons, went soft. "I'm Thalia. That's my sister Annabeth over there with the notebook. Are you Bea?"

Bea nodded once, eyes on Thalia's spiked bracelet.

"You like that?" Thalia held out her wrist. "My friend Zoe gave it to me. She's one of the bravest people I know. You can touch it if you want."

Bea reached out one finger and touched a spike very lightly, then pulled back. "Does it hurt?"

"Only people who mess with my sister," Thalia said solemnly. "Are you going to mess with my sister?"

A tiny, tiny smile. "No."

"Good. Then you're safe." Thalia stood and offered her hand. "Want to walk with me? I'm big-sister certified. Annabeth will tell you. I have references."

Annabeth stepped forward, smiling. "She's okay. She only lectures about safety for like twenty minutes at a time."

"Thirty," Thalia said. "Safety is important."

Percy stepped up beside Annabeth, hands in his pockets so he would not look too big. He was thirteen and tall for his age and he remembered how huge the older campers had looked to him.

"Hey, guys," he said. "I'm Percy. This is Annabeth. We were new not that long ago. I got lost three times my first day and I threw up on the hill. So if you do anything embarrassing, I've already done worse."

Milo stared at him. "You threw up on the hill? The Half-Blood Hill?"

"The very hill," Percy said gravely. "The harpies still talk about it."

Milo laughed despite himself, a short surprised bark. Lucy stopped trying to hide her light-up shoes and took a shuffling step that made them flash pink.

"Do they really light up?" Percy asked. "That's awesome. Can you outrun a hellhound in those?"

"I don't know," Lucy said. Her voice was small but spunky underneath. "Can I try?"

"Not today," Annabeth said quickly. "No hellhounds today. Maybe capture the flag next week if you're cleared."

"I'm Grover!" Grover announced, trotting up with his hat pulled low over his horns and his backpack clanking with tin cans and maps and half-eaten enchiladas. "Welcome orientation tour guide and professional satyr! This is Ella!"

Ella the harpy fluttered down behind him in a flurry of red feathers, clutching three pamphlets she had made herself that morning with crayon and library paste. Each one read WELCOME NEW CHICKS! with a drawing of a harpy that looked alarmingly like Ella wearing a camp shirt.

"Ella welcomes new chicks!" Ella cried. "Ella has made maps! Maps with pictures! 'The dining pavilion is loud like geese! The toilets flush! The lake is hungry but friendly!' Ella loves orientation! Orientation is a circle that starts!"

Bea stared up at Ella with her mouth open. Ella stared back with huge bright eyes and preened.

"Oh, a small one!" Ella breathed. "Small ones love stories! Ella will tell you where the library is! Ella will not scream the prophecy at you until week two!"

"Week two," Grover agreed, handing Bea a piece of cheese wrapped in wax paper like a peace offering. "We pace ourselves now. Lesson learned."

From the direction of the cabins came the sound of boots on gravel, steady and heavy, and Milo went still.

Clarisse La Rue rounded the corner with Silena beside her. Clarisse was broad-shouldered, spear on her back, sea-green eyes like Percy's. Silena smiled immediately, same eyes but crinkled at the corners, arms full of blankets and gummy fish.

"Which one threw up in the van?" Clarisse demanded.

Lucy's hand shot up before she could stop it, then she clapped it over her mouth. "I mean — it was me — sorry —"

Clarisse stared at her. The fog seemed to hush.

Then Clarisse jerked her thumb at Percy. "He threw up worse. First day. All over Grover's shoes. Grover cried."

"I did not cry," Grover said. "I bleated. There's a difference."

"I threw up on the hill too," Percy added helpfully. "It's like a tradition. You're one of us now."

Lucy's shoulders dropped two inches. "Really?"

"Really," Silena said warmly, stepping forward and pressing a blanket into Lucy's arms before Lucy could protest. "And you must be cold. The van ride is always cold. I'm Silena. This scary person is my sister Clarisse. She's also Percy's sister. We're all Poseidon. The sea family. We run warm and we talk loud and we always have snacks."

"We do not always have snacks," Clarisse said.

Silena pulled out the gummy fish and handed one to each new kid. Bea took hers and held it without eating, studying Silena's face.

"You're Percy's sister?" Milo asked. "They said you once picked up a drakon by the tail."

"Only a small one," Clarisse said. "It was biting Silena's shield."

The new kids laughed, nervous but real. Percy caught Annabeth's eye. *See?* That was the trick Thalia had taught them last summer: let the new kids see the older kids tease each other. It made gods and monsters feel smaller.

Chiron clapped his hands gently. "Percy, Annabeth — they are yours. Show them home."

---

The tour started at the cabins, the way it always did, and Grover took the lead with Ella fluttering above him reading her pamphlet upside down.

"Stop one!" Grover announced, spreading his arms wide on the commons. "Cabins! Each god has one! If your godly parent hasn't claimed you yet, you stay in Hermes cabin until they do! Which is fun! Hermes kids are fun! They will steal your socks but they will also teach you to pick locks which is — Chiron, am I allowed to say that?"

"No," Chiron called from the porch where he was pretending not to follow them.

"They will teach you friendship!" Grover corrected quickly. "Ella, cabins!"

Ella swooped down and landed on the Hermes cabin roof, wings spread for balance. "Ella recites! Cabin One: Zeus! Loud like thunder! Cabin Two: Hera! Empty like a held breath! Cabin Three: Poseidon! Smells like salt and brother-snores! Ella has been in Poseidon! Tyson snores like a friendly earthquake!"

Tyson, waiting by Cabin Three with blue muffins, waved hugely. "Hi new friends! I am Tyson! I made muffins!"

Bea hid half behind Thalia's leg. Thalia rested a hand on her head.

"It's okay," Thalia murmured. "He's soft. He cries at poems. Watch."

Tyson offered a muffin to Bea with extreme gentleness. "For you. Small one gets biggest muffin. That is the rule. I just made it."

Bea took the muffin after a long moment and whispered, "Thank you."

"Yay!" Tyson beamed. "She talks! Manners! Good manners mean good heart!"

Milo eyed Cabin Three. "Poseidon cabin only has — how many bunks?"

"Three permanent bunks," Percy said. "Me, Clarisse, and Silena. Plus a guest hammock for Tyson when he comes up from the undersea forges. Silena likes to braid my hair against my will."

"I do not braid against your will," Silena protested. "You fall asleep. That's consent."

"I fall asleep because you're good at it," Percy said.

"You three all share?" Lucy asked, eyes wide. "But — aren't you —" She stopped, embarrassed.

"All Poseidon's kids?" Silena finished kindly. "Yeah. Different moms, same dad. Clarisse and I have shared this cabin for years." She glanced at Annabeth and Thalia, daughters of Zeus standing side by side, one pair of gray eyes and one of electric blue above matching stubborn chins. "Then our little brother came up from Atlantis, and we found out he talks to fish and loses shoes. Families can grow without changing who you are."

"I do not talk to fish," Percy said. "Fish talk to me. I just listen. There's a difference and it's important."

"He loses shoes," Clarisse confirmed to Milo. "If you find a shoe in the lake, it's probably his. Leave it. The lake is hungry."

Milo grinned. "My satyr said the lake eats shoes. I thought he was joking."

"The lake is never joking," Percy and Clarisse and Silena said together, then looked at each other and burst out laughing in exactly the same way, nose wrinkled, head tipped back. Bea watched them with wide eyes and slowly, slowly started eating her muffin.

Annabeth led the next stop, because Annabeth loved plans and maps and knowing where the bathrooms were. She took them to the dining pavilion with its long tables and the bronze braziers for offerings, and she showed them how to scrape food into the fire for the gods.

"Do we have to?" Lucy whispered. "Offer food? What if my god doesn't like pizza?"

"All gods like pizza," Percy said. "It's science. Even Zeus likes pizza. Right, Annabeth?"

Annabeth, daughter of Zeus, rolled her eyes with deep sisterly suffering. "My dad likes pizza with extra thunder. Don't ask. Just offer a little. It's polite."

Thalia watched Annabeth explain seating and offerings, pride and ache crossing her face. She had raised Annabeth small, and now Annabeth led tours with a notebook like she had always belonged.

As if she had heard, Bea drifted over and stood beside Silena, pressing her stuffed seal against Silena's arm. Silena looked down, surprised and delighted, and without missing a beat in her explanation of laundry days, she shifted so Bea could lean against her.

Clarisse saw it and her tough face did something soft and quick. Percy saw it because he was her brother and he knew where to look — the corner of her mouth, the way her spear-hand loosened.

"Okay!" Grover shouted. "Next stop! Armory and arena! Where we learn to not die! Ella, drama!"

Ella backflipped in the air. "The arena! Where Clarisse is queen and also terrifying! 'The spear is a promise and the shield is a hug!' That is what Ella wrote! Ella is paraphrasing Ares manuals!"

"I'm Poseidon," Clarisse growled automatically, but without heat. Training with the Ares kids meant she heard their manuals quoted at her constantly; being their loudest drill leader didn't change her parentage. She touched the little trident charm at her neck that matched Percy's and Silena's. "But I'm still queen and terrifying. Come on, newbies. Time to hold a sword."

---

The arena smelled like hot bronze and cut straw and nervous sweat, and the new kids lined up along the fence while Clarisse paced in front of them with a practice sword in one hand and her best war face on.

It was a good war face. Percy had seen it make seventeen-year-olds drop their shields.

"Rule one," Clarisse barked. "You listen to your counselor. Rule two. You never point a real blade at a friend unless you're sparring and Chiron says so. Rule three. If you panic, you put your shield up and yell. Panicking is fine. Panicking with your shield down is stupid. Got it?"

Milo nodded fast. Lucy nodded. Bea nodded while half hiding behind Silena.

"Good." Clarisse stopped in front of Milo, looming. "You. Big guy. Hold this."

She thrust a wooden sword at him. Milo grabbed it by the blade.

"No!" Clarisse snapped, and Milo flinched so hard his backpack slipped. "Handle! You hold the handle! The sharp end goes that way! Do you want to lose fingers on day one?"

"I — no — sorry —" Milo fumbled the grip, face burning.

Percy winced. He remembered that feeling — Clarisse shouting, everyone watching, his hands shaking on a sword that felt too heavy. His first week, Clarisse had made him cry in this exact ring and then pretended not to notice while Silena slipped him a cookie.

He glanced at Annabeth. Annabeth was already moving.

"Hey," Annabeth said lightly, stepping into the ring with her own practice dagger twirling easy in her hand. "Clarisse is loud because the arena is loud. Watch." She turned to Clarisse. "Can I?"

Clarisse grunted and stepped back, arms crossed, but Percy saw her wink at Annabeth. It was a setup. They had planned this on the porch that morning.

Annabeth knelt in front of Milo so she was not looming, and held out her dagger handle-first. "Like this. Thumb here. Yeah, like that. Loose, not white-knuckles. You want your wrist to move. Like you're holding a big pen."

Milo adjusted. "Like this?"

"Perfect." Annabeth smiled at him, real and warm. "Thalia tells me the first time I held a knife I held it backwards, and she laughed so hard she fell off a picnic table."

"I did not fall," Thalia called from the fence where she sat with Bea. "I descended rapidly due to sisterly amusement."

"I was seven," Annabeth told Milo. "You're already better than I was."

Milo stood a little straighter. "Really?"

"Really. Percy was worse. He held his sword like a baseball bat."

"I did not!" Percy protested from the fence. "It was like a — okay, it was a little like a baseball bat."

Lucy giggled. Even Bea smiled into her seal.

Clarisse crouched down next to Lucy, who was clutching her wooden sword like it might bite. Up close, without shouting, Clarisse's face was different — younger, the scar through her eyebrow less fierce, her sea-green eyes kind.

"Hey," Clarisse said, quieter. "You okay? Swords are a lot on day one."

Lucy nodded, then shook her head. "They're heavy."

"Yeah. They are." Clarisse took Lucy's sword and adjusted the grip strap with quick, practiced fingers. "You don't have to be good today. Today you just learn which end is which and that I'm not going to let anything hurt you in my arena. That's my job. Okay?"

Lucy stared. "Your job is to scare us?"

"My job is to scare monsters," Clarisse said. "You? You're my little brother's new camp family, which makes you mine too. Sea family looks after the people we choose. Even the ones who throw up in vans."

She held out her fist. Lucy bumped it after a second, wonderingly.

"My dad — Poseidon — he told me once," Clarisse went on, glancing at Percy and Silena. Percy nodded encouragingly. He knew the story. Their dad had Iris-messaged them all together one morning last August, awkward and huge and smelling of salt, and said *you are mine and I am proud, be kind to each other.* Clarisse had cried after and threatened to kill anyone who mentioned it. "He said the sea doesn't care if you're big or small. It holds you anyway. That's what we do here. We hold each other up."

Bea peeked out from behind Silena. "Even small ones?" she whispered.

Clarisse turned. Her whole expression opened. "Especially small ones. Come here, tiny. Want to hold my shield? Nobody touches my shield. It's special."

Bea crept forward. Clarisse knelt and settled the big bronze shield over Bea's arm. It covered her from chin to knee. Bea staggered, then laughed — a bright, surprised sound — and everyone laughed with her, relieved.

"See?" Clarisse said gruffly, steadying her. "Strong. You're strong. The shield knows."

Silena slipped an arm around Lucy's shoulders and murmured, "She's soft under the shouting. Don't tell her I told you. She'll make me run laps."

"I heard that," Clarisse said without looking up. "Two laps. Both of you."

"We'll walk them," Silena said serenely. "With snacks."

Milo practiced his grip while Lucy swung and nearly hit Grover, who dove behind a dummy shouting about being too young. Thalia joined Percy by the fence.

"Scare, then safe," Thalia said quietly. "Perfect. That's what big sisters do."

"Watch it, Kelp Head. I'll tell Annabeth about your snoring."

"She already knows. She says I sound like a drowning pig."

"She's not wrong." Thalia grinned, then grew serious again. "Hey. Thanks for — for being her person. When I can't be there. Patrol stuff. I worry."

"Always," Percy said simply. "She's my person. I'll keep her safe."

"I know." Thalia punched his arm lightly. "You're a good kid, Percy Jackson. Don't tell Zeus I said that. He'll smite me out of principle."

---

Lunch was the real test, Grover always said. You could fake your way through cabins and arenas, but lunch showed you where you belonged.

The dining pavilion buzzed in the noon light, tables filling, the breeze carrying salt and grilled cheese. The new kids hovered at the entrance with their trays, overwhelmed by noise and steam and a hundred older campers shouting across tables.

"Where do we sit?" Milo asked, trying to sound casual and failing.

"With us," Percy said immediately. "New kids sit with guides first week. It's law. Chiron wrote it down. On stone."

"On a napkin," Annabeth corrected. "But it counts."

They led the three to the end of the Poseidon table where Silena had saved spots and Tyson had built a small fortress out of bread rolls. Thalia slid in with Annabeth on one side, Clarisse on the other, making a wall of big siblings around the littles. Grover squeezed in with Ella perched on his horns, reciting the menu.

"Grilled cheese! Tomato soup! Ambrosia squares for dessert but only if you are not dying! Ella loves tomato soup! Soup is warm like a hug you can drink!"

Bea sat between Silena and Thalia and made herself very small, seal in her lap. Lucy sat next to Percy and stared at the offering fire.

"Do I have to do it?" she whispered to Percy. "What if I do it wrong?"

"You can't do it wrong," Percy said. "Watch me. I mess it up all the time and the gods still eat."

He scraped a bit of grilled cheese into the bronze brazier. "For Poseidon. Thanks for — for my sisters and my brother and for holding us up." The smoke curled sweet.

Annabeth went next. "For Zeus. Thanks for Thalia. And for — for letting me find my own way." She glanced at Thalia, who blinked fast and looked away.

One by one the new kids offered crumbs, shyly. Milo muttered, "For Hermes, I guess, until — you know." Lucy whispered, "For whoever. Thanks for the van not crashing." Bea stood on tiptoes and dropped in a tiny corner of bread without a word, but her lips moved. Silena squeezed her shoulder.

Then they ate. Grover ran through camp rules with Ella interrupting, and Lucy told him his pipes sounded like a dying goose, which made the table erupt.

Milo, emboldened by laughter and soup, turned to Percy and Annabeth, who were sitting close enough that their knees touched under the table and sharing a cup of nectar without thinking about it. Lucy had been watching them all morning — the way Percy saved Annabeth the corner brownie, the way Annabeth fixed Percy's crooked necklace without asking, the way they finished each other's directions.

"So," Milo said innocently, "are you two dating?"

Silence at the immediate table. A spoon clattered somewhere.

Percy choked on his soup. Annabeth inhaled a crumb and started coughing. Both of them went scarlet — Percy to the tips of his ears, Annabeth to the roots of her curls — and stared at Milo, then at each other, then away very fast.

"What — we — I —" Percy managed.

"We're — that's —" Annabeth said at the same time.

Thalia, who had been drinking lemonade, set her cup down with immense big-sister satisfaction and slung an arm around each of them, pulling them together until their shoulders squished.

"Yes," Thalia announced to the new kids. "They are disgustingly dating. They've been disgustingly dating as long as I've known him. He writes her terrible poems. She keeps them in her notebook and pretends they're good. He drools on her shoulder during war councils. She lets him. It's gross. You're welcome here, but you have to get used to it. It's part of camp orientation now."

"Thalia!" Annabeth squeaked, covering her face. Her ears were on fire.

"Terrible poems?" Lucy leaned forward, delighted. "Like what?"

"No," Percy said quickly. "No poems. No — Thalia, I will tell Jason about the beagle toast—"

"Do it and you die," Thalia said cheerfully. "Percy sulks when she spends all afternoon with a model instead of him. He asks whether holding hands can count as a study break."

The whole table was laughing now, even Bea giggling behind her seal. Silena patted Annabeth's back while Annabeth hid behind her hands.

"Stopppp," Annabeth groaned, but she was laughing too, peeking through her fingers at Percy. Percy grinned back, sheepish and soft.

"It's true," Percy admitted to Milo and Lucy. "I like her. A lot. She's my —" He hesitated, suddenly shy in front of three wide-eyed kids. "She's my best friend. And my girlfriend. Both."

Annabeth lowered her hands. Her gray eyes were bright. "He's my best friend too," she said quietly. "And my boyfriend. Even if he snores like a pig and steals my fries."

"I steal fries out of love," Percy said solemnly.

"Ella loves love!" Ella cried, flapping. "Love is a feather that sticks! Ella will write a pamphlet! DATING AT CAMP: BLUSHING AND ALSO HOLDING HANDS!"

"Please don't," Annabeth and Percy said together, which made everyone laugh harder.

Milo nodded wisely. "My brother has a girlfriend. They do that blush thing too."

"You have a brother?" Thalia asked, seizing the opening like the good big sister she was. "Mortal or—?"

"Mortal. He's fourteen. He thinks I'm at boarding school." Milo's brave face slipped. "I miss him."

The table quieted as Milo said he missed his mortal brother and Lucy whispered she missed her mom. Bea pressed her seal tighter.

Annabeth covered Lucy's hand. "It's okay to miss her. I miss my family every day too, but you'll have people here."

"Express satyr mail!" Grover said, saluting with a breadstick.

Silena gathered Bea close as Bea whispered she missed her grandma who braided her hair. Silena rebraided Bea's hair with blue string while Percy made his bread rolls march and Annabeth folded a napkin owl for Lucy.

Thalia caught Annabeth watching and mouthed, *told you.* Annabeth mouthed back, *big-sister certified.*

---

They did the woods edge, the stables, the climbing wall, then the beach.

The beach was Percy's idea.

The camp beach curved white and bright, the Sound glittering, the wind clean. This was Poseidon territory, where Percy felt most himself, where Clarisse and Silena went quiet in the way they only did near water.

The new kids toed the foam shyly. Milo skipped a stone. Lucy chased a crab. Bea stood at the edge with her shoes off, letting water cover her toes, smiling for the first time all day — a real smile, wide and startled.

"Come on," Percy said, wading in to his ankles. He held out his hands to Milo and Lucy. "First lesson from the sea family. You have to let the water know you."

"Is that real?" Milo asked skeptically, but he took Percy's hand.

"It's real to us," Silena said, taking Bea's hand and Lucy's other hand, forming a chain. Clarisse completed the circle, big and solid, nodding at Thalia and Annabeth to join. The eight of them stood in the shallows while small waves pushed and pulled.

"Close your eyes," Percy said. "Feel it pull. It pulls and it returns. That's what camp does. You leave, you come back. You get scared, you get held."

Annabeth closed her eyes and felt Percy's hand in hers, the cold tug of the Sound. They had gone from new kids who cried at night to big kids holding small hands in the surf.

They stood until Bea laughed when a wave splashed her knees, until Lucy kicked water at Percy, until Clarisse declared a splash war Silena won.

They walked back up the hill damp and sandy and tired in the good way, Bea riding on Thalia's back because her legs were short, Milo carrying Tyson's empty muffin tray like a trophy, Lucy chattering to Annabeth about books.

At the top of the hill, under the boundary pine with the boundary wards glowing, they stopped. The whole valley spread below — cabins with smoke curling, the dining pavilion, the arena, the forest darkening. Home.

Chiron waited there, as if he had known they would end up here. He smiled at the sandy, sleepy group.

"Well?" he asked. "What do you think, new ones? Will you stay?"

Milo looked at Percy. Lucy looked at Annabeth. Bea looked at Thalia and Silena and Clarisse, then buried her face in Thalia's neck and nodded hard.

"Yeah," Milo said. "Yeah, I think — yeah."

"Good," Chiron said gently. "Percy? Annabeth? Any wisdom for them on night one?"

Percy and Annabeth looked at each other. When they first arrived they had been the ones standing here shaking. Now they were the ones with answers, which felt strange and right.

"You will get lost," Annabeth said. "You will mess up. You will miss home. That's normal."

"And you will find people," Percy said. "Even when you don't expect it. Even when they punch you into a lake or tell you you drool." He grinned at Annabeth. "Especially then."

"And if you're scared at night?" Lucy asked small.

"Come find us," Thalia said promptly, setting Bea down but keeping her hand. "Any of us. Cabin One, Cabin Three. We're big siblings now. It's our job."

"Sea family and sky family," Silena added, linking arms with Clarisse and Percy. "We've got you."

Clarisse knelt one last time in front of Milo, Lucy, and Bea, fierce and tender. "Hey. Listen. I was scary today on purpose. Older kids are scary until you know us. But hear me: no monster, no god, no homesickness gets past us without going through me first. Okay? You're camp now. You're ours."

Milo's eyes shone. Lucy threw her arms around Clarisse impulsively, then froze like she had made a mistake. Clarisse, after half a second of shock, hugged her back hard enough to lift her off her feet.

"Careful, newbie," Clarisse muttered, voice thick. "You'll make me soft."

"You already are soft," Silena said fondly. "We all know."

As the sun dropped, the group headed down, Bea on Tyson's shoulders and Lucy on Clarisse's back, Milo walking between Percy and Annabeth.

"So you really held a sword backwards?" Milo asked Annabeth.

"Backwards and upside down," Thalia confirmed. "I have pictures."

"Do you really talk to fish?" Lucy asked Percy.

"They talk to me," Percy said. "I mostly say, thanks for the info, please don't eat my shoes."

"Are you really siblings?" Milo asked Clarisse and Silena and Percy. "All three? Same dad?"

"Same dad," Percy said, slinging an arm around each sister. "Poseidon. God of the sea, lord of horses, terrible at texting. These two are the best sisters in the world. Don't tell Thalia I said that."

"I heard that," Thalia said. "Annabeth is the best sister. Don't tell Jason."

"Jason knows," Annabeth said, taking Percy's free hand and lacing their fingers. She swung their hands a little, and Percy swung back, and they both smiled at the ground.

Milo watched their joined hands and grinned. "You guys are cute. Like, gross, but cute."

"Thank you," Percy and Annabeth said together, blushing again.

Thalia threw her head back and laughed. She pulled Annabeth in for a one-armed hug, then ruffled Percy's hair.

"Yeah, yeah," Thalia said to the new kids. "They're dating. They're in love. They blush every time anyone says it. You'll get used to it. Now who wants extra ambrosia at dinner? First-night rule: new kids get doubles."

Three small hands shot up. Ella shrieked with joy and dropped her pamphlets all over the hill. Grover tried to catch them and fell in the grass. Tyson laughed like thunder. Clarisse threatened to leave them all behind and waited. Silena handed out the last gummy fish.

Percy hung back with Annabeth at the top of the hill, hands still linked.

"We did okay?" Percy said.

"We remembered," Annabeth said.

They walked down together into the noise and light and smell of dinner, new kids ahead and old siblings around, sea and sky holding them all the same.

The sea remembers, Percy thought, echoing Ella. And so does camp. And so will they.
