## Chapter 80: The Second Dance

Spring deepened at Camp Half-Blood, familiar and entirely different.

The last cold patches retreated from the shaded edges of the strawberry fields. The bees grew louder over the rows. The lake held a deeper blue beneath the lengthening light. Dryads renewed the pale blossoms in their bark-brown hair and argued about whose flowers were brightest. Pegasi shed winter tufts that caught on the pasture fence and drifted when the wind hit. Down at the canoe lake another wave of deep-water flowers opened overnight, white buds trembling on the surface like held breath.

Percy Jackson was there before breakfast to see it, because he had been there earlier this spring, because it felt like tradition now.

He was thirteen. He had grown three inches since last spring, enough that his camp shirt finally fit in the sleeves and Amphitrite said on an Iris-message that his voice was doing something interesting. His black hair was shaggy again. Annabeth had threatened to cut it with her architect scissors again. Tyson had offered to comb it with sea gel again. Some things grew without changing.

He crouched on the dock in his jacket, breath puffing, and skipped a stone sidearm and low the way Tyson had taught him. Four hops. Five hops. The naiads surfaced, clapped, told him the lake still liked him because he said please to the water.

"Please," Percy said to the water, out of habit from Atlantis, and skipped another. Six hops.

Mrs. O'Leary, his huge hellhound, thumped her tail against the dock planks and watched each stone with total faith that one day he would throw something she could chase into the lake. Percy scratched behind her ear.

"Another dance," he told her. "Same dock. Same flowers."

Mrs. O'Leary sneezed.

Before the first dance this spring he had crouched on this exact dock and panicked for three days about asking Annabeth to the Spring Dance. He had forgotten speeches. He had knocked over berry juice. He had needed Clarisse La Rue to teach him a box step in secret in the archery range while threatening him with boots. He had stepped on Annabeth's toes twice in one song and on his own pride about eleven times.

Now he and Annabeth had been boyfriend and girlfriend for more than a year. They had their own bench by the lake with initials carved small underneath where Chiron pretended not to see. Thalia, Annabeth's older sister — both of them daughters of Zeus, the same stubborn chin, Annabeth's gray eyes and Thalia's electric-blue ones — had threatened to throw him off Half-Blood Hill six times.

It was small things that stacked like stones. Percy saved her the corner brownie. Annabeth saved him the extra pencil. When his Atlantis algebra would not stay still, she redrew it in straight mortal lines. When she stayed up too late and got cranky, he brought lemonade and sat whittling until she admitted she was tired.

And this morning, watching the white buds open one by one in thin sun, Percy felt the same flip in his stomach as before the first dance, but steadier. Not panic. Anticipation.

The notice would go up today. He knew the words by heart.

---

The notice went up Monday on the Big House porch in Chiron's neat ink, exactly on schedule.

SECOND SPRING DANCE
Big House — Saturday, 7 o'clock
Music by Apollo Cabin
Punch and Cookies Provided
Nice Clothes Encouraged, Armor Discouraged

Underneath, Travis Stoll had added in pencil, just as before: BRING A DATE (OR DON'T. WE HAVE PUNCH.) Connor had added underneath that in smaller pencil: PUNCH IS A DATE IF YOU BELIEVE.

Percy stood staring at it with his hands in his pockets, Mrs. O'Leary panting beside him.

Grover came up behind him, horns a little taller than last spring, polished for the occasion, a mouthful of tin can lid in his cheek. He took one look at the sign, one look at Percy, and grinned so wide his goat eyes crinkled.

"Dude," Grover said. "You're staring again."

"I'm remembering," Percy said.

"At the first dance you stared and spiraled. You put armor on backward. You poured juice on cereal."

"I did not pour juice on cereal," Percy said. "That was one time. And it was apple juice. It was almost cider."

"It was milk-level wrong," Grover said. "So. Big ask? Big plan? Arrow past her head?"

Percy snorted. "I am not shooting anything past her head. Her sister would kill me. Besides, we're already — you know."

"Yeah. You're dating. Two hundred breakfasts."

"Right. But I can't just assume," Percy said, echoing himself from the first dance and hearing it. He smiled a little. "I have to ask her properly. For real. Even if we know."

Grover nodded sagely and swallowed his can lid. "Growth. Chiron would cry. So what's the plan? Flowers? Speech? Sunset bench?"

"No speech," Percy said. "Speeches vanish. Just plain. And early. No three-day panic this time."

"Wow," Grover said. "Who are you and what did you do with Kelp Head?"

"Tyson combed the panic out with sea gel," Percy said solemnly, and they both laughed, and Mrs. O'Leary barked because laughing meant good.

Across the porch, the vines Chiron had drawn around the notice for the first dance were still faintly visible under the new ink, and Percy touched one with a fingertip. Another dance. Same setup. He could feel the difference in his own shoulders. At the first dance his hands had sweated just reading the word DANCE.

"Hey," Grover said, quieter, nudging him with an elbow. "You good? You look — good. Steady."

Percy shrugged, ears a little warm. "Clarisse made me count. One-two-three. It stuck."

---

At lunch the pavilion curtains were tied back, same as before. Little jars of wildflowers sat on the tables.

Percy glanced at the Zeus table where Annabeth sat with Thalia. Annabeth's blond hair was in a neat braid. She wore the light blue sweater Silena had given her in the fall. She talked with her hands, explaining dance traffic flow. Thalia stole grapes off her plate. They looked like sisters, which they were, daughters of Zeus.

"You are staring again," rumbled Tyson happily beside him.

Tyson, visiting for dance week with leave from the undersea forges, was Percy's older half-brother, son of Poseidon, eight feet tall with one big brown eye, was eating spaghetti with a spoon. A noodle hung from his chin, same as before. Some traditions must be protected.

"I'm thinking," Percy said, same as before.

"You are thinking about asking Annabeth to the dance," Tyson said, beaming. "Grover told Juniper. Juniper told Ella. Ella told the harpies, but this time the harpies promised not to tell Annabeth because before the first dance she pretended to be surprised and this time she wants to be actually surprised about the timing."

Percy put his forehead on the table, same as before. "Great. No secrets at camp."

"No secrets," Tyson agreed cheerfully, patting his back hard enough to rattle cups. "But this time you will not be nervous for three days, right? You will ask smooth like Atlantean eel princes? They bow and offer a pearl and do not stammer."

"Atlantean eel princes stammer," Percy muttered into the wood. "I met one. He stammered."

"He did stammer," Tyson conceded. "But he still got a dance. You will get a dance too. I have already sung to the flowers. They are ready."

Percy lifted his head. "You already asked about sea flowers?"

"Pink ones. Like sunrise. I asked the palace gardeners for permission. They will send them if they are in season." Tyson pressed huge hands to his chest. "I did not eat them this time either. I ate kelp instead. It was hard."

Across the room Annabeth looked up and waved two fingers, same as before. Percy waved back. He did not knock over his juice cup this time. Grover caught nothing because there was nothing to catch, and looked faintly disappointed.

"Real smooth," Grover whispered anyway, out of tradition.

"Shut up," Percy said fondly.

---

Percy did not spend three days trying to ask this time.

He spent three hours deciding when, and then he just did it.

Monday after dinner the sky was pink and gold. They walked toward the lake together. Annabeth talked about dock cross-bracing, drawing force arrows in the air. Percy listened because he liked listening to her think.

At their bench they sat shoulder to shoulder.

Percy turned a little to face her. He sat up straight without having to be poked with a spear butt. His hands were steady.

"Hey, Wise Girl?" he said. His voice did not crack this time. It did something interesting on the second syllable the way his mom said, but it did not crack.

Annabeth looked up. There was pencil lead on her cheek, same as before. Some traditions must be protected.

"Yeah?" she said.

Percy had no speech. He had learned speeches vanished. He just said it plain, looking at her, not at his shoes.

"Will you go to the Spring Dance with me? As my date? I know we're already together, but I wanted to ask for real. Before the first dance I asked Thursday at sunset. This time is Monday at sunset. Improved delivery time."

She blinked. Then her face lit like sunrise over water, same as before, brighter because now he recognized the exact second it happened.

"Percy," she said. "Yes. Instant yes. No three-day delivery delay required."

"You sure?" he teased, echoing the first dance. "You've had your dress planned since February again? Silena helping again?"

Annabeth laughed, loud and happy. "Seaweed Brain, I've had the dress for this dance planned since the first dance ended. Silena started sketching while we were still eating oatmeal cookies. Girls plan ahead. Boys ask smoothly on Mondays after a few weeks of practice, apparently."

"Did I ask smoothly?" Percy said. "Rate my smoothness. Be honest. I can take it."

"Nine out of ten," Annabeth said, bumping his shoulder. "Minus one point because you said delivery time like I'm a package."

"Fair," Percy said. "I'll take nine."

They sat shoulder to shoulder while the sky went rose then lavender. Percy's hand brushed hers once — not three times — before Annabeth laced her fingers through his and held on like it was natural.

It was. More natural than it had felt before the first dance. The extra practice since the first dance had worn the dancing nerves smoother.

"At the first dance you said I did it right," Percy said quietly, swinging their joined hands a little. "Even though I panicked."

"You did do it right," Annabeth said. "You showed up and tried. That's what mattered. This time you showed up and didn't panic. That's growth. Chiron will put it in your permanent record. Excellent posture. Practicing."

Percy laughed. "One-two-three."

"One-two-three," she agreed, and leaned her head on his shoulder for exactly three seconds, sweet and simple, before sitting up because patrol walked by and Thalia wolf-whistled from the path.

---

Percy celebrated for one hour before realizing his next problem, same as before but smaller.

He knew how to dance now. Sort of.

Clarisse's secret lessons had stuck. He could box step without killing anyone. He could slow dance without hunching. He could count in his head instead of out loud. But the first dance had taught him that knowing steps in an empty archery range and doing them in a crowded Big House with jellyfish lanterns and Mr. D guarding punch were different sports.

Also, Tyson needed help.

"I want to dance this time," Tyson announced Tuesday morning in Cabin Three, standing huge in the middle of the floor in pajama pants with anchors on them, holding a cloth bundle of sea blooms already misted by Chiron. The fountain in the corner gurgled. "At the first dance I watched and ate cookies and held Mrs. O'Leary's leash during line dances. This time I want to dance. Silena says everyone should dance once."

Percy sat up in his bunk. "Silena's dancing this time?"

"She is teaching," Tyson said proudly. "She says Clarisse hides ballroom like a sword in a sheath and this time the sheath opens. We are all dancing. You will help me practice? You count good now."

Percy grinned. "Yeah. I'll help. But if Clarisse is teaching openly, the world is ending."

"The world is growing," Tyson corrected, very wise. "Like you. Nine out of ten smoothness."

Percy threw a pillow at him.

They met in the same empty archery range after dinner, but this time it was not secret.

Clarisse was already there in boots, unwrapping sea-salt chocolate Percy had brought. Silena stood beside her holding a little speaker.

"My two favorite Poseidon boys," she called. "Come on. No secrets this time."

Clarisse scowled, but it was performative now. "If you tell anyone I got an A in mortal school ballroom, I'll bury you behind the forge," she said to Tyson, who nodded solemnly.

"I will tell everyone you got an A because you worked hard and are graceful like a shark," Tyson said.

"That's worse," Clarisse said. "Don't."

Silena laughed and elbowed her. It was no secret at camp that Clarisse and Silena had gotten close over the year — unlikely sisters, war and love, spear polish and lip gloss, leaving notes for each other beside the spear rack and the mirror in Cabin Three. Silena had a way of making people brave about soft things. Clarisse had a way of making people brave about hard things. Together they were formidable.

"Okay," Silena said, clapping. "Box step review. Percy, show Tyson. You've got this."

Percy stepped forward, stood straight, held his hands up like a beach ball, not strangling it. He heard Clarisse's voice in his head — don't hunch, you're not ashamed of her — and Chiron's — be kind, be steady — and Tyson's — sing if nervous, but only inside your head.

"Right back," he said, demonstrating slowly. "Left side. Right together. Left forward. Right side. Left together. Draw a box. One-two-three."

He did it without looking at his feet. He looked over Tyson's shoulder, not creepy, just right. He counted steady.

Tyson tried, enormous sneakers careful on grass. He went the wrong way and nearly sat on a target. Mrs. O'Leary wove between their legs trying to join, same as before.

"Other right!" Clarisse barked, delighted despite herself. "No wonder Poseidon keeps you both from naval formations."

Silena played a staticky waltz and demonstrated with Clarisse, and Percy grinned, because they were good.

Clarisse, huge scuffed boots, light on her feet. Silena, smooth and smiling, calling steps gently.

"You're good," Percy said again, same as before.

"Shut up," Clarisse said, pleased anyway, same as before. "Hands up. Tyson, you're leading. I'm sorry, Silena."

What followed was comedy, but kinder. Tyson stepped wrong. Percy showed footwork, patient.

Silena beamed. "See? You're a teacher now."

Percy shrugged, ears warm. "Adequate. Clarisse said adequate once. Never forget."

Clarisse snorted. She pulled something from her pocket and tossed it at him — a spare chocolate. "You're better than adequate now, Jackson. Don't let it go to your head."

Then she turned to Tyson and Silena. "You two — you're good people. Don't be idiots Saturday. Dance at least twice."

"We will," Silena promised, squeezing her arm. "All three of us. Promise?"

Clarisse hesitated, the old embarrassment flickering. Then she nodded, short. "Yeah. All three. Someone's gotta keep Tyson from eating the decorations."

"I will not eat decorations," Tyson said dignified. "Only cookies. And once a streamer, by accident."

Silena laughed and linked arms with both of them, war and love and sea, and Percy watched with his hands in slow-dance shape, grinning when a firefly landed on his sleeve like applause, same as before.

One-two-three, he told the firefly. It blinked and drifted off toward the lake. It was strange how much easier counting felt when he was helping somebody else.

Before they left, Tyson stayed in the range and tried the steps by himself. Percy watched from the bench, where Clarisse had sat to retie a bootlace.

The dance looked different when Tyson did it alone. His movements were precise, almost delicate, the way they were when he checked a gear before installing it. He counted under his breath and moved one foot at a time, careful not to scrape the chalked lines.

"Can I ask you something?" Percy said.

"Yes, brother."

"Why do you want to dance this time?"

Tyson stopped. "Because it is fun."

"Sure. But you watched the first one."

"Watching was fun too."

"Then what changed?"

Tyson sat down beside him, making the bench creak. "Last time I thought if I danced I would break something. I am big. People are small. You know this."

Percy nodded.

"I know I can hold things gently," Tyson continued. "I do not always know whether other people know. Some look at me like I am a rock falling off a cliff."

Clarisse tightened the lace and looked over. Percy could see she was listening, but for once she didn't have a joke prepared.

"So this time," Tyson said, "I want to ask someone. If they say yes, then I dance. If they say no, then I dance by myself or watch. I have learned."

"Who did you want to ask?" Percy said.

Tyson thought about it. "Silena is my sister. She promised she would teach. Clarisse said all three of us should dance. I would like that. And maybe I would like to ask Nyssa, because she helped me with the forge work and I think she is funny."

"Nyssa is a good choice," Percy said. "But you should ask her. Not send Clarisse to tell her."

Clarisse snorted. "I wasn't going to."

Percy raised his eyebrows.

"I wasn't," she repeated. "And it's not my job to find my brother a dance partner. He knows how to use words."

Tyson grinned. "Yes, sister Clarisse."

He went toward the forge, where Nyssa was checking the evening equipment list. Percy followed far enough to make sure the path was clear but stopped before they reached her. Tyson asked if he could speak to her for a minute. When she said she was busy, he waited.

After a while she came out holding a measuring tape and a pencil.

"What's up, Tyson?"

"Would you like a dance on Saturday? At the Big House? I know I am very big. I can dance slowly."

Nyssa looked surprised. "Thanks for asking. I don't want a formal partner. I'm going to be helping with the lights most of the evening."

Tyson nodded at once. "Okay."

"But," she added, "if I get a break and there's a group song, I'd be happy to dance in the group."

Tyson's expression brightened. "I would like that."

"Then see you there." She glanced at Percy, who was trying very hard not to be visible. "And tell your brother I can hear him breathing behind that wall."

Percy emerged. "I wasn't doing anything."

"You were hovering."

"That's a hobby."

Tyson was laughing as they walked away. Percy liked that the answer hadn't hurt him. He had asked, received a real answer, and still had a plan for Saturday. It was less dramatic than the movies made it look.

They returned to the range where Silena was putting away the music box. Tyson told her what had happened. She listened without interrupting.

"Then we'll do one song together," she said. "But I want you to be comfortable on the floor too. We can't rehearse everything."

"What if I step wrong?"

"Then you stop and start again. Just like the forge."

Tyson considered that. "At the forge, stopping is important when something is wrong."

"Exactly."

Annabeth arrived near the end with a rolled drawing of the Big House floor. She had been asked to check the dance layout because Chiron wanted a clear path between the door and the refreshments, and because the Stolls had proposed putting a chair directly in front of the only easy exit.

"That chair isn't staying," she said.

Percy looked at the map. "Is this for the dance or a siege?"

"Both benefit from sensible traffic flow."

She spread the drawing on the practice bench. The dancers would enter on one side of the room, the punch table would stand against the opposite wall, and the aisle nearest the doors would stay open. There was plenty of room for smaller campers to move along the edge without having to pass through the center.

Tyson leaned over the drawing. "Where will I stand?"

"Where you like, as long as you can move without blocking anyone." Annabeth tapped a space near the back of the dance floor. "This corner is good for practicing. It's away from the chairs and close to a clear path."

"Can I ask people not to push me?"

"Of course."

"Sometimes they forget."

"Then tell them."

He examined the drawing. "I think I would like to dance here at first. Then maybe the middle."

Percy watched Annabeth mark the spot with a small pencil circle. She didn't label it *Tyson's space* or make it a rule that nobody else could use it. It was simply a place Tyson had chosen for the first song.

"Will you be dancing too?" Tyson asked.

"With Percy," she said.

"Good. He has practiced a lot."

Percy groaned. "We are not telling the entire camp."

"We are," Clarisse called from the equipment racks. "I'm putting it in the announcements."

Annabeth laughed. She rolled up the drawing and slipped it into her satchel.

On the way back to Cabin Three, Tyson told Percy that he liked the practice better than the first dance had sounded. There had been mistakes, people saying no, a map that might need changes, and no guarantee the evening would be perfect.

"Good," Percy said. "That's what real dances are like."

"Have you been to many?"

"Two if you count the first one and the one we're about to go to."

"Then maybe I know as much as you."

Percy laughed. "Probably more."

Tyson went inside to hang up his clean dance shirt where nobody would spill lake water on it. Percy looked back toward the range, thinking about how often he'd wanted his brother to stay in camp because Percy needed the company. Tonight Tyson had made plans that belonged to him. It felt like the right way for a good week to begin.

---

While Percy taught, Annabeth had a nicer time, same as before but softer.

Silena had borrowed the Aphrodite back room from her friends Friday afternoon again. Lights strung. Lemonade out. Brushes by size, ribbons by shade.

"Nothing fancy," Annabeth said. "Just neat."

"When you talk with your hands," Silena and Thalia finished together from different corners, and everyone laughed.

Thalia lounged in the doorway again, arms crossed, self-appointed guard again, in black jeans and boots and a lightning-stitched top, her formal, with extra eyeliner. But this time she did not warn Keep it simple, she’ll trip on all those ribbons. This time Annabeth was thirteen, and Thalia just smiled soft when Silena twisted sections back and pinned them with the same tiny silver owl clips from last Christmas, plus a new pale yellow ribbon for spring.

When Silena turned the mirror, Annabeth whispered, "Oh," same as before.

It was still her. Just shinier. Gray eyes bigger. Hair soft around shoulders. A few weeks surer in the smile, with another evening she wanted to get right.

"You look beautiful," Silena said firmly. "Not grown-up beautiful. You beautiful. There's a difference. More you than at the first dance, if that's possible."

Thalia came close, tough face melting same as before. As sisters, daughters of Zeus, they shared the same chin and the same stubborn stare. Thalia squeezed her hand.

"You look like Mom would have wanted," she said quietly, same words, deeper. "Percy's going to forget how to count again."

"Thalia!" Annabeth blushed to her ears, same as before.

"What? It's tradition. He counts. One-two-three. Cute. Dorky, but cute. Nine out of ten smooth now, I hear."

Silena laughed and gave Annabeth a cookie and the same tiny vial smelling vanilla and rain. "One drop behind each ear. Not perfume. Just you after rain. Tradition."

Annabeth hugged her carefully. "Thank you. For everything. For the first dance too."

"That's what camp sisters are for," Silena said, eyes bright. "Now. Who's doing Thalia's eyeliner? She'll bite people without eyeliner."

"Okay, mush over," Thalia said, blinking fast same as before. "Same threat stands. Eyeliner or casualties."

---

Percy survived teaching. Now he needed a corsage.

The silver wristband from the first dance had stayed with Annabeth as her gift. Thalia had asked to borrow it for repair and redesign, and Percy had received permission to help attach new flowers. Annabeth had kept the sea blooms pressed in her notebook because they wilted after spring tide no matter how much mist Chiron lent. This time they wanted matching.

He found Thalia outside Cabin One Thursday. At the first dance she had dragged him onto the porch for a threatening talk. This time she saw the box and pulled him into a hug.

"No threats this time," she said. "You've been good to her. Steady."

Percy's throat tightened. "Thalia—"

"Don't get mushy," she said gruffly, letting go and ruffling his hair exactly as before. "Here."

She shoved a small box at him. Inside lay the first dance’s silver wrist band, polished, with a tiny owl stamped between waves and lightning, and two small vials.

"Double vial this time," Thalia said. "Annabeth helped redesign."

Percy clutched the box. "Small side up?"

"Small side up," Thalia confirmed. "And tell her she looks nice. Even smart girls like to hear it. Especially them. Same advice, still true."

"I will," Percy promised. "Every time."

Thalia punched his arm lightly, same as before. "If you get nervous, don't count out loud this time. Feel waves. You know waves."

"Everyone tells you everything?" Percy asked, smiling.

"I'm terrifying and huggable now," Thalia said. "It's a gift. Growth."

Tyson returned from meeting an approved Atlantean messenger at the saltwater beach, holding a cloth bundle high. Inside were sea blooms, pink veined gold, plus small white surface blossoms Juniper helped pick.

They fixed them onto the band with sea grass. Pink and silver and green and white.

"She will love it," Tyson said. "It matches her brave smart heart, now with extra growth. Now practice not counting. Feel waves!"

---

Saturday dawned bright. The Big House was closed for decorating. Apollo snuck in. Stolls snuck out with cookies.

Percy did not fail at normal. He held his book right-side up.

Grover was more nervous than Percy. At the first dance he had gone with Ella, the russet-feathered harpy. Juniper, his longtime dryad friend, had recently returned from an extended absence. Ella and Juniper had each asked Grover for clarity about what their relationships meant, and he had tried to answer without rushing either of them.

Silena talked to both girls Thursday. Now Saturday lunch, Grover sat with his head in his hands.

"Breathe," Percy told him. "Don't let everybody else choose the labels. Ask Ella and Juniper what they want, and tell them what you actually mean."

Grover lifted his head. "You think that works?"

"Annabeth says honesty works better than guessing. That doesn't mean they have to agree to share a dance."

In Aphrodite cabin, preparations were calmer, because Silena had already done the hard talk.

Annabeth spun, skirt flaring. She felt pretty and fizzy like cider.

Thalia wolf-whistled. "Round two. Still cute."

Annabeth threw a pillow, same as before. "Stop." She smiled so hard her face hurt, same as before.

A knock came. Grover in his grape-leaf bow tie, holding Ella's wing-tip. Ella's feathers shone with blossoms. Behind them stood Juniper.

"Pretty. Ella is pretty. Juniper says so too," Ella announced. "No choosing. Sharing dances. Is that allowed?"

Juniper stepped forward, eyes bright. "Ella's his sweetheart. I'm his herd. There's room. I don't want to be a problem. I want to be — family. Like Thalia and Clarisse and everyone. Can I walk with you? Not as a date. As — grove?"

Annabeth's heart melted. She crossed the room and hugged Juniper hard. "Of course you can walk with us. Swaying counts double this time. Tyson said."

Thalia untied nothing because Grover's shoes were already untied correctly, so she just fixed his bow tie and punched his shoulder. "Honesty, goat boy. Proud of you. No one cries unless happy crying."

Silena fussed over all three girls' blossoms while everyone watched, heart full. Family, bigger this time.

Then a softer knock. Everyone froze, same as before.

"Him," Thalia sang, same as before. "Places! Hair! Breath! He has the corsage! Bigger corsage! Open!"

---

Percy stood on the porch, heart hammering. He wore dark pants, white shirt sleeves rolled up. His palms sweated around the corsage box.

He knocked. Silena opened. There was Annabeth in yellow, hair half-up with owl clips.

Percy did not forget counting. He chose not to need it. He saw Annabeth in yellow with flowers on the hem, smiling like he was the only person alive.

"Hi," she said softly, same as before.

"Hi." His voice did something interesting but did not squeak. "You — you look really nice. Pretty. Pretty-nice. Both. Still true. More true."

Thalia snorted, same as before. Silena elbowed her, same as before.

Annabeth did not look away, cheeks pink, same as before. "Thanks. You look nice too. Tyson do your hair?"

"Yeah. Sea gel. Then I fixed it," he said loyally, nose wrinkling, and she laughed.

Percy thrust the box forward, same as before, steadier. "Corsage. Tyson got sea flowers from Atlantis again. Juniper helped with land ones. Thalia helped with band again. Chiron gave mist again. Can I put it on? Double vial this time."

Hands shaking only a little, he opened it. Sea blooms glowed pink-gold even in daylight, white blossoms fresh beside them.

Annabeth gasped, same as before, deeper. "Oh, Percy. It's bigger."

"Bigger love after the first dance," Percy quoted Tyson solemnly. "He said. I agree with the large brother."

She laughed, eyes shining. "Thank him and Juniper. Yes, put it on."

Tongue out in concentration, Percy slipped the band on her wrist. Perfect fit. Small side up. Waves and lightning and tiny owl glinting.

"Perfect," he whispered, same as before.

Annabeth admired it. "Best corsage ever again. Excellent load distribution. Great engineering. Now with redundancy. Two vials."

That was his Annabeth, romantic and structural. Relief flooded him, same as before, warmer.

Thalia loomed, same as before — then instead of two fingers to her eyes, she wrapped both arms around both of them and squeezed.

"Remember, Jackson," she said, then laughed. "Just kidding. Threat retired. Hug active. Have fun. Back by ten. Chiron's orders. Feel waves."

"Feel waves," Percy agreed, hugging back one-armed.

Grover, Ella and Juniper came out together, each having agreed to walk as friends. The six of them walked to the Big House as sun sank gold across grass, same path, larger party.

---

The Big House meeting room had become a garden indoors again. Streamers hung from beams. Jellyfish lanterns glowed. Tables held cookies and a crystal punch bowl Mr. D guarded.

"No punch without a cup," Mr. D warned each arrival. "No baptizing friends. Stolls, looking at you. Again."

Chiron stood at the door in his best jacket, horse half brushed shiny. "Percy, Annabeth. Lovely. Excellent posture. Still practicing?"

"Yes, sir," Percy said straighter. "Clarisse taught me. Then I taught Tyson. Don't tell. Actually, tell. She's open about it now."

"Lips sealed, then unsealed," Chiron twinkled. "Growth looks good on all of you."

Apollo set up. A fast song filled the room. Percy and Annabeth got punch and watched Ella teach.

"'Step, slide, step,'" Ella recited. "'No one drops Grover.'"

Grover tripped. Ella and Juniper caught him. They laughed.

Percy's nerves turned good-buzzy, same as before. Then music changed.

Austin strummed soft. Kayla lifted flute. A slow sweet melody drifted like water over stones — the same melody as the first dance, requested by Annabeth because tradition.

Chiron clapped. "Slow dance! Find partners! No dipping unless practiced! Stolls, means you! Again!"

Floor cleared. Travis bowed to Connor. Pollux found a partner. Dryads swayed with satyrs.

Percy turned to Annabeth, remembering: stand straight, don't hunch. He held out palm up as practiced eleven times before the first dance plus twenty times this week in Cabin Three mirror plus twice teaching Tyson.

"May I have this dance?" Voice steady, only warm.

Annabeth set her hand in his, corsage glowing pink-white on his fingers. "Yes. Instant yes."

They stepped on floor. Percy held one hand up like a beach ball, other lightly on her back, not grabbing. Annabeth's free hand rested on his shoulder. They left respectful space as Chiron taught. Close to talk, comfortable to move. Percy remembered Clarisse poking his spine — stand tall — and Chiron's talk — be kind, be steady — and Tyson's advice — sing if nervous, but only inside your head. Then he let all advice settle and felt waves.

"Feel waves," Annabeth whispered, smiling.

"Feel waves," Percy whispered back, swaying. The floorboards smooth under sneakers. Lantern light moving softly across her yellow ribbon. Someone's shoe squeaking. Someone laughing. Punch ladle clinking.

He did not step on her foot.

They circled slowly. Left, side, together. Sway. Her dress swirled. Sea flowers brushed his shirt salt-honey.

"You did good," Annabeth murmured, same as before. "Clarisse taught well. Then you taught well."

"Threatened me with boots," Percy said. "Motivating. Then I threatened Tyson with boots. Circle of life."

She laughed through his hand. "You smell like kelp and cookies again."

"You smell like rain again. Good spring rain. Plus vanilla."

Around them lanterns glowed. Vines swayed. Mr. D smiled faintly, tapping time. Chiron watched soft-handed, misty-eyed.

Nearby Grover and Ella and Juniper swayed in a careful trio, cheek to feather to blossom, Ella quoting, "'Danced until stars came out, and no one was unchosen,'" while Grover held Ella's wing like glass and Juniper's hand like grove, gentle both. His bow tie stayed tied. No one tripped. Juniper cried happy sap tears and Ella pretended not to cry and Grover cried openly because satyr empathy maxed out.

Percy caught his eye. Grover mouthed, SWAYING COUNTS DOUBLE.

It did.

Across the floor, Clarisse danced with Silena, open and unashamed. Then Tyson stepped in, and the three swayed in a circle — war and love and sea — while camp cheered.

Percy felt his chest ache happy. At the first dance Clarisse had hidden ballroom like a sword in a sheath. This time the sheath was open, and she was dancing with her sister and their brother under jellyfish light, and no one thought it was weird. Everyone thought it was right.

The song played on. Percy and Annabeth kept dancing, whole song, without stepping — not once on toes, not once wrong direction, not once counting out loud. When the guitar wobbled, Percy adjusted without thinking. When the flute ran fast, Annabeth squeezed his hand and they slowed together, listening to each other more than music. It was not perfect technique. Silena would later say their box drifted left. Clarisse would say Percy's leading was still unclear half the time. But they never broke hold. They never looked down. They breathed together, swayed together, smiled until cheeks ached.

When the last guitar note hung like firefly, same as before, Percy did something new.

He leaned in, slow enough she could move away, and kissed her cheek — soft, quick, respectful, right where vanilla-rain scent lived. Chiron-approved, Thalia-approved, practiced between dances.

Annabeth went pink to her ears and grinned so wide her owl clips slipped. "Smooth," she whispered. "Ten out of ten this time."

"Growth," Percy whispered back, ears burning red, heart hammering happy.

Clapping. Apollo launched fast song. Stolls stampeded back. Percy and Annabeth stepped off hand-linked, flushed, same as before, new.

"Too much stepping?" Percy asked, leading to cool window chairs, same as before.

"Zero times," Annabeth said, holding up glowing wrist. "Zero. Whole song. No steps. Revenge step declined."

"Clarisse says unclear leading still," Percy said fondly.

"Clarisse says everything's your fault," Annabeth said fondly, same as before. "Love language. But this time she danced three times, so she's softening."

They split warm oatmeal chocolate chip, same as before. Outside evening deepened blue, stars pricking, fireflies over strawberries, same as before.

Annabeth lifted glowing wrist. "Tyson sang again?"

"About a brave little clam's bigger cousin," Percy said.

"Tell him clam family did good. And tell Juniper land flowers perfect."

She leaned head on his shoulder, same as before, longer this time — ten seconds, tired-happy, friends and more. Percy sat still, smiling cheeks aching, same as before, deeper.

Thalia arrived with four punches this time, gave three away — to Percy, Annabeth, and Grover passing by. Menace gone, almost soft in lanterns, huggable upgrade active.

"How was it?" she asked, straddling chair backward, same as before. "Count out loud? Step twice?"

"No," Annabeth said, glowing. "Felt waves. Whole song. No steps. Cheek kiss. Ten out of ten."

Thalia stared, then whooped so loud Mr. D shushed her. "My babies! Growth! No hill-throwing! Hug instead!" She hugged them both hard enough to slosh punch. "I'm so proud I could electrocute something with joy."

Silena floated by checking hair, beaming, same as before. "Adorable. Box step? Drifted left, but perfect from door. And cheek kiss? Chiron cried a little. I saw."

"Perfect from door," Percy admitted, echoing the first dance. "I'll take it."

Evening blurred warm. Percy survived line dance on beat this time. Tyson shook the floorboards. Clarisse and Silena kicked in unison with him. Chiron danced with Mrs. O'Leary. Ella recited the set list and Grover and Juniper applauded.

At quarter to ten Chiron rang bell, same as before.

"Last song! Lovely evening! Clothes hung up, cups in bins, no unsupervised pegasus rides! Second dance running, no punch baptisms, I am so proud!"

Last song slowed softer still, same melody reprised. Percy and Annabeth danced again, easier than ever. No steps, no revenge steps, just swaying mid-floor hand in hand while music played round them, laughing quietly about nothing, foreheads nearly touching.

When it ended they thanked Apollo and walked into spring night with Thalia steps behind. Porch boards cool. Frogs singing. Windows glowing gold behind them.

Air cooled grass and night flowers. Torches and fireflies lit path. Owl called, same as before.

At the fork to Zeus's cabin Thalia tactfully checked for monsters behind a bush where she could see, same as before. Crickets stitched dark. Torch sputtered. Path stones still held little daytime warmth through shoe soles. Annabeth's corsage gave off soft pink-white glow, lighting her fingers from below like a lantern, fuller light this time.

"Thanks for going," Percy said, scuffing dirt, same as before. "Had fun. More fun. Same fun plus growth."

"Me too," Annabeth said. Owl clips glinted torchlight, corsage glowing faint. "You did it right. Ask smooth. Flowers double. Dance whole song. Cheek kiss. All. Ten."

Percy's ears burned. "Clarisse dancing open. Tyson flowers double. Thalia hug. Silena — you look great. Still. More."

"But you showed up and tried," Annabeth said simply, same as before. "That's what matters. You just try smoother now."

She squeezed his hand firm-warm, same as before — then stood on tiptoes and kissed his cheek back, quick and soft, smelling kelp and cookies.

"Goodnight, Seaweed Brain," she said, same as before, brighter.

"Goodnight, Wise Girl," he said, same as before, steadier.

Thalia emerged, arms round both of them at once, pointing at Percy with fake stern. "Ten out of ten! No hill-throwing! Hugs only! Watching you with pride, Jackson!"

Percy laughed, waved, waited till Cabin One window glowed yellow in blue night. Then turned to Cabin Three counting without meaning — feel waves, feel waves — sea-flower and land-blossom scent on shirt, crickets singing, spring soft over camp.

Best dance ever. Stepped zero times.

He passed the forge, dark now. He passed cabins glowing warm. Above stars burned steady.

Another dance. Same setup. Same dock, same notice, same porch, same punch, same song. Same boy asking same girl. Same sisters hugging. Same brothers carrying flowers without eating them.

Different boy. Steadier hands. Smoother ask. Whole song without stepping. Cheek kiss given and returned. Threats retired to hugs. Secret lessons become open dances. Triangle become trio. Two vials instead of one. Yellow dress instead of blue. Zero steps instead of two.

Growth, Percy thought, walking home under steady stars, hand tingling where Annabeth had held it, cheek warm where she had kissed it. Chiron would put it in the permanent record.

One-two-three, the crickets sang. Feel waves, Percy answered, and went home.
