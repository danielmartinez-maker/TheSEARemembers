## Chapter 107: The Words We Choose

Percy had said *I love you* to Annabeth before.

He remembered saying it in their old life, in quiet moments and in danger. He remembered saying it here too, after the eclipse disturbance struck the Hall and Thalia helped shield Annabeth. She had remembered loving him then, even through the terror. The words had never been lost.

The old life they remembered and their new days here both mattered. He knew what that love was worth; he wanted to say what it meant in an ordinary moment too.

The thought followed him all morning.

It followed him through training, where he missed three easy blocks. It followed him to the dining pavilion, where he forgot to take his plate to the fire. It followed him through the first half of Annabeth explaining an improvement to the shrine's drainage before he realized she had stopped talking.

"You're somewhere else," she said.

Percy blinked. "Sorry. I was listening."

"What did I just say?"

"Something about rainwater and the north wall."

"That was five minutes ago." She closed her sketchbook. "What's going on?"

He could have lied. He could have said he was tired. But he'd promised they would talk instead of guessing, and this was the sort of thing that promise was for.

"I remember telling you I loved you before," he said. "After the eclipse. I thought I was going to lose you, and I said it. We both remembered. I keep thinking about how different it feels to say it when nothing terrible is happening."

Annabeth's expression turned thoughtful. "I remember that conversation."

"I know. I just don't know what to do with it now. We decided to make this ours, not assume the older memories decide everything. So I don't want to say it because I said it before. But I also want to tell you what I feel in the quiet days, instead of only reaching for those memories."

She studied him. "Are you asking me to say it back?"

"No. I'm trying to explain why I've been distracted."

"Okay." Annabeth took a breath. "What does the word mean to you now? Not then. Now."

Percy considered. He loved the girl sitting beside him, the same person who shared his memories and kept growing through new days. The way she argued with her own sketches. The way she checked the shrine's drainage after every rain. The way she let Clarisse tease her and then got Clarisse back twice as hard. The way she made a place for him at the table.

"It means I want to keep showing up," he said. "I want to know the person you're becoming. I want you to know me as I am now, not just as the person you remember me being. And when you're scared or happy or angry, I want to be someone you can tell."

Annabeth's eyes warmed. "That sounds like love."

"It feels like it."

She reached for his hand. "Then you can say it when you want. You don't need to wait for me to have the exact same words ready. But don't use it to ask for an answer."

"I won't."

"And if I say it back, it will be because I mean it now. I’m still the girl who said it then, and I mean it here too."

"That's what I want."

Annabeth leaned her shoulder against his. "Good. Then tell me what you were going to say before the storm memory got stuck in your head."

"Something about your drainage system being impressive."

"It is impressive."

"I know."

She laughed, and the moment loosened.

---

Clarisse found Percy later, hauling timber toward the shrine.

"You look like you swallowed a fishbone," she said.

"Maybe I did."

"Was it a small fish?"

"I don't know."

She took the other end of the plank. "That answer isn't like you. What's wrong?"

Percy almost told her. Then he remembered how Clarisse had reacted the last time she saw someone being vulnerable without warning. She'd sit with it, but she didn't always want to have it pushed at her.

"I was thinking about Annabeth," he said.

"You think about Annabeth all the time. That's not new."

"I want to tell her I love her. I have before, here and in our old life, but today I want to say what it means when there’s no crisis. I don't want the memory to decide what happens now."

Clarisse adjusted her grip on the plank. "Then don't make it a big performance. Tell her what you just told me."

"What if she doesn't say it back?"

"Then she doesn't say it back. You can still mean what you said."

Percy looked at her. "You're surprisingly good at this."

"I have Silena. She makes me practice talking about feelings so I don't scare every person I like away."

"Do you?"

"Scare people away? Obviously."

"No. Like someone."

Clarisse narrowed her eyes. "Carry the plank, Percy."

He did.

---

Annabeth had been waiting near the shrine when Percy returned. She was sitting on the low wall, sketchbook open on her knees, pretending to work while watching the path.

Percy set down the plank and wiped his hands on his shorts. "Can I tell you something?"

"You can."

He stood in front of her. He had thought about it all afternoon, rehearsing different versions. The words he settled on were simpler.

"I love you," he said. "Not because I remember saying it before. Not because I expect you to say anything back. I love the person you are with me now. I love how you make room for people. I love that you notice when someone needs help, even when you're trying not to show that you noticed. I love that you argue with the weather when it ruins your plans."

Annabeth's mouth twitched. "The weather is often unreasonable."

"I know. I love that too." He took a breath. "You don't have to answer. I just wanted you to know what I mean when I say it."

Annabeth set her sketchbook aside and stood. She was close enough that he could see the faint pencil smudge on her cheek.

"I love you too," she said. "Not because I remember. I do remember, but that's not why. I love the boy who asked what I wanted instead of assuming. I love the one who lets me be angry and doesn't try to smooth it over. I love how you listen when I talk about things you don't understand yet. And I love that you think a blue shell can be a good present."

"It was a good present."

"It was."

She kissed him. Percy put his hands lightly at her shoulders, giving her plenty of room to pull away. She stayed. The kiss was warm and unhurried, a little clumsy when his nose bumped hers, and full of everything they'd just said.

When they separated, Annabeth's smile trembled at the edges.

"That was my answer," she said.

"I liked it."

"Good."

She kissed him again, shorter this time, then rested her forehead against his. Percy closed his eyes and let the quiet of the camp settle around them.

---

They told their friends at dinner.

Not with a speech. Annabeth simply sat beside Percy at the table and leaned into him while Silena passed around a bowl of roasted vegetables. Percy rested his hand over hers on the bench.

Clarisse noticed. So did Thalia. Silena had already guessed.

"Did you two have another very earnest conversation about the thing?" Clarisse asked.

Percy choked on his water. "What thing?"

"The word that starts with L and isn't 'lake.'"

Annabeth looked at her. "How did you know?"

"You both look like you just finished a very long conversation and got the answer you wanted. Also, Percy has been smiling at his plate for ten minutes."

Silena smiled. "I'm happy for you."

Thalia watched Annabeth carefully. "Are you happy?"

"Yes," Annabeth said. "I am."

Thalia nodded. "Then I'm happy too."

Clarisse lifted her cup. "To the word that starts with L."

"To love," Silena corrected.

"I was being subtle."

"No, you weren't."

They clinked cups. Percy felt Annabeth's hand find his beneath the table. He squeezed it once.

Once again in this altered world, the word held their quiet present as firmly as their remembered life. It belonged here too, at a crowded camp table, between stolen bread and Clarisse's teasing, with Annabeth's fingers threaded through his.

---

That night, Percy walked Annabeth to the Zeus cabin.

At the door she turned back and kissed him once, sweet and certain. Then she leaned her cheek against his shoulder.

"I meant it," she said.

"I know."

"I might not say it every day."

"You don't have to."

"But I want you to know it's true."

"I do."

She kissed his cheek and slipped inside. Percy stayed at the door until he heard her laugh at something Thalia said.

Then he turned toward the Poseidon cabin, where Clarisse and Silena were waiting with a card game and no intention of letting him win.

He went in smiling.
