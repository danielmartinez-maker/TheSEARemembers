## Chapter 24: The Climbing Wall


Percy Jackson woke up knowing two things.

One: today was climbing wall day, which meant the whole camp would gather to watch people get chased by lava and scream in front of their friends.

Two: his left shoe was missing, and Grover was chewing on something that sounded suspiciously like leather.

"Grover," Percy said, sitting up in his bunk and squinting through the morning light slanting into Cabin Three. "Tell me you're not eating my shoe."

Grover froze. He was sitting cross-legged on the floor with Percy's sneaker halfway to his mouth. Crumbs — or whatever the goat equivalent of crumbs was — clung to his chin. His horns had grown in a little more over the last month, fuzzy nubs that he kept bumping on the low doorway and then pretending he meant to do that.

"I'm not eating your shoe," Grover said solemnly.

"You're holding my shoe next to your mouth."

"I'm... smelling it. For moral support."

"My shoe stinks like lake water and old socks. That's not moral support, that's a health hazard."

Grover sighed dramatically and handed the shoe back. "My mom says stress-chewing is a natural satyr coping mechanism. You wouldn't understand. You have excellent teeth and no urge to eat tin cans."

"I have the urge to throw you in the lake," Percy said, but he was grinning as he laced up both shoes. His hair was sticking up in twelve different directions. He tried to flatten it. It sprang right back. He gave up. The sea breeze coming through the open window smelled like salt and pine, and from somewhere up the hill came the distant rumble and hiss of the climbing wall warming up.

Beckendorf and the Hephaestus kids had been tuning it since dawn. Percy could hear the clank-clank-clank of metal, and every few minutes a deep WHOOMPH that made the windows rattle.

Camp-wide climbing wall day only happened once a month. Normally you just climbed with your cabin or whoever happened to be free after sword practice. But today was the big one — teams of three, bracketed, single elimination, with Silena Beauregard and Zoe Nightshade doing live commentary from the top tower through Charles Beckendorf's brass speaking trumpet, rigged with wind-nymph amplification so everyone on the hill could hear.

Percy splashed water on his face from the basin by the door. The water felt good. It always felt good, like the water was happy to see him. He didn't tell people that part because it sounded weird, even for camp.

"You nervous?" Grover asked, pulling on his fake feet sneakers over his hooves and tugging his cap down over his horns.

"About lava? No. Lava's great. Lava's my favorite," Percy said.

"Your voice went up an octave."

"My voice did not go up an octave."

"It went up like two octaves. It was practically dog-whistle range."

Percy threw a pillow at him. Grover caught it with his teeth out of pure reflex and then looked embarrassed.

"Okay, fine, I'm a little nervous," Percy admitted, tying his camp necklace tighter. It only had one bead so far — an Atlantis bead, with a trident and a wave. He touched it for luck. "Last time I did the wall I got lava in my pants. Literally in my pants. I had to jump in the lake and everyone saw my boxers with the sharks on them."

"I remember. I was there. I still have nightmares."

"Who's on our team again?"

"You, me, and Ella," Grover said. "Chiron posted it at breakfast."

"Ella? Ella the harpy? From the library?"

"She's fast, she's tiny, she's great at finding handholds nobody else sees, and she memorized the wall's maintenance manual after reading it once," Grover said. "Also she really wanted to be on a team that wasn't going to yell at her."

Percy nodded. That made sense. Ella was... a lot. She talked fast, quoted books in the middle of conversations, flapped when she got excited and knocked over scroll racks. But she was kind, and she remembered everything, and last week she'd saved Percy from failing a history quiz by whispering, "The Battle of Marathon, page forty-seven, third paragraph, 'the drakon columna—'" under her breath for twenty minutes straight.

"Who are we up against first?" Percy asked.

Grover winced.

"No."

"Yes."

"No, come on, who?"

"Annabeth, Thalia, and Clarisse."

Percy stared. "That's not a team. That's a war crime. Who made those teams?"

"I think Clarisse threatened the roster," Grover said. "Or Thalia bribed someone with electric shock pranks. Or Annabeth just planned it because she wanted a challenge."

Percy sat back down on his bunk. Hard.

Annabeth Grace. Daughter of Zeus. Blonde princess curls that she hated, gray eyes that saw everything, bronze knife she spun when she was thinking. Twelve years old and already scary smart. She could beat Percy at sparring if she got him thinking too much, and he could beat her if he got her laughing too much, and neither of them had figured out how to stop doing both at the same time.

And now they were on opposite teams on lava day, with the whole camp watching.

"This is fine," Percy said. "Totally fine."

"Sure," Grover said. "Also Silena said losers have to clean the Pegasus stables for a week."

"This is not fine."

---


Annabeth Grace loved plans the way Percy loved water.

Which was to say, completely, instinctively, and in a way that made other people roll their eyes.

She was sitting on the porch of Cabin One — Zeus's cabin, all white marble and humming ozone and tall ceilings that made thunder sound louder inside — with a wax tablet on her knees and a stylus behind her ear, sketching the climbing wall from memory.

Thalia Grace was braiding her own hair beside her, lightning crackling faintly between her fingertips every time she got bored. Which was every eight seconds.

"Okay," Annabeth said, tapping the tablet. "The wall is one hundred and twenty feet high, shifts every thirty seconds, four main faces. North face starts easy but avalanches at minute two. South face has the lava vents that open in sequence — left, middle, right, then all three. East face is all overhangs. West face looks safe and then drops you."

"Uh-huh," Thalia said. She was sixteen to Annabeth's twelve, tall, punk, black hair with a silver streak, ripped jeans jacket over her camp shirt even in the warming air. Safety pins in her ears shaped like tiny lightning bolts. She'd been at camp for years, running with Luke and her little sister before they finally reached the valley. Now she lived in Cabin One beside Annabeth, and she was Annabeth's big sister and biggest headache in equal measure.

"Are you listening?" Annabeth asked.

"Nope," Thalia said cheerfully.

"Thalia!"

"Annabeth! It's the lava wall! You climb, you dodge, you don't die, you ring the bell! Strategy is for capture the flag!"

"Strategy is for everything," Annabeth said, but she was smiling. She couldn't help it. Thalia had that effect — like standing too close to a storm. Scary and thrilling at once.

Clarisse La Rue stomped up the cabin steps at that exact moment, spear over her shoulder, red bandana tied tight, war paint from drills with her Ares friends still smeared on her cheek. She was thirteen, built like a battering ram, and grinning like she'd already won.

"Tell me the plan, Grace, so I can ignore it," Clarisse said, dropping onto the porch rail.

"Charming," Annabeth said.

"I try."

Annabeth flipped the tablet around. She'd drawn three stick figures labeled A, T, and C. C had a spear and was stabbing the wall. T had lightning bolts coming out of her head. A had a very large brain and, for some reason, Thalia had added a speech bubble that said "I READ A BOOK ABOUT THIS."

"Thalia, did you draw on my tablet?"

"Maybe."

Annabeth wiped it off with her thumb. "Okay. Here's the actual plan. Thalia, you're our sprinter. You're strongest on overhangs and you recover fastest from slips because —"

"Because I'm awesome," Thalia supplied.

"Because your upper-body strength scores are highest," Annabeth said. "You take point on the first shift. Clarisse, you're anchor. You're best at shoving — I mean, at defensive climbing. If anyone from the other team tries to cut us off or shake the holds, you hold position."

"I can shove," Clarisse agreed. "I love shoving. Shoving is my love language."

"And I'll navigate. I'll call shifts before they happen. I timed the wall last month. It runs on a pattern — thirty-two seconds, not thirty. Beckendorf leaves a two-second grace window to let the gears catch. If we move on my count, we get a head start."

Clarisse squinted. "How do you know it's thirty-two seconds?"

"Because I counted. Four times. With a water clock."

"Who makes a water clock at camp?"

"Me. Last Tuesday. Percy helped."

At Percy's name, Thalia's head snapped up. A slow grin spread across her face.

"Ohhhh," Thalia said. "Percy helped. Percy helped with the water clock. How domestic."

"He just held the bucket," Annabeth said quickly. A little too quickly. Her ears went pink.

"He held the bucket," Thalia repeated, in a mushy voice. "How romantic. Did you guys name the bucket?"

"We did not name the bucket."

"Bucket Jackson," Clarisse said, nodding solemnly. "Strong name."

Annabeth threw her stylus at them. Thalia caught it between two fingers without looking, still grinning.

"Seriously," Annabeth said, trying to sound stern and failing because her cheeks were hot. "Percy's team — Percy, Grover, Ella — they're not weak. Grover's the best balancer in camp, he can stand on a hold the size of a coin. Ella memorized the whole wall schematic, she'll know where every loose rock is. And Percy —"

She stopped.

"And Percy what?" Thalia asked, suddenly sharper. Less teasing, more curious.

Annabeth looked down at her tablet. She could picture him so easily it was annoying — black hair everywhere, sea-green eyes, that stupid lopsided grin when he was about to do something reckless. The way he climbed like the wall was a wave he was trying to ride instead of a puzzle he was trying to solve.

"Percy doesn't climb the wall," Annabeth said quietly. "He feels it. He moves before the shift because he senses the water in the hydraulics or the moisture in the air or something. I don't know how he does it. He just... knows."

Clarisse snorted. "Great. Fish-boy has wall-senses. I'll still beat him."

"Sure you will," Thalia said, but her eyes stayed on Annabeth. Thoughtful. A little too thoughtful.

Annabeth cleared her throat and stood up, tucking the tablet under her arm. "Come on. We need to stretch. And Thalia — no showing off."

"No promises," Thalia said, standing and cracking her knuckles. A spark jumped between them. "Showing off is my second love language after sarcasm."

---


Annabeth reached the wall inspection area before anyone else in her climbing group. Chiron had allowed competitors to examine the safety markers from the ground, but not to climb or touch the moving sections before the demonstration. She intended to use every minute.

The climbing wall had changed since she remembered first seeing it. The structure was familiar enough to make her muscles anticipate the heat, but this version contained a newer set of safety rails and a mechanical stop mechanism installed after several younger campers had reported difficulty hearing the horn from the upper platforms.

She walked the perimeter with her notebook, checking the numbered stations on the diagram. Zoe was helping the instructor mark the inspection points and had tied a short piece of orange cloth to the secondary stop cord.

"Why is that lower than the other one?" Annabeth asked.

"So the instructor on the ground can reach it without stepping into the fall zone," Zoe replied.

"Has it been tested with someone above the second vent?"

"With weighted equipment, not an actual camper. The upper teams rehearsed the descent yesterday."

Annabeth made a note. "Then the demonstration needs to show how the lower group hears the stop command. There are two places where the rock blocks the sound."

Zoe followed her pencil. "That would make it harder for the people nearest the lava vent."

"Exactly."

They took the question to the instructor. Instead of dismissing Annabeth as nervous, he sent a pair of senior campers to stand at the two points and asked Zoe to call the stop. The first call reached both. The second, when the vent machinery was running at demonstration speed, barely reached the upper left position.

"Add a visual signal," Annabeth suggested. "Something that moves when the stop cord is pulled. If the horn gets lost in the machinery, the climber should still know to freeze."

The instructor looked at the gear on the wall. A small flag could be fitted above the stop cord without interfering with the climbing holds. He asked the Hephaestus worker beside him whether it could be installed before the event began.

"Twenty minutes," the worker said. "If we have the right fitting."

"I'll get one," Annabeth offered.

"Not you," Chiron said from behind her. "You've already inspected the route and identified the problem. Let the repair crew do the fitting. You can help check the signal once they've installed it."

She was about to object, then remembered how many times she'd felt responsible for the entire Hall repair because she'd recognized a dangerous mark. Being the person who found a problem did not mean she had to be the person with the tools.

"All right," she said.

Thalia came to join her while the work crew measured the new bracket. She had been stretching by the lower practice rails and had heard enough of the discussion to know Annabeth had delayed the climb.

"Did you find a reason to cancel the whole event?" she asked.

"No. I found a place where the stop signal might not be visible."

"Good. I want to climb, not be trapped up there because everybody assumes loud people can always be heard."

Annabeth smiled. "I thought you'd complain about waiting."

"I'm complaining about waiting. I'm also agreeing with you."

They stood together near the inspection line. Thalia watched the worker fit the small flag above the cord, then helped test it from the lower ground position. Annabeth stood at the first blind corner with her notebook. When Zoe signaled, the flag dropped visibly enough that she could mark the position without raising her head.

"Good," she called.

They repeated it twice at different machinery speeds, then recorded the result for Chiron. Thalia looked pleased.

"Now can I climb?"

"Now you can listen to the safety briefing," Annabeth replied.

"You're worse than the instructor."

"Thank you."

When the group assembled, Clarisse was trying to convince Percy that taking the easy first route would make him look weak. Percy was insisting that knowing which route he could actually finish was the entire point of training.

"You're only saying that because you hate the moving holds," Clarisse said.

"I hate the part where I fall because somebody pretends a mechanical wall is a moral test."

Clarisse gave him a look, then conceded he could take whichever route Chiron assigned. Annabeth was relieved. She had watched him treat the water around the lake as an extension of himself and sometimes forget that not every place with moisture in it belonged to his control.

Silena arrived with the check-in forms. She had assigned each competitor a number and a partner responsible for confirming that they'd returned to the ground. Annabeth signed beside her name, then noticed Percy looking at her route.

"Are you on the north face?" he asked.

"Yes. The route with the offset hold near the second vent."

"That one's ugly."

"That's why I chose it."

Thalia rolled her eyes. "She means the problem is interesting, so now she's compelled to solve it in public."

"That's approximately correct."

Percy grinned. He had a choice to make about his own route. He could try to match hers, which would be impressive if he managed it, or accept the one he'd practiced. She hoped he'd choose on the basis of what he'd learned rather than on the basis of who might be watching.

"What are you climbing?" she asked.

"The west face. Clarisse says it's a coward's path."

"It isn't. It has fewer crossing holds, but the timing is less forgiving near the upper platform."

"See? That's helpful. You can insult my choices after you've measured them."

Thalia snorted. "The west face has a good view of the flag station. You can watch the entire race from there if you don't fall off."

Percy made a face. Annabeth liked the way they could tease without requiring anyone to pretend that a difficult event was easy. They had all been dealing with danger serious enough to make the smallest precaution feel like a judgment. Here they could practice and compete within a structure someone had taken the time to check.

When Silena finished the roster, the instructor asked Annabeth to explain the visual stop signal to the climbers. She did it briefly, pointing out the two blind positions and what each person should do if the flag dropped. She did not mention that the idea had been hers.

Thalia stood beside her and made the entire group demonstrate lowering their hands away from moving holds on command. Several campers complained that the motion was awkward.

"Then practice it," Thalia said. "Nobody wants to watch you fall because your pride got tangled in the machinery."

Annabeth stepped off the platform with her. The climb hadn't begun, but she had already learned something she hadn't expected: Thalia could accept a safety correction without treating it as an attack, and Annabeth could bring a useful observation to the people responsible without taking their tools away. Both would matter after the horn blew.

"Testing, testing! Hi, everyone! Can you hear me?"

Silena Beauregard's voice boomed across Half-Blood Hill, amplified by the brass trumpet and Silena's sheer delight, because everything Silena said sounded like it sparkled.

She was perched on the announcer's platform at the top of the climbing wall with Zoe Nightshade, Hecate's daughter, who brought the severe posture of a former Hunter to everything, including games. Zoe sat beside Silena with her arms crossed, bow across her knees, silver circlet glinting, looking like she'd rather be anywhere else.

"Thou can hear me, daughter of Poseidon," Zoe said dryly. "The entire mountain can hear thee. The gods on Olympus can hear thee. Please stop shouting directly into the trumpet."

"Sorry!" Silena said, quieter, then immediately loud again because she was excited. "Hi! Welcome to Camp Half-Blood's monthly Climbing Wall Extravaganza! I'm Silena!"

"I am Zoe. I was coerced."

"She volunteered!" Silena said, beaming and looping an arm around Zoe's shoulders. Zoe allowed it for exactly two seconds before shrugging her off, but she was fighting a smile.

Below them, the hill was packed. The entire camp spread out on blankets and logs — Hermes kids with popcorn they'd smuggled from the mess hall, Apollo kids with sunblock and guitars, Demeter kids sharing strawberries, Hephaestus kids with clipboards ready to take notes on wall performance, Dionysus cabin pretending not to care while Mr. D himself sat in a lawn chair with a Diet Coke and a magazine that said GOLF.

Chiron stood at the base of the wall in his wheelchair — his human disguise — with a starting flag and a whistle. Argus, with eyes all over, manned the lava shutoff valve in case of actual emergency.

The climbing wall loomed above them all, a massive shifting cliff of rock and wood and metal, chains and pulleys groaning, lava glowing in cracks and channels. It hissed and popped. Every thirty-two seconds — though only Annabeth knew it was thirty-two — whole sections would rotate, flip, drop, or spew fake-but-very-hot lava (Beckendorf swore it was "low-mortality lava," which was not as comforting as he thought).

Two bells hung at the top. First team to ring their bell wins.

"Okay, today's first match!" Silena announced, reading from a pink scroll. "On the west face, in blue headbands — Percy Jackson, Grover Underwood, and Ella!"

Cheers from Poseidon's supporters (Silena on the tower, Clarisse on the other team, and Percy cheering for himself), the satyrs, and the library harpies, who flapped so hard they made a windstorm.

Ella waved frantically from the base of the wall. She was small even for a harpy, with rumpled red-brown feathers, big glasses perched on her beak that the Athena kids had made for her, and a blue headband tied around one wing. "Ella remembers! Ella remembers the west face! Section seven is loose! Section seven says 'creak-creak' before it drops! Don't trust section seven!"

"She's adorable," Silena cooed into the trumpet. "Look at her little glasses!"

"She has memorized the maintenance logs," Zoe said, with genuine respect. "A wise warrior knows the battlefield. Thou hast my approval, harpy."

"And on the east face, in red headbands — Annabeth Grace, Thalia Grace, and Clarisse La Rue!"

The cheer was deafening. Zeus cabin (Thalia and Annabeth) thundered, Ares cabin roared, Clarisse flexed, Thalia shot a spark into the air that exploded like a firework, and Annabeth — Annabeth just tightened her ponytail and stared at the wall like it had personally insulted her.

"Oh, this is going to be good," Silena whispered into the trumpet, which meant everyone heard it. "Friends? Rivals? Sweethearts on opposite teams? Race-to—"

"Silena," Zoe said, elbowing her.

"What? I'm just saying what everyone's thinking!"

"Thou art thinking it loudly into a magically amplified trumpet."

"Okay, okay! Chiron, take it away!"

Chiron raised his flag. "Climbers ready? Remember — no weapons, no pushing below the waist, no summoning lightning on the wall —"

He looked pointedly at Thalia and Annabeth.

"What?" Thalia said innocently. A tiny static shock zapped her hair straight up.

"— and if you hear the emergency horn, freeze and await extraction. Understood?"

Six nods.

"Then... begin!"

He blew the whistle.

---


Grover Underwood had decided, approximately four seconds into the climb, that he hated the climbing wall.

He loved lots of things — nature, music, enchiladas, juniper nymphs named Juniper, naps in sunbeams. The climbing wall was not on the list. The climbing wall was lava and heights and wobbling rocks and Percy shouting "YOU GOT THIS, GROVER!" while hanging one-handed from a hold that was actively smoking.

"I DON'T GOT THIS!" Grover bleated, clinging to a foothold the size of a cookie. His hooves — squeezed into fake sneakers — slipped. He scrabbled, wings of panic flapping in his chest. "WHY DID I AGREE TO THIS?"

"Because you're brave and noble and I promised you extra enchiladas!" Percy called from ten feet above him. Somehow Percy was already ten feet up, moving like water flowing uphill. He didn't seem to climb so much as slosh from hold to hold, always finding the solid one, always shifting his weight a half-second before the rock moved.

"Enchiladas!" Ella chirped beside Grover. She'd fluttered up — technically gliding, technically against the rules, but she only did it in tiny hops and Chiron pretended not to see because she was so earnest. "Ella likes enchiladas. Chapter nine. 'Enchiladas are a traditional Mexican dish.' Ella remembers. Grover, your left hoof is on a stable hold. Your right hoof needs to move up six inches to the blue-speckled rock. That one is bolted. The gray one squeaks."

Grover moved his hoof. The gray one squeaked and dropped three inches. Ella nodded sagely. "Ella told you. Ella is always right about squeaks."

"Thanks, Ella," Grover panted, hauling himself up. His arms burned. Satyrs were not built for upper-body strength. Satyrs were built for panpipes and running away very fast. "You're a lifesaver."

"Ella is a friend-saver," Ella corrected, preening. "Ella saves friends. Page one."

Above them, Percy had paused at a ledge, waiting. He wasn't even breathing hard. A thin sheen of sweat made his hair stick to his forehead, and his eyes were bright, scanning the wall.

"Grover, when I say now, swing left!" Percy shouted.

"Why?"

"Just trust me!"

Grover swung left. A jet of lava erupted from the spot where his head had been, roaring orange and hot. The heat singed his cap.

"HOW DID YOU KNOW THAT?" Grover shrieked.

"I heard it!" Percy said. "The pipes gurgle before they blow! Like — like a stomach growl, but angrier!"

Only Percy Jackson would describe lava hydraulics as a stomach growl and be exactly right.

On the other side of the wall, they could hear the other team — Clarisse's unmistakable bellow: "OUT OF MY WAY, ROCK!" followed by a crash, Thalia whooping, and Annabeth's clear voice counting: "...four, three, two — SHIFT!"

Sure enough, the wall groaned and rotated. Percy's section tilted. Grover yelped as his cookie-hold spun sideways. But Percy was already moving, pulling Ella up by the wrist feathers and reaching down for Grover.

"Got you," Percy said, locking eyes with him. Steady. Sure. "I got you. Climb on my count. One, two —"

Grover climbed. His muscles screamed, but Percy's hand on his wrist was solid, and Ella was chanting, "Stable, stable, squeak, stable — skip the squeak!" like a tiny feathered coach.

From the announcer's tower, Silena's voice floated down: "Oh my gods, look at Team Percy! That teamwork! That trust! That bicep action from Percy! Zoe, are you seeing this?"

"I see a boy who will have lava in his trousers again if he does not move," Zoe said. "Lava vent opening behind him. Three seconds."

Percy moved.

---


Thalia Grace lived for this stuff.

Heights? Love them. Danger? Love it. An audience? Absolutely love it. A chance to show up her little sister and her spear-happy teammate while looking cool? Best day ever.

She was fifteen feet up the east face, hanging from an overhang by her fingertips, legs dangling, grinning like a maniac.

"Thalia! Stop showing off and move!" Annabeth shouted from below her.

"I'm not showing off! I'm... scouting!" Thalia shouted back, then let go with one hand to wave at the Apollo kids, who cheered. Static crackled off her palm. "See? Scouting!"

"You're going to fall and I'm going to laugh!" Clarisse shouted from a ledge to her right, where she was methodically smashing a stuck hold into place with the heel of her hand. Subtlety was not a Clarisse virtue. She treated the wall like it owed her money.

"Love you too, Clarisse!" Thalia said, swinging herself up and over the overhang in one smooth pull-up. Her shoulders burned in the good way. She'd always been strong — daughter of Zeus, stubborn older sister, runaway who'd kept herself and Annabeth alive for years. Climbing felt like running, like storms. Her body just knew.

She perched on top of the overhang and looked across the gap to the west face, where she could just see flashes of blue headbands through the steam and chains.

Percy was climbing like an idiot. A beautiful, graceful, infuriating idiot.

He'd just dodged a lava burst without looking, pulling Grover out of the way at the same time. Thalia's eyebrows shot up.

"Okay, that was actually cool," she muttered.

"What's cool?" Annabeth asked, hauling herself up beside Thalia. Annabeth was precise, efficient — every move planned two holds ahead, chalk on her hands, breathing even. She didn't waste motion. She glanced where Thalia was looking.

"Nothing," Thalia said quickly. Too quickly.

Annabeth's eyes narrowed. She watched Percy for three seconds. He reached back without looking and caught Ella's wrist just as Ella slipped. Like he knew.

Annabeth's expression did something complicated.

"He does that," she said quietly, almost to herself. "He always reaches left when someone slips. He leads with his left hand because his right is dominant for balance."

Thalia stared at her. "How do you know that?"

Annabeth blinked, as if surprised she'd said it out loud. "I've watched him for years. And in sparring here. He always—"

"Uh-huh," Thalia said, grinning slowly. "You've watched him. A lot. Specifically his hands. And his balance. And his—"

"Shut up and climb," Annabeth said, ears bright red, scrambling up the next pitch twice as fast as necessary. She miscounted a hold and slipped. Thalia caught her ankle out of pure reflex.

"Got you, sis," Thalia said.

"Thanks," Annabeth muttered.

"Anytime, lover-girl."

"I'M TWELVE!"

"And already whipped! It's precious!"

Clarisse hauled herself up beside them, breathing like a bull. "Are you two done flirting with each other? We have a wall to win."

"We're sisters, you weirdo!" Thalia and Annabeth shouted in unison, then looked at each other and burst out laughing despite themselves.

From above, Silena squealed into the trumpet: "Sisterly bonding on the east face! So cute! Zoe, aren't they cute?"

"They are delaying," Zoe said. "The blue team has gained four feet while they bicker. Back in the Hunt, I would make them run laps until moonrise."

"She says that with love!" Silena translated.

Thalia shook it off, refocused, and took point again. The wall groaned — Annabeth shouted "SHIFT! NOW!" — and Thalia leapt.

She loved that half-second of air, when the rock fell away and it was just her and gravity and the choice to catch the next hold or fall. She caught it, swung, kicked off a lava-slicked panel that was hot through her sneakers, and landed on a narrow chimney crack.

"YEAH!" she shouted, punching the air. Lightning — just a little, just static — snapped off her fist. The crowd went wild.

"THALIA! NO LIGHTNING!" Chiron bellowed from below.

"THAT WAS STATIC ELECTRICITY!" Thalia bellowed back. "TOTALLY NATURAL! SCIENCE!"

Annabeth sighed so hard Thalia could hear it three holds down. "You're going to get us disqualified."

"Worth it," Thalia said, winking at the Apollo kids, who were now chanting her name.

But as she climbed, she kept glancing across at the other face. Percy. Annabeth watching Percy. Annabeth knowing exactly which hand Percy would use.

Thalia filed that away. For later. For maximum teasing.

---


Clarisse La Rue had three great loves in life: her spear, her little half-brother Percy (though she'd deny it if asked), and talking trash so epic it made grown monsters cry.

"HEY, JACKSON!" she bellowed across the wall, wedging her shoulder under a rotating panel and shoving it into place through sheer spite. "MY GRANDMA CLIMBS FASTER THAN YOU! AND SHE'S A DRAKON!"

Percy peeked around a steam vent. "Your grandma's a drakon? That explains a lot!"

"MY GRANDMA COULD BEAT OUR DAD IN A FIGHT!"

"Our dad's the god of the sea! He'd drown your grandma!"

"THE SEA'S JUST A BIG PUDDLE!"

"YOUR FACE IS A BIG PUDDLE!"

It was, by Clarisse standards, poetry.

Ella, clinging to a hold near Percy, piped up helpfully: "Actually, the sea covers seventy-one percent of Earth's surface and contains approximately three hundred and twenty-six million cubic miles of water. A puddle is typically defined as less than—"

"NOT NOW, BIRD BRAIN!" Clarisse and Percy shouted together, then glared at each other for agreeing.

Grover, wheezing on a ledge, tried his own trash talk: "Yeah! Well! My dad is... a satyr! And he's... very good at... music!"

Silence.

"Good one, Grover," Percy whispered.

"Thanks. I panicked."

On the east face, Annabeth was trying to ignore all of them and focus. "Clarisse, stop engaging. Thalia, stop waving. We need to gain six feet before the next full rotation."

"You sound like a textbook," Clarisse said, but she moved, hauling herself up with brutal efficiency. She wasn't graceful. She was a bulldozer in a red headband. Holds that wobbled for other people held still for Clarisse because Clarisse radiated an aura of "break and I'll break you back."

She reached a lava channel and grinned. Lava didn't scare her. She'd gotten a face full of it last year and her eyebrows had grown back thicker and angrier.

"Bring it," she told the wall.

The wall brought it. A sheet of lava fountained across her path. Clarisse timed it — one, two — and swung through the gap, sneakers smoking, landing hard and rolling.

"CLARISSE LA RUE DOES NOT GET BURNED!" she roared. "LAVA GETS CLARISSE'D!"

The Ares kids went insane. Someone blew an air horn. Mr. D opened one eye, said, "Moderately impressive," and went back to his magazine, which was the highest praise he'd ever given.

Silena was crying laughing into the trumpet. "She's amazing! She's terrifying! She's both! Zoe, say something nice about Clarisse!"

"She... climbs with conviction," Zoe said carefully. "And her insults lack subtlety but possess vigor. She would make a formidable foe. I would recruit her, though I fear she would attempt to spear Artemis."

"She says you're wife material!" Silena translated.

"I said no such thing!"

Clarisse, hearing her name, flexed for the crowd, missed a handhold, caught herself with her teeth on a rope — actually with her teeth — pulled herself up, and spat out hemp fibers.

"Did she just... bite the wall into submission?" Grover asked faintly from the other side.

"Don't stare," Percy said. "She can sense fear. And staring. She can sense staring."

Ella tugged Percy's sleeve with her beak. "Percy. Percy. Annabeth's team is going to trigger the double-shift trap in forty seconds. Page eighty-four. 'Do not place weight on the central gear panel during double-shift.' Annabeth knows. She is counting. She will move on twenty-nine, not thirty-two, to avoid it."

Percy stared at her. "How do you know Annabeth knows?"

"Because Annabeth read the manual too. Ella saw her. In the library. Tuesday. Annabeth takes notes in the margins. Ella remembers her handwriting. Neat. All caps. Very judgmental about commas."

Percy felt a warm, fizzy feeling in his chest. Annabeth in the library on Tuesday, reading the same manual, making judgy comma notes. Of course she did.

"Okay," he said. "Then we move on twenty-eight."

"Why twenty-eight?" Grover asked.

"Because Annabeth will move on twenty-nine, and I want to beat her by one second."

Grover stared. "You are both nerds. You're just nerds about different things. You're water nerds and she's plan nerds and it's disgusting."

"Climb, Grover."

"Climbing, climbing."

---


Halfway up, the wall stopped being a wall and started being a puzzle made of heat and noise.

Steam hissed everywhere. Chains clanked. The rock vibrated under Percy's palms. Sweat stung his eyes. His forearms ached. Below, the camp looked tiny — blankets like postage stamps, faces turned up, Silena's pink dress a bright dot at the top tower.

Percy loved it.

Not the lava — the lava could go jump in the lake — but the way his body felt up here. Light. Sure. Like swimming.

He pressed his palm flat to the rock and closed his eyes for half a second. He could feel it — water, deep inside the wall. Hydraulic fluid, steam pipes, the lake mist in the air, the sweat on his own skin. It hummed. The wall breathed through water, and Percy could hear it breathe.

Gurgle-gurgle-hissss. Left vent in three... two...

"Grover, Ella, hold!" he called.

WHOOMPH. Lava blasted left.

"...now go!"

They scrambled up while the vents recharged. Ella fluttered-hop to the next ledge, Grover bleating the whole way, Percy right behind them, finding holds by feel more than sight.

He'd never told anyone how he did it. Not really. It wasn't like Annabeth's counting or Thalia's strength. It was just... the water told him. When the pipes filled, the rock got a tiny bit cooler. When pressure built, the air got damper. His skin prickled. His gut said move.

"You're doing it again," Ella observed, perched beside him, adjusting her glasses. "Your eyes go unfocused. Like you're listening. Page twelve: 'Percy Jackson listens to walls.' Ella wrote that. In Ella's head. Ella's head-book."

Percy laughed, breathless. "Yeah. I'm listening."

Across the gap, through a curtain of steam, he saw red headbands. Annabeth was leading now, chalk dust on her cheek, ponytail whipping. She moved like a metronome — reach, grip, pull, set. Reach, grip, pull, set. No wasted motion.

She glanced across at him. Just for a second.

Percy grinned and waved with two fingers. Annabeth rolled her eyes so hard he could see it from thirty feet away, but the corner of her mouth twitched.

Then the wall lurched — DOUBLE SHIFT, Beckendorf's voice boomed through a cone — and everything tilted.

Percy's ledge dropped six inches. Grover screamed. Ella squawked and flapped straight up, glasses crooked.

"LEFT!" Percy shouted, grabbing Grover's harness strap. "SWING LEFT NOW!"

They swung. The central gear panel they'd been standing on flipped over, revealing glowing lava channels underneath. If they'd stayed —

"That was page eighty-four!" Ella gasped, clinging to Percy's shirt. "Ella was right! Ella is right!"

"You were right!" Percy said, heart hammering. "Great call!"

But his eyes were on Annabeth. Because Annabeth had moved a second earlier — on twenty-nine, just like Ella said she would — and she'd gotten her whole team clear. Thalia was already on the upper chimney, Clarisse was braced in a corner, Annabeth was reaching down to haul Clarisse up.

She looked across at Percy. Her eyes went wide.

Because he'd moved on twenty-eight. One second before her. And she knew.

She knew he'd known.

---


Annabeth Grace prided herself on not being surprised.

Surprise meant you hadn't planned well enough. Surprise meant you'd missed a variable. Annabeth hated missing variables almost as much as she hated spiders (which was a lot) and passive voice (which was more).

But Percy Jackson surprised her constantly, and it was infuriating.

She'd just executed a perfect double-shift dodge — called it on twenty-nine, gotten Thalia and Clarisse clear — and looked across to see Percy already clear, already settled, already grinning at her like he'd been waiting.

He'd moved on twenty-eight.

Nobody moved on twenty-eight. The pattern was thirty-two. The safe call was twenty-nine to beat the gears. Twenty-eight was reckless. Twenty-eight was guessing.

Unless he wasn't guessing. Unless he knew she was going to move on twenty-nine, so he moved one second earlier just to beat her.

"Did he just—" Annabeth muttered, hauling Clarisse up by sheer adrenaline.

"Did he just out-plan you?" Thalia asked, delighted, swinging down beside her. "Oh my gods. Fish-boy out-planned the planner. This is the best day of my life."

"He did not out-plan me," Annabeth said stiffly. "He... anticipated."

"He anticipated that you'd anticipate the shift and then he anticipated one second earlier to show off," Thalia said. "That's basically mind-reading. That's couple mind-reading."

"We are a couple! We're also trying to win!"

"Middle-school sweethearts! Young love! Rival teams, shared ice cream! The tabloids will weep!"

Annabeth tried to shove Thalia off the ledge. Thalia dodged, laughing, static making her hair float.

"I'm serious," Annabeth said, but she was watching Percy again. He was helping Grover up, one hand on the satyr's back, the other testing the next hold. He did it without looking — left hand out, fingers spread, catching Ella's wingtip just as she wobbled.

Left hand. Always left hand for catches.

"He leads left for rescues," Annabeth whispered.

"What?" Thalia asked.

"Nothing."

"No, say it. Say the weirdly specific thing you know about your boyfriend."

Annabeth sighed. "In sparring, when someone falls, Percy catches with his left. Because his right is his sword hand. He keeps it free. Even when he doesn't have a sword. It's instinct."

Thalia was quiet for a beat. Then, softly: "Huh."

"What?"

"Nothing. Just... huh."

Annabeth frowned. "What huh?"

Thalia shook her head, but her grin had gone a little softer around the edges. Less teasing, more... something else. "You just really pay attention to him, huh?"

Annabeth opened her mouth to deny it. Closed it. Looked at her hands, chalky and scraped.

"Yeah," she said quietly. "I guess I do."

Thalia bumped her shoulder gently. No spark this time. Just warm.

"For what it's worth," Thalia said, "he pays attention to you too."

"How do you know?"

"Because he's staring at you right now."

Annabeth looked. Percy was, in fact, staring. When he realized he'd been caught, he immediately pretended to be deeply interested in a rock. A very boring rock. He nodded at it thoughtfully, as if the rock had said something wise.

Annabeth laughed before she could stop herself. Loud. Real. It echoed off the wall.

Percy looked up, startled, then grinned — that huge, dorky, sun-bright grin that made his whole face go crooked.

Silena's voice drifted down: "Are they... are they having a moment? During a lava climb? During competition? Zoe, this is cinema!"

"This is a tactical error," Zoe said. "They have both stopped moving. Clarisse is about to win the wall single-handedly out of spite."

She was right. Clarisse had used the distraction to power-climb twenty feet, bellowing her own theme song ("CLARISSE! CLARISSE! SHE'S THE BEST! SHE'LL PUT YOU IN A VEST! A STRAITJACKET VEST!") and was now only fifteen feet from the bell.

"CLARISSE!" Annabeth and Percy shouted together, horrified.

They looked at each other, horrified all over again for a completely different reason.

Thalia burst out laughing so hard she almost fell off. "OH MY GODS! YOU EVEN YELL IN SYNC! YOU'RE HOPELESS!"

---


"Okay, folks, we're in the final minute!" Silena announced, leaning so far over the platform rail that Zoe had to grab the back of her shirt. "Team Red is in the lead thanks to Clarisse's absolutely feral final push, but Team Blue is closing fast! Percy is climbing like he's part fish, part spider monkey!"

"He moves well," Zoe admitted grudgingly. "For a boy. For a son of Poseidon. His balance is... adequate."

"She means he's amazing!" Silena said. "Look at him go! Grover's right behind him — come on, Grover! You can do it! Think of enchiladas! Ella's navigating — oh, she just quoted the safety manual at a lava vent and it closed! Did anyone else see that? The lava got shy!"

"Ella told the vent, and I quote, 'Section twelve, paragraph four: Vents must not exceed two thousand degrees during junior games,'" Zoe said, with deep approval. "The operators likely reduced flow out of respect. Knowledge is a weapon. Well done, harpy."

"Thalia's doing triple backflips — no, wait, she's just falling with style — no, she's fine! She's fine! She caught herself! Show-off!"

"I am a show-off!" Thalia shouted up at them, hanging one-handed and taking a bow with the other. "Thank you for noticing!"

"She acknowledges her flaw," Zoe said. "There may be hope for her yet."

"Annabeth's calling counts! Percy’s moving before she finishes calling them! How does he do that? How does she know he's going to do that? Do they rehearse this? Is there a secret practice wall? Zoe, investigate!"

"I will not investigate their courtship rituals," Zoe said firmly. "I was a Hunter. I remain neutral in matters of the heart. Though..."

"Though what? Though what? Tell me!"

"...though the son of Poseidon looks at her as Perseus once looked at Andromeda. With bewildered devotion."

Silena gasped so loud the trumpet feedback squealed. "ZOE! That was romantic! Did you just say something romantic?"

"I said nothing. The wind took my words. Forget them."

"I will never forget them! Campers, did you hear that? Zoe ships it!"

"I do not 'ship it.' I do not know what that means. I am going to shoot that trumpet."

Below, both teams were within ten feet of the top. Clarisse lunged for the red bell rope. Percy lunged for the blue. Thalia, because she couldn't help herself, lunged for nothing and just jumped the gap between two pillars to make the crowd scream.

"MY BELL! MY BELL! MY—" Clarisse grabbed the rope.

Percy grabbed his half a second later.

DING-DING!

Two bells. Almost simultaneous.

Chiron squinted through the steam. Argus blinked all his eyes. Beckendorf checked the pressure gauges.

Chiron raised his megaphone. "Red team by... half a second! Winner — Annabeth, Thalia, Clarisse!"

The east face erupted. Ares kids tackled Clarisse the second she hit the ground. Thalia crowd-surfed on Hermes kids. Annabeth stood at the base, breathing hard, chalk in her hair, staring at the scoreboard like she couldn't quite believe it.

On the west face, Grover collapsed face-first into the dirt. "Ground. Beautiful ground. I love you. Let's never leave each other again."

Ella fluttered down and landed on his back. "Ella came in second! Second is the first loser, page two hundred! But Ella had fun! Fun is winning, Ella decides! Ella rewrites page two hundred!"

Percy sat down hard beside Grover, laughing, shaking lava cinders out of his hair. His hands were scraped, his sneakers were melted at the toes, and he felt fantastic.

Until he looked up and saw Annabeth walking toward him.

---


The crowd was still cheering, but it sounded far away.

Annabeth stopped in front of Percy, hands on her hips, breathing a little fast. Her gray eyes were bright. There was a smudge of soot on her nose.

"You moved on twenty-eight," she said.

Percy stood up, brushing dirt off his shorts. "You moved on twenty-nine."

"I always move on twenty-nine for a double-shift."

"I know."

"How do you know?"

Percy shrugged, suddenly shy. "I dunno. I just... you always take one second. You don't wait for the full count. You think waiting is wasting time. So I thought, if I go one second before you, maybe I'd—"

"Beat me," Annabeth finished.

"Yeah."

She stared at him. He stared back. Grover, still face-down, groaned, "Somebody get me enchiladas and privacy."

Thalia sidled up behind Annabeth, arms crossed, grin huge. Clarisse loomed behind Percy, arms crossed, grin mean.

"So," Thalia said. "You two want to explain how you know each other's counts? How you yell at the same time? How Fish-boy catches left and Planner-girl knows it?"

Percy and Annabeth spoke at once:

"We spar together—"

"We've just been on a lot of—"

They stopped. Looked at each other.

"Jinx," Percy said.

"Don't jinx me," Annabeth said, but she was smiling. Really smiling, not the stressed strategy smile, but the soft one she only got when she forgot to be embarrassed about it.

Clarisse looked between them, then at Thalia. "Are they... doing the thing? The weird eye thing?"

"Yeah," Thalia said. They'd been doing the weird eye thing since Percy arrived, and she should have been used to it by now. Camp could turn the Zeus-Poseidon rivalry into a scoreboard for practically anything, including lava races, but that wasn't a reason to make Annabeth choose between her sister and her boyfriend. Thalia liked winning. She also liked the grin her sister was trying unsuccessfully to hide.

Luke liked to tease her about getting soft whenever she let a Poseidon kid off the hook. *Sea and sky don't mix,* he'd said once, laughing over a capture-the-flag map. Thalia had told him to stop drawing tides on her half of the page.

But looking at these two dorks — twelve years old, covered in soot, grinning at each other after nearly getting melted — Thalia couldn't find the anger. It just... wasn't there. Just a tired, fond ache, like a bruise healing.

She laughed. Actually laughed. Threw her head back and laughed so hard lightning danced in her teeth.

"You two are idiots," she said, ruffling Annabeth's hair and then, before she could think better of it, ruffling Percy's too. Percy ducked, shocked. Thalia Grace usually made Poseidon kids earn the teasing. This one had earned it. "Adorable, lava-dodging idiots. Come on. Losers buy the winners ice cream?"

"We lost," Percy said. "We have to clean Pegasus stables."

"No, no," Clarisse said magnanimously, slinging an arm around Grover and nearly crushing him. "You were only half a second behind. And Satyr-boy bit a rope to save Bird-girl — I saw that. That was hardcore. I'll clean the stables with you. One day. One hour. Don't push it."

Grover teared up. "I love you, Clarisse."

"Don't make it weird."

Ella flapped in circles around them, chanting, "Second place! Fun place! Ella rewrites the book! Chapter twenty-four! The Climbing Wall! Percy is brave! Annabeth is smart! Thalia is lightning! Clarisse is strong! Grover is kind! Ella is Ella!"

Silena's voice floated down from the tower: "Group hug! Group hug! Zoe, they're hugging! Get the ambrosia! This is the cutest thing I've ever—"

"I am not crying," Zoe said stiffly. "There is ash in my eye. From the lava. Which I am too stoic to be moved by."

"She's crying!" Silena announced. "Everyone, Zoe's crying! History!"

"I will end thee."

---


There is only one cure for lava-wall sweat, melted sneakers, singed hair, and a race you want to argue about for hours.

The lake.

It started with Thalia. Of course it started with Thalia.

"LAST ONE IN HAS TO CLEAN THE STABLES!" she shouted, sprinting for the canoe lake in full armor, headband still on, sneakers still smoking.

"HEY!" Clarisse shouted, sprinting after her. "I'M A DAUGHTER OF POSEIDON AND I'M OFFENDED AND I'M GOING TO WIN!"

Percy looked at Annabeth. Annabeth looked at Percy.

"Race you," they said together.

Then both blushed furiously and took off running.

What followed was the kind of chaos Chiron would later describe in the incident report as "unsanctioned aquatic cooldown with excessive splashing" and Silena would describe as "the best afternoon ever!!!"

Thalia cannonballed off the dock, screaming lightning-bolt war cries, soaking the Hermes kids. Clarisse belly-flopped so hard the water fled. Grover waded in carefully, bleating about hoof-pruning, until Juniper splashed him and he dunked her back, both of them laughing. Ella stood at the end of the dock, glasses off, wings tucked, terrified — until Percy and Annabeth each held out a hand.

"Jump together?" Percy asked. "On three?"

Ella looked at their hands. Then at the water glittering blue and gold in the late sun. "Ella is afraid of deep water. Page six. 'Har pies cannot swim.' But Ella trusts friends. Friends are shallower than fear. Ella just made that up. Ella is a poet."

"One," Annabeth said softly. "Two—"

"THREE!" all three shouted, jumping.

Splash. Cold, clean, perfect. The lake closed over Percy's head and for a second everything was quiet and blue and weightless. He opened his eyes underwater and saw Annabeth beside him, hair floating like a halo, eyes open, grinning around a mouthful of bubbles. Ella paddling frantically between them like a feathery cork.

They surfaced, gasping and laughing.

Thalia swam over, slicking her black hair back, silver streak bright. She treaded water and looked at them — Percy shaking water out of his ears like a dog, Annabeth wringing out her ponytail, Ella clinging to Percy's shoulder and reciting, "Water temperature sixty-eight degrees, optimal for cooling, page—"

Thalia smiled. Just smiled. No barb. No storm.

"You did good today, Fish-boy," she said to Percy. "Don't let it go to your head."

"Thanks, Static-cling," Percy said automatically, then froze, waiting to get electrocuted.

Thalia just snorted. "Nice. That was almost good. You'll get there."

Annabeth stared at her sister — her sister in blood and in everything that mattered — like she'd grown a second head. Thalia caught her look and shrugged, flicking water at her.

"What?" Thalia said. "I'm evolving. I'm mature now. I'm sixteen. I'm practically an adult."

"You set Beckendorf's tool belt on fire yesterday," Clarisse called from the dock, wringing out her bandana.

"An adult who makes choices," Thalia corrected, dunking Clarisse.

Silena and Zoe sat on the dock, feet in the water, trumpet abandoned. Silena had a waterproof camera and was taking pictures for the camp yearbook. Zoe had her bow unstrung beside her, watching the Hunters-in-training splash the Demeter kids, a small, real smile on her usually stern face.

"See?" Silena said, bumping Zoe's shoulder. "Aren't you glad you announced?"

"I suppose," Zoe said. "It was... diverting. The children fought bravely. Even the boy."

"Percy?"

"Yes. He has heart. And the daughter of Zeus — Annabeth — has a mind like a drawn bow. Together..."

"Together what?"

Zoe shook her head, standing and stretching. "Together they will either save the world or destroy it arguing about how to save it. Come. Let us fetch ice cream before the Ares cabin eats it all."

As the sun dipped low over Half-Blood Hill, turning the lake to molten gold — real gold, not lava, the good kind — Percy floated on his back, arms spread, ears underwater, listening to the muffled shouts and laughter.

Annabeth floated beside him, close enough that their shoulders bumped when the waves moved. Neither of them moved away.

"Hey," Percy said, staring at the sky. A pegasus flew overhead, trailing sunset pink. "Good race."

"Good race," Annabeth said. "You almost had us."

"Next time."

"Next time I'll move on twenty-eight."

"Then I'll move on twenty-seven."

"Then we'll both fall off and Thalia will win and never let us hear the end of it."

Percy laughed, water sloshing into his mouth. He spat it out. Annabeth laughed too.

Above them, the climbing wall stood silent and cooling, lava dimming to embers. Tomorrow it would heat up again. Tomorrow there would be swords and prophecies and chores and all the heavy stuff of being half-divine.

But today was just lava and lake water and friends shouting your name.

Percy closed his eyes and floated.

Beside him, Annabeth floated too.

And for once, Thalia, watching from the dock with Clarisse, was dripping wet and grinning. She let the moment be.

Thalia watched Percy and Annabeth float until Clarisse splashed her from behind. She caught her friend by the wrist, lost her footing on the wet boards, and pulled them both into the lake.

Percy rolled over to see what the noise was. Annabeth, still floating, didn't even open her eyes.

"Don't help them," she said.

"I wasn't going to."

"Good."
