## Chapter 34: Mythomagic Tournament

Percy woke up to the sound of a conch horn and someone shouting MYTHOMAGIC through a traffic cone.

Not Chiron. Chiron would never shout through a traffic cone. This was the Stoll brothers, running down the cabin row at full sprint, banging pots together and yelling like town criers who had inhaled too much helium.

"HEAR YE, HEAR YE! BIG HOUSE! NINE O'CLOCK SHARP! SINGLE ELIMINATION! BRING YOUR OWN DECK OR CRY!"

Percy sat straight up and smacked his forehead on the low beam above his bunk. Again. The Poseidon cabin had bunks for all three Cabin Three siblings and a visitor, and he still managed to injure himself on every low beam.

"Percy?" Tyson mumbled from the guest bunk below, one giant eye blinking open. "Is it a monster attack? Should I get a stick?"

"No, buddy. It's worse. It's organized fun."

Tyson sat up, beaming. "I love organized fun! Is there breakfast first?"

"There better be. You didn't come up from the forges for an approved two-night visit just to miss pancakes."

Percy stumbled to the window. Outside, Camp Half-Blood was already buzzing. Mist curled off the strawberry fields. The lake was glass-calm and impossibly blue. Up on the Big House porch, Chiron was pinning up a massive bedsheet with a hand-painted bracket on it. In big red letters: FIRST ANNUAL (DO NOT ASK ABOUT LAST YEAR) CAMP MYTHOMAGIC CHAMPIONSHIP.

Underneath, in smaller letters, Mr. D had added: *Winner receives eternal glory, one (1) coupon for free Diet Coke, and Dionysus's respect (value: negligible). Losers will be mocked.*

Grover was already on the porch with a microphone made from a pinecone taped to a spoon, testing it. "Check, check. Is this thing on? Can the dryads in the back hear me?"

Annabeth ran past his window at full speed, hair already in her battle ponytail, notebook under one arm, shoe untied. She skidded, caught herself on the Hermes cabin railing, shouted "PERCY! Tell Tyson I need his Cyclops Eye card if he has doubles! It's for science and victory!" and kept running.

Percy grinned so wide his face hurt.

Okay. Secret time. A secret he hadn't told anyone at camp, not even Annabeth, especially not Annabeth because she would vivisect him with questions.

Percy was good at Mythomagic. Really good.

Not because he was some natural prodigy. Because he remembered it, and he had been quietly practicing the changed cards.

In his original life — the erased history he and Annabeth both remembered, the one where she was Athena's daughter and their camp family had different parents — Mythomagic had been this dumb little card game that nobody cool admitted to playing. He'd played it in the back of the Big House on rainy days with Beckendorf and Silena and Grover. He'd memorized every stat. Zeus 10/10 God Blast. Poseidon 9/9 Earthquake Churn. Hades 8/8 Death Touch. All the monster modifiers. The weird combo rules about terrain. He knew which common cards were secretly broken. He knew the Harpy Feather + Storm Giant combo could wipe a whole field if timed right. The point scale on these newer cards was different, but a trap was still a trap.

In this life, he was twelve. He had only been practicing this edition for two weeks, mostly under Annabeth's lessons, while quietly testing which old strategies still worked. The instincts were still there, like riding a bike, if bikes were made of laminated centaurs.

Percy wasn't going to tell Annabeth. She remembered beating him regularly, and thought he was still hopelessly casual. She'd spent two weeks explaining this edition's "basic resource economy" while he pretended not to understand what a tribute cost was. He nodded along and said things like "so the shiny ones are better?" just to watch her eye twitch.

It was terrible. It was wonderful. He was a monster. He had no regrets.

"Come on, Tyson," Percy said, pulling on his jeans. "We're going to war."

"Card war!" Tyson cheered, pulling on his shoes — on the wrong feet. "I made a deck! It is all Cyclops and sheep! Mostly sheep!"

"That's a bold strategy, big guy. I respect it."

---

People think strategy is about being the smartest person in the room. It's not. It's about preparation beating talent when talent doesn't bring snacks and a spreadsheet.

Annabeth had snacks. She had a spreadsheet. Okay, she had a wax tablet with columns.

By eight-fifteen, she had commandeered the entire library corner of the Big House. Maps of the bracket. Deck lists from seventeen campers obtained through bribery (two chocolate bars), threats (one death glare), and library privilege (Malcolm owed her).

Her deck sat in the center in a blue leather box Thalia had studded for her last summer. Sixty cards, sleeved, alphabetized, blessed by Annabeth herself. Zeus-themed aggro-control. High burst gods, cheap flying monsters for early tribute, three Storm Spirits for card draw, and her secret weapon: two copies of *Aegis Reflection* — a rare trap card that bounced god powers back. Most kids forgot traps existed. Amateurs.

"Okay, talk to me," Thalia said, dropping into the chair across from her. She had her combat boots on the table, a cherry lollipop stick sticking out of her mouth, and her own deck — black box, skull stickers, smelled faintly of ozone — spinning on one finger. "Who's scary?"

Annabeth tapped her tablet. "Tier One threats. You. Me. Obviously. Nico di Angelo — ten years old, undefeated in the Hades cabin, plays Death Mill, nobody understands how he got three copies of *Styx Ferryman*. Sherman Yang — all Ares beatdown, no spells, just punching. And—" She hesitated.

"And?" Thalia raised an eyebrow. The eyebrow had a piercing in it. It made every expression 40% more judgmental.

"And Percy is an unknown variable."

Thalia snorted. "Percy? Seaweed Brain? Yesterday he tried to tribute his own god because quote 'he looked tired'?"

"I know." Annabeth frowned at her notes. "His fundamentals are garbage. He stacks discard piles sideways. He shuffles like he's kneading bread. But Malcolm played him last night and Percy won with a *Nereid Tidecaller* combo nobody runs because the math is too fiddly. He did the math in his head. In like four seconds."

Thalia leaned forward. "Wait, seriously?"

"Malcolm said he just smiled and said 'oh, I've seen this one before' and then wiped him with forty damage from commons." Annabeth chewed her pencil. "He's either secretly studied for months, or he's lucky in a way that violates probability."

"Or," Thalia said, grinning, "your boyfriend's hustling you."

"Being my boyfriend doesn't make him a mastermind." Annabeth threw an eraser at her. Thalia caught it with her teeth. Show-off. "He's just Percy. He can't bluff. When he lies his ears turn red."

"Both ears?"

"Both ears."

Thalia laughed, loud and bright, the laugh that always made younger campers stare. Being Zeus's daughters together was weird sometimes — people expected them to be rivals, lightning sparring at dawn. Instead Thalia braided Annabeth's hair when she was stressed and Annabeth proofread her battle plans and they stole each other's hoodies. Thalia was sixteen, taller, cooler, could summon actual lightning. Annabeth was twelve and had a color-coded binder. It worked.

"Look," she said, softer. "You want to win this. I want to win this. But if Seaweed Brain takes us both out with a deck full of fish jokes, I'm framing his winning card."

"Deal." Annabeth shuffled her deck. The cards were worn at the edges. Zeus mid-throw, cheeks puffed, stupidly powerful. "My plan for early rounds: play fast, save Aegis for semifinals. Scout Percy in round one. If he's real, I adjust."

"And if you face me in the final?"

Annabeth met her eyes. Gray eyes, same as hers. Storm-gray, Chiron called them. "Then I hold nothing back."

"Good." She stood, cracked her knuckles. "Because neither will I, little sister."

She left. Annabeth stared at her bracket.

Percy Jackson, Round One vs. Katie Gardner. Easy win for Percy if he showed up. Katie played an all-plant deck that lost to any fire card and Percy owned *at least* one Volcano.

She wrote in the margin: *WATCH PERCY — R1. Bring popcorn. Bring notebook. Bring interrogation face.*

"Welcome, welcome, welcome to the First Annual Camp Half-Blood Mythomagic Championship!" Grover shouted into his pinecone microphone. His voice echoed across the lawn. Pigeons fled. "I'm your host and professional commentator, Grover Underwood, satyr, lord of the wild, holder of Level 4 Judge Certification which I printed myself this morning!"

Applause. About forty campers sprawled on blankets around eight folding tables. Chiron stood by the bracket in his wheelchair, smiling. Mr. D sat behind sunglasses with a magazine titled *Wine Enthusiast: Juice Edition*. Argus, their hundred-eyed security guy, was running three matches at once and somehow watching all of them.

Ella the harpy fluttered down next to him with a nest made of spare card sleeves and red feathers. "Ella will co-commentate. Ella likes shiny cards. Ella knows all flavor text by heart. *'The Minotaur remembers every maze. Especially yours.'* Ooh."

"Perfect, Ella, you're color commentary."

"Ella is red-brown. Ella is not color commentary. Ella is harpy."

The crowd laughed. Ella preened. She'd been practicing jokes all week.

"Okay! Rules!" Grover bleated. "Single elimination! Sixty-card decks! Starting life points: 8000! Terrain is rolled each match with this very fancy d20 that Connor Stoll definitely did not chew on!"

"I chewed on it a little," Connor admitted.

"Judges are Chiron and yours truly! No real god-summoning! Last year—" Chiron coughed loudly. "There was no last year! There has never been a last year! First annual!"

Laughter. Someone started chanting "CARDS! CARDS! CARDS!"

Grover looked at his clipboard. Round One matchups:

Table 1: Percy Jackson vs. Katie Gardner
Table 2: Annabeth Grace vs. Connor Stoll
Table 3: Clarisse La Rue vs. Will Solace
Table 4: Nico di Angelo vs. Sherman Yang
Table 5: Thalia Grace vs. Travis Stoll
Table 6: Tyson vs. Malcolm... oh no, poor Malcolm.
Table 7: Silena Beauregard vs. Charles Beckendorf
Table 8: Lee Fletcher vs. Chris Rodriguez

"Players to your tables! May the best top-decker win!"

Percy shuffled to Table 1 looking half-asleep, hair sticking up in nine directions, deck held together with a rubber band. Katie had flower crowns on her deck box. It was adorable. It was going to die.

Annabeth sat at Table 2 like she was defusing a bomb. Connor tried to shake her hand. She shook it while already cutting his deck. Psychological warfare. Grover respected it. Grover feared it.

And at Table 4, little Nico di Angelo climbed onto two stacked phone books to see over the table. He was ten, pale as moonlight, wearing an oversized aviator jacket, hair covering his eyes. His deck box was plain black wood. He set it down without a word. Sherman Yang, fifteen and built like a brick wall, cracked his knuckles and said, "Aw, they gave me the toddler table."

Nico didn't look up. "Your deck has forty-two monsters, twelve tributes, no traps. You mulliganed twice against Beckendorf yesterday. Your left sleeve is marked."

Sherman blinked. "What—"

"Judges," Nico said softly, "please check Sherman's left sleeve. *Skajapow.*"

Ella gasped. "Ella likes that word. Skajapow! Skajapow!"

Grover checked. Sherman's sleeve — sure enough, a bent corner on his *Ares Rage* card. Totally accidental, but Nico had spotted it from across the room.

"Okay," Grover whispered into the mic. "Watch the ten-year-old. I'm serious. Watch him."

---

Katie Gardner is one of the nicest people at camp. She runs the Demeter cabin garden, remembers everyone's birthday, once healed Percy's poison ivy with oat paste while he cried. He did not want to destroy her.

But Mythomagic is Mythomagic.

Terrain roll: Ocean. Yes! Home field.

Percy slapped down his opening hand: two *Saltwater Mermen*, one *Coral Reef* terrain boost, one *Nereid Tidecaller*, one *Poseidon, Earthshaker* — the only god in his opening hand, holographic, edges peeling — and a trap he hoped he wouldn't need.

Katie opened with *Sprout Swarm*. Classic Demeter stall. Little 200-point seedlings everywhere. Cute. Useless against tide.

"Um, Percy?" she said sweetly. "Did you know your cards are upside down?"

Percy looked. They were. "It's a strategy. Confuses the enemy. Also me."

She giggled. The crowd giggled. From Table 2, Percy could hear Annabeth muttering numbers to herself while Connor played *Hermes Speed Theft* and stole a card from her hand. She didn't even flinch. She just wrote something down. Scary.

On his turn three, Percy dropped Tidecaller. Its ability: every Water monster gets +300 for each other Water monster. He had three Mermen out. Math: 3 x 300 = 900 each. Plus Coral Reef +200. Suddenly his goofy fish guys were swinging for 1400 each.

Katie's eyes widened. "Percy, that's — that's actually good math."

"I know, right? I surprised myself too."

Turn five, Percy tributed two Mermen for Poseidon. The hologram caught the sun. The whole table went "ooooh."

Poseidon ability: Earthquake Churn — destroy one land card and deal 1000 to everything without Flight. Katie's entire garden had no Flight. Her field melted.

She top-decked desperately — *Kronos's Wheat Scythe*, a good card — but Percy had exactly enough tide counters to finish.

"Good game," she said, shaking his hand. She wasn't even mad. "Your deck smells like the lake."

"Thank you?"

"You're welcome." She handed him a strawberry. "For victory. Demeter tradition."

Percy ate it. Sweet and warm from the fields.

From across the lawn, Annabeth glanced up from her own match — she'd already won, Connor's life points at zero, Connor staring at his hand like it betrayed him — and mouthed at him: "We need to talk."

His ears turned red. He could feel it.

Uh oh.

---

He won in five turns. Five. Against a stall deck that should have taken at least eight to break.

Annabeth cornered him by the drink cooler.

"Percy."

"Annabeth! Hey! Nice win! I saw you — you did that thing with the lightning and Connor made that face like—"

"How long have you been practicing?" Annabeth asked.

He blinked those sea-green eyes. Innocent. Suspiciously innocent. "Practicing?"

"Your Tidecaller math. Your mulligan decision. You kept a one-god hand on an Ocean roll, which is correct only if you understand hypergeometric distribution, which you claimed last week was quote 'a dinosaur.'"

Percy rubbed the back of his neck. Ears: pink. Light pink. Not full red. Hmm. Partial truth? Embarrassment, not lying?

"I just... I like water cards," he said. "They make sense to me. And Tyson helped me build it. Mostly it's dolphins."

Annabeth stared. He stared back. Behind him, Tyson was indeed playing Malcolm at Table 6 and winning by attacking exclusively with sheep. *Sheep.* Three *Golden Fleece Sheep* with 50 attack each, but Tyson had six *Cyclops Shepherd* cards boosting them to 2000 each. Malcolm, son of Athena, tactical genius, was losing to livestock. He looked like he was reconsidering his entire lineage.

"Percy," Annabeth said slowly, "did you net-deck? Did Luke teach you?"

"No, I swear. I just... I've played a lot before. You were there for some of it." He stopped, sadness flickering across his face before he covered it with a grin. "Maybe more stuck than you thought."

Oh. The rainy afternoons in their old camp, when Beckendorf had been alive and Silena had still been Aphrodite's daughter. Annabeth remembered those too. She remembered beating Percy often enough to stop checking whether he was improving. If cards had been another thing he kept safe from that life...

Her interrogation face softened. "Okay, I underestimated you, and I'm sorry."

"No, it's cool. You're scary when you're smart, you know that?"

"I'm always smart."

"Exactly. Terrifying." He bumped her shoulder with his. "Good luck next round. Try not to vaporize anyone."

"Only if they deserve it."

He walked off to watch Thalia's match. Annabeth watched him go, notebook forgotten.

Something was off. His memories were real — she shared them. But "I've played a lot" didn't explain *that* Tidecaller line with cards neither of them had owned before. That was veteran timing.

She wrote: *PERCY = DARK HORSE. If she faces him, ban Ocean terrain? No — can't. Prepare side deck vs Water.*

Then she underlined it three times.

---

"And Thalia Grace absolutely DEMOLISHES Travis Stoll!" Grover shouted as Thalia's *Zeus, King of Gods* dropped for 4000 damage with *Lightning Rod* amplification. Travis's *Hermes Trickster* field scattered. "That match lasted four turns, folks! Four! Someone check if Travis is still conscious! He's just lying on the blanket staring at the sky! He says he's fine! He's seeing birds!"

Ella flapped. "Ella reports Thalia shuffled seven times. Thalia mutters to cards. Thalia says 'come on, babies, Papa needs a new chariot.' Ella does not think gods gamble. Ella thinks Thalia gambles."

"Great insight, Ella!"

Meanwhile, Table 3: Clarisse La Rue vs. Will Solace. Clarisse played like she fought — loud, aggressive, elbows on table. "BOOM! *Ares War Cry*! BOOM! *Spartan Phalanx*! How do you like THAT, Sunshine?"

Will, sweet Will, glowed faintly when stressed — Apollo kid thing — and kept healing. *Apollo's Lyre* +500 life each turn. *Asclepius Field Medic* stall. Clarisse did 3000 damage. Will healed 2500. It was agony. It was hilarious.

"Come on!" Clarisse yelled. "Stay dead!"

"Sorry!" Will said, healing again. "It's my whole thing!"

It took eleven turns, but Clarisse finally broke through with *Boar of Erymanthus* trample. Will shook her hand, still glowing. "Good game! You have really strong... aura! Very red! Very stabby!"

Clarisse grunted, but she looked pleased.

And Table 4 — oh gods, Table 4.

Nico vs. Sherman. Everyone drifted over to watch by turn six.

Sherman opened aggro: *Warborn Brute*, *Bloodlust Spear*, smash smash smash. Nico at 5000 life by turn three. Looked bad for the kid.

Then Nico played *Styx Ferryman*. Pay 1000 life to mill five cards from opponent's deck to graveyard. Then another Ferryman. Then *Hades, Lord of the Dead* — power equals number of cards in opponent's graveyard x 200.

Sherman's graveyard had nineteen cards. Hades swung for 3800. With *Helm of Darkness* making him untargetable.

Sherman top-decked. Monster. Set it face-down. Prayed.

Nico flipped *Elysium Judgment* — trap — destroy face-down, deal damage equal to its defense. Game.

Silent. Then the whole lawn erupted.

Sherman stared at the board. "You're ten."

"And you overextended," Nico said quietly, gathering his cards with small careful hands. "You overextended on turn four. You should have held Spear."

Sherman opened his mouth. Closed it. "Rematch sometime, kid?"

Nico almost smiled. "Okay."

Clarisse, watching from the next table, barked a laugh. "Ha! Ares cabin got farmed by the little guy! Sherman, you're washed!"

Nico looked at her. Calm black eyes. "Your deck has no answer to Flight. You run zero traps that hit air."

Clarisse's grin froze. "Excuse me, Casper?"

"Round Two bracket," Chiron announced, pinning up the sheet. "Clarisse La Rue vs. Nico di Angelo."

The crowd went OOOOH.

Clarisse cracked her knuckles. "Oh, I'm gonna enjoy this. I'm gonna send you back to kindergarten with a deckbox full of tears."

Nico already sat down at Table 1. He stacked his phone books. He waited.

Ella leaned into the mic. "Ella predicts blood. Ella means card blood. Ella is excited and nervous. Ella hid extra pretzels for comfort."

---

Ella likes tournaments. Tournaments have words. Words like *tribute* and *graveyard* and *banished* and *skajapow*. Ella collects words like shiny bottle caps. Ella has 4,213 words. Skajapow is new. Ella loves skajapow best.

Ella also likes luck. Ella brought luck today. Ella brought a blue bottle cap for Percy because Percy smells like salt and is kind to harpies. Ella brought a gold feather for Annabeth because Annabeth smells like lemon and library and gave Ella a bookmark once that says *READ OR DOOM*. Ella brought a smooth black stone for Nico because Nico smells like cold and pomegranates and is quiet like Ella is quiet.

Ella placed them under Table 1 before Clarisse vs. Nico. Blue cap on the left. Gold feather on the right. Black stone in the middle for balance. For luck symmetry.

"Ella, what are you doing under there?" Grover asked, lifting the tablecloth. Grover smells like goat and cookies.

"Ella is placing luck," Ella whispered. "Do not tell. Luck works better secret."

Grover winked. Grover is good at secrets. Grover pretended not to see.

The match started. Clarisse slammed cards. Loud. Nico placed cards. Soft. Like snow.

Clarisse: "*Ares! War! Blood!*" So many exclamation points.

Nico: "Ferryman. Mill five. Pass."

Ella understood. Ella memorized all cards. Clarisse's deck was fire and fists. Nico's deck was patience and graves. Fire burns fast. Graves wait.

Turn seven. Clarisse had Nico at 1200 life. Clarisse stood up. "FINISH HIM! *Erymanthian Boar*, gore attack!"

Nico flipped a trap. *Stygian Toll.* Pay half your life to negate attack and summon a *Skeleton Warrior* for each 500 damage prevented.

Nico at 600 life. Three skeletons.

Next turn, Nico tributed skeletons for *Hades*. Graveyard count: twenty-four. Hades: 4800 attack.

Clarisse had no Flight. Ella knew. Nico knew. Clarisse did not know that Nico knew.

"Hades attacks," Nico whispered.

Clarisse's life: 8000 to 3200 in one swing.

Clarisse sat down slowly. "Okay. Okay, little guy has teeth."

She fought hard after that. *Phobos Fear* to skip Nico's battle phase. *Deimos Panic* to discard his hand. Good cards. Smart plays. Ella respected Clarisse. Clarisse smells like iron and leather and yells but shuffles gently, like cards are baby birds.

But Nico top-decked *River Lethe*. Both players skip next turn — except Hades cabin monsters are unaffected by Lethe. A common misread. Clarisse misread. Nico did not.

Final attack. Hades scythe down.

Clarisse's life: zero.

Quiet. Then applause like rain.

Clarisse stared at the table for a long moment. Her face was red. Ella worried Clarisse would flip table. Clarisse flipped tables once in the mess hall when someone took her pudding. Ella saw.

Instead, Clarisse exhaled. She reached across. She ruffled Nico's hair. Nico went rigid with surprise.

"Good game, Crypt Keeper," she said gruffly. "You — you actually got me. No excuses. Your mill math is disgusting. I respect it."

Nico blinked. "Really?"

"Yeah. But if you tell anyone I said that, I'll feed your deck to a hellhound."

Nico smiled. Small. Real. "Okay."

Clarisse stood, grabbed her deck, paused. "Hey. Semis. Don't choke. Poseidon kid backing Hades cabin, you hear? Someone's gotta beat Sparkles and Prissy."

Ella flapped with joy. Ella loves character growth. Ella read about character growth in a book Annabeth lent her. Chapter Seven. Ella cried. Ella does not cry now. Ella finds pretzel.

Under the table, Ella moved the black stone forward one inch. For Nico. For luck momentum.

Ella whispers to no one: "Skajapow."

---

Thalia loved Annabeth. She loved camp. She loved her dad even when he was a deadbeat lightning bolt. But gods, she loved winning.

Beckendorf played Hephaestus Forge — slow build, big machines, *Automaton Colossus* with 3000 defense. Good deck. Patient deck. Problem: her deck didn't do patient.

Thalia opened *Electra Spark* into *Storm Harpy* into *Zeus's Thunderbolt* discard. Aggro. Fast. Mean. Her opening hand smelled like ozone and bad decisions.

"Thalia," Beckendorf rumbled, smiling. He was huge, gentle, forearms covered in soot. "You always play like the table insulted you."

"Table looked at me funny," Thalia said, slapping down *Pinecone Punk* — okay, real name *Boreas Wind Spirit*, but Silena named it Pinecone Punk and it stuck. 1600 attack, Flight, and if she controlled another Wind monster it could attack directly.

Beckendorf built his wall. *Bronze Bull* defense 2200. *Forge Fire* trap set. Solid.

Thalia drew. *Lightning Rod.* Equip to Zeus. Double his attack if opponent controlled a Machine.

She didn't have Zeus yet. Bluff. She set a trap face-down, attacked with Harpy into his Bull, took damage deliberately to drop her life low enough to activate *Desperate Storm* — draw two when under 4000.

Annabeth, watching from the sidelines with her notebook, muttered "reckless" loud enough for Thalia to hear. Thalia grinned. Reckless worked when you knew your deck's heartbeat.

Turn six, she top-decked him. *Zeus, King of Gods.* Holographic. The beard glinted.

She tributed Harpy + Punk. Summoned Zeus. Equipped Rod. Beckendorf flipped Forge Fire — destroy one equip. Rod died. Damn.

But she had *Thalia's Own Stupid Luck*, as Annabeth called it, in hand. The actual card was *Chaos Lightning*: roll a d6, deal roll x 500, take half as damage to self.

"Ella," Thalia shouted to the broadcast table, "give me a number!"

Ella squeaked, "Six! Six is most! Ella likes six!"

Grover rolled the giant foam die. Six.

Crowd lost it. 3000 damage to Beckendorf's field. 1500 to her. His Colossus cracked. Her life at 1800. His at 2200.

Next turn, Zeus swung directly. Game.

Beckendorf laughed, shook her hand with fingers that could crush walnuts. "Hell of a gamble."

"Hell of a wall," Thalia said. "Your Colossus almost had me."

They both looked at the updated bracket. Across the lawn, Percy had survived a second-round sheep siege from Tyson; Annabeth had beaten Lee Fletcher's Apollo deck despite his spectacular healing spells. Silena, eliminated by Beckendorf, was supplying lemonade and explaining that the deck had not matched her mood anyway. The four winners gathered as Chiron wrote in careful script:

SEMIFINAL 1: Percy Jackson vs. Nico di Angelo
SEMIFINAL 2: Annabeth Grace vs. Thalia Grace

Sisters vs. sisters. Fish vs. ghost kid.

Annabeth walked over, notebook hugged to her chest. "Thalia."

"Annabeth."

"I'm going to beat you."

"I know you think that."

"No traps that miss, no lucky rolls. I'm going to out-resource you by turn eight."

Thalia slung an arm around her shoulders. Annabeth stiffened, then leaned in. "Good. I want your best. No holding back because I'm your big sister."

"You're only four years older."

"I'm infinitely cooler."

"Debatable."

Across the lawn, Percy was teaching Tyson how to side-deck sheep ("No, buddy, you can't side in *more* sheep, you already have max sheep") while Nico sat alone shuffling, small and still. Percy walked over to Nico, said something, made Nico laugh — actually laugh — and offered him a blue bottle cap Ella must have left. Nico took it, turned it over in his fingers like a coin.

Huh. Seaweed Brain had game. Not just cards. People.

This tournament was getting good.

---

"Folks, we are DOWN TO FOUR!" Grover bleated into the pinecone. His hooves ached from pacing. He'd eaten six cheese cubes for stamina. "This is Grover Underwood with Ella Harpyella on color!"

"Ella is Ella," Ella corrected, beak full of pretzel. "But Ella appreciates promotion."

"Semifinal One! Water vs. Underworld! Percy Jackson versus Nico di Angelo! Terrain roll—" Grover rolled. "Oh! Oh wow! GRAVEYARD! Bonus to Dark monsters, Water monsters lose 200 attack! That's huge! That's like taking Poseidon's trident and making it a pool noodle!"

Percy groaned. Nico nodded once, calm.

Grover leaned in. This was the match he'd worried about. Percy played heart-on-sleeve, big splashy gods. Nico played like winter — slow, inevitable. And Nico had already dissected Clarisse.

But Percy... Percy did something weird turn one. He set two cards face-down and passed with no monsters. No defense. Nico blinked.

"Interesting!" Grover whispered. "Percy goes naked! Bold! Stupid! Possibly both!"

Nico attacked directly with Ferryman. Percy at 7000. Percy didn't flinch.

Turn two, Percy flipped *Tidal Gift*: opponent draws two, Percy gains 1000 life for each Water monster in his graveyard. He had zero. So Nico drew two free... wait, why?

Annabeth, watching, scribbled furiously. "He's feeding him," she whispered to Thalia. "He WANTS Nico's graveyard full? No, he wants Nico's HAND full? Why?"

Turn three, Nico milled Percy. Percy smiled.

Turn four, Percy played *Lotus Eaters*. Both players skip battle phase while it's on field. Stall card. Nobody plays Lotus Eaters. It's a joke card. Art is just three guys napping.

Nico frowned. "Why?"

Percy shrugged. "Vacation."

He stalled three turns, building tribute in graveyard, letting Nico mill him deeper. Nico's Hades grew — graveyard count climbing: 10, 14, 18. Hades at 3600.

Then Percy flipped his second face-down from turn one. It had been waiting four turns. *Atlantis Rebirth.* Shuffle selected graveyard cards back into the deck, gain life equal to cards shuffled x 100, and Water monsters gain 100 per card. Its next-turn permission also summoned one Water god without tribute.

He shuffled twenty-two cards. Gained 2200 life. His field: empty. His deck: fat. Nico's Hades: suddenly 400 attack, because only two spent cards remained in Percy's graveyard.

The crowd gasped. Grover dropped his pinecone.

"OH MY GODS!" Grover screamed, picking it up. "HE DECKED THE MILL! HE LET NICO MILL HIM AND THEN RESET! NICO'S BOSS MONSTER IS A WET NAPKIN!"

Ella flapped wildly. "Ella did not see! Ella loves surprise! Skajapow! SKAJAPOW!"

Nico stared. Then — slowly — he smiled. "Cool."

"It can't," he said softly. "We were... we were together, and we were older, and it was harder, but we still won. Together. We remember that. I'm not giving it up."

Her throat tightened. Stupid. Stupid feelings.

"Neither am I," Annabeth whispered.

"Yeah," he said. "I'm trying to keep the good parts real. Better this time. More... more afternoons like this. Less fear. Just... us. And cards. And sheep."

Thalia squeezed her shoulder. Ella murmured in her sleep: "Skajapow..."

Grover raised his juice box. "To the champion! To the runner-up! To the ten-year-old terror! To sheep!"

"TO SHEEP!" they all shouted. Pigeons fled. Mr. D sighed. Chiron smiled.

Annabeth looked at Percy — fish boy, dark horse, keeper of their memories — and thought: next tournament, she was running three Zeuses and a side deck just for him. Next time, no smiles. Next time, war.

Annabeth turned to Percy and pointed at his deck.

"Eleven shuffles," she said. "Next time Chiron imposes a limit."

"Since when are you in charge of that?"

"Since now."

Thalia laughed and offered Annabeth the last juice box. Percy started to protest, then noticed his own deck still needed sorting. Annabeth borrowed two cards to demonstrate the move that would have beaten him, and they were arguing about it long after the tournament table was cleared.
