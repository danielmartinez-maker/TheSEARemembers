## Chapter 106: The Visit That Wasn't a Test

Clarisse knew Poseidon was coming because the lake went quiet.

Not calm. Quiet. The ripples stopped, the naiads vanished beneath the surface, and the usual little sounds of water against the docks seemed to disappear. Clarisse noticed it before Percy did. She was at the forge returning a borrowed shield when the silence slid over the camp.

She found Percy and Silena at the Poseidon cabin, both staring toward the lake.

"You feel that?" Silena asked.

Percy nodded. "Dad's here."

Clarisse stayed by the doorway. For years, Clarisse could count his visits on one hand. Once, when she was small, he’d brought a toy trident she couldn’t lift. Once he’d asked whether she liked camp, then left before dinner. The recent palace visit and barbecue were attempts to change that. She still didn’t know whether a quiet afternoon would follow all the ceremony.

She didn't know what to do with a father who arrived like weather and left like a tide.

The water at the lake's center rose in a smooth pillar. Poseidon stepped out of it in the same broad-shouldered form Percy had described, sea-green eyes and damp white robes. He looked first at Percy, then at Clarisse and Silena.

"My daughters," he said.

Clarisse had prepared for this. She'd thought of a dozen replies, all sharp. None came out.

Silena stepped forward first. "Hello, Father."

Poseidon smiled at her, then turned to Clarisse. "Clarisse. You've grown."

"Even since the barbecue. That happens."

Percy glanced at her, but Poseidon didn't seem offended. He nodded, as if she had given a perfectly reasonable answer.

"I have been away too long," he said. "I will not pretend otherwise."

That wasn't an apology. It wasn't nothing either.

Silena's face was careful. "Why are you here now?"

"To see my children. To speak with them without asking them to travel to Atlantis or Olympus." He looked at Percy. "And because Percy has been missed."

Clarisse heard the *and*. The qualifier. The reason that made this visit make sense to the god. She felt her old anger begin to rise, but Percy was watching her, not Poseidon. He didn't look pleading. He just looked aware.

Clarisse breathed out slowly.

"There's a table by the lake," she said. "If you're staying for lunch, we can use that."

Poseidon looked surprised. "I would be glad to."

"Then help carry the food."

Silena hid a smile.

---

Poseidon did help carry the food. He carried too much of it at once, which made the task less helpful than he intended. Clarisse let him carry the bread basket and took the plates from his other hand.

They ate under the shade trees, with the lake in view. Poseidon asked Percy about Atlantis: his classes, his swimming, whether Triton was still teasing him. He asked Clarisse about her training and Silena about her music. He listened to the answers.

That was the part Clarisse didn't know how to handle. She'd expected the questions to be formal, something a king asked because a king was supposed to ask. But Poseidon remembered details. He knew Clarisse preferred the eastern training ground because the footing was rougher. He knew Silena had started singing in the amphitheater. He knew Percy had been taking lessons in Greek history even though he already knew most of it from another life.

Maybe he'd been watching from far away. Maybe he'd been told. Maybe he had actually been trying, even when he wasn't there.

Clarisse wasn't ready to forgive him. But she could acknowledge that the lunch was not a performance.

When Percy began describing the Atlantean game of currents, Poseidon demonstrated it with four shells and a glass of water. Clarisse and Silena joined in. Clarisse won twice by disrupting the flow with her thumb.

"You cheat," Silena said.

"I adapt."

Poseidon laughed. "That is a Poseidon answer."

Clarisse glanced at him. "Is that a compliment?"

"From me, yes."

She didn't know what to do with that either, so she asked him to play another round.

---

Later, Percy took Poseidon to the Tempest Hall to show him the training pool. Clarisse stayed behind with Silena to clear the dishes. She was grateful for the task. It gave her something to do with her hands.

Silena stacked the plates. "You were polite."

"I was practical. He was carrying food."

"You invited him to lunch."

"He was already here."

Silena put a plate in the basket. "You don't have to decide what you feel about him today."

Clarisse rinsed a cup. "I don't feel anything."

"That's not true."

"Fine. I feel annoyed. And a little glad he remembered the training ground. And annoyed that I'm glad." She dried her hands. "Is that enough complicated for you?"

"For today."

Clarisse leaned against the table. "You think he's going to come back?"

"I don't know."

"Neither do I." She glanced toward the hall. "I don't want him to promise. I want him to show up."

Silena reached for her hand. "Then tell him that."

Clarisse pulled her hand away—not because she didn't want the touch, but because she didn't want to cry over a lunch table. "Maybe later."

---

Poseidon left before dinner.

He said goodbye to each of them separately. Percy got a hug and a promise to send a message. Silena got a small vial of sea-glass beads for her jewelry. Clarisse got no gift. She was relieved and a little disappointed.

At the lake's edge, Poseidon turned to her. "I would like to visit again. Not as a king checking on his children. As your father."

Clarisse crossed her arms. "Then do that."

He nodded. "I will try."

"Don't promise. Just come."

Poseidon looked at her for a long moment. "I understand."

He stepped into the water and was gone.

Clarisse stood at the dock until the lake began making its ordinary sounds again. Percy came up beside her.

"How do you feel?" he asked.

"I don't know."

"That's fair."

She bumped his shoulder. "You always say that."

"Because it's usually true."

They watched the last ripples fade.

"He remembered the training ground," Clarisse said.

"Yeah."

"He might come back."

"Yeah."

"I'm not getting my hopes up."

"You don't have to."

Clarisse looked at him. "You can hope for me."

Percy nodded. "Okay."

Silena appeared with three cups of nectar tea. She handed one to each of them and stood between the siblings as the light thinned over the lake.

For once, neither sister tried to fill the silence.

---

That night, Annabeth found Percy on the dock.

"How was the visit?"

"Good, I think. Complicated. Clarisse is trying not to care. Silena's trying not to hope too hard."

"And you?"

"I was glad to see him. I also wish he'd been there more for them." Percy looked down at their hands. "I don't know how to hold both things at once."

Annabeth interlaced her fingers with his. "You don't have to make them cancel each other out. You can love him and still be angry about what he didn't do."

Percy looked at her. "That's annoyingly wise."

"I know."

He leaned over and kissed her, gentle and sure. She kissed him back, then settled against his shoulder.

They stayed until the stars came out, talking about nothing important: the bad lunch bread, Silena's beads, Clarisse's stubbornness, the shape of the moon's reflection.

When they walked back to the cabins, Percy held her hand openly.

At the fork in the path, Annabeth leaned up and kissed him once more.

"Goodnight, favorite person."

"Goodnight."

He watched her reach the Zeus cabin door, then turned toward the Poseidon cabin. Behind him, the lake shone quietly in the dark.
