## Chapter 42: Slow Dance

The dining pavilion smelled like grilled chicken and bonfire smoke, and Percy Jackson could not concentrate on either one.

It was Friday night, which usually meant the campfire. Usually that meant singing too loud off-key with the whole camp, trying not to get chosen for the sing-along solos by the Apollo kids, and roasting marshmallows until they caught fire. Percy liked campfire nights. They were loud and warm and easy.

Tonight was different. At the head pavilion, Chiron had stood up after the offering bowls had burned down and tapped his hoof against the marble to get attention.

"Tonight," Chiron had said, with that amused glint that meant he knew exactly what kind of chaos he was starting, "the Apollo cabin has prepared a music night. No sing-alongs required. Dancing encouraged."

A ripple had gone around the pavilion. Somebody from the Aphrodite table had squealed. Somebody from the Ares table had groaned like dancing was a personal insult. At the Poseidon table, between Clarisse and Silena, Percy had frozen with his fork halfway to his mouth.

Dancing. Encouraged.

Across the pavilion, at the Zeus table, Annabeth Grace had looked straight at him.

She did not wave. She did not smile in a big obvious way. She just looked at him, her gray eyes steady, and raised one eyebrow the smallest fraction, like she was asking him a question without saying anything at all.

Percy had nearly dropped his fork into his milk.

Now dinner was almost over, the dishes were clearing themselves back to the kitchen, and Percy still had not taken a full breath.

He walked out of the pavilion with his hands shoved deep in the pockets of his shorts, sneakers scuffing on the stone path. The sun was going down behind the hills, turning the sky orange and pink over the water. Crickets were starting up in the grass. The air smelled like salt from the Sound and woodsmoke from the campfire pit that the Hephaestus kids were already building up high.

"You look like you're going to throw up," Clarisse said from beside him.

Percy jumped. He had not heard her come up. Clarisse La Rue fell into step with him like she had been there the whole time, her spear slung over her shoulder, her camp necklace swinging. At thirteen, she already walked like someone who expected the ground to get out of her way.

"I don't look like that," Percy said.

"You do," Clarisse said. "You look seasick on land, which is embarrassing for a son of Poseidon."

"Thanks. Really supportive sister talk."

Clarisse snorted. "Who said I was being supportive?"

They walked together down toward the amphitheater where the campfire was set. Ahead of them, Silena Beauregard was walking with a group of Aphrodite kids, laughing about something, her dark hair catching the sunset light. When she spotted Percy and Clarisse, she broke away and jogged back to them.

At fourteen, Silena carried herself like sunlight. Where Clarisse was all sharp edges, Silena was all warmth, and Percy still sometimes could not quite believe they were both his sisters. Same father, different mothers, the same cabin, but the way Silena immediately slung an arm around his shoulders and Clarisse immediately pretended not to notice felt like family anyway.

"Are you excited?" Silena asked. "I heard Austin tuned his guitar for like three hours. Connor said he threatened to string up anyone who touched the pegs."

"Percy's not excited," Clarisse said. "Percy's terrified."

"I'm not terrified," Percy said quickly. Too quickly.

Silena looked at him, then glanced toward the Zeus table group walking ahead, where a tall girl with black spiky hair and a smaller blonde girl were walking side by side. Thalia Grace had one hand on Annabeth's backpack strap, tugging it lightly every few steps to annoy her. Annabeth kept swatting her hand away without looking.

"Oh," Silena said softly, understanding spreading across her face. "Oh, you want to ask her to dance."

Percy's ears went hot. "So?"

"So nothing!" Silena said. "That's cute. That's really cute."

"It's not cute," Clarisse muttered. "It's a death wish. Dancing in front of everybody? No thanks."

"You dance just fine when you think nobody's watching," Silena said.

"I do not."

"You do. Last winter in the Big House when the radio was on—"

"Finish that sentence and die," Clarisse said flatly.

Silena grinned and squeezed Percy's shoulders. "Ignore her. You should ask her. Annabeth would love that."

Percy looked down at his sneakers. His stomach was doing the same thing it did on a boat in rough water, swooping and rolling. Which was ridiculous. He had faced a Fury in a bus station. He had held up the sky in the original life he still remembered clearly. And the thought of walking across a campfire circle and asking a twelve-year-old girl to dance made his hands sweat.

But then he remembered the way Annabeth had looked at him in the pavilion. Steady. Patient. Like she already knew what he was thinking and she was willing to wait for him to get there.

"I want to," he said quietly. "I just — what if I step on her feet? What if I'm bad at it?"

Silena bumped her shoulder against his. "Then you step on her feet and you both laugh about it. That's part of it."

Clarisse was quiet for a moment. Then she said, gruffly, "Just don't do that dumb shuffle you do when you're nervous. Pick up your feet."

"That was helpful," Percy said. "Thank you."

"You're welcome," Clarisse said, like she had given him excellent advice.

They reached the edge of the amphitheater. The stone steps curved down in a half-circle around a wide dirt floor and a stone fire pit in the center. Logs were already stacked high, and a son of Hephaestus was lighting them with a controlled burst from a bronze torch. Campers were pouring in, claiming seats, spreading blankets, laughing.

The Apollo cabin was set up to one side on a low wooden platform. Guitars, a violin, a couple of drums, a lyre that gleamed gold in the firelight. Austin Lake was tuning, tongue stuck out in concentration. Kayla Knowles was whispering with her sister and giggling.

And off to the side, sitting on the edge of the platform with his legs swinging, was Grover Underwood, holding his reed pipes.

Percy felt some of the tightness in his chest loosen at the sight of his best friend. Grover caught his eye and waved his pipes in the air, grinning wide enough to show all his teeth.

Percy waved back. Okay. Okay. Grover was here. That helped.

---

The fire caught fast.

Flames climbed the stacked logs, crackling and popping, sending sparks spiraling up into the darkening sky. The whole amphitheater glowed orange. Faces turned golden. The smoke curled up sweet and sharp, and for a minute everybody just sat and watched it catch, the way they always did, quiet in that shared way that made Percy think of the ocean at night.

Chiron, in his wheelchair form for the evening, rolled to the front near the fire. Mr. D was there too, slouched in a lawn chair with a can of Diet Coke, looking profoundly bored.

"Welcome," Chiron said. "No campfire songs tonight. Tonight we let Apollo cabin show us what they have been practicing. Listen, relax — and if the music moves you, there is plenty of room to dance."

He gestured to the open dirt circle in front of the platform, worn smooth by years of feet.

Somebody whooped. Somebody else clapped. Austin looked like he might pass out but lifted his guitar anyway.

Percy sat on the stone steps about halfway up, wedged between Grover, who had come to join him before his set, and a Demeter kid who was already braiding grass into a bracelet. His eyes kept drifting, without permission, to the other side of the circle.

Annabeth Grace sat with her sister.

Thalia had sprawled across two whole steps like she owned them, combat boots planted wide, leather jacket over her camp shirt despite the warm evening. Annabeth sat tucked against her side, knees pulled up, chin resting on them. Thalia had one arm draped loosely around Annabeth's shoulders, and Annabeth kept leaning into it like it was the most natural thing in the world, even while she pretended to be annoyed when Thalia tugged on the end of her ponytail.

They looked like sisters. Not just because they both had gray eyes and stubborn chins, or because Chiron had announced it that way when Thalia arrived — daughter of Zeus, older sister to Annabeth, both of them carrying the name Grace like a banner. They looked like sisters in the way Thalia kept shifting slightly to block the wind from hitting Annabeth, and the way Annabeth kept passing Thalia pieces of the granola bar she had smuggled from dinner without even looking.

Percy loved watching them together. In the life he and Annabeth still remembered, Thalia had once been a tree on a hill, and later a Hunter whose visits were rare. Annabeth had carried the missing years quietly. Here her sister had stayed beside her through a childhood Annabeth could not remember, and was still beside her now.

Here, now, Thalia was alive and solid and annoying, and Annabeth got to have a big sister. Percy would never get tired of seeing it.

"You're staring," Grover said, nudging him with an elbow.

"I'm not staring," Percy said.

"You are absolutely staring," Grover said. "Your mouth is even a little open."

Percy shut his mouth.

Grover laughed, a soft bleating sound. He twirled his pipes in his hands. "Are you going to ask her?"

Percy's stomach swooped again. "Everyone keeps asking me that."

"Because everyone can tell you want to," Grover said reasonably. "You get this look. Like a golden retriever who found its person."

"I do not look like a golden retriever."

"You a little bit do," Grover said cheerfully. "It's a compliment. Golden retrievers are loyal. And cute."

Percy groaned and put his face in his hands. "I don't know how to dance, G-man."

"Nobody knows how to dance," Grover said. "We're twelve. Well, I'm technically older in satyr years, but still. You just move. Listen to the music and move. Annabeth doesn't care if you're good at it."

"How do you know?"

"Because she likes you," Grover said simply. "She likes you when you snore on the drops. She likes you when you get seaweed in your hair. She's going to like you when you step on her toes."

"I don't want to step on her toes," Percy mumbled into his hands.

Grover patted his back. "Then watch her feet and say sorry a lot. It'll be fine."

On the platform, Austin strummed a chord. It rang out clear and bright over the fire crackle. Kayla joined in on violin, finding the note, and the chatter in the amphitheater faded as everybody turned to listen.

The music started — a light, bouncy tune first, something easy. A couple of younger kids from the Hermes cabin jumped up immediately and started spinning each other in circles, shrieking with laughter when they got dizzy and fell over. A pair from Demeter and Aphrodite joined in. The dirt circle filled with movement and color and firelight.

Percy watched. His hands were damp. He wiped them on his shorts.

He thought about Annabeth. The same Annabeth who remembered being seventeen and kissing him on a beach, now twelve again beside him. The girl who left him little folded notes in his shield with tiny architectural sketches in the margins. The girl who held his hand under the dinner table when thunderstorms rolled over the Sound and pretended she was just reaching for the bread. The girl who had once spent an entire afternoon explaining to him why the stars in the summer sky made different shapes than the winter ones, and who had fallen asleep mid-sentence with her head on his shoulder.

He wanted to dance with that girl.

He stood up before he could talk himself out of it.

"Whoa," Grover said, looking up. "Where are you going?"

"To — to ask her," Percy said. His knees felt strange, like they belonged to somebody else. "Wish me luck."

Grover beamed. "You don't need luck. But good luck anyway. I'll be up there playing in a minute. I'll play something good, I promise."

Percy nodded, throat too tight to answer, and started down the stone steps.

---

The walk across the amphitheater felt ten miles long.

Percy was very aware of his feet. He was very aware of his arms, which suddenly seemed too long. What did people do with their arms when they walked? He had never thought about it before. Now he could not think about anything else.

He passed the fire. Heat washed over his face. Sparks lifted past his shoulders toward the stars just starting to prick through the blue-black sky.

Thalia saw him coming first.

Her eyes narrowed, then widened with wicked delight. She straightened up, elbowed Annabeth in the side, and said loudly enough for the three rows around them to hear, "Well, well, well. Look who finally grew legs and walked over here."

Annabeth's head snapped up.

She had been watching the dancers, chin on her knees, a small smile on her face. When she saw Percy standing there at the bottom of the steps, her smile changed — softened into something private, just for him.

Percy forgot about his arms.

"Hi," he said.

"Hi," Annabeth said.

Thalia looked between them, grinning. "Hi," she mimicked in a high squeaky voice. "Hi. Great conversation, you two. Real smooth."

"Thalia," Annabeth said, without looking away from Percy, "shut up."

"Make me, baby sister," Thalia said, but there was no edge in it. She ruffled Annabeth's ponytail.

Percy swallowed. His heart was hammering so loud he was sure the Apollo kids could hear it over their guitars. He looked at Annabeth — really looked at her. Firelight in her blonde hair. A smudge of marshmallow on her cheek from earlier. Her hands clasped tight around her knees like she was nervous too, and somehow that made it easier.

Because if she was nervous too, then they were nervous together.

"Annabeth," he said, and his voice cracked a little on the middle of her name. He cleared his throat and tried again. "Annabeth Grace, do you — do you want to dance with me?"

It came out too fast. Too loud. A kid from the Nemesis cabin two rows up turned to look.

Annabeth blinked. Then she smiled — a real smile, wide and bright, that crinkled the corners of her gray eyes.

"Yeah," she said. "Yeah, I do."

She stood up, brushing off her shorts. Thalia made a loud kissing noise.

"Thalia!" Annabeth hissed, cheeks going pink.

"What?" Thalia said innocently. "I didn't say anything. I just have dry lips."

Percy held out his hand. He had not planned to do that. It just happened — his hand came up, palm open, a little shaky.

Annabeth looked at it for half a second. Then she slipped her hand into his.

Her fingers were warm. Smaller than his, with calluses on the tips from pencils and climbing and dagger grips. Her palm fit against his like it had been measured for him.

Percy's brain went very quiet. All the buzzing worry stopped. There was just her hand in his, and the music, and the firelight.

"Don't step on her feet, Jackson," Thalia called after them as they turned toward the dance circle. "She'll stab you."

"I will not!" Annabeth said over her shoulder.

"She will!" Thalia said cheerfully. "I've seen her practice knives!"

Percy laughed, shaky but real, and led Annabeth down to the edge of the dirt circle. His hand was still holding hers. He did not want to let go.

The fast song was ending. Austin finished with a flourish, Kayla dragged her bow across the strings, and the little kids spun to a stop, panting and giggling. Applause rippled around the steps.

"Thanks!" Austin said into the microphone, voice cracking. "Uh — this next one's slower. So, you know. If you want. Slow dance. Yeah."

He looked terrified. Kayla patted his shoulder.

Percy looked at Annabeth. Annabeth looked at Percy.

"Slow dance?" Percy asked quietly. "Is that okay? We can sit back down if you want—"

Annabeth squeezed his hand. "I want to," she said. "Just — I don't really know how."

"Me neither," Percy admitted. "Grover said just move and say sorry a lot."

Annabeth laughed, a small breathy sound. "Okay. We can be bad at it together."

They stepped into the circle together.

---

The slow song started with guitar, soft and simple. Then the violin came in, sweet and low. Then — Percy's head turned — Grover's pipes, threading through the melody like water through stones.

Grover sat on the edge of the platform now, hooves tucked under him, cheeks puffed out as he played. His eyes were closed. The notes from his pipes sounded different from the other instruments. Wilder. Older. Like wind in tall grass and rain on leaves. Percy had heard Grover play a hundred times on the hill with the sheep, or in the woods when he thought nobody was listening, but this was different. This was Grover playing for people, on purpose, and putting everything he had into it.

It sounded like growing things. It sounded like friendship.

Percy turned back to Annabeth. They stood in the middle of the dirt circle, hands still linked, while other couples moved around them — an older pair from Aphrodite swaying easily, Silena dancing with an Aphrodite friend, laughing with her head tipped back.

"We're supposed to —" Percy started, then stopped. "Do you put your hands on shoulders? Or waist? I don't — my mom and Paul danced in the kitchen once, but I was like seven and I hid because it was embarrassing—"

Annabeth was looking at him with so much patience it made his chest ache. "How about this?" she said softly. She lifted her free hand and rested it lightly on his shoulder. "And you put your hand — here?" She gestured with her chin to her shoulder.

Percy carefully placed his hand on her shoulder. It felt too big and clumsy there. He was afraid to press down.

"And then we hold hands with the other ones," Annabeth said, lifting their joined hands slightly. "Like this?"

"Okay," Percy said. "Okay, yeah."

They stood like that, stiff as boards, swaying the tiniest bit side to side while the music played around them.

"This is dancing," Percy said solemnly. "We are dancing."

Annabeth giggled. "We're terrible."

"We're the worst," Percy agreed. "We're setting dancing back fifty years."

She laughed harder, and her shoulders shook under his hand, and Percy felt himself relax by slow degrees. He started to actually hear the music instead of just the pounding in his ears. Guitar. Violin. Grover's pipes. The crackle of the fire. Annabeth laughing.

He moved his foot. She moved hers. He stepped left. She stepped right. They bumped knees.

"Sorry," Percy said immediately.

"It's okay," Annabeth said.

He stepped on her toe.

"Sorry!"

"Percy, it's okay, really—"

He stepped on her other toe.

"Sorry!"

Annabeth was laughing so hard she had to stop moving. "Percy! Stop saying sorry!"

"But I keep stepping on you!"

"It's fine! Sneakers are padded!" She was still holding his hand, still close, eyes shining with laughter and firelight. "Just — look at me instead of my feet. That might help."

Percy looked at her.

That helped and also did not help at all, because looking at Annabeth Grace up close, with the fire turning her eyes to molten silver and her hand warm in his, made his thoughts scatter like minnows.

But his feet seemed to do better when he was not thinking about them. They found a rhythm. Slow. Swaying. Step, step, pause. Step, step, pause. Annabeth followed, then started to anticipate, then started to lead a little when he hesitated.

"See?" she whispered. "We're doing it."

Percy glanced down. Their sneakers moved in the dust, scuffing small clouds. Left. Together. Right. Together. Not graceful. Not smooth. But together.

"We're doing it," he repeated, amazed.

The song went on. The fire popped. Grover's pipes rose and fell.

Percy became aware of the rest of the world again in soft pieces. The way Annabeth's hand on his shoulder had slid just slightly so her fingers curled into the fabric of his camp shirt. The way his own thumb kept brushing absently over her knuckles where their hands were joined, back and forth, without him telling it to. The way the light caught the small gold owl bead on her necklace.

Neither of them talked for a while. They did not need to.

---

On the steps, Thalia Grace watched them dance.

She had started out ready to tease. That was her job, as far as she was concerned. Baby sister gets a boyfriend, big sister makes his life miserable. It was practically a sacred law. She had already planned three different nicknames for Percy based on how red his face got when Annabeth held his hand, and she was workshopping a dramatic reenactment of his voice crack for later.

But as the slow song settled over the amphitheater, the jokes dried up in her throat.

She watched Percy step on Annabeth's feet three times in a row. She watched Annabeth throw her head back and laugh, not annoyed at all. She watched them stop, recalibrate, start again, slower this time, eyes locked on each other like there was nobody else in the circle.

Thalia felt something strange and tight pull behind her ribs.

It was not jealousy. She checked for that first, because Thalia always checked. She did not want to dance like that. The thought of holding somebody's hand and swaying in front of everybody made her skin itch. She liked her space. She liked running fast and hitting hard and sleeping under open sky with nobody in arm's reach.

No, it was not jealousy. It was something quieter.

Annabeth looked happy.

Not the sharp, fierce happy she got when she solved a puzzle or won a sparring match or finished a blueprint that actually worked. Not the bright, brittle happy she wore around their dad when she was trying too hard to be perfect. This was soft. Un guarded. Her whole face open, eyes crinkled, mouth curved, cheeks pink from laughing.

Thalia had not seen her sister look like that in a long time. Maybe ever. In the short months since Annabeth had begun asking questions about things Thalia thought they both remembered, she had spent most of her time trying to bridge the distance. Staying up too late telling stories. Teaching Annabeth how to throw a punch with her whole hip behind it. Braiding her hair too tight on purpose to make her yell.

She loved her sister fiercely, with the helpless ferocity of someone whose years together had suddenly become unfamiliar to the person she loved.

But she could not give Annabeth this. This soft, steady happiness. This belonging with someone who looked at her like she was the fixed point in a spinning world.

Percy could.

Thalia leaned forward, elbows on her knees, chin in her hands, and watched them turn in a slow, wobbly circle in the dust. Percy said something. Annabeth shook her head, laughing, and leaned her forehead briefly against his shoulder before straightening up again. Percy went pink to the tips of his ears but did not move away. If anything, he held on a little tighter.

"Kids are gross," said a voice beside her.

Thalia glanced over. Clarisse had dropped onto the step next to her without asking, which was very Clarisse. She had two cups of water in her hands and a blanket folded over one arm. She set one cup down on the step between them pointedly and kept the other.

Thalia raised an eyebrow. "Since when do you bring me water?"

"Since Silena told me to stop brooding and be nice," Clarisse said flatly. "Drink it or don't. I don't care."

Thalia picked up the cup and drank. It was cold. "Thanks."

Clarisse grunted. She stared out at the dance circle, jaw working. Her eyes kept flicking to the same spot Thalia's did — to the dark-haired boy in the middle turning in slow circles with the blonde girl, both of them smiling like idiots.

"Your sister's going to get her toes broken," Clarisse said.

"Your brother's trying his best," Thalia said.

"He's got two left feet. Both of them mine, apparently, because there's no way Poseidon gave him those."

Thalia snorted. "He asked her, though. In front of everybody. That takes guts."

Clarisse was quiet. Then, grudgingly, "Yeah. It does."

They sat in silence for a minute. The music swelled. Grover hit a high, clear note that hung over the fire like a star.

"Do you ever —" Clarisse started, then stopped. She scowled at her water like it had offended her. "Never mind."

"What?" Thalia asked.

Clarisse jerked her chin toward the circle. "They're just kids. Twelve. Holding hands like it's some big deal. It's stupid."

Thalia studied her. Clarisse's face was set in its usual hard lines, but her hands were tight around her cup, and she kept glancing at Silena dancing easily in the circle, laughing, free.

"It's not stupid," Thalia said quietly.

Clarisse shot her a look. "I didn't say I thought it was stupid."

"You literally just said it's stupid."

"I meant —" Clarisse huffed. "I meant other people probably think it's stupid. Not me. I don't care."

Thalia smiled a little. "Sure you don't, La Rue."

"I don't," Clarisse insisted. "Why would I care if Kelp Head learns to dance? Not my problem if he falls on his face."

But her eyes stayed on them. And after a minute, she unfolded the blanket over her knees and said, almost too low to hear over the music, "He looks happy, though."

Thalia nodded, throat suddenly tight. "Yeah. He does."

"She does too," Clarisse admitted. "Your sister."

"Yeah."

They did not say anything else. They just sat side by side, two older sisters who would both deny they were feeling anything at all, watching two twelve-year-olds learn to slow dance in the firelight and pretending they were not moved by it one bit.

---

Percy had lost track of the song.

He did not know if they were still on the same one or if it had changed into another one without him noticing. Time felt bendy around the edges, the way it did underwater when light filtered down and everything slowed.

Annabeth's hand was still in his. His shoulder ached a little from holding it up so stiffly, but he did not want to move. Annabeth's fingers on his shoulder had relaxed, her thumb tracing small absent circles against his collarbone through his shirt. Every time she did it, Percy's breath caught.

"You okay?" Annabeth whispered. "You're quiet."

"I'm good," Percy said. "I'm just — thinking. Is that okay? Can I think while dancing?"

Annabeth smiled. "You can do whatever you want."

He thought about how her eyes looked when she laughed. He thought about the note she had slipped into his pocket that morning — a tiny sketch of the amphitheater with two stick figures in the middle and an arrow pointing to them that said us later? with a question mark. He had kept it. He kept all of them, folded carefully in the drawer of his cabin nightstand.

He thought about being seventeen in the life they remembered. Holding her hand on a beach while the sun went down. Knowing exactly how she took her tea and how she sounded when she was about to win an argument and how she felt falling asleep against his side during movies. Waking up twelve again with his heart pounding and his face wet, reaching for someone who was three cabins away and five years younger than the girl in his memory.

It had been confusing at first. Aching. Like missing someone who was standing right next to you.

But moments like this made it clearer. He did not need to rush to get back to seventeen. He liked twelve. He liked finding what they wanted together now, slower this time, sweeter. A new evening in a relationship they both remembered.

Their first slow dance at this camp. Her hand in his. Dust on their sneakers.

"Can I tell you something?" Percy whispered.

Annabeth nodded, eyes wide and trusting.

"I'm glad it's us," he said. "I'm glad we get to do this — like this. Young. Without all the scary stuff yet. Just music and fire and stepping on your feet."

Annabeth's eyes shone. She blinked fast. "Me too," she whispered back. "I like practicing with you."

"Practicing?"

"For later," she said simply. "For when we're older. We'll already know how."

Percy grinned. His chest felt too full. "We'll be experts. Best dancers at camp."

"We're already the best dancers at camp," Annabeth said with absolute confidence.

"We stepped on each other like twelve times."

"Artistic interpretation," Annabeth said solemnly.

Percy laughed, and as he laughed, Annabeth leaned in.

They had kissed before, in both lives. Tonight neither hurried to close the space completely; Annabeth smiled at him while the music slowed, and the warmth in his chest was enough. They had time for another song.

Instead, Annabeth leaned her forehead gently against his.

Her skin was warm from the fire. Her breath brushed his chin. Percy went very still, then carefully tilted his head so his forehead rested against hers. Their joined hands lifted slightly between them. Her fingers on his shoulder curled in.

The world narrowed to that small point of contact. Forehead to forehead. Breath to breath. Music wrapping around them like water.

Percy's eyes burned. He closed them.

He could hear Grover's pipes, clear and tender. He could smell woodsmoke and Annabeth's shampoo and the faint salt that always clung to his own skin. He could feel Annabeth's hand holding his like an anchor.

This, he thought. He wanted to remember this exactly like this. Not the grand battles or the prophecies or even the remembered kisses at seventeen. This. Twelve years old. Slow dancing badly. Forehead leaned against hers while the fire crackled and his best friend played pipes and his sisters watched from the steps.

When the song ended, neither of them moved right away.

Applause broke out around them. Annabeth pulled back just enough to look at him, eyes bright, cheeks flushed. Percy smiled at her, helpless and happy.

"Did we do okay?" he whispered.

Annabeth squeezed his hand. "We did perfect."

---

The next song was faster, and the circle filled up again with spinning, laughing campers. Percy and Annabeth stepped out of the way, still holding hands, both a little dazed.

"Water?" Percy asked. "You look thirsty. Dancing is hard work."

"So thirsty," Annabeth said dramatically, fanning herself. "My partner wore me out with his fancy footwork. Right on my toes."

"I said sorry!"

"You said sorry fifteen times!"

They were both laughing as they climbed the steps. Percy led her back toward where Thalia had been sitting — and stopped.

The steps were empty except for Thalia and Clarisse sitting shoulder to shoulder like unlikely bookends. And in front of them, spread across three whole steps, was a blanket, folded neatly, with two cups of water on it and a small plate of cookies that definitely had not been there before.

Clarisse saw them looking and immediately scowled. "What? Silena saved those. Not me. I just — I was sitting here. Don't make it weird."

Thalia grinned widely. "She saved you seats, she means. She fought off three Hermes kids for this spot. It was violent. It was beautiful."

"I did not fight anybody," Clarisse snapped. "I told them to move and they moved because I am intimidating."

"She saved you seats," Thalia repeated to Annabeth and Percy, enunciating clearly.

Annabeth looked at Clarisse, eyes soft. "Thanks, Clarisse."

Clarisse looked away, ears pink. "Whatever. Sit down before I change my mind and take the blanket back."

Percy and Annabeth sat. The stone was warm from the fire and from other people sitting there all evening. The blanket was soft under them — Silena's, Percy recognized, pale blue with embroidered flowers. The water was cold. The cookies were chocolate chip, still a little warm.

Percy handed Annabeth a cup first before taking his own. She bumped her shoulder against his in thanks.

Thalia leaned over Annabeth's head to smirk at Percy. "So. Jackson. How were the toes? Did my sister survive?"

"My toes survived," Annabeth said.

"Barely," Percy said. "I'm going to practice. So next time I don't—"

"Next time?" Thalia interrupted, eyebrows shooting up. She looked at Annabeth. "Next time?"

Annabeth went scarlet. "Thalia!"

"What? I'm just repeating what he said. Next time." Thalia clutched her chest dramatically. "My baby sister is growing up. Having next times."

"You're the worst," Annabeth said, but she was laughing, leaning into Percy's side a little.

Percy put his arm carefully around her shoulders, the way he had seen Thalia do earlier. Annabeth settled against him like she belonged there, head tipped to rest lightly against his temple. Her hair tickled his cheek.

On the platform, the Apollo kids were starting another song. Grover rejoined them, lifting his pipes again. He caught Percy's eye across the fire and winked.

Percy lifted his water cup in a silent toast. Grover grinned and started to play.

Clarisse stood up abruptly. "I'm getting more cookies," she announced to nobody in particular. "Don't — just stay there. I'll be back."

She stalked off toward the snack table. A moment later, Silena broke away from the dance circle and chased after her, catching her arm, saying something that made Clarisse roll her eyes but slow down so they could walk together. Silena glanced back over her shoulder at Percy and gave him a big thumbs-up and a smile so proud it made Percy's throat tighten.

Sisters. He had sisters now. Two of them. One who pretended not to care while saving him seats and fetching cookies, one who cheered him on like he had won a war just by asking a girl to dance.

He looked down at Annabeth tucked against his side, at Thalia sprawled on her other side, humming along to the music and tapping her boots in the dust, at Grover playing with his eyes closed and his heart wide open.

The fire crackled. Sparks rose. Music filled the hollow of the hills.

Annabeth tilted her head up to look at him. Her gray eyes caught the firelight. She smiled, small and private, and laced her fingers through his where their hands rested on the blanket between them.

Percy smiled back. He squeezed her hand once, gently.

They sat like that through three more songs, not dancing, just listening, hands linked, shoulders touching, while the stars came out one by one overhead and the music played on.
