## Chapter 38: Brothers and Sisters

### Percy

Percy knew something was happening the second he walked into the dining pavilion and saw Chiron smiling like he'd planned something.

Which, with Chiron, usually meant he *had* planned something.

"Good morning, campers," Chiron said, trotting to the center of the pavilion with a scroll in his hand. Mr. D was behind him, upside-down in his chair reading a magazine that said *Wine Connoisseur: Diet Edition* on the cover. He wasn't paying attention at all.

"As many of you know, next week is our spring fair," Chiron continued. "But before we get to stalls and games, I thought we would try something new this year. A day set aside — not for quests, not for training scores, but for family."

Clarisse, beside Percy, groaned loudly enough to hear. "Oh great. Kumbaya time."

Chiron ignored her with the ease of long practice. "I am calling it Siblings Day. Many of you are the only child of your godly parent here at camp. Others have half-siblings you have never truly spent time with. Today, we celebrate the brothers and sisters — blood, cabin, and chosen."

Travis Stoll stood up. "Does that mean I have to be nice to Connor?"

"No," Chiron said kindly. "It means you have to be nice to *everyone's* Connor."

Laughter rippled through the pavilion.

"Activities will run all day," Chiron said. "Water games at the lake this morning, organized by the Poseidon cabin —"

Percy choked on his orange juice. "What?"

"— a friendly sparring tournament after lunch, organized by Clarisse with the Ares cabin," Chiron went on, nodding at her, who suddenly sat up straighter, interested despite herself. "And a cooking contest before dinner, surface recipes versus — well. We have a guest judge for that."

That's when Percy felt it. A shift in the water pressure in the air, like the lake had just inhaled. A familiar, slightly smug presence at the edge of his senses.

The dining pavilion doors swung open, and Triton walked in.

Not in full Atlantean armor this time. No trident of office, no shark-tooth cape. Just dark blue jeans, a Camp Half-Blood t-shirt that said STAFF on it stretched tight across his shoulders, and his hair tied back in a warrior's knot. He looked younger like that. Less like a prince and more like — well, a big brother trying too hard to look casual.

The Poseidon table erupted. Silena clapped, Clarisse thumped the bench, and Percy nearly knocked over his goblet.

"Triton!"

Triton grinned and ruffled Percy's hair as he passed, which was deeply unfair because Triton was seven feet tall and Percy was twelve and had no defense against it. "Little brother. I heard you needed supervision for water games."

"I'm a counselor now, I'll have you know," Percy said, trying to fix his hair. "I supervise people."

"Mm." Triton sat down next to him — on a bench clearly not built for sea gods — and stole a piece of his toast. "And yet Chiron emailed me."

Percy stared. "Gods email?"

"Demigod dads group chat," Triton said solemnly. "Your father sends too many wave emojis."

Across the pavilion, Annabeth was watching them with bright, interested eyes. She was sitting with Thalia — Thalia Grace, daughter of Zeus, Annabeth's big sister and cabinmate, back from an early patrol — and both of them were whispering.

Thalia caught Percy's eye and jerked her chin toward Triton. *That's him?* she mouthed.

Percy nodded. *Be nice.*

Thalia grinned like she had never been nice in her life.

Chiron clapped his hands. "Mr. D, will you do the honors?"

Mr. D lowered his magazine a fraction. "Ugh. Fine. Rule one: no maiming. Rule two: no killing. Rule three: if you burn down the kitchen, you clean it. Have fun or whatever."

"An inspiring speech," Chiron said dryly. "Cabins, check the schedule on your tables! Siblings Day begins in twenty minutes!"

The pavilion burst into chatter. Silena Beauregard from Cabin Three was already on her feet, waving at Percy. "Percy! We're on cooking setup together! Come find me after water games!"

Clarisse stomped over to their table, spear still on her back because Clarisse brought her spear to breakfast. "Jackson. Tell your fish brother if he floods my arena, I'm gutting him."

Triton raised an eyebrow. "You must have been drilling with the Ares cabin again, sister. Father says they keep borrowing his most formidable daughter. Mostly with a headache."

Clarisse blinked, then laughed — a short, surprised bark. "Okay. I like him."

Thalia and Annabeth arrived a second later, Thalia with her arms crossed, her black hair streaked with silver, her leather jacket over her shoulders despite the heat.

"So," Thalia said, looking Triton up and down. "You're the famous big brother."

"And you are the famous big sister," Triton said. "Annabeth speaks of you constantly. Mostly with threats to anyone who insults you."

Thalia smirked at Annabeth. "Good. Reputation intact."

Annabeth rolled her eyes, but Percy could see she was glowing. They had set aside yesterday for just the two of them. Having her big sister join the rest of her friends today made her stand a little taller.

"Alright, Seaweed Brain," Annabeth said, bumping Percy's shoulder. "You ready to lose at your own water games?"

"I am the son of Poseidon," Percy said with dignity. "I do not lose water games."

"You lost the canoe race last week," Annabeth pointed out.

"That was strategic losing. To build morale."

"Sure it was."

Thalia clapped Annabeth on the back hard enough to make her stumble. "Come on, little sis. Let's go watch Fish Boy eat his words."

Triton stood, and Percy realized with a sinking feeling that Triton and Thalia were about to walk side by side toward the lake — two eldest children, both tall, both confident, both with that *I am in charge here* walk. It was like watching two storms decide to move in the same direction.

"This should be interesting," Silena murmured, appearing at Percy's elbow with her clipboard. She linked her arm through his. "Come on, little brother."

Percy blinked. "Huh?"

"Siblings Day," Silena said, as if it were obvious. "Clarisse's your sparring sister, I'm your cooking sister, Thalia's Annabeth's sister and your friend, and Triton is our actual brother too. You have a lot of big siblings today, Percy. Try to keep up."

She dragged him toward the lake, laughing, and Percy, still learning how to live with the siblings this history had given him, followed her into the noise, thinking maybe that wasn't such a bad problem to have.

### Clarisse

Clarisse La Rue did not do sentimental.

Sentimental was for Aphrodite kids who cried at sunsets and Apollo kids who wrote songs about friendship. Clarisse did drills, and sparring, and yelling until people got it right. That was love, as far as she was concerned. If she yelled at you, it meant she thought you were worth the breath.

So when Chiron announced Siblings Day, her first instinct was to fake a sprained ankle and hide in the armory.

Her second instinct, when she heard she was in charge of the sparring tournament, was: *Oh. Oh, this could be good.*

By ten o'clock, the arena was hers. She'd raked the sand flat, set up the weapons racks — blunted swords, padded spears, shields with extra straps for the little kids — and painted a bracket on a big piece of pine board in red paint.

TOURNAMENT OF SIBLINGS, it said. LOSERS WASH DISHES.

A crowd was already gathering.

"Listen up!" Clarisse bellowed, and twenty heads snapped toward her. Good. "Rules are simple. Single elimination. Siblings fight together in first round — pairs. Then winners split and fight each other. No powers that can hospitalize people. Looking at you, Zeus kids."

Thalia, leaning on the fence with Annabeth, gave her a two-fingered salute. A tiny spark crackled between her fingers, just to be annoying.

"No targeting the judge," Clarisse added, pointing at herself. "Anyone cries, you get a hug and a rematch. That's a Silena rule, not mine, don't look at me like that."

Silena, sitting on the fence with a first-aid kit and a cooler of juice boxes, blew her a kiss. "Love you, scary!"

Clarisse's ears went red. "Shut up and get in pairs!"

Percy and Triton stepped into the ring together, and the whole arena went *ooooh*. Percy with Riptide in pen form tucked behind his ear, grinning nervously. Triton twice his height, holding a borrowed xiphos like it was a toothpick, looking insanely amused.

"We're going to get destroyed," Percy whispered loudly to Triton.

"Nonsense," Triton said. "I once held a pass against three sea monsters for six hours. You once — what did Father say you did? Held up the sky?"

"For like, five minutes. And Annabeth helped."

"Then we will manage against children."

"These children train six hours a day!"

Their opponents were the Stoll brothers, Travis and Connor, who were bouncing on their toes and whispering to each other in that twin-telepathy way that meant trouble.

Clarisse blew her whistle. "Begin!"

What happened next was chaos in the best way.

Travis went straight for Percy, because everyone always went straight for Percy. Percy sidestepped, dropped his shoulder the way Clarisse had taught him last month, and swept Travis's legs. Travis hit the sand laughing.

Connor tried to flank Triton. Triton simply... stepped aside, caught the back of Connor's breastplate with one large hand, and lifted him off the ground like a kitten.

"Brother," Triton said solemnly, "where would you like to be placed?"

"Down! Down! Put me — AHHH —"

Triton set him down gently outside the ring line. "Out of bounds. My condolences."

The crowd howled. Clarisse marked the bracket, shaking her head and grinning despite herself. Sea family was cheating, but it was *funny* cheating.

Next match: Thalia and Annabeth versus two Ares boys — Sherman and Ellis.

Clarisse leaned forward on the fence. She'd sparred Thalia once, two years ago, and still had a dent in her shield to prove it.

Thalia fought like weather. Fast, loud, a little wild, shield always moving. Annabeth fought like math. Quiet feet, eyes always calculating three moves ahead, striking only when it counted.

Sherman charged Thalia head-on — mistake — and took a shield to the chest for his trouble. Ellis tried to get around Annabeth, and Annabeth, all twelve-year-old ninety-pounds of her, dropped, rolled, and tapped the back of his knee with her blunt sword. He folded.

"Yield!" Sherman wheezed from the sand.

Thalia hauled him up by the wrist. "Good charge, though. You committed. Next time don't scream when you do it."

Sherman stared at her like she'd handed him the moon. "Yes, ma'am."

Annabeth was already pulling Ellis up and showing him the footwork. "See? If you shift here, you don't fall. Try it."

Clarisse watched them and felt something odd and tight in her chest. She shoved it away. Sentimental. Ugh.

Her own match came in the second round. By tradition, the organizer fought the winner of the camper vote. The camper vote was unanimous: everyone wanted to see Clarisse versus Thalia.

"Solo bout!" Clarisse announced, tying her hair back. "First to three touches. No powers. Just skill."

Thalia entered the ring rolling her shoulders, spinning her spear once. "You sure, sea girl? I don't want to embarrass you in front of the kids."

Clarisse grinned, feral. "Trash talk. I like it. Let's go, sparkles."

The fight was *good*. Clarisse came in low and hard, the way her dad taught her — pressure, pressure, don't give them space to think. Thalia gave ground, blocked, blocked, then exploded forward with a spin that nearly took Clarisse's head off — blunted, but still.

One touch Thalia. One touch Clarisse. The crowd was on its feet screaming.

Second touch — Clarisse faked high, struck low, caught Thalia's thigh. Thalia answered twenty seconds later with a shield bind and a tap to Clarisse's shoulder that Clarisse didn't even see coming.

Two-two. Final touch.

They circled. Sweat ran into Clarisse's eyes. She could hear Silena screaming her name, Percy yelling "GO CLARISSE," Triton clapping slowly like a king at games.

Thalia lunged. Clarisse parried, turned her hips the way she'd drilled a thousand times alone at dawn, and —

*Tap.* Her spear tip rested lightly against Thalia's chest.

Silence. Then the arena exploded.

Thalia stared down at the spear, breathing hard, then threw her head back and laughed. "Good! That was GOOD!"

She grabbed Clarisse in a headlock — Clarisse, who did not do hugs — and rubbed her knuckles hard into her scalp. "Okay, little sister. You got me."

"I'm not little," Clarisse protested, but she was laughing, and her face was red, and she didn't shove Thalia off. "And you're not my sister."

"Siblings Day," Thalia said, releasing her and offering a real handshake, forearm to forearm, warrior to warrior. "I get to claim you for today. Deal?"

Clarisse gripped her forearm hard. "Deal. But tomorrow I'm back to being terrifying."

"Wouldn't have it any other way."

From the sidelines, Percy cupped his hands around his mouth. "GROUP HUG!"

"No!" Clarisse and Thalia shouted together, then looked at each other and cracked up.

Clarisse marked the bracket with a flourish. She caught Silena's eye across the arena, and Silena mouthed *told you* at her.

Clarisse mouthed back *shut up*, but she was smiling.

Maybe — *maybe* — sentimental wasn't the worst thing.

### Silena

Silena Beauregard had three great loves in life: her siblings in Cabin Three, beautiful things, and food.

So when Chiron asked her that morning if she would organize the cooking contest, she had nearly hugged him off his horse legs.

By noon, the pavilion kitchen — usually off-limits to campers under threat of harpy attack — had been thrown open, with long tables set up outside under the sunshades. Two kitchens, two teams, one hour, one theme.

The theme, drawn from a helmet by little Pollux, was: *Home.*

"Each team will cook a dish that tastes like home," Silena announced to the gathered crowd, clipboard in hand, hair tied up in a scarf. "Judged on taste, presentation, and story. Our judges are Chiron, Mr. D —"

"I'm only here because Chiron promised me it wasn't poison," Mr. D said, not looking up from his newspaper.

"— and our guest of honor, Triton of Atlantis!" Silena finished with a flourish.

Triton bowed from his seat at the judges' table, which creaked ominously under him.

"The teams!" Silena said. "Team Surface: led by me, with Annabeth Grace, Clarisse La Rue, and Connor Stoll, who I grabbed because he was stealing tasting spoons!"

Connor waved a spoon guiltily. "Quality control."

"Team Sea: led by Percy Jackson, with Triton — yes, he's judging *and* competing, we allowed it because he's a prince and pouted — and Thalia Grace as honorary sea sibling for the day!"

Thalia, wearing an apron that said KISS THE COOK OR PERISH over her leather jacket, raised a fist. "I burn water. This will be great."

Percy, wearing an apron that said ATLANTIS HAS BETTER SNACKS, looked terrified. "Silena, I can barely make toast."

"That's why you have a literal sea god and Thalia to chop things," Silena said cheerfully. "One hour! Your time starts — now!"

What followed was the most beautiful chaos Silena had ever orchestrated.

On the Surface side, she moved like a general. "Annabeth, you're on the story — you think in structure, build us a narrative on the plate. Clarisse, you're on protein — you don't flinch at heat or blood, grill the chicken. Connor, you wash and chop and do NOT eat the ingredients — Connor, put down that cheese —"

"I was testing it!"

On the Sea side, things were... different.

"Okay," Percy said, staring at their table of ingredients. "Triton, what's Atlantean home food? Like, seaweed?"

Triton looked personally offended. "Seaweed? Little brother, do you think we graze?"

"I mean — no?"

"Atlantean cuisine is ancient and refined," Triton said, rolling up his sleeves. "Citrus-cured fish with sea salt and tidal herbs. Kelp-noodle broth that simmers for days — we will make a quick version. And cloudbread."

Thalia held up a bag of flour. "I can do bread. My mom taught me before —" She stopped. The smile flickered. "Before things got bad. She made this dumb sweet bread with honey when it rained. Said the storm needs sugar. Storm needs sugar."

Percy glanced at her. Quietly, he said, "Then we make storm-sugar bread. For home."

Thalia nodded, throat working, and turned to the flour like it had personally insulted her.

Silena watched from across the tables while whisking a honey-mustard glaze, her chest aching a little. That was the thing about Siblings Day — everyone brought their homes with them, even the broken pieces.

Her own team was building something warm and deliberate: grilled chicken with honey glaze, roasted summer vegetables, cornbread with whipped butter, and strawberries with cream. Camp food, but elevated. The food they'd all eaten a hundred times around the campfire, made with care.

"It's our Sunday dinner," she explained to Annabeth as they plated. "Our dad — Poseidon — he doesn't cook, obviously. But my *cabin* does. Sunday nights, the three of us cook together, no matter how bad the week was. The Aphrodite kids taught me this recipe. That's my home."

Annabeth was quiet for a moment, arranging strawberry slices in a careful spiral. "My home... it's weird. I remember Frederick's house in San Francisco — quiet, orderly, everything labeled. That belongs to my old life. And then camp — chaotic. And then..." She glanced toward Thalia, who was laughing at something Triton said while kneading dough with far too much force. "Thalia smells like pine and ozone. She tells me I used to hide in her jacket when it rained. I can't remember doing it, but I like wearing it now. That's home too."

Silena bumped her shoulder gently. "Put that on the plate, sweetie. That's the story."

Annabeth smiled — small, real — and added a sprig of mint to the center of the spiral. "For Thalia. She likes green."

Time was called. Both teams presented.

Team Surface went first. Silena spoke for them, because that was her gift — words that made things shine. "This is Sunday Dinner. It's not fancy. It's the meal you make when people are tired and you want them to stay a little longer. Home isn't a place. It's the people who save you a seat."

Chiron's eyes were suspiciously bright. Mr. D actually put down his newspaper and ate a full strawberry. "Acceptable," he muttered, which from Mr. D was a standing ovation.

Then Team Sea. Percy stepped forward, holding their platter with both hands like an offering. Triton stood behind him, arms crossed, uncharacteristically serious. Thalia held the bread, a golden, slightly lopsided loaf drizzled with honey.

Percy took a breath. "Uh. Hi. So — I'm learning what family feels like at camp. Blue food on birthdays used to mean a tiny apartment with my mom — long story — and I still miss that. Here there's Atlantis, and Amphitrite, and our dad, and a brother who's like, a thousand years old and steals my toast —"

"Accurate," Triton said.

"— and camp, and Annabeth, and Clarisse yelling at me until I learn things, and Silena feeding me —" Percy gestured around, ears red. "So we made three homes. Cured fish for Atlantis, because Triton says home should taste like the sea and be a little show-offy —"

"Refined," Triton corrected.

"— show-offy," Percy repeated firmly, to laughter, "and broth for the road, because demigods are always traveling, and storm-sugar bread for Thalia's mom, and for everyone who taught us something before they left."

Thalia set the bread down carefully. Her voice was rough. "It's sweet. Storms need sugar."

Chiron tasted each dish slowly. He closed his eyes at the broth. "Tidal herbs," he murmured. "I have not tasted this since — well. A very long time."

Mr. D ate the fish, ate the bread, and pointed at Triton. "Your brother talks too much. But the fish is excellent."

The judging took ten minutes of intense whispering. Finally, Chiron stood.

"Team Surface wins on presentation. Team Sea wins on story. On taste —" He smiled. "A tie."

Both tables erupted in cheers and protests and food being thrown — affectionately — across the aisle. Clarisse stole a piece of storm-sugar bread and declared it "actually good, don't let it go to your head." Triton stole an entire ear of Silena's corn and ate it in three bites.

Silena stood in the middle of it with her clipboard pressed to her chest, watching Percy get a smear of honey on his nose from Thalia and Annabeth laugh so hard she snorted, and thought: *This. This is why I do this.*

Beautiful things didn't have to be perfect. They just had to be shared.

### Thalia

Thalia Grace had been the eldest sibling for as long as she could remember.

First to Annabeth — bossy, brilliant, seven-year-old Annabeth with scraped knees and a thousand questions, hiding in Thalia's jacket during thunderstorms that Thalia herself had probably caused without knowing. Then to Luke — well, Luke was older, but Thalia had always felt older, felt responsible in a way Luke never quite did. Then, after they reached camp, to the youngest campers. Frightened new arrivals looking up at her, asking how to hold a spear, how to be brave.

Being the eldest meant you didn't get to fall apart. You held the sky so other people didn't have to.

She'd never really thought about what it was like to meet someone who *got* that. Until Triton.

It happened during the water games — which, by the way, were insane.

Percy, given permission to "go a little overboard" by Chiron, had gone *way* overboard. He'd built a floating obstacle course on the lake with Triton's help: rolling logs, rope swings, a waterslide made of solidified mist, and — Thalia's personal favorite — a diving contest off the back of a summoned hippocampus.

She was sitting on the dock after wiping out spectacularly on the logs — to Percy's endless delight — wringing out her hair, when Triton sat down next to her. His feet didn't quite fit on the dock. Everything about him was too large for the surface, and yet he sat there casually, letting the sun dry his shoulders.

"You fight well," he said without preamble. "Clarisse is fortunate to have lost to someone worthy. She would not have accepted defeat otherwise."

Thalia snorted. "High praise from the peanut gallery. You just picked up Connor Stoll like a grocery bag."

"A skilled grocery bag. He nearly had me." Triton smiled, then grew serious, watching the lake. Percy was in the middle of it, standing on top of the water like it was solid ground, holding up a squealing Annabeth so she could reach the rope swing. Silena was cheering from a canoe. Clarisse was threatening to tip the canoe.

"You love him," Triton observed. "My brother. All of you do."

Thalia shrugged, uncomfortable with mushy talk. "He's a good kid. Annoying. Idealistic. Thinks he can save everyone."

"He gets that from our father."

"Yeah, well." Thalia picked at a splinter on the dock. "Annabeth's the same. Thinks if she's smart enough, fast enough, no one gets hurt."

Triton nodded slowly. "The burden of the eldest is thinking the sky will fall if you look away. The burden of the youngest is thinking they must prove they can hold it too."

Thalia stared at him. "Okay, that's — weirdly perceptive for a guy who just learned what a high-five is."

"I have many siblings," Triton said simply. "Percy, Clarisse, Silena, Tyson — different households, different needs. I am the eldest son, the heir. For a thousand years, I believed if I did not carry Atlantis, it would crumble. If my siblings quarreled, I had failed. If my father was distant, I must bridge the distance." He glanced at her. "It is exhausting, is it not?"

Thalia felt something crack in her chest — a wall she hadn't known she was holding up. "Yeah," she admitted quietly. "It is."

They sat in silence while the lake splashed and shouted. Annabeth shrieked as Percy dropped her — gently — into the water. Clarisse tipped the canoe after all, and Silena dragged her in after.

"Can I tell you something terrible?" Thalia said at last. "When we first reached camp — when Chiron said we could stay — part of me wanted to hide in bed for a week. Let somebody else be the responsible one. I wanted to just... sleep. I still checked Annabeth's blankets before I could close my eyes. Knowing help was there didn't mean I knew how to let go."

Triton was quiet for a long moment. Then: "When I was young — very young, by our standards — I ran away. For a decade. I lived as a mortal fisherman in a village that no longer exists. No palace, no siblings, no father to disappoint. I mended nets. I was happy." He smiled wryly. "Then a storm took the village fleet, and I could not stand by. I called the waves without thinking, and they knew me. So I went home."

"Did you want to?"

"No. Yes. Both." He shrugged, massive shoulders rolling. "Family is a tide. It pulls you back whether you swim or not. The trick — which took me nine hundred years — is learning to float instead of fighting it. To let them hold *you* sometimes."

Thalia looked at him, this ancient prince in a borrowed camp shirt, and felt absurdly like crying. She didn't. She didn't like crying in front of sea gods. Instead she shoved his shoulder hard.

"Race you to the hippocampus," she said.

Triton blinked. "What?"

"You heard me, old man." She was already on her feet, backing toward the water. "Loser admits Zeus kids are better swimmers than Poseidon kids!"

"That is blasphemy! That is biologically —" Triton stood, all seven feet of him, grinning wildly. "You are on, little storm!"

"I'm not little! I'm —" Thalia dove, and Triton dove after her, and they hit the water in twin explosions of white foam while Percy cheered and Annabeth shouted "THALIA'S GOING TO DROWN THE PRINCE, SOMEBODY STOP HER" and nobody stopped anyone.

Thalia lost — obviously, he was a *merman* — but she made him work for it, and when they surfaced, both laughing and gasping, Triton clasped her forearm the way warriors did.

"Honorary little sister," he declared. "Whether you consent or not."

"I consent," Thalia said, surprised to find she meant it. "But if you ruffle my hair like you do Percy's, I will tase you."

"Noted and feared."

Later, during the story circle — the last event of the day, everyone sprawled on blankets by the campfire as the sun went down — Thalia found herself sitting between Annabeth and Triton, with Percy across from her roasting a marshmallow to a perfect golden brown (a skill he'd learned from Silena that afternoon and was absurdly proud of).

"Tell the pinecone story again," Annabeth begged, tugging Thalia's sleeve. "Please?"

Thalia groaned. "No."

"Tell it," Clarisse said, already grinning. "I need blackmail."

"Fine!" Thalia threw up her hands. "When Annabeth was seven, she decided pinecones were grenades — monster grenades — and she stockpiled like two hundred of them under our tent. Luke and I woke up buried in pinecones because she'd set a 'trap' for a hellhound that was actually just a raccoon."

Everyone howled. Annabeth went scarlet. "It *could* have been a hellhound!"

"It was a raccoon, Annie," Thalia said fondly, using the old nickname before she could stop herself. Annabeth didn't protest. She leaned her head on Thalia's shoulder, and Thalia put her arm around her automatically, like she'd never stopped.

Triton went next. "When Percy was a baby, before he came to live in Atlantis —" he began, and Percy groaned.

"Noooo —"

"— Father summoned me to the surface in a panic because the child had summoned a hurricane in his bathtub. A *bathtub hurricane*. The bathroom was flooded, the rubber duck was orbiting, and Sally — your mother — was standing in the doorway with a mop, completely calm, saying, 'He gets excited about bubbles.'"

The circle *lost it*. Percy hid his face. "I've never heard that story!"

"She told Father. Father told everyone in the palace for a month." Triton's eyes were warm. "He was so proud he nearly flooded the throne room."

Percy peeked through his fingers, grinning despite himself. "Really?"

"Really."

Clarisse told a story about her little brother — mortal, back home — teaching her to braid friendship bracelets so she wouldn't be lonely at camp. Silena told one about her older sister in the mortal world doing her makeup before her first dance and crying because "my baby sister is growing up." Even Mr. D, passing by with a soda, muttered, "Ariadne once got me a mortal birthday card that played music. I kept it," before walking away quickly while everyone stared.

The fire crackled. The stars came out. Someone started passing around the leftover storm-sugar bread.

Thalia looked around the circle — at Annabeth asleep on her shoulder, at Percy leaning against Triton's side, at Clarisse braiding Silena's hair without being asked, at all these kids who were not her blood but were somehow hers — and thought about tides, and floating, and letting yourself be held.

Maybe being the eldest didn't always mean holding the sky alone.

Maybe sometimes it just meant making sure everyone had a seat by the fire.

### Annabeth

Annabeth Grace liked schedules. She liked plans, and lists, and knowing what came next.

Siblings Day had no schedule that survived contact with campers — the water games ran long because no one wanted to get out of the lake, the tournament ran long because everyone demanded rematches, the cooking contest ran long because Mr. D demanded seconds — and Annabeth found, to her own surprise, that she didn't mind at all.

She was sitting on the beach after dinner, knees pulled up, watching the last light turn the lake gold. Thalia sat next to her, skipping stones with sharp, practiced flicks. Seven skips. Eight. Thalia had always been good at this.

"Penny for your thoughts, Wise Girl," Thalia said.

Annabeth smiled at the old nickname — Luke's nickname, once, but Thalia had claimed it too. "Just thinking. About parents."

Thalia made a face. "Ugh. Which one? Mine's a lightning bolt with commitment issues."

"Zeus," Annabeth said. "Our dad." She hesitated. "Do you ever — do you ever feel like he's watching? Like, expecting something?"

Thalia was quiet. The stone in her hand stilled. "Every day," she admitted. "When we came to camp, I thought — okay, no more expectations. Chiron wants us safe before he wants us to be heroes. He just wants me to be steady. But then there's still this voice, you know? *Be great. Be worthy. Don't embarrass me.*"

"Yes," Annabeth breathed. "Exactly that."

"Here's what I've learned, Annie." Thalia tossed the stone — nine skips, a new record — and turned to face her. "He's Zeus. He's going to be disappointed in everyone eventually, because no one can be a god. That's his problem, not ours. Our job isn't to be perfect daughters. Our job is to be good sisters."

Annabeth swallowed hard. "Am I? A good sister?"

Thalia stared at her like she'd grown a second head. "Are you kidding? You built us somewhere quiet. You kept working — you kept going. And now you're looking after Percy like he's your personal rescue quest —"

"I do not —"

"You absolutely do. You have a whole color-coded plan for keeping him alive, don't lie."

Annabeth felt her face heat. "...It's not color-coded. It's alphabetical."

Thalia laughed so hard she fell backward into the sand. "Gods, I missed you."

They sat in comfortable silence while the waves lapped. Down the beach, Percy and Triton were walking together, ankle-deep in the water, talking seriously about something. Percy was gesturing wildly. Triton was listening with his head tilted, nodding.

"He's good for you, you know," Thalia said casually. "Seaweed Brain."

Annabeth's brain short-circuited. "What — he — we're together, you know that! You don't have to announce it every time I look at him!"

"Okay." Thalia held up her hands, grinning. "Friends. Sure. That's why you look at him like he hung the moon and why he looks at you like you *are* the moon."

"I do not — he does not —" Annabeth crossed her arms. "You're the worst big sister ever."

"And yet you love me." Thalia stood and offered her hand. "Come on. Story circle's starting. And if you're nice, I'll let you win at sparring tomorrow."

"You never let me win."

"Exactly. Builds character."

Annabeth took her hand and let Thalia pull her up. As they walked back toward the fire, Annabeth slipped her hand into Thalia's — as Thalia said she had when she was seven, crossing streets in Richmond — and Thalia squeezed it tight.

For once, Annabeth didn't need a plan. She had her sister. She had her friends. She had a whole beach full of siblings, blood and chosen.

The future — prophecies, quests, wars on the horizon that the adults whispered about — could wait until tomorrow.

Tonight was for family.

### Triton

Triton, heir of Atlantis, commander of the Pelagic Guard, eldest son of Poseidon, had attended councils that decided the fate of oceans.

He had never been more nervous than when Percy asked him, quietly, during their walk after dinner: "Are you glad you came? Or was it boring? Mortal camp stuff?"

They stood at the edge of the lake, the water curling affectionately around Percy's ankles, reaching for Triton like an old friend.

Triton considered his answer carefully. He was not good at soft words — the sea did not reward softness — but this small brother, this brave, kind, reckless child who had held up the sky and befriended a daughter of Zeus and earned the loyalty of his two formidable sisters through sheer earnestness, deserved honesty.

"I am glad," Triton said. "More than glad." He knelt — actually knelt in the sand, bringing himself to Percy's eye level. "Percy. Do you know why Father asked me to come? Truly?"

"Because Chiron needed supervision?" Percy guessed.

"Because I asked to come." Triton smiled at Percy's surprise. "After your visit — after I saw you return to camp when the palace was waiting — I went to Father and said, *I want to know him here.* My brother. The one who wants a life on the surface as well as under the sea. The one who reminds Father to look beyond his own kingdom."

Percy's eyes were very bright. "Triton —"

"And today," Triton continued, "I watched you lose a canoe race on purpose so a nervous Hermes kid could win. I watched you teach a Demeter girl to swim. I watched you stand up for your girlfriend — the daughter of Zeus, despite our fathers' quarrels — when an Ares boy teased her about her height. You did not hesitate. You said, *She's family.*"

Percy shrugged, ears red. "She is."

"Yes." Triton placed a large hand on Percy's shoulder. "That is why I came. To see the family you built. And to tell you — Atlantis is your home too. Whenever you want it. Not as a prince with duties, but as a brother with the room you grew up in, still waiting."

Percy stared at him. Then, to Triton's utter shock, Percy hugged him — arms around his neck, face pressed into his shoulder, fierce and wholehearted.

Triton froze for half a second — no one hugged the heir of Atlantis — then wrapped his arms around his little brother and held on tight.

"Thank you," Percy mumbled into his shirt.

"Anytime, little brother."

They walked back to the fire together, Triton's arm around Percy's shoulders, Percy's arm around Triton's waist — as far up as he could reach — and joined the circle just as Chiron stood to close the day.

"Well," Chiron said, looking around at the sleepy, sunburnt, honey-sticky campers sprawled on blankets. "I think Siblings Day was a success."

"Losers wash dishes!" Clarisse shouted, to groans and laughter.

"Tomorrow, we return to regular training," Chiron said. "But tonight — one last tradition. Anyone who wishes to claim a sibling — not by blood, but by choice — may do so now."

Silence. Even blood siblings were exchanging promises. Silena stood, walked to Clarisse, and held out a woven bracelet — red and gold. "Sister?" she said softly.

Clarisse, tough, terrifying Clarisse, took it with shaking hands and tied a matching gray-and-pink one onto Silena's wrist. "Sister," she agreed gruffly.

Thalia stood, walked to Triton, and offered a leather wristband — a spare. "Brother? Eldest to eldest?"

Triton accepted it with a bow that was somehow both mocking and deeply respectful, and tied a cord of Atlantean sea-glass around her wrist in return. "Sister. Try not to tase me at family dinners."

"No promises."

Annabeth stood, walked to Percy, and held out — a piece of blue string. Percy laughed when he saw it.

"Is this —"

"From the friendship bracelets Clarisse's brother taught her," Annabeth said, ears pink. "Silena taught me. It's lopsided. It's terrible. But — boyfriend? Still the one I choose?"

Percy held out his wrist immediately. "Duh."

As Annabeth tied the string with fumbling fingers, Percy looked around the fire — at Triton smiling at Thalia, at Silena leaning on Clarisse's shoulder, at Chiron watching them all with shining eyes, at camp, at home — and felt something expand in his chest, warm and huge and a little scary.

He remembered a childhood when family had been just him and Sally against the world. Everyone here remembered the boy raised in Atlantis, with Amphitrite and Triton and sisters waiting at camp. He hadn't recovered those years, but the people in front of him were real. Annabeth was real. They yelled at him, fed him, sparred with him, teased him, saved him.

He had a big family now. A weird, loud, mismatched, impossible family.

And as Annabeth finished the knot and punched his shoulder lightly, as Triton ruffled his hair and Thalia threatened to push him in the lake and Clarisse shouted at everyone to go to bed and Silena handed out goodnight cookies — Percy thought that maybe, just maybe, that was the best part of the day.

"Okay!" Clarisse bellowed. "Bed! Now! Siblings Day is over! If I catch anyone awake in ten minutes, I'm assigning extra laps!"

Everyone scattered, laughing. Percy lingered a moment, looking at the fire, at the bracelets on wrists all around, at the stars reflected in the lake like a second camp below.

Triton paused beside him. "What are you thinking, little brother?"

Percy grinned up at him. "Just — I'm glad you came. And I'm glad I have to wash dishes tomorrow. Because it means I lost to family."

Triton laughed, loud and bright, and threw his head back so the sound rolled across the water.

"Come on," he said, steering Percy toward the cabins. "Before Clarisse assigns us both laps."

"She wouldn't —"

"She would."

They ran, two sons of Poseidon, one ancient and one twelve, racing the moonlight back home — surrounded on all sides by family and friends.

*End.*
