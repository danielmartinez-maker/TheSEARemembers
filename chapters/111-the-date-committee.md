## Chapter 111: The Date Committee

Clarisse declared herself chair of the date committee over breakfast.

"You need to take Annabeth somewhere," she said, dropping onto the bench beside Percy. "Not the lake. You go there every night."

"I like the lake."

"Everyone knows you like the lake. That's why it's not a date."

Silena sat across from them. "A date is when you choose something together. It doesn't have to be fancy."

"Good," Percy said. "Because I don't know how to do fancy."

"We noticed," Clarisse said.

Silena ignored her. "Ask Annabeth what she'd like. Don't plan the entire thing without her."

"I was going to ask."

"After we explained how," Clarisse said.

Percy looked between them. "Are you two always this helpful?"

"No," they answered together.

---

Annabeth chose the amphitheater.

Not for a performance. She wanted to sit in the empty seats after dinner and draw the stage from different angles, testing how the evening light moved across the stone. Percy brought a blanket and a paper bag of strawberries. She brought her sketchbook and three pencils.

"This is a date?" she asked.

"If you want it to be."

"I do."

They sat halfway up the steps. Annabeth sketched the arch and columns; Percy ate strawberries and tried not to get juice on the blanket. She showed him how the columns narrowed slightly toward the top, an old trick to make them look straighter from below.

"You see buildings differently than I do," Percy said.

"How do you see them?"

"As places to hide from monsters."

Annabeth laughed. "That's a start."

When the sun dipped behind the trees, she set the sketchbook aside. Percy offered her the last strawberry. She split it in half and gave him the larger piece.

"You always give me the bigger half," he said.

"You always notice."

"I notice you."

Her smile softened. "I know."

She reached for his hand. They sat quietly while the camp settled around them. Somewhere, Clarisse shouted at a camper to stop swinging a sword near the cabins. Silena's laugh floated from the dining pavilion. The world kept moving, and they were allowed to sit still inside it.

"I like dates that don't ask me to perform," Annabeth said.

"Then we can do this again."

"We can do other things too. But yes."

Percy leaned close enough to ask without speaking. Annabeth nodded. He kissed her, soft and brief, and she rested her forehead against his afterward.

From the path below, Clarisse called, "We're not spying!"

Silena added, "We are absolutely not hiding behind the column!"

Annabeth shut her eyes. "They're terrible at this."

"They care."

"I know. That makes it worse."

They packed up together, laughing, and walked back to the cabins with their hands linked.
