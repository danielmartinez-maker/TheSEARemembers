## Chapter 142: Night Swim

The cabin smelled like warm wood and salt and the faint coconut of Silena's leave-in conditioner, and Percy Jackson lay on his bunk at eleven forty at night listening to all three of those things settle.

August heat had not let go even after lights-out. It pressed low over Cabin Three, thick against the screens, humming with crickets and the far-off wash of Long Island Sound. The fountain in the corner trickled, too loud in the dark. A moth beat itself softly against the outside of the porthole, tick, tick, tick. Percy's sheet stuck to the back of his knees. He kicked it off and stared at the ceiling where moonlight laid a pale bar through the blinds.

He wore his faded orange CAMP HALF-BLOOD tee, washed so many times the letters had cracked, and gray athletic shorts with a drawstring that never stayed tied. His feet were bare, soles still gritty from the afternoon at the lake shore, toenails clipped short, a thin white scar across his left ankle from a canoe dock nail two summers ago. His camp necklace with its clay beads rested against his collarbone, damp with sweat. He could feel his own heartbeat in his throat, too fast for lying still.

Across the narrow aisle, Clarisse snored once, turned over hard enough to make her bunk creak, and went quiet. She had fallen asleep in her sleep clothes the way she always did when she was pretending she was not nervous — dark gray tank top with the neck stretched out, black mesh athletic shorts with a white drawstring, red bandana still tied around her forehead to keep her short dark hair out of her eyes, bare feet with callused heels hanging half off the mattress. Even in sleep her hands were fisted. Even in sleep she looked ready to argue.

Above her, on the top bunk, Silena was very deliberately awake. Percy could tell by her breathing. She lay on her side facing the wall, her long dark hair in a loose nighttime braid tied with a pale blue ribbon, wearing her oversized pale blue sleep shirt with the small embroidered trident over the heart and navy pajama shorts patterned with tiny white anchors. Her bare feet were tucked under the edge of her blanket, toenails painted a chipped sea-glass green. The blanket itself was folded navy precision even though she was supposed to be asleep. She was humming under her breath, so quiet it was almost teeth, not air.

"You're awake," Percy whispered.

"You're awake," Silena whispered back, without turning.

"Clarisse is faking."

"Clarisse is faking," Clarisse said, eyes still closed, voice flat. "Shut up, both of you. I'm trying to chicken out in peace."

Percy grinned at the ceiling. The sheet stuck to his palms now too. The air tasted of salt and warm pine from outside and the copper penny of his own nervous mouth. He rolled onto his side and propped his head on his hand. The floorboards were cool under his dangling fingers when he reached down. A mosquito whined near the door and then went silent when it hit the salt sachet Silena hung there every June.

"Two nights ago at the campfire you said midnight," he said.

"I say a lot of things at campfires," Clarisse said. She opened one eye. Moonlight caught the brown of it. "Most of them involve daring you to eat something."

"You said," Silena said, rolling over now, her braid thumping softly against the bunk rail, her voice lilting with mischief, "and I quote because Ella wrote it down on a marshmallow stick, 'If Mural Unveiling goes clean with no touchdowns and Storm Drill goes clean with no nosebleeds, we earn a stupid summer thing. Midnight lake. Just us. Bioluminescence peak. No excuses.'"

"It was the sugar talking," Clarisse muttered.

"It was you talking," Percy said. "After three s'mores. Thalia seconded it. Annabeth drew a star chart on a napkin to prove the new moon is tonight and the glow will be best. You shook on it. I saw."

"I shake on a lot of things." Clarisse sat up. The bunk groaned. Her tank top twisted at the shoulder, showing the strap of her black sports bra underneath, and her bandana slipped. She yanked it straight. Her bare shoulders shone faintly with sweat. The scar along her jaw, thin and white from last summer's dracaena, stood out in the moonlight. "Doesn't mean I want to float in a dark lake where I can't see the bottom."

There it was. Percy felt it land in his stomach, small and real. Clarisse La Rue, who had held a cotton swab steady for two days around four-hundred-year-old paint, who had driven flow into a storm channel with her jaw set, afraid of lake dark.

Silena sat up too, blanket pooling around her knees, her sleep shirt slipping off one shoulder. Her eyes were soft, not teasing for once. The coconut smell strengthened when she moved. "You said the sea talks to you. The lake doesn't. That's what you told me that night after."

Clarisse glared at her. "I talk in my sleep. It doesn't count."

"It counts when you're awake and scared," Silena said gently. "It's okay. We all—"

"I'm not scared," Clarisse said. "I just don't like it. There's a difference. Sea's mine. Lake's... soup. Cold soup with fish looking at you."

Percy laughed before he could stop himself, and Clarisse threw her pillow at his face. It smelled of sun and sweat and the cheap lavender soap from the camp store. He caught it, sneezing.

"Ow. Soup fish. Noted. Great reason to break a blood oath."

"It wasn't blood."

"You spit on your palm," Silena reminded her. "Ella called it binding."

"Ella calls everything binding." But Clarisse swung her legs over the side. Her bare feet slapped the wood. The sound was loud in the quiet cabin. Outside, an owl called once from the direction of the climbing wall, and something small rustled in the strawberry fields. "Fine. We're doing it? Tonight? New moon, glow bugs, whole deal?"

Percy's heart kicked. The dare had lived in his chest for two days like a swallowed star, bright and prickly, through mural unveiling and storm drill and birthday cake underwater. He had thought about the lake at midnight every time the sun went down, how the water would look black and glassy, how the stars would sit in it.

"Thalia said midnight at the north dock," he said. "Annabeth's bringing the dry bag with towels and the glow chart. We bring—"

"We bring everything because we're Poseidon and we're pack mules," Silena said, already climbing down, her bare feet finding each rung. Her shorts rode up; she tugged them straight without thinking. The moonlight turned her hair almost blue-black. "Towels, water shoes, note for Chiron, bug spray that actually works, and your manners."

"My manners are packed," Percy said.

Clarisse stood, stretching, her spine popping. Her tank top rode up showing a strip of stomach, pale in the moon. "I still think this is stupid. Sneaking out. Chiron's going to kill us with disappointment."

"That's why we leave a note," Percy said. "Like we promised. Not sneaking. Late-authorized."

"That's not a word."

"It is tonight."

---

The note took twenty minutes, which was nineteen minutes longer than Percy expected.

They did it at the little desk under the porthole, shoulder to shoulder, in the wash of Percy's flashlight laid on its side. Dust floated in the beam. The wood smelled of lemon polish and old sunscreen. Outside the screen, night insects knocked and hissed in the grass, and the Sound breathed slow and far.

Percy wore the same cracked orange tee and gray shorts, now with his feet shoved into untied sneakers for the walk, laces dragging. His hair stuck up in back. He kept chewing the cap of the pen. Clarisse had pulled on her gray camp hoodie over her tank top, unzipped, sleeves pushed to the elbows, black mesh shorts still on, bare feet now in battered blue water shoes she had not yet strapped. Her bandana was retied tight. Silena had thrown her sheer white cover-up shirt over her sleep clothes for warmth, the fabric thin enough that the blue of her sleep shirt showed through, navy anchor shorts, bare feet tucked into her sandals, braid over one shoulder. The coconut and salt smell clung around them, human and familiar.

Silena wrote in her neat looping hand on a sheet torn from Percy's sketchbook, tongue caught between her teeth. The pen scratched.

*Dear Chiron —*

"No, dearest," Clarisse said. "If we're going to die, be formal."

"Dear Chiron," Silena said firmly, "Midnight dare payoff from campfire 8/19. Mural clean + drill clean = lake swim. North dock, canoe lake, just us five. New moon bioluminescence. PFDs and buddy system and water shoes. No deep middle. Back by 2. Woke Will, he knows where we are. Camp wards steady. No monsters expected, but Thalia has spear + Annabeth has whistle. Love, Cabin Three + Zeus Two. P.S. Percy packed manners."

"Add that Clarisse is brave," Percy said.

"Add that Percy is annoying," Clarisse said.

Silena added both, solemnly, and drew a tiny trident and a tiny lightning bolt in the margin. The ink shone wet. Percy felt his throat tighten looking at it — how many notes had they left Chiron over the years, *gone to woods, back by dinner, don't send harpies* — and how this one felt different because they were choosing it, not running to it.

"Will?" Clarisse asked. "You woke Will?"

"Shh." Silena folded the note. Her cover-up sleeve slipped; she pushed it back. The white fabric whispered. "I Iris-messaged him after dinner. He said quote, 'Hydrate, pee before you go, and if Annabeth gets pale you get out.' He also said he won't tell Chiron unless we're late. That's practically blessing."

Percy's sneakers squeaked when he stood. He grabbed the dry bag — orange, rubbery, smelling faintly of old lake water — and stuffed in striped towels, water shoes, bug spray, flashlight, and gummy sharks. The rubber squeaked under his fingers.

Clarisse watched him, arms crossed. "You're bringing snacks to a swim?"

"They're gummy. They don't crumb." He zipped the bag. "Prepared."

Silena pinned the note to the outside of the cabin door with a shell hair clip, where Chiron always looked. The paper fluttered in the night breeze. The clip clicked. For a second they all stared at it, three siblings in mismatched sleep clothes turned expedition team, breathing the warm night air that smelled of pine sap and cut grass and distant strawberries gone overripe.

"Okay," Clarisse said quietly. "Okay. Let's go get the sky girls before I change my mind."

They slipped out. The door hinges made no sound. The porch boards were cool under Percy's sneakers. The camp slept around them. A single light burned in the Big House. The moon was a sliver, and the stars were everywhere.

Silena linked her arm through Percy's and Clarisse's as they crossed the grass. Clarisse bumped his shoulder hard — *I'm here* — and Percy bumped back.

At the edge of the path to Cabin One, two shadows detached from the dark.

---

Annabeth Grace had always been able to stand still like a deer, and that night she stood at the corner of the Zeus cabin with her arms wrapped around herself, watching them come.

Percy's heart kicked and settled. She wore her orange CAMP HALF-BLOOD tee knotted at the waist over a steel-gray one-piece, legs bare, feet in gray water shoes, blonde hair in a high ponytail, yellow notebook under one arm and whistle on a blue lanyard. She smelled faintly of lemon soap.

Beside her, Thalia leaned against the wall with her spear across her back. At eighteen she was taller than all of them, black hair tied back with an electric blue streak. She wore a black racerback tank swimsuit with black swim shorts and an unzipped black hoodie, feet in black water shoes, silver bracelet on her wrist. Pine and ozone clung to her.

"You're late," Thalia said, but she was grinning. Her teeth flashed white. "We thought Poseidon chickened."

"We thought Zeus fell asleep," Clarisse shot back, immediate. Her voice was low to keep from waking the cabin, but the tease cracked through. "Nice pajamas, pinecone. That hoodie swallow you whole or do you like the bat look?"

"Better than your bandana," Thalia said. "You look like a pirate who lost his ship. A cute pirate. A small pirate."

"I'm taller than you with shoes on."

"You're not wearing tall shoes. You're wearing soup shoes."

"Soup shoes?" Annabeth whispered, falling in beside him. "Should I write it down?"

"Don't encourage them," Percy whispered back. "Hi."

"Hi." She bumped his elbow. "You bring the gummy sharks?"

"Of course."

"Good. That's basically a food group." She glanced at Clarisse ahead, who was already bickering with Thalia about who carried the flashlight, and lowered her voice. "She okay? Silena said she was quiet at dinner."

Percy glanced at his sister's back. He could smell lake already under the pine.

"She's doing it anyway," he said. "That's her okay."

Annabeth linked her pinky through his for half a second before letting go. Thalia rolled her eyes. "No lovebird stuff on the march," she announced. "Regulations."

"We're walking," Percy protested.

"Hand-holding while walking is gateway," Thalia said. "Next you'll be sharing towels."

"Smell that?" Annabeth said at the fork. Cold air pooled downhill, smelling of wet leaves and jasmine. A bullfrog called. "Cold air sinking. Water'll be warmer than air."

"Nerd," Clarisse said affectionately, and bumped Annabeth's shoulder. Percy in his orange tee and gray shorts and sneakers, Clarisse in gray hoodie over tank and mesh shorts and blue water shoes with red bandana, Silena in white cover-up over blue sleep shirt and anchor shorts and sandals — all three smelling of pine and sweat and coconut — crowded to see the chart.

Annabeth flipped open her notebook. "New moon. Milky Way over the lake by 12:30. Naiads seeded foxfire plankton. Best when darkest. Don't splash stupid."

"If we splash stupid?" Thalia asked.

"We'll still see it, just blurry," Annabeth said. "Splash smart for photos. No flash. Long exposure only. Silena, I brought your camera in the dry sack."

Silena clapped her hands once, soft. Her cover-up bellied. "You remembered!"

"I remember the important things," Annabeth said, and glanced at Percy, and blushed.

The path narrowed. Tree roots knuckled through dirt. Percy took the lead without thinking, Poseidon habit, testing footing with his sneakers before the others stepped. The beam of his flashlight bobbed — pine needles, a slick rock, a stripe of pale mushroom glowing faintly at the base of an oak. The smell thickened: rot-sweet leaves, frog mud, lake water. His shirt stuck to his shoulder blades. Mosquitoes whined and died in the bug spray halo around Silena.

Thalia fell back to walk with Clarisse, deliberately, the older friend taking care of the younger, her hoodie rustling, her spear tapping her spine with each step. Percy heard snatches: "...stay shallow if you want... no shame... I hate caves, same thing..." and Clarisse's gruff "...not hate... just don't like blind..." and Thalia's laugh, low and kind, "...yeah, blind sucks, we'll be loud so fish know..."

He felt a rush of love so sudden it stung his eyes. His sisters and the Grace sisters, teasing each other down a dark path to do a scary thing together. Silena hummed ahead, her white shirt a ghost, her anchor shorts dark. Annabeth walked beside him, her ponytail brushing his shoulder every few steps, her whistle swinging. The stars pressed through the branches in shards. The lake called, green and quiet.

---

The canoe lake at midnight was a black mirror with breathing edges.

They came out of the trees onto the north dock and stopped all at once. Percy's sneakers left the dirt and hit wood with a hollow thunk. The dock ran twenty feet out over still water, gray boards silvered by starlight, rail posts with peeling white paint, a coil of rope, three overturned canoes on the grass to the left like sleeping seals. The air here was cooler by five degrees, clean and green, smelling of lily pads and sun-warmed wood cooling and the faint iron of mud. Water lapped at the pilings with a sound like tongues clicking, soft and regular. Bullfrogs called from the far reeds. A fish jumped somewhere out in the middle with a liquid plop.

Above, the sky was thick with stars.

Percy tipped his head back. The Milky Way ran overhead. The air tasted of cold and pine.

"Oh," Silena breathed. "Oh, look at it sitting in the water too."

She was right. The lake held the sky. The water was so still Percy could see the pilings going down into dark, and that disappearance made his stomach dip.

He felt for the lake, instinct. Sea was loud. Lake was quieter, warm on top, cool underneath. No monsters. The camp wards hummed faint, steady without a child keeping vigil. Safe.

"Okay," Thalia said, dropping her spear, shrugging out of her black hoodie. Underneath her black racerback suit and black swim shorts, bracelet glinting, ponytail retied. "Regulations. One: water shoes stay on past ankles. Two: buddy system, no middle. Three: if anyone says out, we all get out. Four: Percy, no tricks. Let it be lake."

"Damn," Percy said, setting down the dry bag. His orange tee stuck, gray shorts stuck. He kicked off sneakers and socks. "There goes my fountain show."

"You can do one trick at the end," Thalia allowed. "Small. For glow."

"Generous."

"I'm generous. I'm eldest sky." Thalia cracked her knuckles. Static popped faintly. Silena beside her in white cover-up over blue sleep shirt and anchor shorts, sandals lined neat, green toenails glinting, smelled of coconut in the cool lily-and-mud air.

Annabeth knelt by the dry bag in her knotted orange tee over steel-gray one-piece, water shoes on, ponytail over shoulder. She unrolled the striped towels and set her notebook with a rock, whistle beside it.

Clarisse stood at the end of the dock. Wind pressed her gray hoodie. Her blue water shoes were strapped, bandana retied. She stared where pilings vanished.

Percy shrugged out of his orange tee, revealing navy trunks with white stripe, necklace still on. He padded down the dock and stopped beside her.

"Hey," he said quietly. "You good?"

Clarisse didn't look at him. "It's stupid. I swim in ocean. Lake's ten feet and I'm— Can't feel bottom. Like blindfold."

"It's not stupid," Percy said. "Sea's loud. Lake whispers. Stay shallow with you? No middle. I won't go anywhere." He held out his hand.

Clarisse stared. Behind them towels snapped. "You're such a barnacle," she muttered, but took his hand. "You tell anyone and I'll drown you."

"Tell anyone what?" Percy said. "You never say anything. You're stone. You're Clarisse."

She huffed, almost a laugh, and squeezed harder. Her hoodie sleeve brushed his bare arm, fleece soft. For a second they stood at the end of the dock holding hands like little kids, two Poseidon siblings in mismatched swim clothes under a spilled sky, and Percy felt the lake waiting below, patient, and his sister's pulse hammering in her palm.

"Okay," she said. "Okay. Let's change before mosquitoes eat us alive."

---

Changing was elbows and teasing behind a towel wall, lake breeze cool on bare skin, fabric smelling of sun and dryer sheets, water lapping at pilings.

Thalia and Annabeth held up striped towels. "Ladies first," Thalia announced. "Eyes to the sky."

Percy tipped his head back. His navy trunks were already under gray shorts, so he shucked the shorts. "Just one boy, suffering."

"Suffering builds character," Silena called. She emerged in her teal tankini top with white ties, navy board shorts, white cover-up tied at her waist, black water shoes. "Ta-da."

"Gorgeous," Annabeth said, stepping out in her steel-gray one-piece with tiny yellow bolt at the hip, gray water shoes, whistle at her neck. "Water shoes check."

"Hot nerd check," Thalia said, arm around Annabeth in her black suit and shorts. "My baby sister."

"Ew, stop," Annabeth laughed.

Clarisse changed fast, back turned — gray hoodie off revealing black one-piece with red side panels, blue shoes strapped, bandana still tied.

Percy, in navy trunks, bare chest, puffed up. "Okay. Who's first?"

"Not it," three girls said.

Thalia laughed, loud and delighted, the sound rolling over the water. A frog stopped, then resumed. "Cowards. Watch how sky does it." She walked to the end of the dock in her black suit and shorts, water shoes slapping, ponytail swinging, and sat down, legs dangling. The water took her feet with a soft gulp. She hissed. "Yep. Cold. Warmer than air, Annabeth, you were right, but still rude." She splashed once with her toes, droplets silver. The star reflections shattered and reformed. "C'mon, soup girl. Shallow end's right here with me."

Clarisse stared. Then, with Percy still holding her hand loosely, she walked down the dock. Her water shoes thumped. The boards bent faintly. She sat heavily beside Thalia, not dangling yet, knees up, arms around them. Her black-and-red suit pulled across her back. Her bandana fluttered. The lake breathed below her toes.

"Ankles first," Thalia said quietly, all tease gone. Her voice was low, big-sister. Her hand settled between Clarisse's shoulder blades, warm and steady, bracelet cool. Ozone faint around her, comforting, like a blanket with static. "Just toes. Listen to frogs. Name five sounds."

Clarisse exhaled shakily. The air left her in a white puff. "Frogs. Water. Crickets. Your mouth. My heart."

"Good. That's five. Toes now."

Clarisse lowered her feet. Water closed over her blue shoes with a soft sucking sound. She jerked, then forced still. Her knuckles whitened on her knees. Her breathing hitched. The lake lapped at her ankles, black and starry. A water strider dimpled the surface nearby.

"Cold," she whispered.

"Cold means alive," Thalia said. "Breathe with me. In—" She breathed, audible. Clarisse tried to match. Percy's chest ached watching. Silena, in her teal top and navy shorts and cover-up tied at her waist, sat on Clarisse's other side without a word and took her other hand, lacing fingers. Her bare feet — she had swapped sandals for black water shoes — dangled too, toes just kissing. Her coconut smell wrapped around them. Annabeth, in gray suit and gray shoes, sat next to Silena, close enough that their thighs touched, and put her feet in too, hissing through her teeth, ponytail falling forward. Percy, in navy trunks, bare feet, took the end beside Annabeth, and slipped his feet in. Cold closed over his ankles like a handshake.

They sat in a row, five pairs of feet in black water, five different swim clothes dampening with spray, five towels warm behind them, and the stars burned and doubled.

"Okay," Clarisse said after a minute, voice smaller. "Okay, that's... not soup. That's... feet."

"High praise," Percy said, and nudged her knee with his. Water splashed, cold on his thighs. His trunks clung. droplets ran down his calves. "Soup feet."

She snorted, wet and real, and splashed him. The drops hit his chest, cold and bright, tasting of green and mud when one hit his lip. He gasped. Annabeth laughed, clear and delighted, and kicked a sheet of water at Thalia, who shrieked theatrically and kicked back, drenching Silena's board shorts. Silena squealed, her teal top spotting dark with water, her cover-up sarong splashed, and kicked with both feet, sending a wave over all of them. The dock rang with splashes and shrieks and the hollow boom of feet drumming boards. Star reflections broke into glitter. Frogs went silent, then resumed, offended.

Percy laughed until his stomach hurt. The water on his skin was cold and alive, his navy trunks soaked at the crotch where waves hit, his necklace dripping, his hair sticking to his forehead. Annabeth's gray suit was dark with splashes, her ponytail dripping, her whistle beaded. Thalia's black suit shone, her shorts soaked, her blue streak plastered to her cheek. Clarisse — Clarisse was laughing, her bandana damp, her black-and-red suit wet at the thighs, her blue shoes churning white foam, her fear still there but thinner, like ice in sun.

"Okay!" Thalia shouted, standing, water streaming off her shoes, her shorts dripping, her suit clinging. Her thighs were goosebumped. Her voice echoed. "Regulation slide! Butt-entry, no diving, hold the ladder! Sky goes first to prove no monsters!"

She turned, gripped the dock ladder — aluminum, cold — and lowered herself. Water took her calves, knees, thighs in her black shorts, waist where suit met skin, chest where racerback stretched. She gasped, then grinned, teeth white, and pushed off, treading. Ripples ran outward, silver-black. Her ponytail floated for a second. Her bracelet glinted. The water around her stayed dark for a heartbeat — then bloomed.

Light.

---

Bioluminescence does not photograph well, and Silena would spend a week trying, but in person it was like swimming in stars that had melted.

Thalia moved her arm in a slow sweep — black suit sleeve cutting — and the water lit turquoise-green around her forearm, a cloud of tiny sparks that hung and faded. She gasped. Her gray eyes went wide. Her breath fogged. "Oh — oh, gods — Annabeth, look—"

Annabeth was already sliding in, careful on the ladder in her gray suit and gray shoes, whistle tucked inside her neckline so it wouldn't drag, ponytail high and dry. The cold took her ankles, calves, knees, thighs where suit ended, hips, ribs. She hissed, then bit her lip, then let go and pushed. Her kick — strong, even — trailed twin ribbons of light. Her arms, pulling, left swirling galaxies. The glow clung to her suit, outlining her torso in green fire for a second before dripping off. Her skin shone. Her eyes reflected it. She laughed, breathless, and the sound bounced off water.

"It's dinoflagellates!" she called, treading, her voice bright with scientist joy. Her gray suit was black now with wet, her shoulders beaded, her ponytail swinging with each turn. Water shoes made her kicks heavy and slow. "They flash when disturbed! Mechanical stimulus! It's — it's like —" She swept both arms wide, and light bloomed around her like wings. "Percy! Feel that? Warm on top!"

Percy slid in next, barely using the ladder — Poseidon showoff — in his navy trunks, bare feet finding mud then pushing. Cold hit his groin and stole his breath, then eased. The lake held him, green and soft, weeds brushing his calves like fingers. He ducked his head, came up sputtering, tasting mud and green and summer, water streaming from his hair into his eyes, salt gone, lake in its place. His necklace dripped. His trunks bellied then clung. Around his waist, light pulsed with each movement, turquoise and gold, clinging to his skin. He laughed, loud, and slapped the surface — a galaxy exploded.

"Soup's haunted!" he shouted. "Haunted soup!"

Clarisse stood on the dock, alone for a second, in her black-and-red suit and blue shoes, bandana damp, arms wrapped around herself, watching her siblings bloom with light. The dock creaked under her weight. The towels lay soft behind her. The night pressed cool on her wet shoulders. Her breathing was fast. The black water lapped at the pilings below, starry and glowing faintly where the others moved, dark and blind where they didn't.

Silena, still on the dock in her teal top and navy shorts and white sarong, bare feet now in black water shoes, climbed to her feet and stood beside her sister. The boards were cold through her shoes. The breeze lifted her braid. She did not touch Clarisse yet, just stood, shoulder to shoulder, gray hoodie abandoned, teal fabric bright even at night. The lake breathed. The stars burned.

"You don't have to go middle," Silena said quietly. Her voice was soft, carried only to Clarisse and Percy treading below. Coconut and lake and sister. "You don't even have to let go of ladder. Just sit on edge and put legs in and I'll sit with you. Or we stay here and watch. Dare said midnight lake. It didn't say how far."

Clarisse's jaw worked. Her hands opened and closed. Her suit pulled at her shoulders. "I said no excuses."

"You said a lot of things after s'mores," Silena said, teasing gently, bumping her hip. Her sarong whispered. Her water shoes squeaked. "Eldest sister override: you get to be scared and do it anyway, or be scared and not, both count as brave if you stay with us."

Below, Percy trod water, his navy trunks dark, his chest shining with glow, his hair plastered. He looked up at Clarisse, treading easily, arms moving slow. Light swirled around his elbows. His eyes were dark and steady, not teasing now. The water lapped at his chin when he stopped kicking for a second, then he resumed. Mud soft under his toes when he stretched down — shallow near dock, chest-deep.

"Hey," he called up, quiet. The lake carried it. A frog called behind him. "Stay with you? Ladder-side. I won't go anywhere. Promise."

Clarisse stared down at him — her baby brother glowing faintly, hand out again. Behind her, Thalia treaded farther out in black, watching like a lifeguard, her ponytail dark on the water. Annabeth floated near Thalia in gray, giving space, her whistle glinting. Silena waited beside her, teal and white and steady, braid over shoulder.

"Okay," Clarisse whispered. "Okay, barnacle. Ladder-side."

She sat on the edge, legs in — blue shoes vanishing into black and light — and hissed at the cold. Her suit rode up; she yanked it. Her bandana slipped; she left it. Silena sat beside her, their thighs touching, teal and black-red side by side, both breathing fast. Thalia called softly, "Good, good, breathe, feet first, you got it," and Annabeth added, "Warm layer's on top, stay high, kick small," scientist-coach, gentle.

Percy moved to the ladder, treading, close enough to touch. His hand found the aluminum beside Clarisse's white-knuckled grip. Cold metal, warm skin. Light swirled around his fingers where he touched. His trunks floated slightly. His feet found mud and pushed, holding steady. The water around him glowed with each small kick, lighting Clarisse's shoes green-gold from below.

"Feel that?" he murmured. "Shallow here. I'm standing. Bottom's right there. Mud and sand. No fish staring. Well, one minnow. He's judging your shoes."

Clarisse choked a laugh, tears standing bright on her lashes, starlit. Her grip eased a fraction. Her blue shoes kicked once, small, and light bloomed around her ankles like fireflies underwater. She stared, mouth open. The glow lit her calves, her suit's red panels, her hands on the ladder. "Oh—"

"Yeah," Percy said softly. His voice cracked. The lake moved around them, warm top, cool under, green taste on his lips. His sister's fear sat between them, real and thin. "Pretty, right?"

"Yeah." She kicked again, slightly bigger. More light. Her breathing slowed. "Yeah, okay. Okay, I'm— I'm in. Ladder. Don't—"

"I won't," he promised. "I've got you. Silena's got you. We're all here."

Clarisse lowered herself one rung, water to her knees in her suit, then waist, gasping. Her black-and-red fabric darkened. Her skin goosebumped. Her bandana dripped. She kept one hand on the ladder, one hand in Silena's, and Percy stayed at her elbow, treading, his navy trunks brushing her leg, his shoulder ready. Light swirled around all three of them, turquoise clouds.

Then Silena began to sing.

---

Silena Beauregard had never had a big voice. At campfire she was harmony, not lead, soft under Thalia's belt and Annabeth's clear tone. But in water, at midnight, with her sister shaking on a ladder, she sang like the sea remembered her.

She wore her teal tankini top tied snug, navy board shorts floating around her thighs, white sarong trailing like foam, black water shoes dangling as she floated on her back, braid fanned.
