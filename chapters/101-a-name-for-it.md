## Chapter 101: A Name for It

Percy had been dating Annabeth through the displacement and their time here. Every remembered year counted too.

In the life he remembered, they had years of anniversaries, a whole history of firsts, and enough private jokes to make an outsider assume they shared a language. In this life, they were thirteen, and one memorable kiss had happened during a storm when lightning was trying to tear the camp apart. A later kiss had happened because they planned it. Since then, there had been notes, dances, hand-holding, a cheek kiss or two, and a growing number of people at camp who gave them knowing smiles.

But he wanted to check that their familiar words still felt right here.

Percy had been turning that over for days. It seemed ridiculous to ask a girl he had already loved for years whether she wanted to be his girlfriend. She remembered those years too, and they had kept choosing each other. He still wanted to ask what she needed now.

So, after breakfast, he found her by the shrine she had built and asked if they could talk.

Annabeth set down her pencil. "That sounds ominous."

"It's not. I hope."

"You hope?"

"I practiced this in my head and it sounded much better there."

She closed the sketchbook on her lap. "All right. Start over."

They sat on the low stone edge of the shrine's foundation. Someone had left fresh flowers in the niche for Hades's children; the petals were still wet with morning dew. The camp hummed around them: practice swords clacking in the arena, naiads arguing over the canoe dock, Mr. D complaining that the strawberry juice was too sweet.

Percy rubbed his palms on his shorts. "I know what we were before. I remember the whole thing. But I don't want to act like those memories make decisions for you now."

Annabeth watched him, careful and quiet.

"We hold hands. We write notes. We kiss sometimes. And I like all of that. I like being with you. But I don't want to assume we need exactly the same things we did at seventeen, or that our old habits answer every question about living here."

"So you're asking me what I want?"

"Yeah. I think so." He winced. "I may have taken a long route to get there."

Annabeth's mouth curved. "A very long route. You could've just asked."

"I did ask. Eventually."

"You're right. You did." She turned the pencil between her fingers. "I want to be with you. I’m still the girl who loved you then, and I’m choosing you here too. I want to because when something happens, you're the person I look for. When you're not around, I wonder what you're doing. And when you smile at me, I get distracted from whatever I was thinking about. It's inconvenient."

Percy laughed, relief going loose in his chest. "Very inconvenient."

"I like being your girlfriend," she added. "But maybe we could have a word that feels just ours here too."

"That's fine. We don't need another label."

"I didn’t say that." Annabeth set the pencil aside. "We have a label. Maybe we can pick a nickname that belongs to us here."

"Like what?"

She considered. "My person?"

Percy smiled. "That sounds a little like we're in a prophecy."

"Then you think of one."

"Partner?"

"That's for quests."

"Teammate?"

"That's worse."

"Favorite person?"

Annabeth's expression softened. "That one might work."

"So we're favorite people?"

"And boyfriend and girlfriend." She held out her hand, palm up. "Keep asking me what I need when we’re older. I’ll keep asking you too."

Percy took her hand. "Deal."

The touch was small and familiar. It still made him feel as though the ground had shifted under his feet — in a good way, like stepping onto a boat as it began to move.

"I want something else too," Annabeth said.

"What?"

"As we keep doing this, we don't hide it from our friends. I don't mean making a scene. I just don't want to pretend you're a stranger when you're not."

"Okay."

"And we go slowly. We check in with each other. If something feels wrong, we say so. No assuming that our old memories mean we already know what the other person wants."

"Agreed."

"Good." Annabeth squeezed his hand. "Then you're my favorite person, Percy Jackson."

"And you're mine, Annabeth Grace."

She leaned in and kissed him. It was gentle, unhurried, and familiar without being exactly like the ones he remembered. When she drew back, she left her forehead resting against his for a moment.

"That seems like a good plan," she murmured.

"I was hoping you'd say that."

"Don’t get smug. We still have to tell Thalia what we talked about."

Percy's smile faltered. "Right."

---

Thalia was at the archery range, where she was losing an argument with a target.

"You're aiming too low," Annabeth said.

Thalia lowered her bow. "I know where I'm aiming."

"Then the arrow missed because the wind shifted."

"The wind did not shift."

Annabeth looked up at the bright, still sky. "It might have."

"You're enjoying this."

"A little." She glanced at Percy, and he saw the question in her eyes: *now?* He nodded. "Thalia, can we talk?"

Thalia rested the bow against the rack. "That depends. Is this one of those conversations where everybody's being mysterious and I'm supposed to guess what's going on?"

"No," Percy said. "We can just tell you."

"Good. I hate guessing games."

Annabeth looked at Percy, then back at her sister. "We talked about us. About what we want now, not what we remember wanting before."

Thalia's face went carefully blank. "And?"

"We're together," Annabeth said. "We’re still boyfriend and girlfriend. We also found a nickname we like. We want to keep checking what each of us needs."

Thalia's eyes flicked to their joined hands. She didn't look angry. That was something. She didn't look entirely comfortable either.

"You both decided this?" she asked.

"Yes," Annabeth said.

"And you're taking it slowly?"

"Yes."

"And nobody's pressuring anybody?"

Percy started to answer, but Annabeth spoke first. "No one is pressuring me. Percy asked what I wanted, and I told him."

Thalia studied her sister, checking for signs of hesitation. Annabeth let her look. After a moment, Thalia's shoulders eased.

"Okay," she said. "I can live with that."

"You can?" Annabeth asked, surprised.

"I reserve the right to be annoying about it."

"That was never in question."

Thalia pointed at Percy. "And you. If she says slow down, you slow down. If she says no, you hear no. If she gets uncomfortable, you don't make her explain it three times."

"I will."

"Good." Thalia nodded once. "And if you make her cry—"

"Thalia," Annabeth warned.

"—I'll be very disappointed in him," Thalia finished, with exaggerated innocence. "And then I will make his life unpleasant in ways he can't prove."

Percy considered this. "Fair."

Annabeth rolled her eyes, but she was smiling. "Thanks for not making this harder."

Thalia's expression softened. "I don't want to make it harder. I just want you safe."

"I know."

"And happy," Thalia added, quieter. "You're allowed to be happy, Annabeth. Even if I worry the whole time."

Annabeth squeezed her hand. "You can worry. Just don't try to choose for me."

Thalia nodded. "I'm working on that."

She picked up her bow again, then lowered it. "Actually, I need a break. There's lemonade at the pavilion. Are you two coming?"

"Sure," Percy said.

They walked together, Annabeth between them. Thalia talked about the target's terrible attitude. Annabeth corrected her explanation of wind drift. Percy listened and carried the bow.

Their relationship remained. The conversation had made their plan for living it clearer.

---

At lunch, Clarisse noticed their hands beneath the table.

She didn't say anything until Percy reached for the last piece of cornbread and Annabeth reached for it at the same time. Their fingers bumped. Percy let her take it.

Clarisse watched this exchange with narrowed eyes. "What was that?"

"Cornbread," Percy said.

"You surrendered the last piece of cornbread."

"She wanted it more."

"You always want the last piece."

"I can make an exception."

Clarisse looked at Annabeth. "You have him trained."

"I'm not trained," Percy protested.

Silena leaned across the table. "She's holding his hand under there."

Percy glanced down. Their hands were still joined in his lap, mostly hidden by the table. Annabeth's thumb moved once across his knuckle.

Clarisse made a face. "Finally."

"Finally what?" Percy asked.

"Finally you stopped overthinking and said what was bothering you."

Silena smiled. "We’ve been waiting for you two to remember that you can talk about the small things too."

Percy's ears warmed. "We weren't—"

"You were," Clarisse said. "It's fine. You're both weird."

Annabeth's hand tightened around his. "We didn't want to assume."

Clarisse's teasing subsided. "That's actually smart. More people should do that."

Silena nodded. "And you can still take your time. Nobody's keeping score."

Percy looked around the table at his sisters. He had never expected to have sisters. He had never expected to sit at a family table with them, let alone talk about his feelings as if they were ordinary things that could be set down between the salt and the water pitcher.

He was grateful enough that he couldn't find the right words.

Clarisse shoved the cornbread basket toward him. "Take one. You look like you're about to get sentimental."

Percy took a piece. "I am sentimental."

"Then chew with your mouth closed."

Annabeth laughed, and Silena laughed with her, and Clarisse pretended she hadn't made a joke.

Under the table, Percy and Annabeth kept holding hands.

---

That evening, the two of them sat on the dock as the sun went down. They had spent the afternoon apart — Percy at the Tempest Hall with Clarisse and Silena, Annabeth at the shrine with Thalia, finishing a small inscription. It had been nice to do their own things and know they would meet again.

Annabeth leaned her shoulder against his. "I liked today."

"Me too."

"It felt normal."

Percy looked at the lake. "I don't know if anything about us is normal."

"I don't mean normal like ordinary. I mean we didn't have to pretend."

He understood. He didn't have to hide how he felt when she sat close. She didn't have to pull away when someone looked over. They could just be together, and their friends could know, and the world didn't fall apart.

"We should keep doing that," he said.

"Being together?"

"Not pretending. Checking in. Taking it slow."

Annabeth turned toward him. "And kissing?"

"If you want."

"I do."

She kissed him. It lasted a little longer than the one at the shrine, still gentle, still a little awkward when Percy bumped her nose, but it was theirs. When they parted, she laughed against his cheek.

"You have to stop doing that."

"I'll try."

"You said that last time."

"I forgot."

"I noticed."

She rested her head on his shoulder, and he put his arm around her. They watched the first stars appear. Somewhere behind them, campfire songs began, the sound carrying softly through the trees.

"Favorite person," Percy murmured.

"Favorite person," she agreed.

The words were small. The promise inside them wasn't.

---

That night, Thalia walked past the Poseidon cabin on her way back from the Big House. The windows were lit. Through one, she saw Percy and Annabeth sitting at the table with Clarisse and Silena, playing cards. Annabeth had her socked feet tucked beneath her on the bench; Percy was leaning over her shoulder, clearly cheating by reading her hand.

Clarisse swatted the back of his head.

Silena laughed.

Thalia stopped in the path. She had spent so long thinking of her sister as someone who needed protecting that it took effort to see the other thing: Annabeth was safe enough to laugh. Safe enough to sit with people who loved her. Safe enough to choose Percy without asking permission.

Thalia pressed her palm to the cold glass for one second, then lowered it.

She was still going to worry. She knew that. But she could learn to worry from a little farther away.

Inside, Annabeth looked up and caught sight of her. She lifted her hand in a wave.

Thalia waved back, then kept walking.

She could hear Clarisse yell that Percy was cheating, and Percy protest, and Silena declare that everybody was cheating except her. Annabeth's laugh rose over them all.

Thalia smiled in the dark and let the sound follow her back to the Zeus cabin.
