## Chapter 69: Silena's Song

The campfire had been burning for an hour before Silena agreed to sing.

It was a Friday night in April, the kind of night Camp Half-Blood did best. The air had gone crisp at the edges but the fire kept the amphitheater warm. The sky was a deep, star-crowded blue. The logs in the center pit crackled and threw sparks up in orange spirals, and the Apollo cabin had already done their usual opening set — three fast songs on lyres and drums that got the Hermes cabin standing on benches and the Ares cabin banging their shields in time.

Percy Jackson sat on the third row of the stone steps with his sisters on one side and his best girl on the other, which was, he thought, pretty much the perfect seat.

To his left, Clarisse La Rue had her boots up on the step below and a half-eaten s'more in one fist. To her left, Silena Beauregard had her knees tucked up under her oversized blue sweater, her dark hair braided back with little shells tied into the end, twisting the edge of her sleeve around and around her finger.

To Percy's right, Annabeth Grace sat close enough that their shoulders touched. She had her notebook closed for once, which was how Percy knew it was a real night off. Beyond Annabeth sat Thalia Grace in her black leather jacket, her silver hairband catching the firelight, her boots crossed at the ankle like she was trying very hard to look like she didn't care about campfire songs at all.

Grover sat on the step just below them, his reed pipes in his lap, his legs folded under him, already half-dozing in the way goats did when they were happy and full of enchiladas.

"All right!" Kayla Knowles stood up in front of the fire with her guitar. She was Apollo cabin, fifteen and loud and completely in charge of campfire nights. "That was the warm-up. Now for the real show."

Austin Lake, her brother, tapped his drum twice. "We got a request. Actually, we got like twenty requests. All for the same person."

The fire popped. Someone in the back whistled.

Kayla grinned straight at the third row. "Silena! Get up here!"

Silena froze.

Percy felt it more than saw it — the sudden stillness beside Clarisse. Silena's fingers stopped twisting her sleeve. Her breath caught.

"No," she whispered, just loud enough for their little group to hear. "No, no, no. Kayla, no."

"Yes!" Travis Stoll shouted from two rows back. "Silena! Come on!"

"WE LOVE SILENA!" Connor Stoll cupped his hands around his mouth.

Pollux from Dionysus cabin started clapping. Then Austin joined in on the drum, a slow coaxing beat, and then the whole amphitheater was clapping in rhythm.

Silena sank lower in her sweater. "I can't. I told her maybe. I didn't mean tonight."

Clarisse turned and looked at her sister. Really looked at her. Her scowl, which was usually set to a permanent low simmer, softened around the edges.

"Hey," Clarisse said quietly, so only their row could hear. "What's the rule?"

Silena glanced at her. "What?"

"The rule," Clarisse said. "On my shield. What does it say?"

Silena's mouth twitched, despite her panic. "You don't have a rule on your shield about singing."

"I should," Clarisse said. "I should paint *Listen to your loud sister* on it. Come on. You sing in the cabin every night."

"That's different," Silena hissed. "That's in the shower. With the water running. So no one can hear me mess up."

"You never mess up," Percy said, leaning forward so he could see her around Clarisse. "You're good, Silena. Like, really good. I hear you through Tempest Hall and I stop swimming. That's how good."

Annabeth nodded hard. "He does. He literally floats there like an idiot with his mouth open. I've seen it."

"I do not float with my mouth open," Percy protested.

"You absolutely do," Annabeth said, and bumped his shoulder with hers. "But he's right. You're good."

Silena looked between them, her cheeks going pink in the firelight. "You guys are just saying that because you're my — because we're —"

"Because we're your family and we're legally required to lie?" Clarisse said. "No. I'm your sister and I never lie to make you feel better. I lie to make monsters feel worse. You're good. Remember Mrs. Alvarez?"

Silena blinked. "What?"

"Mrs. Alvarez," Clarisse repeated. "Second grade. Choir tryouts. You remember."

Silena stared at her, and something in her face shifted. Surprise, then something warmer and older.

Percy looked between them. "Who's Mrs. Alvarez?"

"Our music teacher," Silena said softly. "When we were little. Before camp. Our moms took us to the same music classes in New York."

Clarisse nodded, her eyes still on her sister. "Tell them what she said."

"She —" Silena laughed a little, embarrassed. "She said I was too quiet. She put me in the back row and told me to mouth the words."

"And then what happened?" Clarisse prompted, like she'd told this story before and wanted the right ending.

"Then there was the winter concert," Silena said, glancing at Percy and Annabeth. "And the girl who was supposed to sing the solo got stage fright and threw up on the risers. And Mrs. Alvarez was panicking. And Clarisse — Clarisse was seven, she was in the audience with my mom — she stood up and shouted, *MY SISTER CAN SING IT!* Really loud. In the middle of the auditorium."

Percy tried to picture seven-year-old Clarisse shouting in an elementary school concert and failed only because he could picture it too easily.

"What did you do?" Annabeth asked.

"I cried," Silena said. "Behind the risers. I cried for like five minutes. And Clarisse climbed up on the stage — she wasn't even supposed to be up there, she was wearing light-up sneakers — and she sat with me behind the piano and said, if I didn't sing, she'd sing it for me, and she couldn't even clap on beat. So I had to do it to save everyone."

Clarisse smirked. "Tactical motivation."

"She held my hand until the curtain opened," Silena said. Her voice had gone quiet. "And then she went back to her seat. And I sang. 'Silent Night.' I was eight."

"She brought the house down," Clarisse said. She said it flatly, like a war report. Like it was a fact you could not argue with. "Mrs. Alvarez cried. Your mom cried. I didn't cry because I was seven and tough, but I almost did. And after, Mrs. Alvarez said she'd been an idiot."

The clapping had gotten louder. Kayla was gesturing wildly. Austin had switched to a little fanfare.

Silena looked at Clarisse. Clarisse looked back, steady and sure.

"Same deal as second grade," Clarisse said. "You sing. I sit here. If anyone laughs, I throw them in the lake. Deal?"

Silena swallowed. Then she nodded, just once.

"Deal," she whispered.

Clarisse stood up and hauled Silena to her feet by both hands like she was pulling her out of deep water. The amphitheater erupted.

"YEAH!" Percy yelled, on his feet too, whistling with two fingers the way Triton had taught him. Annabeth was on her feet beside him, clapping hard. Thalia let out a sharp patrol whistle that made Grover jump awake.

"Go, Silena!" Grover bleated, scrambling upright and nearly dropping his pipes.

Silena walked down the stone steps toward the fire with Clarisse half a step behind her, like a bodyguard. Halfway down, Silena turned and grabbed Clarisse's wrist.

"Stay?" she whispered.

Clarisse blinked. "Up there? In front of everyone?"

"Please. Just for the first song. Please."

For a second Clarisse looked like she wanted to argue. Clarisse La Rue did battlefields, not stages. But Silena was looking at her with those big dark eyes, and Clarisse sighed the sigh of a sister who knows she has already lost.

"Fine," she muttered. "But I'm not singing."

"Just stand there and look scary so I don't throw up."

"I can do that," Clarisse said. "That's literally my best thing."

They took their places by the fire. Kayla handed Silena her spare guitar, but Silena shook her head.

"Can I — can I just sing? No music for the first one? It's an old one. It's better without."

Kayla nodded and stepped back. Austin set his drum down. The fire crackled. The whole camp went quiet in that sudden, total way crowds did when they knew something real was about to happen.

Silena stood very still for a moment, her hands clasped in front of her sweater, her eyes on the flames. Clarisse stood a half-step behind her with her arms crossed, feet planted, chin up, glaring out at the audience like *go on, I dare you to make a sound.*

Percy leaned forward, his elbows on his knees. Annabeth's hand found his in the dark between them and squeezed.

And Silena opened her mouth and sang.

The first note was so clear and so pure that Percy actually stopped breathing.

It wasn't loud. It didn't need to be. It rose off the firelight like mist off the Sound in the morning, high and sweet and aching. A lullaby, but not the kind mortal parents sang. This was older. Salt-older.

*Hush now, little shell-child, the tide is coming in,*
*The moon is pulling silver threads across your skin,*
*Sleep upon the harbor, sleep upon the foam,*
*Every wave that leaves the shore will bring you home.*

Percy's chest went tight. He knew that song. Not from camp: Amphitrite had hummed it during his last palace visit, sitting beside him while the water outside the coral windows went dark. She had told him she used to sing it over his bed when he was very small and Atlantis seemed too loud with currents and counselors. He could not remember those nights. He could remember her voice now, queenly and tentative and trying so hard, and the ache of wanting the childhood she described.

Beside him, Annabeth's fingers tightened around his.

Silena sang the second verse with her eyes closed now, her hands unclasping, one palm opening toward the fire like she was offering it something.

*Fishermen are lighting lanterns on the bay,*
*All the nets are mended for the break of day,*
*Close your eyes, my sailor, let the deep ones keep*
*All your dreams in pearls until you fall asleep.*

Her voice did something on the word *pearls* — a little turn, a little lift, like a dolphin arching out of a wave — that made Kayla press both hands to her mouth. Austin closed his eyes. In the front row, little Nico di Angelo, trying very hard to be too cool for lullabies, leaned his head against Bianca's shoulder without even noticing he was doing it.

Percy glanced at Clarisse.

Clarisse wasn't glaring anymore.

She was watching her sister with an expression Percy had never seen on her face before. Not the battle-scowl. Not the annoyed sibling eye-roll. Something open and fierce and completely unguarded. Pride, so big it almost looked like pain. Her arms were still crossed, but her hands had gone loose. Her mouth had softened. The firelight caught something wet at the corner of her eye, and she wiped it away angrily with the back of her wrist like it had no business being there.

Silena finished on a note so soft it was almost breath.

*Every wave that leaves the shore will bring you home.*

Silence.

One full heartbeat. Two.

Then the amphitheater exploded.

Cheering, whistling, stamping on the stone steps until dust shook loose. Travis Stoll stood on his bench shouting. Connor was crying and pretending he wasn't. Mr. D, who had been half-asleep in his wicker chair with a Diet Coke, sat up and clapped twice, slowly, which from him was practically a standing ovation. Chiron smiled from his wheelchair at the edge, his hands folded, his eyes bright.

Silena stood there blinking in the sudden noise, her face scarlet.

Clarisse turned to her, and in front of the whole camp, without a word, she put both hands on her older sister's shoulders and pressed their foreheads together, hard, the way Poseidon kids did when words weren't enough.

"See?" Clarisse said, loud enough for the first few rows to hear. "Told you. House. Brought down."

Silena laughed, wet and bright, and hugged her so hard Clarisse stumbled back a step.

"Okay!" Kayla jumped up, wiping her eyes. "Okay! Wow! So that happened! Silena Beauregard, everybody! Daughter of Poseidon, voice of an actual Siren but, like, the good kind!"

"I'm not a Siren!" Silena protested, laughing, still holding Clarisse's sleeve.

"Could've fooled me!" someone shouted from the Ares cabin, and everyone laughed.

Silena stayed by the fire, a little steadier now. Clarisse tried to slip back to her seat, but Silena caught her hand.

"Stay for one more?" Silena said. "Please? The next one's fun. You don't have to sing. You just have to — be here."

Clarisse looked at the crowd, at the fire, at her sister's hopeful face. She groaned.

"Fine," she said. "But if you make me harmonize I will end you."

She stayed, leaning against a log with her arms crossed, trying to look casual and failing because she kept smiling.

"So," Kayla said, "Silena says the next one needs accompaniment. And we have a very special, very brave volunteer."

Percy went cold.

"No," he said.

"Yes," Annabeth said beside him, grinning wickedly. "Go on, Fish Boy."

"No, no, no," Percy said, sliding down in his seat. "I said I'd hold the shell. I didn't say I'd play it in public."

It had started that afternoon. Percy had found a big conch in Tempest Hall and discovered that if you blew into it just right you could make a deep boom that echoed off the tile. Silena had looked up from braiding Annabeth's hair and said, dreamily, "Oh, that would sound so pretty under the sailing song."

And Percy, who would have agreed to fight the Minotaur again if Silena asked him in that voice and Annabeth was watching, had said, "Yeah, I could do that."

He could not do that.

"Percy Jackson!" Kayla called. "Get down here with your horn!"

The camp turned and looked at him. Travis started chanting. "PER-CY! PER-CY! PER-CY!"

Annabeth leaned in close, her gray eyes dancing. Her blonde hair was coming loose from the braid Silena had done, curling around her face in the heat. "Go on," she murmured. "You promised your sister."

"She's my sister, not my captain," Percy muttered, but he was already standing, the big pink conch in his hands. He'd stashed it under the bench like an idiot, hoping everyone would forget.

"Break a leg!" Thalia called, saluting him with a marshmallow on a stick.

"You'll be great!" Grover said earnestly. "Just — you know. Breathe from your diaphragm. Like pipes. Except louder. And wetter."

Percy trudged down to the fire to a chorus of cheers and Stoll-whistles. Silena beamed at him.

"Okay," she whispered as he took his place beside her. "So you just do the low boom on the chorus. Like this — *boom — boom-boom.* Like waves hitting the hull. You remember?"

"I remember in theory," Percy whispered back. "In practice, last time I summoned seagulls."

"That was one time," Silena said.

"It was three times," Clarisse said helpfully. "And one pelican. That pelican looked traumatized."

"Just try," Silena said, patting his arm. "If it's bad, it'll be funny. Funny's good too."

Percy lifted the conch to his lips. The camp went quiet, expectant. Annabeth cupped her hands around her mouth. "You got this, Seaweed Brain!"

Percy puffed out his cheeks and blew.

What came out was not a foghorn boom.

It was a sound like a goose being stepped on by a Cyclops.

A high, wobbling, sputtering *BWAAAAAAAP* that cracked halfway through and ended in a sad little squeak. The fire seemed to flicker in shock. A second later, from somewhere out over the woods, three seagulls answered in alarm.

The amphitheater was silent for one terrible second.

Then it detonated.

Laughter rolled down the stone steps like a wave. Travis fell off his bench. Connor was howling. Even Chiron was chuckling into his hand. Thalia laughed so hard she dropped her marshmallow into the fire. Grover clapped his hooves against the step, wheezing.

Percy's ears burned bright red. He lowered the conch and stared at it like it had betrayed him.

"I hate this thing," he announced. "This is a cursed shell. This shell hates me."

Silena was laughing so hard she had to hold her stomach. Clarisse was laughing too, loud and unrestrained, pointing at him.

"Fish Boy!" Clarisse gasped. "You — you sound like — like a drowning tuba!"

"Thank you, sister," Percy said with great dignity. "Your support means everything."

Annabeth was wiping tears from her eyes, leaning against Thalia, who had an arm around her shoulders and was shaking with laughter.

"Again!" someone shouted. "Do it again!"

"No!" Percy said. "Absolutely not. This shell and I are getting a divorce."

Silena caught her breath and wiped her eyes. "No, no — Percy, that was perfect. Do you know what? Keep it. Just — just play it exactly like that. It'll be our foghorn. Our very broken foghorn."

"Are you sure?" Percy said. "I don't want to ruin your song."

"You could never," Silena said simply, and Percy felt his whole face go warm for a different reason. "Ready? Sailing song. Two, three —"

She nodded at Grover. Grover lifted his pipes to his lips, his fingers dancing. A bright, reedy tune spun out — quick and merry, all sunlight on rigging and wind in sails. Kayla joined on guitar, Austin on drum, and Silena clapped twice and launched into the chorus, her voice switching from lullaby-sweet to brassy and joyful like she'd flipped a switch.

*Oh, haul away, haul away, the tide is on the run,*
*The Nereids are laughing in the morning sun,*
*Raise the sail and bail the pail and sing until we drop,*
*The sea goes on forever and the singing never stops!*

The camp clapped along. Percy, taking his cue, lifted the conch and let out another tremendous *BWAAAP* right on the downbeat.

It was awful. It was perfectly, hilariously awful, a half-beat late and wobbling like jelly. But somehow, under Silena's soaring voice and Grover's pipes, it worked. It sounded like a real ship's horn heard through three miles of fog and a school of fish. A little kid from the Hermes cabin started marching in place. Nico stood up and pretended to haul a rope. Bianca swung him around.

Silena sang the verse, dancing a little step-step-spin by the fire that made her sweater flare.

*My father owns the horses with the white-capped manes,*
*My mother counts the starfish when it rains,*
*My brother caught a whirlpool and he rode it to the shore,*
*And my sister punched a shark — now hear the shark roar!*

Clarisse made a startled noise. "Hey! I never punched a shark!"

"You punched a mechanical shark!" Silena sang back, improvising, pointing at her. "At the boardwalk! It totally counts!"

The camp roared. Clarisse covered her face with both hands, but she was grinning ear to ear.

"That shark had it coming!" she shouted, and everyone cheered louder.

Grover's pipes trilled higher. Kayla strummed faster. Percy blew his conch again — *BWAAAAP-boom* — and this time a little spray of seawater shot out the end and hit Austin's drum with a *splut.* Austin yelped. The camp laughed harder.

Up on the third row, Annabeth nudged Thalia.

"Come on," Annabeth said.

Thalia raised an eyebrow. "Come on what?"

"Harmony," Annabeth said. "Silena said she wanted sisters harmonizing on the next chorus. You and me."

Thalia stared at her like she'd suggested they jump into the canoe lake in full armor. "Abs, no. I sing like a dying harpy. My patrol partners banned me from singing on the road because I scare the monsters before we can shoot them."

"Perfect," Annabeth said. "I'll be precise, you'll be loud, it'll balance out. Come on. For Silena."

Thalia looked down at the fire, at Silena spinning and laughing, at Clarisse clapping along now without even realizing it, at Percy blowing that stupid shell with his cheeks puffed out and seawater dripping off his chin.

She sighed. "Fine. But if I break windows, it's your fault."

They stood up together. Annabeth, thirteen and neat in her orange camp shirt, her braid half-undone. Thalia, seventeen and spiky in black, her hairband glinting. Sisters — not just in Zeus, the way the camp counted it, but in blood, in mom, in the same sharp nose and stubborn chin. They made their way down to the fire, hand in hand, while the camp cheered.

Silena's face lit up. "Yeah? You guys?"

"We'll try," Annabeth said, a little pink. "No promises. Thalia's been warned."

"I've been warned," Thalia confirmed solemnly. "Luke once threw a pinecone at me mid-verse."

"Okay," Silena said, bouncing on her toes. "So the chorus repeats, and you two take the high harmony on *morning sun* and *never stops.* Like —" She sang the line softly to show them, her voice curling around the notes. Annabeth listened with her head tilted, the way she listened to everything, like she was taking it apart to see how it worked.

"Got it," Annabeth said.

"Kind of got it," Thalia said.

"Percy, horn!" Silena called. "Grover, pipes! One, two, three —"

They hit the chorus together.

*Oh, haul away, haul away, the tide is on the run,*
*The Nereids are laughing in the morning sun —*

Annabeth and Thalia came in on harmony, and it was — well.

It was enthusiastic.

Annabeth sang exactly on pitch, exactly on time, each note placed like a brick in a wall. Thalia sang a third above her, twice as loud, half a beat early, with a wild storm-girl vibrato that wobbled all over the place. Together they sounded like a very determined owl being chased by a very joyful eagle.

Percy, mid-blow on his conch, heard it and his eyes went wide. He nearly dropped the shell.

But Silena — Silena just grinned wider and wove her own voice around theirs, smoothing the edges, lifting them up, turning the wobble into something warm. She caught Annabeth's eye and nodded *keep going,* caught Thalia's eye and mouthed *louder.*

*Raise the sail and bail the pail and sing until we drop,*
*The sea goes on forever and the singing never stops!*

They finished in a tangle of laughter. Thalia had her arm around Annabeth's shoulders. Annabeth was leaning into her, giggling. Percy blew one last triumphant *BWAAAP* that ended in a squeak, and Grover finished with a flourish of pipes that made the fire flare green for a second — satyr magic, show-off magic — and the camp went wild.

"ENCORE!" Travis bellowed.

"ANOTHER!" Connor echoed.

Silena was flushed and glowing, her braid coming loose, shells jingling. She looked at Clarisse, who gave her a thumbs-up. She looked at Percy, who was still red-eared and holding his cursed shell like a dead fish. She looked at Annabeth and Thalia, arm in arm, and at Grover, who was beaming and out of breath.

"Okay," Silena said into the noise. "Okay, one more. But this one's — this one's different. This one's not a sea song. Well, it is. But it's — Percy? Can you come here?"

Percy blinked. "I'm already here. I'm right here. I've been humiliated here for ten minutes."

Everyone laughed. Silena took his hand and pulled him forward to the very edge of the firelight, so the flames lit both their faces gold.

"So," Silena said, suddenly shy again, twisting her sleeve. "Percy's my little brother. He's been at camp long enough to know better, and he's already — he's already my favorite tide pool to yell at when he leaves wet towels on my bunk."

"Hey!" Percy said.

"It's true," Clarisse called from her log. "You do. You're disgusting."

"And he's been learning the shell-horn," Silena continued, to more laughter, "because he's sweet and because I asked him to. And he said — he told me this afternoon — he said if he ever got to play in front of everyone, he'd want to play something for Annabeth. Because she's —" Silena glanced at Annabeth, who had gone very still on the steps below, her cheeks pink. "Because she's his girlfriend and best friend. And she braids his bracelets too tight and steals his bacon and does his math homework when he pouts."

"I do not pout!" Percy protested, but his voice cracked, and the camp *awwwed* so loudly he wanted to sink into the fire pit.

Annabeth had both hands pressed to her face. Thalia was grinning at her like a shark.

Silena leaned in close to Percy, so only he could hear. "You still want to do it? The verse you wrote? You don't have to."

Percy swallowed. His hands were sweating on the conch. The verse — gods, the verse. He'd made it up that afternoon while trying to get the shell to make any sound that wasn't a dying goose. He'd been sitting on the dock with his feet in the water, thinking about the way Annabeth looked when she figured out a puzzle, her whole face lighting up, and the words had just — come out. Stupid, simple words. He'd whispered them to Silena, mortified, and she'd grabbed his hands and said, *Oh, Percy. You have to sing that. She'll love it.*

Now, in front of the whole camp, with Annabeth staring at him wide-eyed, it felt like the stupidest idea he'd ever had. Stupider than fighting Clarisse with toilet water.

He looked at Annabeth.

She lowered her hands from her face just enough to peek over her fingertips. She mouthed, *Do it.*

Percy took a deep breath.

"Okay," he said into the sudden hush. "Okay. So. I'm not a singer. I'm — I'm really, really not a singer. Silena's the singer. I'm the — the horn guy. The bad horn guy."

Laughter, gentle now, encouraging.

"But — Annabeth. This is for you. Sorry in advance to everyone's ears."

He lifted the conch, blew a surprisingly clean low note — *BOOM* — actually almost right for once, and then, off-key and too fast and with his voice cracking on every other word, he sang.

*Oh, Annabeth is smartest, she reads all the books,*
*She stabs with a bronze knife and she kills with her looks —*

He winced at *looks,* realizing too late how that sounded, and the camp laughed and *awwwed* at the same time. Annabeth's face went from pink to scarlet. Thalia elbowed her hard.

Percy kept going, louder, worse, his ears burning, his voice wobbling all over the melody Silena had taught him.

*She plans all the battles and she never gets lost,*
*She'd outswim a shark and she'd outsmart a boss,*
*So haul away, haul away, just hear what I say,*
*I'm glad you're my best friend at Camp Half-Blood today!*

The last note was flat. Really flat. Painfully flat. It cracked and slid sideways and landed somewhere near the woods.

Silence.

Percy stood there, holding the conch, wishing Poseidon would open up the earth and swallow him whole. *Dad, any time now,* he thought desperately. *Big hole. Right here. Please.*

Then Annabeth stood up.

She stood up on the stone steps, her hands pressed to her mouth, her eyes shining in the firelight — not with tears, not quite, but with something bright and overwhelmed — and she shouted, "THAT WAS AWFUL!"

Everyone laughed. Percy grinned, relieved.

"THAT WAS THE WORST SINGING I'VE EVER HEARD!" Annabeth shouted, climbing down the steps two at a time, her braid bouncing. "YOU MISSED EVERY NOTE! YOU RHYMED *BOOKS* WITH *LOOKS*!"

"I KNOW!" Percy shouted back, laughing. "I'M A HORN GUY, NOT A SONG GUY!"

She reached him at the edge of the fire and threw her arms around his neck so hard he stumbled back a step, conch and all. The camp erupted in the biggest *AWWWW* of the night, so loud it startled the seagulls again. Travis and Connor were on their feet, whistling. Kayla was clutching her guitar to her chest. Even Mr. D smiled into his Coke.

Percy hugged her back, one arm around her waist, the other still holding the stupid shell, his face buried in her hair which smelled like campfire smoke and strawberries.

"You liked it?" he whispered, just for her.

"I loved it," Annabeth whispered back, fierce and certain, the way she said everything that mattered. "Don't ever sing it again. But I loved it."

Thalia, watching from the steps with Grover, pressed a hand to her heart and mouthed *oh my gods* at Chiron, who chuckled.

Clarisse, from her log, shouted, "GET A ROOM, YOU TWO! THIS IS A FAMILY SHOW!"

"You're just jealous because no one's written you a song!" Percy shouted back, still holding Annabeth tight.

"I WILL END YOU, FISH BOY!" Clarisse shouted, but she was laughing, and she didn't get up, and Silena caught her eye and smiled like *see?*

Silena stepped back up to the fire then, her eyes bright, and lifted her hands. Grover lifted his pipes. Kayla lifted her guitar. Austin tapped his drum.

"Last chorus!" Silena called. "Everybody! Even if you're bad! Especially if you're bad!"

And the whole camp sang.

Two hundred voices, on key and off, Apollo kids harmonizing, Hermes kids shouting, Ares kids bellowing. Silena in the middle, her voice soaring over all of it like a gull over waves. Grover's pipes dancing. Percy's conch booming — *BWAAAP-BOOM* — still awful, still perfect. Annabeth and Thalia side by side, shoulders pressed together. Clarisse, one arm around Silena, singing at the top of her lungs and completely tone-deaf and not caring at all.

*Oh, haul away, haul away, the tide is on the run,*
*The Nereids are laughing in the morning sun,*
*Raise the sail and bail the pail and sing until we drop,*
*The sea goes on forever and the singing never stops!*

The song rolled out over the amphitheater, over the strawberry fields, down to the Sound where the water lifted in time, just a little, like the sea itself was listening.

---

The fire had burned down to embers when it finally ended.

Campers drifted off in twos and threes, humming. The Stolls were already plotting to steal Percy's conch. Nico was asking Grover if pipes came in skull designs.

Down by the fire, the little family lingered.

Silena sat on a log with a blanket around her shoulders that Clarisse had fetched without being asked. She was glowing, tired and happy, her voice hoarse. Clarisse sat beside her, close enough that their knees touched, whittling a point onto a marshmallow stick with a little more care than the task required.

"You were good," Clarisse said gruffly, not looking up. "Like, really good. Like — Atlantis choir good."

Silena leaned her head on her sister's shoulder. "You stayed."

"You asked," Clarisse said simply. "Duh."

"You hated every second," Silena teased.

"I hated most seconds," Clarisse corrected. "The part where Fish Boy sounded like a dying goose was okay."

"Hey!" Percy said from the next log over, where he sat with Annabeth tucked against his side under half the blanket, Grover's head in his lap, Thalia's boots up on the stones. "I peaked at the end. That last boom was almost clean."

"It sprayed water on me," Thalia said. "I felt it from three rows up. I thought it was raining."

"It was holy water," Percy said solemnly. "Blessed by my horn."

Annabeth snorted into his shoulder. "You're an idiot."

"Your idiot," Percy said, a familiar promise that still made his ears go red when everyone could hear.

Annabeth looked up at him. The ember-light caught the gray in her eyes and turned it gold. She smiled, slow and soft, and bumped her forehead against his chin.

"Yeah," she said quietly, so only he could hear. "My idiot."

Thalia made a gagging noise. "Okay, lovebirds, I'm leaving before I get diabetes. Silena —" She stood and stretched, her joints popping. "You were amazing. Like, actually. My patrol would recruit you just to sing around the fire, and we hate everyone."

Silena beamed. "Thanks, Thalia."

Grover sat up, rubbing his eyes. "You made the fire turn green, did you see? That was me. That was — I got excited. Your voice does that. It makes the wild things listen. That's — that's really special, Silena. Pan would've liked it."

Silena's eyes went shiny again. "Thanks, Grover."

Chiron wheeled over then, his blanket tucked around his legs, his smile kind and tired. "My dear," he said to Silena, "that lullaby — I haven't heard it sung so well since — well. Since your father was young and the sea was kinder. You have a gift. Thank you for sharing it."

Silena swallowed hard. "Thank you, Chiron."

Mr. D wandered past with his empty Coke, paused, and said, "Good set, Beauregard. Don't let it go to your head," which from him was practically a love letter. Then he added, over his shoulder, "Jackson. Never sing again."

"Yes, sir!" Percy said brightly, and everyone laughed.

When the adults had gone, Clarisse stood abruptly and held out her hand to Silena.

"Come on," she said.

"Where?" Silena asked, letting herself be pulled up.

"Beach," Clarisse said. "You, me, tide. Now. Before curfew. I want to hear the quiet one again. Without all these —" She waved vaguely at the camp. "People."

Silena hesitated, glancing at Percy and Annabeth.

"Go," Percy said, smiling. "We'll clean up."

"You sure?" Silena said.

"He's sure," Annabeth said. "Go. Sister time."

Clarisse and Silena walked off toward the dunes together, their shadows stretching long behind them in the ember-light, Clarisse's arm slung around Silena's shoulders, Silena's head on Clarisse's shoulder, already humming.

Percy watched them go, his chest full and achy in the good way.

"They're good sisters," Annabeth said softly beside him, reading his mind the way she always did.

"Yeah," Percy said. "Clarisse acts tough, but she'd burn down the world for Silena. Getting to know my sisters. The palace visits have been all brothers and politics. This is different."

Annabeth laughed. "Thalia threatened to shave my head if I didn't sleep last night. Does that count?"

"Absolutely counts," Percy said. "Sister law."

They sat in comfortable quiet for a minute, listening to the embers crackle, to Grover's soft snoring — he'd fallen back asleep with his pipes clutched to his chest — to Thalia whistling tunelessly as she walked up the hill, to the distant sound of Silena's voice drifting back from the beach, faint and sweet, singing just for her sister and the sea.

Annabeth leaned her head on Percy's shoulder. Percy rested his cheek on top of her head. His hand found hers in the blanket-dark, pinky hooking around pinky the way it had through the winter.

"Hey, Percy?" Annabeth murmured.

"Yeah?"

"That verse. Did you really write it on the dock?"

"Yeah," Percy said, embarrassed. "It took me like an hour. Rhyming is hard."

"It was perfect," Annabeth said. " Off-key and awful and perfect. I'm keeping it. I'm writing it in my notebook so I don't forget."

Percy smiled into her hair. "Even the *books-looks* part?"

"Especially the *books-looks* part," Annabeth said. "It's my favorite terrible rhyme."

From the beach, Silena's song rose and fell, and Clarisse's laugh — loud, surprised, real — carried up the hill. Grover piped one sleepy, happy note in his dreams. The embers glowed. The stars wheeled overhead, and the sea kept time below.

Percy squeezed Annabeth's hand.

"Best campfire ever?" he whispered.

Annabeth squeezed back.

"Best campfire ever," she agreed.
