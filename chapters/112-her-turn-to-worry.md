## Chapter 112: Her Turn to Worry

Annabeth knew Percy was hiding a worry because he got quieter around the water.

He still laughed. He still trained. He still showed up for dinner and stole Clarisse's bread when she wasn't looking. But he kept glancing toward the Sound, especially on days when the wind came from the east.

She waited until they were alone at the dock.

"Are you worried about Atlantis?"

Percy stopped swinging his feet. "A little."

"You haven't heard from your family?"

"I got an Iris-message from Amphitrite last week. Everyone's fine. Triton sent a picture of a hippocamp with a ridiculous haircut." He smiled faintly. "It's not that. Sometimes I think about how easy it would be to go home and stay there."

Annabeth let the silence sit. Percy looked at the water, and she gave him room.

"Do you want to?" she asked.

"I don't know. I love them. They're my family. But I also want to be here. With you. With Clarisse and Silena. I feel like I have to choose, and I don't want to make anyone feel left behind."

Annabeth took his hand. "You don't have to decide forever today. You can visit Atlantis and come back. You can miss them and still be happy here."

"What if they want me to stay?"

"Then you talk to them. You tell them what you want. You don't decide for them that your leaving will hurt them more than your staying would hurt you."

Percy looked at her. "You sound like Thalia."

"I've been listening to her."

He leaned his shoulder against hers. "Thanks."

"You're allowed to want more than one home."

The words settled between them. Percy squeezed her hand.

---

That evening, Clarisse noticed Annabeth had been quiet at dinner.

"Your turn to worry about him?" she asked, passing Annabeth the water pitcher.

Annabeth paused. "Is it that obvious?"

"Only to people who know what worried looks like." Clarisse glanced toward Percy, who was arguing with Silena about whether seaweed counted as a vegetable. "He gets homesick. He just doesn't want anyone to feel responsible."

"I know."

"Good. Then don't try to fix it. Sit with him. Let him miss people. That's usually enough."

Annabeth nodded. Clarisse's advice was blunt, but kind.

After dinner, Annabeth found Percy and asked him to walk with her. They went around the cabins once, then twice, talking about nothing until he started telling her about his recent visits to Atlantis: the palace's blue ceilings, Amphitrite's gardens, Triton's terrible jokes.

Annabeth listened. She asked questions. She didn't try to make him feel better.

When they reached the Poseidon cabin, Percy paused at the door. "Thanks for letting me talk about them."

"I want to know your whole life, not only the parts that include me."

He looked at her with a tenderness that made her cheeks warm. "I love that about you."

She kissed him once, then squeezed his hand. "I'll see you tomorrow."

"Tomorrow."

As she walked back to the Zeus cabin, she realized she was not only learning to be loved by Percy. She was learning how to love him without trying to keep him all to herself.
