## Chapter 140: Last One In

Percy woke to cold air on his face and Silena's voice hissing through the screen door of the Poseidon cabin.

"Last one to the lake is a barnacle. I'm serious. I'm going without you."

The bunk room held the sour-sweet smell of slept-in blankets and cedar planks and the faint iron tang of the sea that always clung to the cabin no matter how many windows they opened. The texture of his wool blanket scratched at his chin as he shoved it off. The temperature of the floorboards shocked his bare soles, pine wood gone icy overnight, gritty with tracked-in sand near the door. Somewhere outside a jay screamed once and then again, sharp sound cutting through the soft hush of pines. Through the high windows the light came gray-blue and thin, dawn not yet burned pink, dust hanging in the beams.

Percy sat up with toothpaste still chalky on his tongue from the night before, mint gone bitter, and yanked his clothes off the chair where he had left them folded. He wore a faded orange Camp Half-Blood T-shirt, the cotton thin at the shoulders and frayed at the hem where the stitching had come loose, the black Pegasus cracked from too many washes, salt stiff in the fabric because he had never properly rinsed it after the canoe races last week. His board shorts were teal with a white drawstring knotted twice, the quick-dry fabric slick under his palms, the inner mesh tugging at his knees, sand still caught in the back pocket from yesterday. His black hair stuck up in every direction, damp with sweat at the temples, a cowlick flat against his crown. He didn't bother with shoes. He shoved his battered blue plastic watch onto his left wrist, the strap chewed at the edge where he bit it when nervous.

Across the room Clarisse was already up, hopping on one foot while she yanked at something. She wore a black one-piece swimsuit, the thick Lycra matte and chlorine-faded at the seams, straps digging pale lines into her shoulders, and over it an orange camp shirt tied in a hard knot at her waist, the sleeves cut off ragged at the shoulder, the tails flapping. Her dark brown hair was pulled into a high, severe ponytail that stuck straight out, a black elastic wound three times, stray hairs frizzed around her ears. Her feet were bare, toenails painted chipped red, a thin white scar across her right ankle. She had her camp bead necklace on, six beads clacking together.

"You drool in your sleep," she told Percy without looking at him. "You sounded like a drowning goat."

"You're just mad because I'm faster than you," Percy said, his voice still gravelly.

Silena leaned in the doorway with her arms crossed, grinning. She wore a cornflower-blue sundress, lightweight cotton with tiny white embroidered anchors along the hem, the straps thin over her shoulders, the skirt swirling above her knees, and beneath it the bright coral straps of her swimsuit showed at the neckline and back, the sundress fabric soft and sun-warmed from where it had hung by the window. Her dark hair was in two loose braids that hung over her shoulders, the ends tied with blue string, flyaways glowing in the dim light. She wore brown leather sandals with thin straps, the soles worn smooth, a silver dolphin anklet on her right ankle that chimed faintly when she shifted. Her camp beads were braided into her left braid.

"Both of you are slow," Silena said. "I was awake first. I brushed my hair already. That means I win on technique."

"You brushed your hair to run to a lake?" Clarisse said. "That's your problem. Vanity slows you down."

"Jealousy slows you down more," Silena said sweetly. "You have bedhead like a drowned raccoon."

Clarisse snorted and shoved past her, shoulder-checking the doorframe. "Race you. No powers. No calling the water. Just feet."

Percy scrambled after them, the cold door handle stinging his palm. The outside air hit his lungs, pine resin and wet earth and the distant mineral smell of the lake, algae and mud and sun on water even before the sun was up. The path from the cabins was packed dirt mixed with pine needles, the texture lumpy under his arches, pinecones hard as knuckles when he stepped wrong, dew soaking instantly into his shirt cuffs and the hem of his shorts, temperature dropping as they left the shelter of the cabins and hit the open slope.

Silena kicked off her sandals after ten steps and carried them, her bare feet slapping. "Rules! Touch the dock post! No shortcuts through the poison ivy!"

"I know where the poison ivy is," Clarisse shouted over her shoulder, already ahead, her tied shirt tails flying. "You fell in it last year!"

"I did not fall! I was pushed by a son of Hermes!"

"Excuses are slow!" Percy yelled, laughing, his breath puffing white.

He could hear all of it at once, the sound layered: their feet pounding, Silena's anklet chiming with each stride, Clarisse's breathing hard through her nose, his own heartbeat in his ears, the lake lapping faintly below, a woodpecker starting up somewhere to the east. The light changed as they ran, the trees thinning, the sky opening from gray to pearl to a thin wash of peach along the horizon, sunlight catching the tops of the pines and turning the needles gold-green while the ground stayed in blue shadow.

Silena surged ahead on the downhill, dress billowing, laughing breathlessly. "Eat my wake, barnacles!"

"You're the barnacle!" Clarisse panted. "Your dress is a parachute!"

"It's aerodynamic!" Silena called. "It's fashion!"

Percy cut to the inside of the curve, his calves burning, the cold air tasting coppery in his mouth, his T-shirt sticking to his back with sweat despite the chill. He caught Clarisse's tied shirt tail and tugged it lightly, just enough to spin her half a step.

"Cheater!" she barked, but she was laughing, shoving him with an elbow that caught his ribs.

"Slowest pays for sodas!" he gasped.

"You're the slowest!" they both shouted at him in unison.

They burst out of the treeline together, and the lake opened up, flat and silver-pink, mist hanging low over the surface in shreds, the smell suddenly stronger, mud and green growing things and cold clean water. The dock stretched out, wood silvered with age, damp and slick underfoot. Percy hit it first by half a step because Clarisse slipped on the dewy grass at the edge and Silena got her dress caught on a bramble, and he slapped the dock post with both hands, whooping.

"Ha! Who's slowest now?"

"You had the inside line!" Clarisse said, bending over, hands on knees, her ponytail swinging, her cut-off camp shirt riding up to show the black Lycra beneath, her chest heaving. "Run it again. I dare you."

"You slipped," Silena said, limping up, picking a leaf from her skirt, her braids half undone, her cheeks flushed crimson, her sundress damp at the hem and clinging to her thighs. "That counts as slowest. Barnacle Clarisse."

"I am not a barnacle. Percy drools. He's the barnacle."

"I do not drool," Percy said, grinning so wide his cheeks hurt. The planks were freezing through his soles, rough grain biting, water seeping up between the boards where the lake breathed beneath. He tasted lake spray and morning, clean and a little weedy on his lips. "Okay, I might drool a little."

"Admitted," Silena said triumphantly, pointing at him with a sandal. "Slowest drooler. You owe us sodas."

"Fine," Percy said. "But I touched first. So technically you're both slower than the drooler. Think about that."

Clarisse straightened, still breathing hard, and shoved her damp bangs off her forehead with the back of her wrist. "You cut the corner by the birch root. I saw you. You always cut that corner."

"Because you always go wide," Percy said. "You run like you’re trying to trample the whole path flat."

"I run like a warrior," Clarisse said. "You run like a heron with sore feet."

Silena dropped her sandals onto the dock with two soft thuds and sat on the edge to pick bramble thorns from her hem, her fingers careful with the blue cotton. "Both of you run like baby deer. I was watching your knees. It was tragic. I’m the only one with form. My camp counselor told me I have excellent posture."

"You pay her to say that," Clarisse said.

"I tip in compliments," Silena said, lifting her chin, her braids slipping over her shoulders, the blue strings damp. "And anyway, I let Percy win because he looked so desperate. His arms were windmilling. I feared for his safety."

Percy sprawled flat on the dock, arms spread, letting the cold bite through his thin tee into his shoulder blades. "Keep telling yourself that. I saw you stop to fix your braid halfway down the hill. Vanity cost you three seconds."

"It cost me nothing," Silena said, kicking water up at him with a bare foot, droplets scattering cold across his teal shorts. "Beauty takes time. Speed is for people with no hair to manage."

Clarisse lay down beside him with a groan, her tied shirt bunching under her back, her black Lycra squeaking faintly on the wet wood. "Next time we race at noon when the grass isn’t trying to kill me. And no tugging shirts. Percy cheats."

"I tugged once," Percy protested, staring at the sky where the pink had deepened to gold at the edges, clouds thin as gauze. "Gently. With affection."

"Affectionately cheating still counts," Silena declared, flopping back beside them, her sundress spreading, the damp hem cool against Percy’s arm. The three of them lay in a row, breathing hard, watching mist tear and drift over silver water, listening to the dock creak and the first bees stir in the shore grass.

Annabeth heard them before she saw them, which was usual for Poseidon kids at dawn.

She and Thalia came down the shore path with a canvas breakfast bag banging against Thalia's hip, and the sound of shouting and slapping wood carried across the water, bright in the still air. The smell of the path was different on this side, dry grass and warm dust and the yeasty smell of the muffins in the bag. The texture of the canvas strap dug into Thalia's shoulder where Annabeth had made her carry it. The temperature had risen just enough that Annabeth's nose was no longer numb, though her fingers still ached with cold where they poked out of her sleeves. She could taste the orange juice she had sipped while packing, acidic and sweet on her tongue. The light slanted low through the birches, dappling the path gold and green, catching dust and pollen and making the spiderwebs between branches shine silver.

Annabeth wore a faded orange Camp Half-Blood T-shirt, two summers old, the orange washed to a dusty peach, the neckline stretched loose and the hem frayed where she had picked at the stitching during long capture-the-flag planning sessions, the Pegasus logo cracked down the middle. Over it she wore an oversized gray hoodie, heather-gray fleece with a frayed right cuff where she chewed it when thinking, the zipper half up, the hood bunched behind her neck, the kangaroo pocket sagging with a water bottle and a spare hair tie. Her denim shorts were medium wash with frayed hems that tickled her thighs, the back pocket torn at the corner, a safety pin holding the button because the original had popped during sword practice. Her honey-blonde hair was pulled into a high ponytail, flyaways frizzed around her temples, the elastic slightly too loose so the tail listed to the left. She wore white ankle socks with gray toes, one sliding down, and battered white sneakers with green grass stains along the sides and the left lace double-knotted. Her camp bead necklace hung outside her shirt, the beads clicking when she walked, and a thin leather cord with a small owl charm circled her right wrist.

Thalia walked beside her, tall and easy, her black combat boots thudding despite the early hour. She wore a black ribbed tank top, the fabric stretched soft with washing, the armholes loose, a tiny hole near the left hem, and olive-green cargo shorts that hung to her knees, the pockets bulging with a knife, a hairbrush, and a crumpled map she refused to throw away, the canvas fabric faded at the seams and stained with pine pitch on the right thigh. Her short black hair stuck up in choppy spikes, still damp from where she had splashed water on her face, a silver streak at her temple catching the sun. She wore a black leather cord necklace with two beads and a small silver arrow, and a spiked bracelet on her right wrist that clinked against the breakfast bag. Her eyes were lined with yesterday's eyeliner, smudged at the corners.

"They're going to fall in before we get there," Thalia said, jerking her chin toward the dock where three figures wrestled by the post. "Five drachmas says Percy goes in first."

"I'm not betting against Percy and water," Annabeth said. "He lives for this."

"Smart girl." Thalia bumped her shoulder lightly. "You bring the sunscreen?"

"It's in your left pocket. With the map from 1972."

"It's not from 1972. It's vintage."

"It's a napkin with a ketchup stain."

Thalia laughed, loud and startled, the sound bouncing off the water. As they got closer the lake smell strengthened, cool and green, and the Poseidon kids turned, waving.

"Breakfast!" Silena shouted, her blue sundress flashing as she jumped up and down, her braids bouncing, her bare feet slapping the dock. "Tell me you brought the good muffins!"

"We brought muffins," Annabeth called back. "Whether they're good depends on your standards."

"My standards are low at dawn," Clarisse said, jogging to meet them, her bare feet thudding on the planks, her tied camp shirt flapping open to show the black one-piece beneath, her ponytail swinging hard. "Gimme."

Thalia held the bag above her head. "Manners, barnacle."

"I'm not a barnacle! Percy is the barnacle!" Clarisse jumped for the bag, and Thalia sidestepped easily, her boots scraping wood.

Percy ran up behind Clarisse, his teal board shorts dripping at the hem because he had splashed his legs at the dock edge, his orange camp tee clinging to his shoulders with sweat, his hair wild, his plastic watch fogged. "She slipped on grass! I won! I demand recognition!"

"You demand deodorant," Thalia said, wrinkling her nose but grinning, pulling him into a quick headlock with her free arm, knuckling his crown. "When did you last shower, Seaweed Brain?"

"Yesterday! In the lake! It counts!"

Annabeth felt her face warm despite the cool air. She tugged her gray hoodie tighter, the fleece soft against her chin, and held out a paper-wrapped muffin to Percy. "Here. Before your sisters eat them all."

"My hero," Percy said, taking it, his fingers brushing hers, callused and cold. He unwrapped it fast, and the smell of blueberry and butter and warm sugar rose up, mixing with lake air.

Silena flopped onto the grass at the shore edge, smoothing her sundress over her knees, the cotton whispering, her coral swimsuit strap slipping off one shoulder which she pushed back with an impatient flick. "Picnic formation. Sit. Thalia, give me coffee or I perish."

"It's apple juice," Thalia said, dropping the bag and sitting cross-legged, her cargo shorts pulling tight, her boots dusty with pollen. "You're sixteen. No coffee."

"I am practically ancient," Silena said mournfully, accepting a muffin and a juice box. "Ancient and uncaffeinated."

Clarisse sat hard beside her, her tied shirt coming loose so she retied it with sharp tugs, the knot thumping against her stomach. "You want coffee, ask Chiron. He'll give you a lecture about your growing bones."

"You'd like that, wouldn't you?" Silena said, nudging her with a shoulder. "You love lectures."

"I love hitting things," Clarisse said. "It's different."

Annabeth settled on the worn gray blanket Thalia spread, the wool scratchy through her denim shorts, the ground lumpy with roots beneath. The sun climbed enough to warm the crown of her head, light pooling gold on the muffin tops and glinting off the juice boxes. She bit into her own muffin, taste bursting, blueberries jammy and tart, crumb buttery and crumbling onto her faded tee where she brushed it off impatiently. The sound around them softened into morning business, water lapping the dock posts with a hollow knock, a fish jumping with a sharp plip, bees bumbling in the clover by her sneakers.

Thalia leaned close, her voice low and teasing, for Annabeth alone. "You packed extra for Percy, didn't you? I saw you grab four muffins when there are five of us and you only eat half."

Annabeth elbowed her, hard, her hoodie sleeve bunching. "I packed extra because Poseidon kids eat like sharks. Don't start."

"I'm just saying," Thalia murmured, her eyes dancing, the silver in her hair flashing as she tilted her head. "Your ponytail is extra neat today. And you wore the owl bracelet. For swimming."

"My ponytail is always neat," Annabeth whispered fiercely, but her ears burned. "Shut up and eat your muffin."

"Protective big sister observation," Thalia said, holding up her hands, her spiked bracelet glinting. "My baby sister is growing up and sharing muffins. I'm emotional."

"You're annoying," Annabeth said, but she leaned her shoulder into Thalia's arm for a second, the black tank fabric warm from the sun, solid and familiar. Thalia knocked her gently back, protective and easy.

Across the blanket Percy caught her eye and mouthed thank you with his mouth full, crumbs on his chin, his green eyes bright. Annabeth rolled her eyes, but she couldn't stop the grin spreading, wide and helpless, tugging at her cheeks.

Percy lay on his back on the dock after breakfast with his arms spread and watched the clouds shred apart.

The planks pressed ridged lines into his shoulder blades through his thin camp tee, the wood sun-warmed now on top but still damp underneath where water seeped up, the texture rough with splinters near the edges that he avoided by memory. The smell of hot wood and lake algae and coconut sunscreen Silena had slathered on filled his nose. The temperature had climbed fast, sun pressing heavy on his face and bare shins, breeze cool where it slipped under his shirt and lifted the hem of his teal board shorts. He could still taste blueberry and apple juice, sweet-sour at the back of his throat. The sound was water slapping the pilings in a steady rhythm, gulls crying far off near the canoe racks, Clarisse arguing with Silena about who had to reapply sunscreen to whose back. The light was white-gold and bouncing, so bright off the water that even with his eyes squinted he saw red webs on his lids.

He wore the same faded orange camp tee, now with a blueberry smudge on the front that he had tried to wipe off and only smeared, the fabric damp with sweat at the collar and back, and his teal board shorts, the drawstring loosened, the quick-dry fabric already dry from his earlier splashing, sand gritty in the seams. His feet were bare, soles pink and pruned slightly from dangling them in the water, his blue plastic watch ticking faintly. His hair had dried into wild curls crusted with a little lake salt, falling over his forehead.

Annabeth sat beside his hip, cross-legged, her gray hoodie now tied around her waist by the sleeves, the fleece arms knotted loose, the hood flopping against her denim shorts. Her faded orange tee rode up slightly at the back where she leaned, showing a strip of sun-pinked skin. Her white sneakers were off, lined up neatly with her socks stuffed inside, her bare feet pale with grass stuck to one heel. Her ponytail had loosened, strands sticking to her neck. She was rubbing sunscreen into her arms, the white lotion shiny.

Clarisse stood at the dock edge, hands on hips, her black one-piece gleaming wet already because she had dunked her feet and splashed her face, droplets beading on the Lycra, her cut-off camp shirt still tied at her waist but now damp at the tails, the knot slipping. Her ponytail was tighter, flyaways flattened with water, her red toenail polish bright against the gray wood. She wore no shoes, no jewelry except her beads, which she had tucked inside her shirt so they wouldn't slap.

Silena sat on the edge with her legs dangling, her blue sundress pulled up to her thighs to keep it dry, the cotton wrinkled and damp at the hem, her coral swimsuit visible where the dress gaped at the neck, the bright fabric shiny with sunscreen. Her braids had come fully undone and her blonde hair hung loose and wavy to her shoulder blades, tangled with bits of grass, the blue strings still tied near the ends. Her silver anklet flashed as she kicked her feet, sending up sparkles. Her brown sandals sat forgotten in the grass with Annabeth's sneakers.

Thalia leaned against a piling, arms crossed, her black tank top riding up to show the waistband of her olive cargo shorts, the canvas stiff with dried lake water from where she had waded, the pockets emptied of map and knife for safekeeping. Her black boots were unlaced and loose, socks pushed down, her spiked bracelet left on the blanket with her necklace to avoid lake rash. Her hair was spiky with water where she had dunked her head.

"Okay," Percy said, sitting up, the blood rushing to his head, the dock tilting pleasantly. "Lesson time. Annabeth wants to learn the underwater roll. No laughing when she beats all of us."

"I don't need lessons," Annabeth said quickly, her cheeks pinker than the sun explained. "I can swim fine."

"You swim like a very determined librarian," Thalia said fondly. "It's adorable. You kick like you're mad at the water."

"I am mad at the water when it gets in my nose," Annabeth said.

"That's why you learn the roll," Percy said. He stood, his knees popping, and offered her his hand, pulling her up. Her hand was slick with sunscreen, warm and small in his. "Come on. Shallow first. I'll do it with you."

They waded in off the side where the bottom sloped gently, and the cold shot up Percy's legs, delicious shock, the lake bottom silty and soft between his toes, pebbles smooth and scattered, weeds brushing his ankles like fingers. The water smelled greener up close, mineral and sun-warmed on top, icy underneath where his feet stirred mud. Annabeth gasped when it hit her denim shorts, clutching his forearm.

"My shorts are going to weigh a ton," she said, laughing nervously, her gray hoodie tied at her waist dipping into the water so she yanked it higher, the fleece darkening with wet at the edge. Her faded tee clung instantly, dark orange and translucent at the shoulders, the fabric cold against her skin.

"You can change after," Percy said. "Or steal my shirt. It's drier. Sort of."

"Your shirt has a blueberry on it," she said.

"It's fashion," he said solemnly, echoing Silena, and she snorted, water rippling around her thighs.

He showed her first, taking a breath and tucking tight, rolling forward underwater in a tight ball, the world going green-gold and silent, pressure soft on his ears, his tee billowing, his shorts ballooning slightly, his hair streaming. He came up shaking water, droplets flying, the sound rushing back, gulls and laughing and lapping. The sunlight shattered on the surface around him, blinding white.

"Your turn," he said, wiping his eyes. "Hold your nose if you need to at first. Tuck your chin. I’ve got your waist so you don’t drift."

Annabeth glanced at the dock where their siblings watched, Thalia with her arms crossed and a proud, protective crease between her brows, Clarisse shouting encouragement that sounded like threats, Silena clapping.

"Don't watch me!" Annabeth called.

"We're absolutely watching!" Silena called back, her sundress fluttering as she leaned forward, her bare feet splashing. "You're beautiful! Roll!"

"You're terrible," Annabeth muttered, but she gripped Percy's shoulders, her fingers digging in, her owl bracelet pressing his skin.

"Hey," Percy said quietly, so only she heard, water beading on his eyelashes. "It's just us. Breathe out through your nose the whole time. Like you're humming. I won't let go."

She nodded, her gray eyes steady on his, and ducked.

He felt her tuck, her denim shorts rough under his palms, her tee slick, her body tense then suddenly fluid as she turned over, bubbles streaming silver, her ponytail swirling like seaweed, her hoodie sleeves floating up. She came up sputtering, nose wrinkled, coughing once, her face streaming, her hoodie soaked and heavy.

"I got water up my nose!" she accused, laughing and coughing, wiping her face with both hands, her tee plastered, her shorts dripping.

"You hummed?"

"I forgot to hum! I panicked!"

"You rolled straight, though!" Percy said, grinning, pushing her wet bangs off her forehead. "That was perfect rotation. Again?"

She did it again, and again, each time longer, humming audibly underwater, a buzzing sound that rose through the water, bubbles tickling Percy's forearms. On the fourth try she didn't hold her nose, she came up clean, shaking her head like a dog, droplets hitting Percy in the face, her eyes shining.

"I did it!" she shouted to Thalia, triumphant, treading water, her hoodie ballooning around her waist, her sneakers forgotten on shore suddenly irrelevant.

Thalia whooped, pumping a fist, her black tank flashing in the sun. "That's my sister! Librarian no more!"

Clarisse cupped her hands. "Okay, but can you do it without making that face? You looked like a startled carp!"

"You're a startled carp!" Annabeth shouted back, laughing so hard she swallowed water, coughing, clinging to Percy's shoulder. The taste of lake filled her mouth, weedy and fresh, and she spat it out, giggling.

Silena slid into the water in her sundress with a shriek at the cold, the blue cotton blooming around her like a jellyfish, her coral suit bright beneath the wet fabric, her hair streaming. "Group roll! Everybody! On three!"

Percy caught Annabeth's hand under the water, their fingers tangling, pruned and cold, and squeezed once before letting go to tuck. They rolled together, five splashes, green light and bubbles and the muffled thump of feet pushing off silt, coming up in a circle gasping and laughing, sun blazing white overhead, water ringing in their ears.

Clarisse floated on her back with her ears underwater and listened to the lake chew on the dock.

With water sealing her ears the world went thick and humming, her own breathing loud, the lap of small waves against her shoulders like slow drums. She opened her eyes to blinding blue sky, sun a white coin burning through haze, light fracturing on her wet eyelashes into rainbows. The smell was sunscreen and hot Lycra and faintly the coconut from Silena's bottle mixed with algae. The texture of water held her up, silky and cool against her neck and calves, her black one-piece slick and tight, the seams pressing lines into her skin. The temperature difference made her shiver pleasantly, sun scorching her face and front while the lake cooled her back. She could taste salt and lake on her upper lip where spray had dried. When she lifted her head the sound rushed back, Silena snoring lightly on the dock, Percy whispering to Annabeth, Thalia skipping stones with sharp plinks.

She wore her black one-piece, the Lycra now fully wet and clinging, the legs riding slightly, water beading along the high neckline, and her orange camp shirt still tied at her waist, the knot soaked and heavy, the cut-off sleeves dripping, the fabric translucent where wet. Her ponytail floated around her head like dark seaweed, elastic straining, her bead necklace floating up to tap her chin. Her bare feet broke the surface occasionally, red polish flashing.

On the dock Silena lay on her stomach on a striped towel, chin on folded arms, dozing. Her blue sundress was spread out beneath her to dry, the cotton dark with damp patches, the anchor embroidery raised under her cheek, her coral swimsuit straps bright against her sun-pinked shoulders where the dress had slipped down. Her blonde hair spilled loose over her arms, drying into waves, gritty with a little sand at the ends. Her brown sandals were tucked under the towel to keep it from blowing, her silver anklet glinting at her heel. Her breathing was soft and even, sunscreen sweet on her skin.

Percy lay nearby on his back with one arm over his eyes, his teal board shorts damp and sandy at the cuffs, his orange camp tee rucked up to show his stomach where a water droplet traced down. His hair stood in damp spikes, his plastic watch ticking against the wood. His bare feet were crossed at the ankles, toes pink.

Annabeth dozed with her head on her folded gray hoodie as a pillow, the fleece damp and smelling of lake and fabric softener, her faded orange tee twisted at the shoulder, her denim shorts buttoned still with the safety pin glinting, the denim stiff as it dried, chafing her thighs. Her white sneakers sat beside her with socks draped over them, grass seeds stuck to the laces. Her ponytail had slipped to the side, mouth slightly open, owl bracelet glinting on her wrist where her hand curled near her cheek.

Thalia sat with her back against the dock post, long legs stretched out, her black tank top damp with sweat at the neckline, her olive cargo shorts dusty and sun-hot, the canvas rough under her palms. Her black boots were off now, black socks with a hole at the big toe, toenails unpainted and short. Her spiked bracelet was back on, her necklace back around her throat. Her head tipped back, eyes closed behind cheap sunglasses she had produced from somewhere, mouth open slightly.

Clarisse paddled slowly to the ladder, climbed out quietly so the aluminum wouldn't clang, water sheeting off her suit, her tied shirt plastered to her hips, droplets running down her legs. She grabbed her towel, a thin white one with blue stripes, rough with over-washing, and flopped down hard next to Silena, deliberately jostling the dock.

Silena startled, snorting. "Wha — I'm awake. I was resting my eyes."

"You were snoring," Clarisse said, grinning, wringing her ponytail over Silena's leg. Cold droplets hit sun-hot skin and Silena yelped.

"Rude! That's assault! Percy, arrest her!"

Percy didn't move his arm from his eyes. "Can't. Napping. Also you snore louder."

"I do not snore," Silena said indignantly, propping up on elbows, her sundress slipping off one shoulder entirely, showing the coral suit beneath, her hair sticking to sunscreen on her cheek. "I breathe melodically."

"You sound like a clogged drain," Clarisse said affectionately, nudging her with a wet foot. "A pretty clogged drain."

"You're just jealous because you float like a brick," Silena retorted, flicking water from her towel at Clarisse. "I saw you. You sank twice."

"I did not sink. I was practicing tactical submersion."

"Tactical submersion," Percy repeated, lifting his arm to peer at them with one eye, his grin crooked. "New excuse. Barnacle approved."

"Who's the barnacle?" Clarisse demanded, sitting up, her tied shirt dripping onto the hot wood with soft ticks, the smell of heated water rising. "You lost the bet about muffins. You said Thalia would bring chocolate. She brought blueberry."

"I said I hoped," Percy said. "Hoping isn't betting."

"It is when sodas are on the line," Silena said, flopping back down, her voice sleepy, her hand patting blindly for Clarisse's ankle and squeezing it. "You owe us. Both of you owe me. I'm the middle child. I suffer."

"You suffer the most comfortable," Clarisse said, but she let Silena hold her ankle, and stretched out herself, her wet suit cooling the planks beneath her, her eyes squinting against the brutal light bouncing off the water, white and gold and green. The heat pressed down, cicadas starting up in the trees with a rising whine, a motorboat far off buzzing faintly.

For a while nobody spoke, just breathing and water and the creak of the dock as it shifted. Clarisse felt the sun bake the water from her skin, salt tightening, her shoulders pinking. She thought about how Percy had looked holding Annabeth steady in the water, patient and sure, and how Silena had clapped like a maniac, and how Thalia had watched Annabeth with that fierce older-sister focus, ready to jump in if she coughed too hard. It made something loosen behind her ribs, warm and easy.

"Hey," she said quietly, to no one in particular, staring at the sky. "Good summer already. Huh?"

Silena's hand tightened on her ankle, sleepy and sure. "Best one," she murmured.

Percy reached over without looking and flicked water at Clarisse's foot. "Told you I'd win the race."

"You didn't win anything," Clarisse murmured, smiling with her eyes closed. "Go back to sleep, seaweed."

Silena shifted onto her side, her sundress rustling, her coral strap digging a pink line she scratched absently. "Clarisse? Tell the story about the winter you fell through the ice at your mom's place. Percy hasn't heard it with the funny voices."

"I don't do voices," Clarisse said.

"You do when you're half asleep," Percy said, his voice muffled by his arm. "Come on. Big sister story time. I'll be quiet."

Clarisse snorted, but she stared up at the sun-bleached sky and felt the tale rise anyway, familiar as the scar on her ankle. "Fine. I was nine. The pond behind the apartments froze. I told the boys I could cross it fastest. I got halfway and went straight through to my knees. Water like knives. My socks froze to my boots. Mom wrapped me in every towel we owned and called me her little walrus for a month."

Silena laughed softly into her arms, the sound sleepy and fond. "You were always showing off."

"Somebody had to," Clarisse said. "You were busy being adorable. Percy was busy drooling somewhere in another state."

"I was three," Percy protested faintly. "I had excellent excuse."

"You still drool," Clarisse said, and heard Silena's breathy laugh and Percy's exaggerated sigh, and felt the dock rock gently as Annabeth turned over on the blanket and Thalia muttered in her sleep about left paddles. Gulls wheeled high, tiny white cuts against blue, their cries thin with distance.

Thalia woke because the light changed.

It slid from white to amber across her closed lids, warming her cheeks, and the air cooled a fraction, breeze picking up off the lake with the smell of evening, grilled corn from the distant dining pavilion and pine cooling and mud exhaling. The texture of the dock post rough against her back shifted as she straightened, her black tank top sticking to her spine then peeling away. The temperature of the planks under her legs had gone from scorching to comfortably warm, heat stored in the wood humming into her calves through her cargo shorts. She could taste salt and sunscreen and the last of apple juice, sour on her tongue. The sound was paddles knocking, someone laughing near the canoe racks, Silena humming off-key as she braided her hair again, Clarisse skipping stones with violent focus. The light slanted low, turning the lake to hammered gold, long shadows striping the dock, gnats dancing in the beams.

She wore the same black ribbed tank, the neckline sun-faded slightly, a new water spot near the hem, and her olive cargo shorts, the cuffs rolled once now to keep them out of the water, the right pocket unbuttoned where she had taken her knife out to clean her nails and put it back. Her black socks were dusty, her boots still unlaced, her spiked bracelet loose on her wrist, her silver-arrow necklace catching the low sun. Her black hair was dry and wild, spikes flattened on one side from napping, the silver streak bright.

Percy sat at the dock edge with his feet in the water, his teal board shorts dry now and sun-hot, salt lines white at the seams, his orange camp tee faded paler in the afternoon sun, the blueberry stain baked in, his hair a salt-curled mess, his plastic watch still ticking. His bare feet kicked slowly, sending gold ripples.

Annabeth sat beside him, her gray hoodie back on and zipped to the middle because the breeze chilled her damp tee, the fleece soft and smelling of sun and lake, sleeves pushed to her elbows. Her faded orange tee was nearly dry, stiff with salt, her denim shorts dry and pale at the stress lines, frayed hems tickling. Her white socks were back on, sneakers still off, laces trailing. Her ponytail was redone, neater, higher, flyaways damp-curled at her nape, owl bracelet back on her wrist. Her feet dangled next to Percy's, paler and smaller, toes curling when cold water lapped.

Clarisse paced the dock with a flat stone, her black one-piece dry now, Lycra matte again, her tied camp shirt retied tighter, tails dry and flapping, her ponytail re-tightened, beads tucked safe. Her bare feet slapped wood, red polish dusty.

Silena perched cross-legged, re-braiding, her blue sundress dry and light again, cotton floating in the breeze, anchor hem bright, coral swimsuit peeking where she leaned, her silver anklet dusty, her sandals back on her feet but unbuckled. Her hair was half up in one braid, the other half loose, blue strings in her teeth.

"Thalia's awake," Percy announced. "The queen returns."

"Watch it, barnacle," Thalia said, her voice gravelly with sleep, stretching her arms overhead, her tank riding up. She stood, joints popping, and ruffled Percy's hair hard as she passed, her fingers catching in salt tangles. "You kept my sister alive?"

"She kept me alive," Percy said, glancing at Annabeth, his ears pink. "She did five rolls. Five. Without nose-holding."

Annabeth ducked her head, pleased and embarrassed, tugging her hoodie cuff over her hand. "Four and a half. One was crooked."

"It was straight," Percy insisted.

Thalia sat beside Annabeth on the other side, bumping her hip, protective and warm. "Told you. Determined librarian becomes determined shark."

"I'm going to be a shark that reads," Annabeth said solemnly.

"Scariest kind," Thalia agreed solemnly, and Annabeth laughed, bright sound that made Percy's grin widen.

"Hey," Thalia said lower, nudging Annabeth's sneaker with her boot, her tone shifting teasing and gentle. "You did good today. You didn't even glare at the water once."

"I glared twice," Annabeth said. "When it went up my nose."

"Progress," Thalia said. "Next you'll be challenging Percy to races."

"No way," Annabeth said quickly, glancing at Percy. "He'd win. He's part fish."

"Part barnacle," Clarisse corrected from down the dock, without turning, launching a stone that skipped four times with sharp plips. "Get it right."

"Hey!" Percy protested, laughing. "Family loyalty!"

"You drool," Clarisse and Silena said together, and Silena dissolved into giggles with string still in her teeth.

Thalia slung an arm around Annabeth's shoulders, her tank strap warm, her bracelet cool against Annabeth's hoodie. "You cold? You want my jacket? It's in the canoe."

"I'm fine," Annabeth said, but she leaned into her anyway, just for a second, with an ease she had learned again since arriving, though Thalia remembered years of being her shelter. Thalia squeezed once, hard, then let go before it got mushy, but her hand lingered on Annabeth's ponytail, smoothing a flyaway.

"So," Thalia said loudly, standing and clapping once. "Canoe before sunset? Losers paddle?"

"I always paddle," Percy said, standing, water dripping from his calves, his board shorts flapping. "Because my sisters are tyrants."

"Correct," Silena said sweetly, finishing her second braid, tying it off, tossing them over her shoulders, her sundress swirling as she stood. "Tyrants in sundresses. Get the paddles, little brother."

"I'm not that little," Percy muttered, but he jogged to fetch them, bare feet slapping, his tee flapping.

Clarisse grabbed the canoe with him, her tied shirt straining as they dragged it, aluminum scraping sand and then floating with a hollow thunk. The water inside was warm from the sun, smelling of metal and lake. They all piled in with practiced chaos, Thalia in front with her boots thudding the hull, Annabeth in the middle with her hoodie sleeves flapping, Silena's dress bunching around her knees, Clarisse's wet shirt tails dripping, Percy's watch glinting as he pushed off.

The lake at golden hour was glass, each paddle stroke sending out long ripples that caught light and threw it back, orange and rose. The air tasted of coming night, cool and green. A heron lifted off the far shore with slow wingbeats, sound like sheets snapping. They paddled without hurry, teasing about steering, splashing each other, Silena trailing her fingers and shivering at the cold underneath the warm top layer, Annabeth leaning over to watch minnows dart, Percy pointing out a turtle on a log, Clarisse threatening to tip them all if they didn't paddle straight, Thalia threatening to throw Clarisse in first.

Annabeth spread the picnic blanket as the sun touched the treetops and thought that summer had a specific weightlessness she wanted to remember.

The blanket was the same gray wool, now shaken free of sand, the texture nubby under her knees as she smoothed it, smelling of sun and lake and old campfires. The temperature had dropped into perfect balance, warm air on her arms, cool grass seeping through her denim shorts where she knelt. She could taste the lemonade Thalia poured, tart and sugary, condensation on the paper cup. The sound was soft, cutlery clinking in containers, Percy humming while he unpacked, Silena describing the sunset in great detail to no one, Clarisse crunching a carrot too loudly on purpose, crickets starting up in the grass. The light was honey-thick, low and sideways, turning everything gold-edged, Annabeth's ponytail glowing, dust motes hanging.

She wore her faded orange camp tee, the fabric now soft with wear and sun, the blueberry smudge from Percy's muffin transferred to her sleeve where they had bumped, her gray hoodie unzipped and pushed off her shoulders to her elbows because the evening was warm enough, fleece pilled at the cuffs. Her denim shorts were dusty at the back from the dock, frayed hems sandy, safety pin still holding. Her white sneakers were back on, laces tied properly now, socks clean-ish, grass wiped off. Her owl bracelet was twisted twice around her wrist, her beads warm against her throat. Her hair was coming loose again, strands lifting in the breeze.

Percy flopped beside her, his teal board shorts sandy at the back, his camp tee stretched at the neck from where he had pulled it to wipe his face, the blueberry stain dark, his hair damp again from splashing at the shore, curls flattened. His bare feet were dusty, sand between his toes, his plastic watch scratched with a new mark from the canoe. He smelled of lake and sun and boy-sweat and sunscreen, familiar and comforting.

Clarisse sat cross-legged opposite, her black one-piece hidden now under her tied camp shirt which she had untied and worn loose like a jacket over it, the orange fabric hanging open, sleeves flapping around her elbows, the tails dusty. Her ponytail was lopsided, elastic stretched, her beads out again, her bare feet tucked under her thighs, red polish chipped further from the day. She had a paper plate heaped with sandwiches and carrots, eating fast.

Silena knelt gracefully, her blue sundress smoothed over her knees, the cotton glowing in sunset light, the anchor hem dusty with sand, her coral swimsuit straps tied neatly now, her hair in two perfect braids again, flyaways tamed with water, blue strings neat. Her sandals were off, lined up, her silver anklet catching gold light. She poured lemonade with great ceremony.

Thalia stretched out on her side, propped on an elbow, her black tank top glowing warm in the light, her olive cargo shorts rolled at the cuffs, dust brushed off, her black boots unlaced and loose, socks still on, spiked bracelet glinting, necklace flashing. Her hair was tamed into damp spikes, eyeliner reapplied crookedly in one eye which Silena kept trying to fix.

The food was simple, cold chicken sandwiches with lettuce that had gone slightly wilted and still tasted peppery and fresh, carrots sweet and crunchy, grapes cold and bursting, lemonade sharp. Annabeth ate with her shoulder touching Percy's, their hands brushing when they reached for grapes at the same time, his fingers cold from holding his cup. He didn't move away, and neither did she, and Thalia caught her eye over the cups and raised an eyebrow so high it disappeared under her bangs. Annabeth stuck her tongue out at her, quick, and Thalia grinned into her lemonade.

"Best day," Silena declared, lying back with her arms spread, her dress billowing, her braids pooling on the blanket, staring at the sky going pink and lavender. "Rate it. Ten out of ten?"

"Nine," Clarisse said immediately, through a mouthful. "Points off because Percy drooled in the canoe."

"I didn't drool! I was paddling!"

"You drool when you concentrate," Silena said. "It's cute. Like a puppy."

"I'm not a puppy," Percy said indignantly, but he was laughing, his cheeks flushed gold in the light. "I'm a shark. Annabeth said."

"You're a barnacle shark puppy," Clarisse said. "Rare species."

Annabeth leaned her head on Percy's shoulder for three seconds, just long enough to make Silena squeal, the cotton of his tee rough against her cheek, sun-warm. "My barnacle shark puppy rolled with me," she said. "So he's my favorite."

"Betrayal!" Silena clutched her chest, her sundress rustling. "After I shared my sunscreen!"

"You shared it after making me beg," Annabeth said, laughing, sitting up, her hoodie slipping off one shoulder. "And you got it in my eye."

"It was an accident of love," Silena said solemnly.

Thalia tossed a grape at Percy, who caught it in his mouth, choking slightly, everyone cheering. The sun sank lower, light going rose-gold on the water, the lake perfectly still, reflecting clouds like spilled paint. The air cooled enough that Annabeth pulled her hoodie back up, zipper clinking, fleece welcome. Mosquitoes whined faintly, and Silena produced bug spray that smelled of lemons and chemicals, spritzing everyone while they sputtered.

"Tell us the plan for the summer," Silena said, sitting up and hugging her knees, her blue sundress tenting over them, her braids falling forward. "Annabeth always has a plan. Lists. Diagrams. I want to hear it."

Annabeth laughed, brushing grape seeds from her denim shorts. "It's not a plan. It's ideas. Finish the canoe route to the north cove. Teach Percy the names of all the constellations even though he only remembers the rude ones. Help Clarisse beat the Ares kids at volleyball without starting a war. Learn to dive without plugging my nose."

"Your wrist action is excellent," Percy said quickly, too quickly, and Silena and Thalia both made exaggerated kissing noises until Annabeth threw grapes at both of them, laughing so hard her sides ached, the taste of lemonade bright on her tongue.

Percy walked back to the lake at midnight because Clarisse dared him, and because he never turned down a dare from his sisters.

The night air was cool enough to raise goosebumps on his arms, the smell of night-blooming jasmine from the cabin gardens and damp pine and the lake's cold breath, mineral and deep. The texture of the path was different in the dark, dew-slick grass soaking his ankles, dirt cool and soft, a root catching his toe. The temperature of the breeze lifted the hairs on his neck, delicious chill after the warm cabin. He could still taste lemonade, sour-sweet when he swallowed, and toothpaste fresh because Annabeth had made them all brush before sneaking out. The sound was huge at night, crickets roaring, frogs plunking along the shore, water lapping louder without day noise, their whispers too loud, Silena giggling and shushing herself. The light was moonlight, silver-blue pouring over everything, turning the lake to mercury, shadows black and sharp under the trees, their faces pale.

He wore his teal board shorts, the drawstring double-knotted, the fabric cool and a little damp still from the day because he hadn't changed, and a clean orange camp tee, a different one, less faded, the fabric stiff from the camp store, the Pegasus bright, because his morning shirt smelled of lake and blueberry and Annabeth had wrinkled her nose at it. His feet were bare, soles toughened from the day, his plastic watch glowing faintly at the dial. His hair was messy from sleep started and interrupted, curls sticking up. He carried a striped towel over one shoulder, rough cotton smelling of detergent.

Clarisse stalked beside him, her black one-piece under her camp shirt which was tied again at her waist, the knot tight, the cut-off sleeves pale in moonlight, the fabric whispering with each step. Her hair was down for once, dark and loose to her shoulders, damp at the ends because she had splashed water on her face to wake up, a black elastic around her wrist. Her bare feet moved silent, red polish dark in the moonlight, beads hidden under her shirt to keep them quiet. Her towel was tucked under her arm.

Silena tiptoed dramatically, shushing every few steps, her blue sundress over her coral swimsuit again, the cotton ghostly pale in moonlight, the anchor hem brushing her knees, the fabric cool. Her hair was in a single loose braid over one shoulder for sleep, flyaways silvered, blue string trailing. Her silver anklet chimed softly with each step despite her trying to muffle it with her hand, her brown sandals dangling from her other hand by the straps, soles slapping together.

Annabeth came next, holding Thalia's hand, her faded orange camp tee dark in the moonlight, her denim shorts pale at the frayed hems, her gray hoodie zipped to her chin, fleece soft against her cheeks, hood up over her ponytail which poked through the back. Her white sneakers were on but unlaced, laces dragging, socks mismatched in the rush, one white, one gray. Her owl bracelet clicked against Thalia's bracelet. Her eyes were wide and bright, sleepy and excited.

Thalia led, confident in the dark, her black tank top a shadow, her olive cargo shorts flapping, pockets jingling with flashlight she refused to turn on, her black boots laced tight now, tread crunching softly, spiked bracelet jingling faintly, necklace glinting when moonlight hit. Her hair was wild spikes silvered at the tips, eyes reflecting.

They reached the dock, wood pale and slick with dew, cold biting through Percy's soles, boards creaking loud in the quiet. The lake stretched black-silver, mist curling, moon path trembling.

Percy stopped at the root of the dock. The end looked farther away in the dark than it had that afternoon. He knew the water there was deep enough for the regular summer jumps, but knowing a place by daylight was different from checking it at midnight.

"Hang on," he said.

Clarisse looked back. "What?"

"The rope."

"What rope?"

"The throw line. It lives in the red box by the dock shed."

Silena looked toward the box, half hidden beneath a shelf. "I thought we were jumping, not sailing."

"We're jumping into water we can barely see. We can take thirty seconds and make sure the rescue line hasn't been left in a knot."

Thalia took her flashlight out. Percy saw the instinctive protest on Clarisse's face, then saw her swallow it. She walked over to the red box herself, lifted the lid and found the coiled line and orange flotation ring. The line had twisted around the ring's strap during the last practice session. She freed it without a word.

Annabeth came closer, shoes squeaking on the boards. "Who checked the end of the dock today?"

"Me," Percy said. "At noon."

"Is that enough?"

"No." He took the flashlight from Thalia and swept the beam along the boards. The end was clear, though a towel someone had left on the third plank made a pale shape he could have mistaken for a loose board. He collected it. "Somebody forgot this. We can put it back in the shed."

Thalia stood behind Annabeth, her arms folded, watching the light cross the water.

"We could just not do it," she said.

Clarisse snorted. "You were the one who wanted a midnight dare."

"And now I'm the one asking whether it's worth it."

"Are you scared?"

"I'm responsible for my sister."

Annabeth took a step away from her. "I'm standing right here."

"I know that."

"Then ask me."

Thalia looked at her. Something in her face loosened, then tightened again. Percy had seen that movement before. It was one thing for Thalia to say Annabeth could choose. It was another to watch her choose something that might make Thalia nervous.

"Do you still want to jump?" Thalia asked.

Annabeth looked out at the black water and hesitated long enough that nobody could pretend she wasn't considering it.

"I want to," she said. "But I don't want us all to jump at once unless one person is already in the water. And I want the ladder checked."

Clarisse raised her hand. "I'll go first. I can tread and watch the landing."

"You're not a lifeguard," Thalia said.

"I'm a trained swimmer who knows this dock. That matters, though it isn't magic."

Percy looked at the water. The open section ended well before the reeds, and nobody planned to swim beyond the buoy line. He had to resist the temptation to say he could make the lake safe. He could influence water, yes, but that was not the same as knowing every moving person and every hazard at once.

"Then we stay inside the marked section," he said. "No diving headfirst. No racing to the other shore. We get out when we're cold or tired."

"That's not very daring," Silena said.

"Being out here in this cold in our bathing suits is more than daring enough."

Silena inspected the ring and set it beside the dry towels. "I would like to make one formal objection to freezing on purpose."

"Noted," Annabeth said. "Objection overruled by the person who brought three towels."

"Four," Silena corrected. "I'm not sharing a wet one."

They checked the ladder. One rung was slick with algae. Percy wiped it with the spare towel they'd found and tested his foot against it; it would still be slippery, and he said so. Clarisse pointed to the upper rung, where their hands could hold while they climbed. Thalia set her flashlight by the towel pile with its beam turned toward the ladder rather than the dark water. The throw line lay coiled beside it, free of knots.

No one cheered at this part. Percy was glad. It would have felt silly to cheer for a ladder, and he knew that was exactly why campers sometimes skipped checking one.

Annabeth came to stand beside him. She took his hand, and her fingers were cold through his.

"You didn't want to be the last one in," she said.

"Still don't."

"Then you're about to lose."

"I've survived worse."

She looked at the water, swallowed and smiled as if she'd made an irritating calculation and decided it was accurate.

"All right," she said. "Clarisse goes first. Then us. I reserve the right to scream."

"That's the entire point," Percy said.

"Okay," Clarisse whispered, too loud, her breath fogging. "Rules. Jump off the end. No powers, Percy. No warming the water. Scream if you want. Cold will steal your breath."

"You're trying to scare Annabeth," Thalia whispered back, protective, arm around Annabeth's hooded shoulders, the gray fleece rustling. "Don't. She's braver than you."

"I'm not scared," Annabeth whispered, though her hand tightened on Thalia's, her sneakers squeaking on dew. "I'm cold already. Jumping seems stupid."

"It's tradition," Silena whispered, her teeth chattering, her sundress fluttering, her anklet chiming as she bounced to stay warm. "First swim of summer has to include a midnight jump. Otherwise summer doesn't count. Chiron told me. Sort of."

"Chiron told you no such thing," Percy whispered, laughing, his breath white, his towel slipping.

"He implied it," Silena said with dignity, untying nothing yet, hugging herself, her braid swinging. "With his eyes."

Clarisse stepped to the edge, her tied shirt flapping, her bare toes curling over the wood. "I'll go first. So you babies see it's fine. Count me."

She yanked her camp shirt off over her head in one motion, revealing the black one-piece stark against her pale skin in moonlight, the Lycra gleaming, and tossed the shirt to Silena who caught it. She bounced once, ponytail holder still on her wrist flapping, then ran three steps and launched, shouting with delight, hitting the water with a huge silver explosion, sound shattering the night, droplets ringing.

She surfaced gasping, laughing, treading, water streaming, moonlight on her face. "Cold! So cold! Get in! Percy, you're slowest again!"

"Am not!" Percy tore his tee off, the clean cotton catching on his hair, balling it and tossing it onto the dock, his shoulders pale and goosepimpled, his board shorts dark with dew, his watch glinting. He grabbed Annabeth's hand for a second, squeezed. "Together? On three? Sisters too?"

Annabeth pulled her gray hoodie off slowly, folding it with care despite shivering, the fleece moon-pale, placing it on her sneakers to keep it dry, standing in her faded tee and denim shorts, goosebumps rippling her arms, her ponytail dark. "You'll hold my hand until the edge?"

"Always," Percy said simply, lacing his fingers with hers, her hand icy.

Thalia stripped off nothing, her black tank and cargo shorts staying for the jump because she hated being cold after, but she kicked her boots off with two thumps, peeling socks, her bare feet pale, and rolled her shorts higher. "I'll hold your other hand," she told Annabeth, taking it, her spiked bracelet cool. "Grace sisters jump together. Poseidon barnacles can splash."

"Hey," Silena protested, wriggling out of her blue sundress with a shimmy, the cotton whispering down to reveal her coral swimsuit bright even at night, the fabric sleek, her braid swinging, her anklet flashing as she dropped the dress onto the dock and stepped out of her sandals. "We are majestic. Watch and learn."

The four on the dock lined up at the end, toes over the edge, wood freezing, water black and glittering below, breath fogging, hands linked, Silena grabbing Clarisse's wet hand as Clarisse treaded below with arms up, moonlight silver on droplets, night jasmine and cold water filling their noses, crickets screaming.

"One," Thalia shouted, her voice cracking with cold and joy, her tank strap slipping.

"Two," Silena screamed, laughing, her coral suit bright, her braid flying.

"Three!" they all screamed together, jumping as one, hands flying apart midair, Annabeth's shout bright and fearless, Percy's whoop, Thalia's wild laugh, Silena's shriek, Clarisse catching them with open arms and cold spray, the water closing over their heads like silk ice, shocking and perfect, moonlight fracturing green-silver through the churn, the taste of night lake clean on their tongues, the night's dare sealed.
