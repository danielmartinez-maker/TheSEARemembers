## Chapter 16: Capture the Flag

Percy sat cross-legged on Cabin Three's floor with a bowl of goldfish crackers and a practice map between his knees. The room smelled of cedar, salt and leather conditioner from Clarisse's recently repaired shield. He was trying to look like an ordinary twelve-year-old preparing for capture the flag, rather than someone who'd spent years planning alongside the girl on the opposing team. Every time Clarisse drew another line on the map, he imagined where Annabeth would disagree. His sister had already learned to recognize that expression.

This was harder than it sounded.

"Okay," Clarisse said, standing in front of the cabin's big map table with a spear she was using as a pointer. She was thirteen, already taller than Percy by a good two inches, with her father's dark hair pulled back in a severe ponytail and an expression that said she had been born ready for war and mildly disappointed by everyone else's lack of readiness. "Standard rules. Two flags, two jails, creek is the boundary. Zeus team has the north woods, we have the south. First team to bring the other flag across wins. No maiming."

Silena, lounging upside down on her bunk with her long hair spilling toward the floor, raised a hand. "Define maiming."

"No permanent dismemberment," Clarisse said.

"Clarisse," Silena said sweetly, "that's so generous of you."

"I'm growing as a person," Clarisse said flatly. "Shut up and listen. Their team has Thalia, which means their defense is going to be aggressive and loud. She likes to put her best fighters on the front line and dare you to come through. Annabeth is going to be running their offense or calling plays. Your girlfriend. Try to remember which team you're on."

Percy choked on a goldfish cracker.

Silena sat up so fast she nearly fell off the bunk. "Percy? You okay?"

"Fine," Percy wheezed. "Cracker went down wrong. Annabeth. Right. Extremely smart. We might be doomed."

"Daughter of Zeus," Clarisse said, like that explained everything. "Thalia's little sister. They've been training together all week, which means she's probably been drilling her on formations. So we prepare for Zeus-style play. Direct assault, lightning-fast flanks, no subtlety."

Percy stared at the map. His brain, unhelpfully, supplied about forty memories at once: Annabeth at seventeen, hair pulled back, charcoal smudging her fingers as she sketched a siege plan on the back of a receipt in the Camp Half-Blood mess hall. Annabeth at sixteen whispering, *If Thalia puts her veterans up front, we go under, not through — creek bed, third bend, where the roots make a tunnel.* Annabeth at fifteen laughing, *You always telegraph your left hook, Seaweed Brain.*

He could not say any of that. Because here, he was twelve. She was twelve. And giving away every old strategy he knew felt unfair when Annabeth hadn't agreed to it.

"So what's our plan?" he asked, aiming for casual and landing somewhere near extremely suspicious.

Clarisse gave him a look. "You're our secret weapon, little brother. You grew up in Atlantis, you've got water tricks nobody here has seen yet, and you're fast. I'm putting you on creek patrol with Silena. If anyone tries to cross at the shallow bend, you drown 'em — metaphorically."

"Mostly metaphorically," Silena added cheerfully. She flopped back down and grinned at Percy upside-down. "Don't worry, Perce. I'll protect you from the big scary Zeus kids."

"I am literally a Poseidon kid with a trident," Percy said.

"And I am literally older and wiser," Silena said. "I'm fourteen. That's practically an adult."

"That's practically eighth grade," Percy said.

"Same thing," Silena said.

Someone knocked, and Grover stuck his head in, ears — well, his curly hair barely hiding the little goat horns — twitching nervously. "Uh, hi? Chiron said I'm with Poseidon team? Is that okay? I brought my reed pipes and three kinds of cheese."

"Grover!" Percy brightened. "You're on our team? Yes. Best day ever."

"I'm also the official commentator," Grover said, edging inside with a satchel that clinked suspiciously. "Well, self-appointed. Ella said if I narrate the game like those mortal sports announcers she loves, she'll share her snacks. So I'm doing play-by-play."

"Where is Ella?" Silena asked.

"Already in the woods," Grover said solemnly. "She left an hour ago with a backpack bigger than she is. She said something about 'establishing supply lines' and 'never fighting on an empty stomach.' I didn't ask follow-up questions."

Clarisse pinched the bridge of her nose. "Great. Our team is a twelve-year-old Atlantean, a satyr with performance anxiety, a harpy with a snack-based military doctrine, and Silena."

"Hey," Silena said.

"You know what you did," Clarisse said.

"I shared my lip gloss with the Aphrodite cabin and now I'm a security risk?" Silena said.

"Yes," Clarisse said.

Percy was still looking at the map, and his eyes kept drifting to the north side, to the little X Clarisse had drawn where she thought the Zeus flag would be: the old lightning-split oak on the ridge. It was a good guess. It was also wrong, because Annabeth would never put a flag where everyone expected it. Annabeth would put it somewhere annoying and clever and slightly mean, like inside the hollow of a fallen tree surrounded by poison ivy that she had somehow convinced everyone was *strategic ground cover.*

He knew this the way he knew his own heartbeat. Which was the problem.

"Maybe," he said carefully, "we shouldn't assume they'll do the obvious thing. What if — I mean, what if this Annabeth girl does something weird? Like hides the flag off the ridge?"

Clarisse frowned. "Why would she do that? The ridge is the best defensive position."

"I dunno," Percy said, shrugging with every ounce of twelve-year-old innocence he could fake. Which, given that he was mentally seventeen and trying to conceal a likely breach of team strategy, was not much. "Just — girls are sneaky?"

Silence.

Silena stared at him. "Percy Jackson. Did you just say 'girls are sneaky' while sitting in a cabin with your two sisters?"

"I misspoke," Percy said quickly. "I meant — tactically unpredictable! Due to — brains. Big brains. Respectfully."

"Mm-hm," Clarisse said, eyes narrowed. "You've trained with her. Are you giving me useful advice or trying to get assigned wherever she'll be?"

Percy's brain short-circuited. "I'm not! I just — strategy! I like strategy! I read — uh — I saw a documentary. About flags."

Grover, bless him, chose that moment to trip over his own hooves and spill cheese cubes across the floor. "Cheese! No! The brie is rolling! Someone stop the brie!"

The war council dissolved into chaos, which was fine by Percy, because it meant nobody noticed the way his ears had gone bright red.

---

The Zeus cabin was all high ceilings and crackling static and the faint smell of ozone, and Annabeth sat on the edge of a bunk trying to look like she belonged there, which was ridiculous because she had lived in the Athena cabin for five years in her other life and could still remember exactly which bunk had the wobbly leg.

But here she was a daughter of Zeus. Twelve years old. Blonde hair braided back, gray eyes sharp, trying very hard not to stare out the window toward the Poseidon cabin.

Thalia stood in the center of the room with a whiteboard she had clearly stolen from the Big House. She was sixteen, tall and punk and magnetic, with black hair spiked with a strip of electric blue and a silver cord tied around her wrist to mark her as team captain, and she commanded a room the way storms command the sky — by being impossible to ignore.

"Listen up!" Thalia said, slapping the whiteboard. On it, in aggressive red marker, she had written: WIN. Underlined three times. "Poseidon team thinks they're hot stuff because they've got numbers and a home-field advantage on the water. We're going to prove them wrong by being faster, meaner, and smarter."

Zoe Nightshade, sitting with perfect posture and polishing her camp knives, raised an eyebrow. "You have me on your side, too."

"Exactly," Thalia said, pointing at her. "Zoe's on border patrol with me. Nobody crosses the creek on our watch. Nico — " She turned to where Nico di Angelo, young and trying to look older, sat swinging his legs and holding a plastic sword like it had personally offended him. "You're with Annabeth on offense. You wanted to do offense, right?"

Nico lit up. "Yeah! I want to steal the flag! I practiced my sneaking! I'm really good at sneaking! Watch — " He slid off the bunk and vanished behind a curtain. A second later his voice came from inside a wardrobe. "Did you see me sneak? That was sneaking!"

"Amazing sneaking, dude," Thalia said gravely. "Ten out of ten. Annabeth, you're calling the offensive plays. Chiron says you've got a tactical brain. That true?"

Annabeth's mouth went dry. She had a tactical brain the way the ocean had water. In her real life — her seventeen-year-old life — she had once planned a capture-the-flag victory in under four minutes while simultaneously braiding Percy's hair and eating a sandwich. But here she was still getting used to unfamiliar powers and a smaller reach. Old plans needed testing before she trusted them.

"Sure," she said. "I like puzzles."

"Good," Thalia said. "Because Clarisse is running Poseidon's defense, and Clarisse fights like a bulldozer with abandonment issues. She'll stack her heavies at the creek crossing and dare us to try. So we don't try. We go around."

Annabeth looked at the map Thalia had drawn. Thalia had marked the presumed Poseidon flag location at the big sea-carved grotto near the lake — good cover, easy water access, classic Poseidon. It was a solid guess.

It was also wrong, because Clarisse would never put the flag somewhere that obvious when she could put it somewhere maximally aggressive, like right behind her own front line so she could guard it personally and yell at anyone who came near.

Percy would know that. Percy had sparred with Clarisse enough times in both lives to know exactly how she thought.

Annabeth's chest ached suddenly, sharp and sweet. She missed him with a physical force that surprised her even now — missed the easy way they'd fit together on a battlefield, the shorthand, the way he always knew where she'd be without looking.

She couldn't show it. Not here. Not with Thalia watching her like a hawk watches a particularly interesting mouse.

"Actually," Annabeth said slowly, "can I suggest something? What if we don't assume Clarisse does the obvious thing? Poseidon kids — they're water kids. They think in three dimensions. They might put the flag somewhere mobile. Or somewhere wet."

Thalia blinked. "Wet."

"Like — near the creek, but hidden," Annabeth said, cheeks warming. "So they can defend it with water tricks. If I were them, I'd — " She stopped herself just in time. *If I were them and I'd spent years dating their best swimmer.* "I'd put it in the willow hollow. Where the creek bends. Lots of cover, water on two sides."

Zoe looked up, interested despite herself. "That is… not foolish. The willow hollow is difficult to approach without making noise. The mud betrays footsteps."

"Right!" Annabeth said, a little too eagerly. "Mud. Betrayal. Tactics."

Thalia was staring at her. Not hostile — curious, with something softer underneath. Thalia was sixteen and had spent years raising Annabeth. Her protectiveness could be touching and deeply inconvenient, especially when the subject was Annabeth's boyfriend being on the opposing team.

Which — fair. He did. They couldn't help it.

"Okay," Thalia said slowly. "Willow hollow. We'll scout both. But Annabeth — how'd you come up with that so fast? You saw Clarisse's last defense. How much are you relying on that?"

Annabeth's brain, which could calculate siege trajectories in her sleep, went completely blank.

"I read a book," she blurted. "About — willows. And hollows. Tactical willow hollows."

Nico emerged from the wardrobe wearing someone's helmet backwards. "I love books about hollows!"

Thalia kept looking at Annabeth for one more second, head tilted, storm-gray eyes narrowed. Then she grinned, sudden and bright. "Okay, bookworm. You're on flag-hunting duty with Sneaky McWardrobe here. Let's go win this thing."

Annabeth exhaled. Crisis averted. For now.

---

Grover Underwood had taken his duties as self-appointed play-by-play announcer extremely seriously. He had a stump near the canoe lake with a good view of both woods. He had his pipes. He had his cheese. He had a notepad labeled OFFICIAL SPORTS NOTES DO NOT EAT (the DO NOT EAT was underlined because Ella had already tried once).

Ella the harpy fluttered down beside him with a backpack that clanked like a traveling kitchen. She was small and red-feathered and fast-talking, with huge eyes and a habit of repeating phrases she'd heard and hoarding shiny things and snacks with equal devotion.

"Ella! Ella is here! Ella brought snacks!" Ella announced, upending the backpack. Out tumbled granola bars, ambrosia squares wrapped in wax paper, three apples, a bag of pretzels, two juice boxes, and what appeared to be an entire roasted chicken leg wrapped in a napkin. "Snacks are morale! Morale is victory! Victory tastes like pretzels!"

"Welcome, folks, to the one hundred and — uh — " Grover fumbled with his notes. "To today's Capture the Flag! I'm Grover Underwood, joined by my co-host Ella, who is our sideline nutrition correspondent."

"Nutrition! Ella likes that word! Ella will shout it during tackles!" Ella said, already climbing a low branch with pretzels in each talon. "Tackle! Nutrition!"

Down by the creek, the two teams were lining up. Chiron stood on the dining pavilion steps with his bow, white stallion body gleaming in the afternoon sun.

"Warriors!" Chiron called. "You know the rules! The creek is the boundary! The jail is by the amphitheater! No killing, no maiming, no summoning the dead — " He looked pointedly at Nico, who waved innocently. "— no flooding the entire field — " He looked pointedly at the Poseidon side, where several children waved innocently. "— and the game ends at dinner or when a flag is captured! Shake hands!"

The two lines shuffled forward. Percy, in a blue bandana, ended up facing Annabeth, in a gray bandana. They were three feet apart. They had agreed not to share strategy before the match. By their silent mutual agreement, this was turning out to be harder than it should have been.

"Hi," Percy said quietly, shaking her hand for exactly the regulation-approved length of time.

"Hi," Annabeth said, squeezing once, quickly, where nobody could see. "Creek bend. Third root tunnel. If you go for our flag."

"Willow hollow. Mud on the left, dry on the right," Percy murmured, eyes on Chiron. "If you go for ours."

"Percy — " Annabeth breathed. "We're on opposite teams."

"I know," Percy said. "Isn't it great? We get to be rivals. Very normal. Very platonic rivals who have never kissed behind the forge."

"Shut up," Annabeth whispered, but she was smiling. "You're on."

"Hey!" Thalia barked, appearing behind Annabeth like a thunderhead. "No fraternizing with the enemy, Grace. Come on."

"Coming," Annabeth said quickly, stepping back. Percy stepped back. Their hands parted. It felt like a rubber band snapping.

Thalia eyed Percy the whole way. Percy tried to look non-threatening, which was difficult because he was holding a trident and grinning like an idiot in love.

Grover, from his stump, blew a tentative note on his pipes. "And the teams separate! Tension is high! Cheese is… also high! Ella, any thoughts?"

"Ella thinks the small boy with sea-green eyes is staring at the small blonde girl!" Ella reported loudly, leaning so far off her branch she nearly fell. "Staring is reconnaissance! Reconnaissance needs snacks! Ella will deliver snacks to the star-crossed reconnaissance!"

"Ella, no — " Grover started, but Ella was already gone in a flurry of red feathers, a granola bar clutched in each foot.

---

The horn blew, and the woods exploded.

Percy had played capture the flag a hundred times. He'd played it as a scrawny new kid, as a seasoned veteran, as a guy who once held off an entire team with a broken sword and a water balloon. But he'd never played it like this — twelve years old, heart hammering, every sense dialed to eleven because somewhere in the north woods was Annabeth, and his whole body knew it like a compass knew north.

"Go! Go! Go!" Clarisse roared, and the Poseidon team scattered.

Percy sprinted with Silena toward the creek, feet pounding the pine-needle trail. The afternoon light came down in shafts through the canopy, and the air smelled like sap and mud and coming rain — Zeus weather, which meant Thalia's team would have a little extra static in their favor. Percy could feel it prickling on his skin.

They reached the shallow bend — their assigned post — and dropped into the ferns. The creek gurgled ahead, clear and quick over smooth stones. On the far bank, Zoe was already in position, an arrow nocked, eyes scanning.

"Okay," Silena whispered, pulling out a mirror to check her hair. "You watch left, I watch right, and if anyone crosses we scream really loud and Clarisse comes running."

"That's the plan?" Percy whispered. "Scream?"

"Worked last year," Silena said. "Also, I brought cookies." She produced two slightly squashed chocolate-chip cookies from her armor. "One for you, one for me. For stamina."

"Silena, we're on guard duty — "

"Guard duty with cookies is better than guard duty without cookies," Silena said firmly, pressing one into his hand. "Amphitrite told us to look after you. Hydration and cookies count."

Percy took the cookie. It was impossible to argue with Silena when she invoked instructions from a queen. Amphitrite hadn't raised his sisters, and Percy appreciated that Silena could take a kind remark from his mother without pretending it settled the harder questions about Poseidon.

They crouched in the ferns, munching. Across the creek, Zoe's eyes flicked to them. She sniffed disdainfully.

"Thou eatest on duty?" she called. "Chiron specifically said no food near the ward markers. Is that chocolate chip?"

"Want one?" Silena called back cheerfully. "We have extras! No poison, promise! Mostly!"

Zoe hesitated visibly. "The hunt does not accept bribes."

"It's not a bribe, it's cross-team diplomacy!" Silena said.

Zoe's nose wrinkled. Then, to Percy's absolute delight, she lowered her bow half an inch. "…What kind of chocolate?"

And that was when the first scream went up from the far left — a Zeus-team feint at the deep crossing — and everything went sideways.

Percy was on his feet instantly, cookie forgotten. "That way!"

"No, wait — " Silena grabbed his wrist. "That's too obvious. Clarisse said — "

But Percy was already moving, because he knew that scream. That was Nico's war-cry, which meant Nico was exactly where Nico was supposed to be: being loud and visible while someone quieter went the other way.

*Classic Annabeth,* he thought, with a surge of pride so strong it nearly knocked him over. *Loud decoy, quiet infiltration. Gods, she's good.*

He reversed direction and sprinted right, toward the narrow root tunnel at the third bend — the one he'd told Annabeth about, the one she would absolutely use because it was the best route and because he had told her about it and she trusted him.

He burst through the ferns and nearly collided with Annabeth head-on.

They both froze.

She was muddy to the knees, braid coming loose, gray bandana tied around her forehead, eyes wide. Nico was behind her, covered in leaves, plastic sword raised. For one heartbeat, nobody moved. The creek chuckled behind them. A jay screamed overhead.

"Oh," Annabeth said.

"Oh," Percy said.

Nico looked between them. "You're the enemy! En garde!" He brandished his sword. "Annabeth, do the tactic! Do the flanking maneuver with the thing!"

Annabeth didn't move. Percy didn't move. They stared at each other with the exact expression of two people who had spent the last five minutes sprinting toward each other on purpose and were now required to pretend it was an accident.

"Hi," Percy said again, a little breathless.

"Hi," Annabeth said.

"This is awkward," Nico observed. "Are we fighting or hugging? Thalia said no hugging the enemy."

"No hugging!" Percy and Annabeth said in unison. Then glanced at each other, horrified.

Nico squinted. "You guys said that at the same time. That was weird."

"We're — " Annabeth started.

"— just — " Percy said.

"— in sync because we're both smart!" Annabeth finished loudly. "Tactically! I anticipated he'd anticipate me! Anticipation!"

"Yeah!" Percy said. "What she said! I totally knew she'd be here! Because — uh — footprints! I saw footprints! Very… footprint-y!"

Nico looked down. The ground was solid rock. There were no footprints.

"Huh," Nico said.

Behind Percy, Silena came crashing through the ferns, sword drawn. "Percy! You — oh." She stopped, taking in the scene: Percy and Annabeth standing three feet apart, both red-faced, Nico looking confused. "Oh, good, you caught them! Nice work!" She raised her sword. "Surrender, Zeus scum! You're on our side of the creek!"

Annabeth snapped back into focus. She grabbed Nico's sleeve. "Run!"

She shoved Nico toward the creek and turned to follow — and her foot slipped on a wet root. She went down hard on one knee with a sharp gasp.

Percy moved before he thought. He was at her side in two strides, hand on her arm. "Hey — you okay? Your knee — "

Silena stopped. Zoe, arriving at a run from the other side, stopped. Nico stopped. Everyone stared at Percy holding Annabeth's elbow with the gentleness of someone handling something precious.

Annabeth stared up at him. "Percy."

"Sorry," he muttered, letting go like she'd burned him. "Reflex. You — you fell."

"I fell on purpose!" Annabeth said quickly, scrambling up, face flaming. "Tactical falling! To — to lure you in!"

"Good lure!" Percy said, backing away. "Very luring! I was totally lured!"

Zoe looked between them slowly, silver eyes narrowing. "Curious," she said.

Silena was grinning like a shark who'd just been handed a beach ball. "Uh-huh. Very tactical. Super normal."

"JAIL THEM!" Nico shouted, suddenly remembering the game. "JAIL THE BLUE TEAM! GET 'EM, ZOE!"

And the chase was on.

---

Annabeth's knee throbbed, her heart hammered, and her brain was screaming at her in three different volumes.

*That was too close. That was way too close. He touched your arm like you'd break and everyone saw and now Silena knows and Zoe knows and —*

She splashed through the creek with Nico, water icy around her ankles, and didn't stop until they were deep in Zeus territory under the cover of a massive fallen oak.

Nico collapsed against the trunk, panting. "Thatwasawesome! Did you see me sneak? I sneaked so good! And then you did tactical falling and he did tactical catching and it was super tactical!"

"It was not tactical," Annabeth muttered, wringing out her braid. "It was stupid. I slipped. On a root. In front of everyone."

"But he caught you," Nico said, eyes shining. "That was nice. I thought Poseidon and Zeus were enemies."

"We are enemies," Annabeth said firmly. "In this game. Right now. He was just — being polite. Because he's… polite. Annoyingly polite. With stupid green eyes and stupid reflexes."

Nico nodded sagely, like a boy who understood nothing but was prepared to pretend otherwise. "Girls are weird."

"Don't start," Annabeth warned, but there was no heat in it. She peeked over the log. No pursuit — Silena must have held Percy back, or Zoe had called a retreat to reposition. The woods were quiet except for distant shouting and Grover's pipes drifting faintly from the lake: a warbling, off-key rendition of what might have been the national anthem.

She pulled out the little wax tablet Thalia had given her for sketching plays and stared at it without seeing it. Her hand shook slightly.

This was the problem with remembering. In her real life, she and Percy had fought side by side so long they'd developed a language without words — a glance, a shift of weight, and the other knew. *Go left. I've got your back. Trust me.* That language didn't turn off just because they were twelve and wearing different-colored bandanas.

She had known he would be at the third bend. He had known she would use the root tunnel. They'd told each other at the handshake, sure, but even without that — they would have known. It was inevitable as tide.

And Thalia was going to notice. Thalia already noticed everything.

As if summoned by the thought, Thalia dropped out of the trees above them, landing in a crouch that sent leaves flying. Sparks crackled faintly in her hair.

"Report," Thalia barked. "Did you get across? Where's their flag?"

Nico saluted with the wrong hand. "We got across! We saw the enemy! Annabeth did tactical falling and Percy did tactical catching and then we ran away really fast!"

Thalia's eyebrows climbed. "…Tactical what now?"

"He caught me when I slipped," Annabeth said quickly, before Nico could elaborate. "It was nothing. I think their flag is in the willow hollow, like we thought. Silena and Percy are guarding the bend, so the approach from the east is covered, but the west side — where the bank overhangs — that's blind. If we loop wide past the amphitheater and come in from behind — "

Thalia held up a hand, studying her. "Hold on. Back up. Jackson caught you?"

"He was being chivalrous or whatever," Annabeth said, aiming for dismissive and landing on defensive. "It doesn't matter."

"It matters if he forgets which team he's on around you," Thalia said bluntly.

Annabeth's entire circulatory system relocated to her face. "He knows which team he's on! He can catch me without handing over the whole match!"

Nico looked between them, delighted. "Oooooh, crush! Percy likes Annabeth! I'm telling Ella! Ella loves crushes! She writes them down in her shiny book!"

"Everybody knows we're together!" Annabeth said, too loud. A bird startled out of the tree above them. "He can still compete against me! It's not a diplomatic crisis!"

Thalia crossed her arms, but her mouth was twitching. "Relax, bookworm. I'm teasing. Mostly." The teasing faded, replaced by something more serious. "But listen — if Jackson's being friendly, use it. He's their weakest link. New kid, young, soft-hearted. If you can get past him, you can get to that flag."

Annabeth felt like she'd been punched. *Weakest link. Soft-hearted.* Percy, who had held the sky. Percy, who had refused immortality. Percy, who had once carried her through a volcano while his own skin burned.

"He's not weak," she said quietly, before she could stop herself.

Thalia blinked. "Okay. Sure. He's not weak, he's just twelve. Same thing."

Annabeth looked away, jaw tight. She wanted to say: *You don't know him. You don't know what he's survived, what we've survived. He's the strongest person I've ever met and he's kind anyway, which is harder.*

She couldn't say any of it. So she said, "West approach. Past the amphitheater. Nico and I can do it in twenty minutes if Zoe covers us."

Thalia studied her for a long moment, then nodded slowly. "Okay. Do it. And Annabeth — " She gripped Annabeth's shoulder, fierce and warm. "Be careful. Clarisse fights dirty. If you get caught, don't be a hero. Let us come get you."

It was such a big-sister thing to say that Annabeth's throat closed up. Thalia, alive and sparking with life, who in Annabeth's original timeline had spent years as a tree — looking at her like she mattered.

"Okay," Annabeth managed.

"Good," Thalia said, and then she was gone, vaulting back into the trees with a crackle of static.

Nico tugged Annabeth's sleeve. "Are we going? Can we go now? I want to do more sneaking!"

Annabeth shouldered her pack, checked her knife, and pushed everything else down deep. "Yeah. Let's go steal a flag."

---

Thalia Grace had excellent instincts. This was a documented fact. Chiron said so. Zoe said so, albeit with more archaic vocabulary. Her instincts had once told her a Cyclops was hiding in a janitor's closet in Ohio, and she'd been right, and she still brought it up whenever anyone doubted her.

Right now, her instincts were telling her something was deeply weird about Percy Jackson and Annabeth Grace.

She crouched in the branches of the lightning-split oak — the decoy flag location, because Annabeth had convinced her to move the real flag to the hollow under the roots of the fallen elm, which Thalia had to admit was genius — and replayed the last hour.

Exhibit A: At the handshake, Jackson and Annabeth had talked for maybe ten seconds, too quiet to hear, and both walked away looking dazed.

Exhibit B: Annabeth had predicted the Poseidon flag location with suspicious accuracy. Willow hollow. Which was, Thalia had confirmed on a scouting run, exactly where Clarisse had put it. How much of that had Percy let slip?

Exhibit C: Jackson had predicted — or guessed, or *something* — Annabeth's crossing point. He'd been waiting at the third bend. Annabeth had gone straight to the third bend. That wasn't luck. That was coordination.

Exhibit D: The catching thing. Thalia hadn't seen it, but Zoe's retelling had been vivid. *He moved like the sea answering the moon,* Zoe had said, which was extremely Zoe and extremely unhelpful, but the gist was: the Poseidon boy had crossed half a clearing in a heartbeat to catch his girlfriend, and then apologized like he'd committed a crime.

Thalia frowned at a beetle.

"Thalia! Thalia is brooding! Brooding burns calories! Calories need snacks!" Ella landed on the branch beside her with a thump that nearly knocked them both off, and shoved a granola bar into Thalia's hand. "Ella brings snacks to all warriors! Ella is neutral! Ella is Switzerland! Switzerland is cheese! Cheese is — "

"Ella," Thalia said, "have you noticed anything weird about Percy and Annabeth?"

Ella's huge eyes went even huger. "Weird! Weird like star-crossed! Star-crossed like Romeo and Juliet! Ella read Romeo and Juliet! Everyone died! Ella cried! Ella ate three muffins!"

Thalia stared. "Star-crossed… what?"

"Ella sees things! Ella sees the small sea boy looking at the small sky girl during meals! Looking like she is the sun and he is a very happy fish!" Ella flapped excitedly, scattering crumbs. "Ella sees the small sky girl braiding her hair the way Atlantean warriors braid for luck! Ella sees them finishing each other's sentences! Sentence-finishing is true love! True love needs protein!"

"Ella," Thalia said slowly, "are you saying they're coordinating during the match?"

"Ella is saying Ella brings extra snacks for strategy-keeping!" Ella said, pressing two more granola bars into Thalia's hands and launching herself off the branch. "Secrets! Snacks! Switzerland! Bye!"

Thalia sat alone with three granola bars and a growing sense of unease.

Thalia had known they were together. What annoyed her was how easily they coordinated while supposedly playing against each other. Looking, braiding, finishing sentences: fine. Handing over a flag because Percy liked the person stealing it: less fine.

Annabeth had told her that their old lives included years of fighting side by side. Thalia couldn't remember those years or expect that closeness to vanish on command. She could ask her sister to take the game seriously.

She was being protective again, she knew. This time she tried to keep the objection small enough to fit the problem: no throwing the match.

Still. She climbed higher, crackling faintly, and scanned the south woods for a flash of blue.

Clarisse La Rue did not have feelings about capture the flag. Capture the flag was war. War did not involve feelings. War involved spears and shouting and winning.

Which was why it was extremely annoying that she currently had feelings.

She crouched behind the willow curtain with her spear across her knees, watching the hollow where the blue flag hung from a low branch, rippling faintly in the breeze. It should have been a perfect defensive position. Water on two sides, thick mud that sucked at boots, willow fronds that hid everything. Annabeth had called the position exactly. Clarisse already knew the girl as Thalia's sister, Percy's girlfriend and the one who'd revised the evacuation routes after the storm. That didn't make her a teammate today. It made her an opponent who noticed the paths other campers forgot to guard, and Clarisse had no intention of losing just because Percy liked the way Annabeth drew maps.

Her eyes cut to Percy, who sat beside her, dripping wet from a quick dip in the creek he'd used to "check the water" and whittling a stick with intense focus.

"You're quiet," Clarisse said.

"I'm thinking," Percy said.

"About what?"

"About how mud works," Percy said. "And — tactics. And whether Grover's commentary is audible from here, because I swear I just heard him say 'an absolute barn-burner at the creek, folks.'"

As if on cue, Grover's voice floated through the trees, amplified by his pipes: "AND ZOE NIGHTSHADE INTERCEPTS ANOTHER CROSSING ATTEMPT! THAT'S HER FOURTH INTERCEPTION! THE HUNT IS ON FIRE, FOLKS! ELLA, YOUR THOUGHTS?"

"TACKLES NEED SNACKS!" came Ella's distant shriek. "SNACKS! SNACKS FOR THE TACKLED!"

Clarisse snorted despite herself. Then she sobered. "Percy. Can I ask you something?"

"Sure."

"Are you planning to let your girlfriend steal our flag?"

Percy's whittling knife slipped and nearly took off his thumb. "What? No! We're enemies! In the game! That's — no!"

Clarisse watched him turn the color of a boiled shrimp. "Uh-huh."

"It's not like that!" Percy said desperately. "I know her plans because we train together. That should be useful. To us. Our team. Very useful."

"Percy," Clarisse said, keeping her voice below the other defenders' hearing. "Annabeth's your girlfriend. I know that. Silena knows that. I don't need a lecture on why you like her. I need to know whether you'll guard this flag when she is on the opposite team." Her hand stayed on the worn grip of the spear. "I made you my partner today because you said I could count on you. Don't make me regret believing it."

Percy blinked. "Thalia glares at everyone."

"She glares at you specifically," Clarisse said. "Because you're a Poseidon kid hanging around her little sister. She's protective. I'm protective. It's what we do. I'm just saying — be careful, okay? If you like someone, take it slow. No need to rush. You're twelve. You've got time."

Percy stared at her, and for a second his expression was so old — so achingly, impossibly old — that Clarisse forgot how to breathe. Grief, love, amusement, and something like wonder flickered across his face all at once.

"Yeah," he said quietly. "Time. I hope so."

Then Silena came sliding down the bank, covered in mud and triumphant. "False alarm on the east! It was just Grover trying to get a better vantage point and falling in the creek! He's fine! Ella fished him out with a pretzel rod! But — heads up — I saw movement past the amphitheater. Two figures, going wide. I think it's Annabeth and the little skull kid."

Clarisse was on her feet instantly, spear up. "Show me."

Percy stood too, and Clarisse saw his hand tighten on his trident — not with fear, but with something like pride.

"She's going wide," he murmured. "Past the amphitheater. Of course she is."

"How'd you know she'd do that?" Silena asked.

Percy opened his mouth. Closed it. "Lucky guess?"

Clarisse and Silena exchanged a look. The look said: *our little brother is lying about something, and we're going to find out what, and then we're going to tease him mercilessly.*

But later. Right now, there was a flag to defend.

"Positions!" Clarisse barked. "Silena, high ground! Percy, water! If they come through the creek, I want them wet, muddy, and regretting their choices!"

"Aye-aye!" Silena said, scrambling up the bank.

Percy waded into the shallows, trident low, eyes on the tree line. The water curled around his ankles like a happy dog, and Clarisse felt the familiar prickle of Poseidon power — the creek brightening, the current quickening, responding to him.

*Not the weakest link,* she thought, remembering what Thalia had supposedly said. *Not even close.*

"Welcome back to Capture the Flag, folks! Grover Underwood here, damp but dignified after my heroic immersion in the creek — Ella, tell them I was heroic."

"Grover fell! Splash! Heroic splash! Splash needs towels! Towels need snacks!" Ella, perched on Grover's horns with a juice box, waved at absolutely no one. "Ella has visited both teams! Ella has delivered twelve granola bars, seven pretzels, and one emergency cookie! Morale is high! Blood sugar is stable!"

"That's right, Ella! Now, let's review the first half! Zeus team holds the north woods with an iron fist — Thalia Grace has personally sent four Poseidon raiders to jail, where they are reportedly playing cards with the jail guard and having a great time! Zoe Nightshade remains undefeated at the creek! And rookie sensation Annabeth Grace has crossed enemy lines not once but twice!"

"Annabeth is sneaky! Sneaky like a fox! Foxes are orange! Orange is a snack color!" Ella added wisely.

"On the Poseidon side, Clarisse La Rue is a defensive wall! Silena Beauregard has bribed — uh, diplomatically engaged — three separate Zeus scouts with baked goods! And Percy Jackson — folks, Percy Jackson is something special in that water! He redirected a whole crossing current to sweep two attackers back to their own side without even touching them!"

"He waved! The water waved back! Water is friendly! Friendly water needs no snacks, but Ella gave it a pretzel anyway!" Ella tossed a pretzel into the creek ceremoniously.

"Now, I've been getting reports of some… unusual cross-team diplomacy," Grover said, lowering his voice conspiratorially. "Can you confirm, Ella?"

"Ella confirms staring! Ella confirms catching! Ella confirms sentence-finishing!" Ella counted on her talons. "Ella also confirms that the sea boy braids his hair with a strip of gray cloth! Gray is a sky color! Sky colors on sea boys mean — "

A horn blew. Grover leapt up, nearly dislodging Ella. "AND WE'RE BACK! Second half is underway! Remember, folks, game ends at dinner! Which, judging by the smell from the dining pavilion — is that pizza? Is that pizza I smell? — might be soon! Stay tuned!"

Annabeth moved through the laurels like a whisper, Nico two steps behind her, trying to imitate her and snapping every twig in the forest.

"Shh," she breathed.

"Sorry," Nico breathed back, stepping directly on a branch that cracked like a gunshot. "Sorry. Sneaking is hard. I'm more of a dramatic-entrance guy."

"Just stay low," Annabeth whispered. Through the leaves ahead, she could see it — the flash of blue. The Poseidon flag, hanging in the willow hollow, exactly where she'd predicted. Her heart leapt. *Got you.*

Then she saw the defenses.

Clarisse, front and center, spear ready, eyes sweeping. Silena up on the bank with a bow she barely knew how to use but held with cheerful menace. And Percy — knee-deep in the creek, trident in hand, water swirling around him in slow, deliberate spirals.

He looked… magnificent. Twelve years old and already a force of nature, sea-green eyes bright, hair curling with damp. Her chest tightened with pride and longing and frustration all at once, because he was in her way and he was beautiful and this was a game and none of it was real and all of it mattered.

*Okay,* she thought. *Think. Clarisse charges. Silena panics and shoots wide left — she always shoots wide left when she's nervous, she did it in the other life too. Percy — Percy will go for Nico first, because Nico's the obvious threat, which leaves me — *

Percy looked straight at her hiding spot.

Not in her general direction. At her. Through three layers of laurel and willow fronds, he looked directly into her eyes, and his mouth curved into the smallest, most private smile.

Her breath caught. He couldn't see her. It was impossible. Unless —

Unless he just knew. The way she would have known where he was. Compass to north. Tide to moon.

Nico tugged her sleeve. "Now? Do we go now? I'm ready to be a hero!"

"Wait — " Annabeth started, but Nico was already charging, plastic sword held high, screaming at the top of his lungs: "FOR HADES AND SNACKS!"

Clarisse whirled. "Contact! West bank!"

Silena loosed a padded practice arrow that went — exactly as predicted — wide left, thunking into a tree six feet from Nico. Nico shrieked with delight and kept running. Clarisse charged to meet him, spear lowered for a disarming sweep.

And Percy — Percy didn't go for Nico. He held his ground, eyes still locked on Annabeth's hiding spot, and mouthed two words: *Come on.*

It was an invitation. A challenge. A dare three years in the making.

Annabeth burst from cover.

She hit the mud at a sprint, feet sliding, knife out. Percy raised his trident — not to strike, but to sweep the water up in a shining curtain between them, a wall of creek-water that glittered in the late-afternoon sun. It was showy. It was unnecessary. It was pure Percy — buying her time, giving her a screen to move behind, disguised as defense.

She dove through a gap in the curtain he left — exactly where she knew he'd leave it, low on the left — and rolled under Clarisse's guard as Clarisse turned, too late, shouting.

Percy "slipped" in the mud at the critical moment, going down with a spectacular splash that soaked Silena's bow and sent her arrow spiraling into the sky.

"Percy!" Silena shrieked, laughing despite herself. "You — oh my gods — "

Annabeth was at the flag. Her fingers closed on the blue cloth. She yanked it free with a triumphant cry —

— and Percy's hand closed around her wrist. Not hard. Just enough to stop her. They stood frozen in the willow hollow, water dripping, mud splattered, flag stretched between them like a banner.

"Got you," Percy said softly, grinning.

"Got me?" Annabeth said, breathless, laughing despite everything. "I've got the flag, Seaweed Brain."

"You've got half a flag," Percy said. "I'm holding the other half. This is technically a tie."

"This is technically you cheating," Annabeth said.

"This is technically me being brilliant," Percy said.

Around them, chaos: Clarisse tackling Nico (gently, picking him up under the arms like a kitten while he kicked and giggled), Silena trying to un-soak her bow, Zoe arriving at a dead run with Thalia right behind her —

"STOP!" Thalia shouted, skidding into the hollow, sparks flying. She took in the scene: Annabeth and Percy, frozen, hands joined on the flag, faces inches apart, both flushed and bright-eyed and grinning like idiots.

Thalia's jaw dropped. "What — what is happening here?"

"Hi, Thalia," Percy and Annabeth said in unison. Then froze, horrified, again.

Silence. Dripping water. Grover's distant voice: "FOLKS, I DON'T BELIEVE WHAT I'M SEEING! IT'S A FLAG-GRAB WITH A HAND-HOLD! ELLA, ARE YOU SEEING THIS?"

"ELLA SEES LOVE! LOVE NEEDS SNACKS! ELLA IS THROWING SNACKS!" A rain of granola bars pelted the hollow.

Thalia pointed at them, finger shaking. "You. Two. Explain. Right now. Why are you helping the enemy grab our flag?"

"We're disputing possession!" Percy said, letting go like the flag was on fire. "We're holding flag! There's a difference! It's — competitive flag-holding!"

"We're rivals!" Annabeth said, clutching the flag to her chest. "Fierce rivals! Who anticipate each other! Tactically!"

"She anticipated me right into the mud," Percy said.

"He slipped," Annabeth said.

"I slipped tactically," Percy said.

"You slipped because you're in love with mud," Annabeth said.

"You love mud," Percy shot back. "You married mud. You — " He stopped, ears scarlet, because *married* had come out of nowhere and hung in the air like a flare.

Clarisse, still holding a squirming Nico, looked between them slowly. A grin spread across her face — wicked, delighted, sisterly. "Ohhhhhh," she said. "Oh, I see the strategy now."

"Get what?" Thalia demanded. "Somebody explain before I call a penalty!"

Nico, dangling from Clarisse's grip, raised his hand. "I know! They're in love! Like Romeo and Juliet! But with more mud! And Ella said — "

"Nico!" Annabeth and Percy yelped in unison. Again. They glanced at each other in despair.

Zoe lowered her bow very slowly, a strange, soft expression crossing her usually stern face. "Ah," she said quietly. "Young warriors. The heart chooses early, sometimes. Even those of us trained in Hecate's arts can recognize affection."

"Okay, timeout!" Thalia said, holding up both hands, static crackling between her fingers. "Everybody stop talking! Annabeth — you're on my team! You're supposed to hate him!" She jabbed a finger at Percy. "Him! The fish boy! Can you stop coordinating for ten minutes?"

Annabeth opened her mouth. Closed it. Looked at Percy. Percy looked back, helpless and fond and twelve years old with seventeen-year-old eyes.

What could she say? *We remember dying for each other. We remember slow dancing in the Hades cabin at Nico's birthday party. We remember every capture-the-flag game for five years, and we learned each other's moves the way you learn breathing.* The truth was embarrassing. They had let being glad to see each other wreck both teams' plans.

"We just — click," she said finally, small and honest. "On the field. We've had a lot of practice. I keep knowing where he's going to be."

Percy's expression softened completely. "Same," he said quietly. "We know each other's moves. I forgot we were supposed to use that against each other. Does that make sense?"

Thalia stared at them. The static around her hands flickered and died.

Clarisse set Nico down gently. Silena climbed down from the bank, muddy and beaming. Zoe sheathed her knives. And into the strange, tender quiet, Ella fluttered down with a bag of pretzels and announced: "ELLA KNEW! ELLA ALWAYS KNEW! ELLA BROUGHT EXTRA FOR THE REVEAL! THE REVEAL NEEDS SALT!"

And then — salvation, in the form of a conch horn from the dining pavilion, followed by Chiron's amplified voice: "DINNER! PIZZA NIGHT! GAME OVER IN TEN MINUTES — FINISH UP!"

Nico gasped. "PIZZA? PIZZA NIGHT? WE HAVE TO STOP! PIZZA IS MORE IMPORTANT THAN WAR!"

Silena raised both hands. "I second that! I move we call it a draw! All in favor?"

"Aye!" shouted half the hollow.

Thalia looked at the flag — still clutched in Annabeth's hands, blue as the sea — then at Percy, standing dripping and hopeful in the creek, then at Annabeth, who looked back with pleading gray eyes.

Thalia sighed a sigh that seemed to come from her boots. "Fine. Draw. But — " She pointed at Percy. "You. Fish boy. You and me are going to talk."

Percy's grin flickered. "Talk? Like — friendly talk? Or lightning talk?"

"Depends on your answers," Thalia said darkly, but there was no real heat in it. She turned to Annabeth, and her expression softened into something almost vulnerable. "And you — are you okay? Did you get hurt? Your knee — "

"I'm fine," Annabeth said, and smiled — a real smile, warm and unguarded. "He caught me."

Thalia looked between them once more, shook her head slowly, and muttered, "I'm too young to be a protective big sister. I'm sixteen. This is ridiculous."

"It's very noble," Zoe said solemnly. "For once, your concern has been usefully directed."

"Shut up, Zoe," Thalia said, but she was smiling.

Chiron had not intended the final ten minutes of capture the flag to turn into a negotiation about whether pizza counted as a legitimate victory condition.

He could hear the argument before he reached the willow hollow. Clarisse was insisting that a tie could be declared only after the horn, Thalia had an equal claim to the flag and Silena wanted someone to check Annabeth's knee before they continued shouting about territory. Nico was holding half the blue banner and suggesting a rule by which whichever team provided the best dessert would automatically win.

"That's not a rule," Clarisse said.

"It should be."

"That's worse."

Percy stood beside the creek with mud on his sleeves, looking at Annabeth as though they had both suddenly remembered they belonged to opposing teams. Chiron could tell from their expressions that the real argument had almost nothing to do with flags. He had seen children lose their heads during a competitive game before. Usually the problem was pride or fear of embarrassment. Today it appeared to involve both.

"May I have the banners?" he asked.

The shouting stopped. Nico relinquished his end of the blue flag reluctantly. Annabeth handed over the other end, leaving a muddy print on the cloth. Chiron held up the banner and checked that it was still attached to its pole.

"Neither flag has crossed the opposing team's finish line," he said. "No one has completed the victory condition."

"But she took it," Percy said.

"She did. Then the defense prevented her from leaving the hollow with it. That is an incomplete capture."

Annabeth studied the flag, then Percy. "He also deliberately left a gap in the water screen."

Chiron looked at Percy. "Is that true?"

Percy hesitated. He could make a technically defensible argument that he had expected her to take the opening and had planned to catch her on the other side. He had actually wanted to see what she would do. For a few seconds he'd forgotten his team was depending on him to stop her.

"Yes," he said. "I left the gap."

Clarisse opened her mouth. Silena caught her sleeve.

"And did you agree beforehand to help the opposing team?" Chiron asked Annabeth.

"No. We both knew where the other one would move. It wasn't a plan we made tonight."

"Then you must learn the difference between knowing someone well and deciding to use that knowledge against your own assignment." Chiron turned toward the two team leaders. "What do you propose?"

Clarisse wanted the remaining minutes. Thalia wanted an explanation of whether Percy was helping Annabeth deliberately. Neither felt satisfied with a draw, which was reasonable: both teams had spent the afternoon trying to win.

Silena offered a compromise. "Let the last ten minutes run, but restart everyone at the midfield line. No water screens, no lightning, no shadow travel. Use ordinary movement and the marked paths."

Nico looked wounded. "No shadow travel? That's my entire idea."

"Then you'll have to discover another idea," Clarisse told him.

Chiron considered the time. There was not enough daylight to reset both teams properly, and several campers had already left their assigned positions to head toward the pavilion. Continuing would reward whichever side happened to be nearest the unattended flags. Declaring a tie wasn't an admission of failure; it was a decision not to pretend a fair finish could be manufactured out of a disrupted game.

"We'll record an incomplete match," he said. "No winner, no loss. Both teams can choose leaders for a rematch on Friday, and I'll circulate a clarification about which powers are allowed."

Clarisse looked dissatisfied. "That means we don't get credit for holding the creek."

"You may claim credit for excellent defense. You'll have to wait until Friday to claim a victory."

Thalia examined Annabeth's muddy knee. "And we need someone to check that."

Annabeth started to object. Chiron held up a hand.

"You fell hard enough to stop the match. A healer can inspect it before dinner. That isn't a judgment on your strategy."

Annabeth looked at the worn fabric over her knee. Percy had caught her arm before she went down into the creek. He had been quick and kind, which made him a good friend and a lousy opponent in that moment. She could be grateful without pretending the game had ended properly.

"All right," she said.

The teams began gathering the scattered supplies. Clarisse recovered the last spear from the mud. Silena collected arrows and checked that none had been left in the reeds. Nico found his helmet upside down beneath the willow and accused the tree of cheating.

Thalia stood beside Percy while Annabeth followed the healer up the path.

"I know you care about her," she said. "I haven't forgotten that. But when you both sign up to play, other people count on your decisions too."

"I know."

"Do you?"

He looked at the blue flag in Chiron's hands. "I didn't really think about that when she ran through the gap."

"Then think about it before the rematch." Thalia paused. "I'm not asking you to stop knowing her. I'm asking you to remember who else is on the field."

He nodded. She left him to help Nico gather the helmets.

Percy stayed by the creek until Silena came back for the last bowstring. He was embarrassed by how much he'd wanted to laugh when Annabeth grabbed the flag and how little he'd thought about the other people who'd spent the afternoon playing seriously.

"Are you angry?" he asked his sister.

"A little. I was trying to hold the bank with a wet bow while you made a dramatic waterfall for the opposing team."

"I thought I could catch her afterward."

"You did catch her. That wasn't the same thing as stopping the capture properly."

"I'll do better Friday."

Silena looked him over. "You can make up for it by helping us repair the banner. Nico tore one of the ties when he tried to wear it."

Percy accepted the assignment. He was relieved that she had chosen something he could do rather than demanding an apology he would have delivered three times without making the next game any fairer.

At the pavilion, Annabeth had already had her knee checked and was sitting with a wrapped cloth around it. Thalia was bringing her water. Percy sat at the far end of the bench until Annabeth glanced up.

"I heard we're having a rematch," she said.

"Chiron ruled the game incomplete."

"I heard that too."

He watched the other campers filing toward the tables, still arguing about which team had been ahead when the horn sounded.

"Sorry I ruined your victory."

"You didn't. I ruined it too." She shifted her knee carefully. "I could have taken the flag and run. I stopped because I wanted to see what you'd say."

"You wanted to argue."

"Naturally."

He laughed. She looked toward the banners Chiron had hung to dry.

"Friday, no favors?" she asked.

"No favors."

"And no dramatic water curtains?"

"That seems unreasonable. Chiron said no water screens, though, so I suppose I'll comply."

"Then I'll have to beat you on foot."

"You're welcome to try."

They stayed on opposite sides of the table when the food arrived, one with each team, and exchanged a look over the plates that wasn't an agreement to cheat. They were allowed to be glad to see each other and still owe their teammates a fair match.

The jail was empty. The flags were abandoned (the blue flag ended up tied around Nico's shoulders like a cape; the gray Zeus flag was last seen being used by Ella as a picnic blanket). Both teams converged on the dining pavilion in a muddy, laughing, starving mob, exactly as Chiron had clearly intended.

Pizza night at Camp Half-Blood was sacred. The harpies served slice after slice — pepperoni, veggie, ambrosia-drizzled dessert pizza that made Grover weep openly — and for a while there was no Zeus or Poseidon, no rivalry, just kids being kids.

Percy ended up on the pavilion steps with a plate stacked dangerously high, Annabeth beside him — not touching, because Thalia was three tables away watching like a hawk with extra lightning — but close enough that their knees almost brushed. Close enough.

"So," Annabeth said, stealing a pepperoni off his plate. "Tactical falling."

"Tactical slipping," Percy corrected, stealing it back. "I was brilliant. You said so. In the hollow. You said 'brilliant.'"

"I said 'cheating,'" Annabeth said.

"You thought 'brilliant,'" Percy said. "I could tell. I can always tell."

Annabeth bumped his shoulder with hers, quick and covert. "You left the gap low-left on purpose."

"You went for the gap low-left on purpose," Percy said. "We're disgustingly predictable. To each other."

"To everyone, apparently," Annabeth said, glancing toward Thalia, who was deep in conversation with Clarisse but kept glancing over. "Thalia's going to interrogate me tonight. Clarisse is going to interrogate you. We're terrible at hiding this."

"We're twelve," Percy said. "We're supposed to be terrible at everything. It's called being authentic."

Annabeth laughed — the real laugh, the seventeen-year-old laugh that crinkled her eyes and made his heart do stupid backflips. "Authentic. Sure."

Nearby, Grover was delivering his closing commentary to anyone who'd listen: "And so ends the greatest Capture the Flag in recent memory! Final score: Poseidon-ish, Zeus-ish, Pizza: victorious! Ella, final thoughts?"

Ella, draped over a railing with pizza in each talon and sauce on her beak, declared: "ELLA'S FINAL THOUGHT IS THAT WAR IS TEMPORARY BUT SNACKS ARE FOREVER! AND THAT THE SEA BOY AND THE SKY GIRL SHOULD SIT TOGETHER AT DINNER MORE OFTEN! TOGETHERNESS NEEDS GARLIC KNOTS!"

Half the pavilion cheered. Percy felt his face ignite. Annabeth hid behind her pizza slice.

Silena flopped down on Percy's other side, muddy and radiant. "You owe me a cookie," she told him. "For the bow-soaking incident. Also, you're welcome for not telling Clarisse about the gray cloth."

Percy's hand flew to his hair — to the thin strip of gray fabric braided behind his ear, cut from the hem of Annabeth's old camp shirt. He'd worn it since Annabeth gave it to him at camp. He thought nobody had noticed.

Silena winked. "Sisters notice everything, little brother. Don't worry. Your very obvious crush is safe with me. Mostly. Until I need blackmail."

Clarisse dropped onto the steps with them, plate in hand, and ruffled Percy's wet hair hard enough to nearly knock him over. "Good game, squirt," she said gruffly. "You held the creek like a real Poseidon. Dad'd be proud."

Percy beamed. "Thanks, Clarisse."

"But if you make googly eyes at a Zeus girl during battle again, I'm throwing you in the lake," Clarisse added.

"Noted," Percy said.

Across the pavilion, Thalia slid onto the bench beside Annabeth — displacing two terrified Ares kids without even looking — and slung an arm around Annabeth's shoulders. "Good game, bookworm," she said loudly, for everyone's benefit. Then, quieter, just for Annabeth: "You scared me out there. Don't do that."

Annabeth leaned into her, brief and genuine. "Sorry. Thanks for having my back."

"Always," Thalia said fiercely. Then, even quieter: "And hey — if you and Jackson want different teams next time, tell me before I draw up the plan, okay?"

Annabeth's throat tightened. She nodded, unable to speak.

Thalia stood, crackling faintly, and raised her pizza slice to the Poseidon side. "HEY! Fish faces! Good game!"

Clarisse stood, raising hers back. "Good game, spark plugs!"

"REMATCH NEXT WEEK!" Nico screamed, blue flag-cape billowing as he ran laps around the pavilion. "I'LL BE READY! I'VE BEEN PRACTICING MY SNEAKING!"

"NO SHADOW-TRAVELING TO THE FLAG!" Chiron called from the head table, without looking up from his pizza.

"No promises!" Nico called back.

Zoe, passing with her own plate, paused beside Percy and Annabeth. She looked at them — really looked, silver eyes ancient and knowing — and said softly, "Guard thy hearts well, young ones. And guard each other. The world is kinder to those who face it together."

Then she walked on, leaving them staring after her.

Grover trotted up, notebook full, cheese in his hair. "Okay, official post-game interview! Percy, Annabeth — any comments on the alleged cross-team coordination, the hand-holding flag-grab, and Ella's claim that you're star-crossed?"

Percy and Annabeth looked at each other. A whole conversation passed in a glance — *say nothing, smile, deny everything, pizza* — the language without words, alive and well at twelve.

"No comment," they said in unison. Then burst out laughing.

"That's going in the notes!" Grover said, scribbling furiously. "NO COMMENT (IN UNISON) (SUSPICIOUS) (ADORABLE)!"

Ella landed on the table, scattering napkins. "Ella declares this game a draw! A draw means everyone wins! Winning means dessert pizza! Dessert pizza for the secret sweethearts! Ella brought extra!"

She dropped an entire dessert pizza slice onto Percy's plate and one onto Annabeth's, beaming.

At sunset Grover announced the final score a third time, adding enough commentary that Clarisse threw a roll at him. He caught it, took a bite, and declared himself the real winner.

Percy sat beside Annabeth on the pavilion steps. Their knees touched. On the other side of the table Silena was already debating a rematch, and Thalia was arguing that the rule about taking prisoners needed to be rewritten.

Annabeth leaned closer. "We should've won."

"Which team?"

"Exactly."
