## Chapter 143: The Dock Repair

Percy Jackson woke to the smell of broken wood and lake mud, and for half a heartbeat he thought the storm was still happening.

Rain was gone. That was the first thing his body understood before his eyes were even open. No drumming on the shell windows of Cabin Three, no wind pulling at the eaves, no low pressure sitting on his teeth the way it had for two days straight. Instead there was a flat, washed-out quiet, the kind that comes after weather has taken what it wanted and left. Gulls cried thin over the canoe lake. Somewhere down the hill a screen door banged. And underneath it all, threaded through the salt and pine that always lived in the cabin walls, was the sharp green smell of snapped lumber and turned-up silt.

He sat up. His bunk creaked. Across the room Clarisse La Rue was already standing at the window in an oversized gray camp tee and flannel sleep shorts, bare feet flat on the cool floorboards, dark hair sticking up in a storm of its own, staring toward the lake with her hands on her hips.

"Don't," Silena Beauregard mumbled from the top bunk without opening her eyes. She wore a faded blue pajama tee with a peeling kraken on the front and soft cotton shorts, one leg hitched over her blanket, dark curls spilling over her pillow and catching the morning light. "Whatever you're about to say, Clarisse. Don't say it dramatic before coffee."

"It's bad," Clarisse said anyway, dramatic.

Percy swung his legs over the side of his bunk. His own tee, a sun-bleached orange Camp Half-Blood shirt gone dusty at the collar, stuck to his back where he'd sweated in the night. His navy sleep shorts were twisted. The floorboards were cold under his bare feet, gritty with tracked-in sand from yesterday's storm watch. He padded to the window and leaned around Clarisse's shoulder.

Through the pines, past the volleyball pit and the trail that switchbacked down to the canoe lake, he could see it. Or rather, he could see where it wasn't.

The dock was wrong.

Normally from Cabin Three's high window the dock made a clean brown line straight out into the glass-green water, with the little T at the end where the canoes tied up and where he and Annabeth liked to sit with their feet dangling. This morning the line was broken. The middle sagged sideways. Two deck boards stuck up at angles like bad teeth. One whole piling at the far end leaned, and the bench at the T was gone entirely, probably in the reeds. A pale splintered scar showed where the stringer had split.

"Oh, no," Percy said.

"Oh, yes," Clarisse said grimly. "Storm ate it."

Silena rolled over, blanket sliding, and peered down with one eye open. Her voice was still thick with sleep. "Tell me it's just fog. Lie to me. I'm eldest-ish, I deserve lies before eight."

"It's the dock," Percy told her. "Middle's caved, end's leaning. Bench is missing."

Silena groaned and sat up, swinging her bare feet over the ladder. Her toenails were still painted sea-green from last week, chipped now. "That bench was my favorite thinking bench. I named it. That was Sir Sitsalot."

"You name everything," Clarisse said.

"It's called affection, you hammer-handed goblin," Silena said, climbing down. Her tee rode up, showing a stripe of sun-warmed stomach. She shoved Percy lightly with her hip to get window space, shoulder to shoulder to shoulder with her siblings, three heads in a row. Her hair smelled like coconut shampoo and rain. Clarisse smelled like iron and sweat even half-asleep. Percy smelled like salt. Together they smelled like Cabin Three. "Ugh. That's not a fix-it-with-tape situation. That's a rebuild situation."

Percy loved that dock in the ordinary way.

"We have to fix it today," Percy said. "Before the wood warps in the sun."

"We have to fix it before Beckendorf sees it and cries," Clarisse said. "He loves that dock. He sistered that stringer himself two summers ago."

Silena was already pulling her rolled jeans from the footlocker, and a dusty work tee. "Bathing suits under, work clothes over. Gloves if we have them. Annabeth's going to want to design. Thalia's going to want to hit things. Grover's going to worry. Ella's going to document. Tyson's going to carry half the forest. It's a whole thing. We need breakfast first or we'll die."

"Seconded," Percy said.

Clarisse looked back at the lake, jaw set. Her gray tee stretched across her shoulders where she'd slept on it wrong. Her bare feet left faint dusty prints on the floorboards as she turned. "Nobody dies before lunch. Rule one. I'm making eggs."

---

The dining pavilion smelled like bacon grease and wet canvas and lake wind coming up the hill, and the whole camp buzzed with post-storm damage reports.

Percy slid onto the Poseidon bench with his plate, already dressed for work whether the day liked it or not. He wore a dusty faded orange tee gone soft at the seams, with a dark sweat stain already starting at the collar from the humid walk over, and his favorite rolled jeans, cuffs turned up twice to show his bare ankles and his battered bare feet in flip-flops he would ditch the second they got to the lake. A faded blue sweatband was pushed up around his forehead to keep his damp black hair back, and his leather work gloves stuck out of his back pocket, fingers flopping. His arms were bare and brown from the summer, salt crusted at his elbows from yesterday's rain.

Across from him Clarisse demolished toast with her elbows wide. She had changed into a charcoal-gray work tee, dusty across the chest and sleeves from where she'd already been digging in the shed for nails, the sleeves cut ragged at the shoulder to give her arms room. Her rolled jeans were dark wash, cuffs thick and uneven, one higher than the other, and she wore thick socks with her work boots unlaced for now, her scuffed work gloves tucked through her belt loop. A red sweatband held her short dark hair back from her face, already damp at the temples. Sawdust clung to her forearm where she'd leaned on a plank.

Silena sat sideways on the bench, legs tucked under, with her notebook and a pencil. She wore a sun-bleached teal tee with the sleeves rolled to show her shoulders, a faint dusting of white paint on the hem from the mural project still not washed out, and light-wash rolled jeans with the cuffs folded neat and even, showing her bare feet in canvas slip-ons. Her sea-green cord bracelet was knotted around her wrist, her work gloves folded precise in her lap, and a thin white sweatband held her dark curls in a high puff, a few strands escaping to stick to her sunscreened neck. She smelled like oranges and cedar from the Aphrodite cabin visit to borrow extra tape.

Annabeth Grace arrived with her own plate and dropped onto the bench beside Percy so close their knees knocked, and stayed there. She wore a dusty gray work tee borrowed from the Athena cabin, with a small owl stamped over the heart, the collar stretched from being pulled on fast, and sturdy rolled jeans, cuffs pinned with a single stitch so they would stay even in water, her bare feet in old water sandals she could kick off to wade. A navy sweatband striped with white pushed her blonde ponytail high, flyaways already frizzing in the humidity, and her brown leather work gloves were tied together by the fingers and slung around her neck like a necklace. Her gray eyes were bright, pencil already behind her ear, notebook already open to a blank page. She smelled like graphite and sunscreen and lake air.

"You saw?" she said without preamble, nudging Percy's ankle under the table with her sandal. Rubber squeaked on stone.

"Saw," Percy said. "Middle's dropped, end's leaning, Sir Sitsalot is gone."

Thalia Grace swung in from the Zeus table with her plate held over her head to get through the crowd, and plopped down on Percy's other side, crowding him. She wore a black Metallica tee gone dusty gray at the shoulders from attic storage, the sleeves ripped off entirely to show her pale, freckled arms and the silver bracelet at her wrist, and black rolled jeans with the cuffs sloppy and frayed, one dragging, her bare feet in battered combat boots with the laces loose. A black sweatband with a lightning bolt stitched in silver held her spiky black hair back, the blue streak in it bright even in pavilion shade, and her work gloves, fingerless and cracked, stuck out of her back pocket. She smelled like ozone and pine sap and bacon.

"Sir what?" Thalia said around a mouthful of egg.

"Bench," Silena said mournfully. "We named him. He's probably in the cattails communing with frogs."

Thalia snorted. Static lifted the fine hairs on her forearm and made Percy's sweatband tickle. "Lake Summer just started and already we're doing manual labor? I was promised lounging. I was promised Annabeth doing math while I napped."

"You can nap on a plank and call it supervising," Annabeth said, flipping her notebook. Her knee pressed harder against Percy's, warm through denim. "I looked from the trail. It's not total. Two bents failed where the stringer split. Probably rot plus lateral load from waves. If the mud didn't take the footings, we can reset two posts, sister the stringer, redeck six to eight boards, re-rail the T. One day if we have hands."

"We have hands," Clarisse said. "We have idiot brother hands." She jerked her chin at Percy. "Noodle arms, but enthusiastic."

Percy choked on orange juice. Tang burned his nose. "Excuse me? Noodle arms? I held a river last summer."

"You held a river with your face all scrunched up like a pug," Clarisse said, utterly deadpan, stealing his bacon. "I saw. Silena, back me up. Pug face."

Silena tapped her pencil against her teeth, eyes dancing. "Confirmed. Extreme pug. Though to be fair, you have grown. Last year those arms couldn't haul a paddle. Now they can haul maybe a small paddle. A child-sized paddle."

"Betrayed by my own blood," Percy said, pressing a hand to his dusty tee over his heart. His sweatband slipped. Annabeth pushed it back up for him without looking, fingers brief and warm on his forehead, and his ears went hot. "Annie, defend me. Tell them I have perfectly adequate arms."

Annabeth examined him with exaggerated seriousness, head tilted, pencil tapping. Her sandal hooked around his ankle under the table, steadying. "Hmm. Assessment. Biceps present. Triceps rumored. Lats theoretical. Verdict: noodle-adjacent. But wiry noodles. Al dente."

The whole bench erupted. Thalia howled and slapped the table so hard her plate jumped. Grover, arriving with his own tray and smelling like wet wool and alfalfa, laughed so hard his horns shook. Even Tyson, back from the undersea forges on a repair-day visit approved by Chiron, ducking under the pavilion eave with a tray stacked with six peanut-butter sandwiches, boomed with laughter that shook the marble.

"AL DENTE!" Tyson shouted, delighted. His single eye shone. He wore a giant dusty white work tee that strained across his huge shoulders, Camp Half-Blood letters stretched wide, and enormous rolled jeans cut off at the knee because no full length fit him, his huge bare feet slapping stone in flip-flops made from tire scraps. A yellow sweatband, tiny on his broad forehead, held back his curly brown hair, and his work gloves were the size of dinner hams, tucked in his pocket. Peanut butter stuck to his thumb. "BROTHER IS PASTA! GOOD PASTA!"

"I hate all of you," Percy said, grinning so wide his cheeks hurt. "I'm eating my eggs in hostile territory."

"You love us," Silena said, patting his dusty sleeve and leaving a floury handprint. "Who else would tell you truthfully about your pasta status? Now eat. Protein. You're going to need it when Clarisse makes you carry lumber and cries."

"I don't cry," Clarisse said.

"You cry when Beckendorf welds beautifully," Silena said.

"That's different. That's art."

Grover squeezed in beside Silena in his yellow work tee and rolled jeans over furry legs, green sweatband between horns. "Naiads are upset, silt everywhere. Ella's at the lake documenting."

Ella swooped in, rose tee and shorts, blue sweatband, satchel of tablets. "Ella recorded! Splinter forty-one inches! Lean twelve degrees! Do not fix before before-pictures!"

"We would never," Annabeth said solemnly, reaching to steady Ella's satchel before it tipped into the oatmeal. Her work glove necklace swung. "Rule one: Ella documents. Rule two: Percy doesn't drown. Rule three: Thalia doesn't hammer her own thumb."

"Hey!" Thalia protested. "That was one time. With a rock. And I was nine."

"You bent three nails last week on the passage stairs," Annabeth said sweetly, bumping Thalia's dusty shoulder with her own. "I counted. I have a table."

Thalia pointed her fork at her sister. Static snapped. "You and your tables, baby. Keep it up and I'll hammer your notebook to the dock as bracing."

"You'd miss," Annabeth said. "You bend nails."

"I bend nails with style!"

Percy laughed into his juice, shoulders shaking, sweatband slipping again. Under the table Annabeth's sandal still hooked his ankle, Silena's slip-on pressed his other foot, Clarisse's boot knocked his shin hard enough to bruise when she reached for more bacon. Tyson patted his back hard enough to slosh juice. Grover's hoof tapped his foot in happy rhythm. Ella flapped and scattered crumbs. Thalia leaned her full weight against his arm, warm and crackling faintly, and stole his last bite of toast.

It was loud and dusty and already sweaty, and the lake glinted through the trees below, broken and waiting.

"Okay," Percy said, standing with his tray, dusty tee sticking to his spine. "Let's go fix our dock."

---

The canoe lake at nine in the morning smelled like summer trying to remember itself after a storm.

Percy noticed mud and pine and lake silt, cloudy and tired.

Light came down in white-gold shafts through dripping leaves, catching mist still rising off the water. Dragonflies ticked over the reeds. A red-winged blackbird scolded from the cattails where, sure enough, something brown and bench-shaped lurked sideways in the mud.

"Sir Sitsalot!" Silena cried, pointing with her tape measure. Her slip-ons were already off, bare feet sinking into cool mud with a soft sucking sound. Her rolled jeans, light-wash and neat, were already spattered at the cuffs. Her teal dusty tee clung at the back where she'd sweated through on the walk down. Her white sweatband was damp. "He lives! He's just muddy and traumatized!"

"He's furniture, not a soldier," Clarisse said, but she sounded relieved. She'd laced her work boots now, heavy tread caking with mud, her charcoal dusty tee darkening with sweat between her shoulder blades, her red sweatband already soaked. Her work gloves were on, leather creaking as she flexed. "Leave him. We'll fish him later. Damage first."

Annabeth was already on her knees at the shore end of the dock, water sandals kicked off, bare feet tucked under her, rolled jeans damp at the pinned cuffs where spray kissed them. Her dusty gray Athena tee pulled tight across her back as she leaned, ponytail swinging, navy sweatband bright against her hair. Her brown work gloves were on now, fingers a little long, pencil still behind her ear even with gloves. Her notebook lay open on a dry plank, weighted with a rock, pages fluttering. She smelled like sun-warmed cotton and graphite when Percy crouched beside her.

Percy ditched his flip-flops in the grass and rolled his jeans one more turn, high over his calves, showing his bare feet and ankles, already muddy. His dusty orange tee stuck to his shoulders. His blue sweatband dripped. His leather work gloves felt stiff and hot as he pulled them on. Lake mud squelched between his toes, cool and silky, pebbles sharp here and there. He could feel the lake tugging at his senses even through the silt, restless and murky, like a kid with a scraped knee.

"Okay," Annabeth said, tapping her sketch. Ink shone wet. "Look. Here's what I think. Don't touch yet, Ella needs photos, but look with eyes."

They crowded around as Annabeth showed profile, waterline, X marks, split stringer.

"North bent held," she said, pointing with a gloved finger. "South bent racked twelve degrees, Ella's right. That's why the T leans. Middle stringer split along grain for about four feet where rot had started. Beckendorf's sister is fine, it's the original timber next to it that went. Deck boards popped where the frame twisted. Railing on the east side tore out. Footings?" She looked at Percy. "Need eyes down there. You're eyes."

Percy felt the familiar lift in his chest, part pride, part nerves. Underwater was his. Even silty, even after a storm. "I can dive it. Water's gross but I can see."

"You can feel," Silena corrected gently, squeezing his shoulder through dusty cotton. Her bare foot nudged his in the mud, sisterly. "Don't show off. Just tell us if the mud ate the posts or if they're still set."

"And don't come up with a fish in your mouth again," Clarisse said. "That was disgusting. You looked like a seal."

"That was one time! I was twelve! The fish was already dead!"

"You chewed," Clarisse said flatly. "I saw chew."

Thalia cackled, slapping Percy's back. Her fingerless glove rasped on his tee. "Seal Boy! That's your new name! Goodbye Seaweed Brain, hello Seal Boy!"

"I'm not—" Percy started, then saw Annabeth's shoulders shaking, her ponytail bouncing, her gray eyes crinkled with laughter behind her sweatband. "Annie, not you too."

Annabeth bit her lip, trying for professional. Failed. "Sorry. Data suggests seal-like behavior. I have to record." She wrote SEAL BOY in the margin and underlined it twice. Her bare toes curled in the mud, happy.

"Betrayed again," Percy sighed, but he was grinning. He pulled his sweatband off and tossed it to Silena. His hair fell damp over his forehead. "Hold that. And my gloves. And my dignity. I'm going in."

He stripped his dusty orange tee over his head in one pull, showing his sun-browned shoulders and the lean lines of a kid who'd grown an inch over spring and hadn't quite filled it yet. Lake air kissed his skin, cool after humidity. He stood in just his rolled jeans, cuffs high, bare feet muddy, chest bare and already beading with sweat. Clarisse whistled low.

"Look at that. Noodles with nipples," she said fondly.

"Clarisse!" Silena gasped, delighted and scandalized, covering Ella's ears. Ella shook her off, scribbling.

"What? I'm appreciating his growth as an athlete. He's gone from spaghetti to linguine. It's progress." Clarisse flexed her own arm, charcoal sleeve riding up to show her solid bicep, dusty and sun-freckled. "Compare to gun show. This is what hauling lumber does. You should try it sometime, Pasta."

"I haul lumber!" Percy protested, tee in his hands. "I haul plenty!"

"You haul dramatically and then ask for snacks," Thalia said, leaning back on her hands, boots sprawled, black tee gaping at the ripped sleeves to show her sports bra strap. "I watched you carry a canoe last week. You made Wheeze Noises."

"I made Effort Sounds! There's a difference!"

Annabeth stood and took his tee, folding it with quick, fond hands. Her dusty gray tee brushed his bare arm, cotton soft and warm. Her bare foot stepped on his in the mud, toes curling, grounding. "Go on, Seal Boy," she said softly, eyes bright, teasing and proud at once. "Show us the footings. Then we'll let you carry something heavy to prove you're not pasta."

"Fine," Percy said, stepping to the dock edge in his rolled jeans, bare feet gripping slick wood. "But when I come up heroic and muddy, I want it noted."

"Noted," Ella said seriously, stylus poised over fresh blue wax. "Ella notes: brother goes in. Noodle status pending review."

Percy dove.

---

Lake water closed over Percy's head with a soft, silty rush, cool and green-brown and full of floating bits, and his whole body exhaled.

Below, light filtered in murky columns. Percy checked the north posts, solid, then the middle bent, one post loose in soupy mud, then the south bent, racked but set.

He surfaced under the T with a gasp, shaking water from his eyes, gripping a piling with slick hands. Sun hit his wet shoulders, warm instantly. Water streamed from his hair down his back, beading on sun-browned skin. His rolled jeans clung heavy to his legs, cuffs dripping. His bare feet found the cross-brace underwater and hooked there.

"Report!" Annabeth called from shore, on her knees, notebook up, pencil ready, sweatband bright. Her dusty tee glowed in sun. Behind her Clarisse shaded her eyes, Silena held his sweatband and tee like offerings, Thalia leaned forward with hands cupped around her mouth, Grover's ears pricked, Ella's stylus hovered, Tyson waved hugely.

"North is good!" Percy shouted, treading, voice echoing off water. Drops flew when he shook his head. "Mud's firm! Middle east post is loose, hole's soup, needs reset and pack! Middle west is leaning but set! South both set but frame racked, bolts pulled! Footings are there! We don't need new pilings! Just reset one and straighten!"

Annabeth's whole face lit up, ponytail bouncing as she scribbled furiously, bare feet kicking in mud. "Yes! That's what I hoped! No new piles! We can do this in a day!"

"Seal Boy delivers!" Thalia yelled, cupping hands. "Did you eat a fish while you were down there? Be honest!"

"I did not eat a fish!" Percy yelled back, laughing, water lapping his chin. "But I saw Sir Sitsalot! He's in the reeds ten feet out, stuck! Tell Tyson!"

Tyson boomed with joy, yellow sweatband flashing. "TYSON WILL RESCUE SIR SITSALOT! HOLD ON, BENCH!"

Clarisse waded in to her boot tops to offer a hand, charcoal tee stretching as she reached, red sweatband dark with sweat. Her work gloves gripped his slippery wrist. "Come on, Linguine. Out. Time to earn your pasta."

Percy let her haul him up onto the broken dock, bare feet slipping on algae-slick wood, rolled jeans streaming. She pulled hard, bicep bulging under dusty sleeve, and he came up dripping and laughing, water sheeting off his bare chest and soaking his denim. She steadied him with a hand flat on his back, warm through cold wet skin, sister-solid.

"Good eyes," she said quietly, teasing dropped for a second. Her dark eyes were proud. "Real good."

"Thanks," Percy said, breathless, grinning. Droplets hung on his lashes. "Now can I have my shirt? I'm heroic and chilly."

Silena arrived with his tee and sweatband, bare feet light on the planks, teal tee fluttering. She tossed the sweatband around his neck like a towel and helped him pull the dusty orange tee back over wet skin, cotton sticking and clinging to his shoulders, turning darker with damp. She smoothed it down over his back with both hands, sisterly and brisk, then ruffled his wet hair. Lake water and coconut and sun.

"There," she said. "Heroic and damp. Now stay out of the water until we need you to set posts. You'll wrinkle."

Annabeth came down the dock carefully, testing each board with her bare foot before trusting weight, notebook tucked under her arm. Her gray dusty tee was damp at the hem from spray. Her pinned jean cuffs showed her muddy ankles. Her navy sweatband held. She stopped in front of Percy, looked up at his dripping hair and wet tee clinging to his collarbones, and smiled so wide it crinkled her eyes.

"Perfect report," she said. "Just what I needed." She reached up and picked a weed from his shoulder, fingers lingering a second on wet cotton. Her work gloves were tucked in her pocket for now, hands bare and warm. "You were right about the mud."

"I know my mud," Percy said solemnly.

"Seal Boy knows his mud!" Thalia announced to the lake at large, arms spread, black tee flapping. "Carve it on the dock!"

Ella carved it in wax instead, muttering, "Ella records: footings good. Seal Boy accurate. No fish eaten. Noodle status under review. Ella draws turtle witness."

Grover clapped his hooves on the planks, yellow tee bright. "That's great news! No new piles means no big auger, means we don't disturb the naiads' shelf! Juniper will be so relieved! I'll tell the reeds we're staying shallow!"

"Tell them after you carry something," Clarisse said, already heading for shore, boots thudding on half-sound boards. "Lumber won't haul itself. Come on, Pasta. Time for gun show versus noodle show."

---

The lumber pile behind the canoe shed smelled like hot pine and sawdust and old oil, and by ten-thirty the sun had turned it into an oven.

Percy stood in the dusty yard in his damp orange tee now drying stiff with lake salt, sleeves pushed up to his shoulders, rolled jeans still damp at the cuffs and spattered with mud, bare feet dusty now that the mud had dried to a pale film, blue sweatband back on his forehead and soaked through. His leather work gloves were back on, palms sweating inside. Sawdust floated gold in the air, tickling his nose. A bead of sweat ran from his temple down his jaw and soaked into his collar. His arms already ached from carrying two deck boards up the trail.

Clarisse stood beside the rack like a general, charcoal dusty tee dark with sweat in a V down her back, sleeves cut ragged showing her strong brown arms gleaming with sweat and dusted with sawdust that stuck to her sunscreen. Her rolled jeans, dark wash and uneven cuffs, were pale with dust at the knees. Her work boots were caked, red sweatband wrung out and retied. Her leather work gloves were scuffed white at the palms. She hefted a twelve-foot two-by-eight onto her shoulder in one smooth motion, wood thudding against muscle, and didn't even grunt.

"Okay," she said, turning to Percy with the plank balanced effortlessly. Sawdust sifted onto her boot. "Watch and learn, Al Dente. Bend knees, not back. Grip wide. Up in one go. Like this." She strode up the trail, plank steady, boots crunching pine needles, calling back without turning. "Try not to cry!"

"I don't cry carrying wood!" Percy shouted after her, grabbing his own plank. It was heavier than it looked, rough against his gloves, splinters catching. Pine smell sharp in his nose. He bent, lifted. His shoulders screamed. His damp tee pulled across his back. His bare feet slipped in dry dust. "Ungh!"

Silena appeared at his elbow, teal dusty tee tied up in a knot at her waist now to keep it out of sawdust, showing a stripe of sun-warmed brown skin at her midriff. Her light-wash rolled jeans were dusty too, neat cuffs now smudged. Her bare feet were filthy in the good way, soles brown. Her white sweatband was gray with dust. Her work gloves, smaller and neater, were on, tape measure clipped to her pocket. She smelled like sawdust and oranges.

"Tip," she said sweetly, sliding her hands under his plank near the middle to take half the weight without asking. Her shoulder pressed his arm through dusty cotton, steadying. "Don't lift with your ego. Lift with your legs. And breathe out on the lift or you'll turn purple, and Annabeth will document Purple Percy and you'll never live it down."

"I'm not purple," Percy wheezed, legs shaking, sweat running into his eyes and stinging under his sweatband. Sawdust stuck to his wet forearms. "I'm... exerting."

"You're puce," Thalia said, passing them with a post on her shoulder, black ripped tee sticking to her back, black sweatband dark, fingerless gloves gripping. Her combat boots kicked dust up onto Percy's bare feet. Her bare knees flashed where her frayed jeans rode up. Static made sawdust cling to her arms in a fuzzy halo. "Puce Percy! New name! I'm collecting them today!"

"Collect nails instead!" Annabeth called from the shed door, where she measured and marked with a carpenter's pencil. Her gray dusty tee had a dark sweat patch between her shoulder blades. Her pinned rolled jeans were dusty at the thighs where she'd knelt on planks. Her bare feet were gray with dust. Her navy sweatband was soaked. Her work gloves were off for marking, hands bare and smudged with graphite. Sawdust stuck to her sunscreened forearms and glowed. "Thalia, that post goes to the T, not the middle! Read the mark! I marked it twice!"

Thalia squinted at the chalk mark, then at Annabeth, then at the post. "This is the T post? This stubby thing?"

"It's not stubby, it's cut to length! I measured twice! Silena confirmed!"

Silena nodded, patting Percy's plank. "Confirmed. Thalia, you're carrying precisely forty-eight inches of perfection. Don't insult it."

Thalia kissed the post loudly. "Sorry, perfect post."

Tyson carried four planks at once, two under each massive arm, like toothpicks. His giant white dusty tee was dark with sweat in huge patches, stretched tight. His cut-off rolled jeans showed his massive calves, huge bare feet leaving deep prints in dust. His tiny yellow sweatband had disappeared into his hair. His huge gloves hung from his teeth while he carried. Sawdust powdered his beard. "TYSON IS LUMBER MOUNTAIN! BROTHER IS LUMBER HILL! SMALL HILL! CUTE HILL!"

"Thanks, big guy," Percy panted, staggering under his single plank with Silena's help. His arms trembled, noodle status confirmed. Sweat dripped off his nose onto pine. His gloves slipped. "I'll... be a mountain... someday..."

"You are my hill," Tyson said earnestly, beaming. "Best hill."

Ella flapped overhead with a coil of rope, rose dusty tee flapping, shorts flapping, bare scaly feet dangling, blue sweatband bright. "Ella records lumber carry! Clarisse carries one plank easy! Tyson carries four! Thalia carries post and insults it! Percy carries one plank with sister assist! Sister assist noted! Noodle theory supported!"

"Traitor harpy!" Percy gasped, laughing despite his burning shoulders. Sawdust in his teeth. "I thought... we were friends..."

"Ella is scientist! Science has no friends! Only data!" Ella scribbled mid-air and nearly flew into a pine.

Annabeth met them at the lake end and they dropped the plank. Clarisse dropped hers beside his.

"Not bad, Linguine," she said, punching his dusty shoulder lightly. Her glove was off now, knuckles warm and rough. "You only whimpered twice. Last year you whimpered three times carrying a paddle."

"Growth," Silena said, handing Percy a canteen. Her teal tee stuck to her shoulders. Her white sweatband left a pale line on her forehead. Her bare feet shifted in mud, toes curling. "Our baby's becoming al dente. Soon he'll be rigatoni. Firm. With ridges."

Percy drank, water cool and metallic, running down his chin onto his dusty tee. "I hate this family."

"You love this family," all three girls chorused, plus Thalia from the dock where she hammered a temporary brace, plus Tyson's boom, plus Grover's bleat, plus Ella's squawk.

Percy looked at Annabeth, who stood so close their dusty sleeves touched, her bare foot stepped on his in the mud to keep him steady, her eyes laughing up at him through sweat-damp lashes.

"Yeah," he admitted, bumping her shoulder with his. "Yeah, I do."

---

By eleven the sun stood straight over the lake, white and hammering, and the work settled into rhythm the way rowing settles, painful then smooth.

Percy stood knee-deep in lake water beside the middle bent in just his rolled jeans pushed high over his thighs now, cuffs dark and heavy, bare feet sunk in soft silt that oozed cool between his toes, bare chest gleaming with sweat and lake water, blue sweatband tied around his wrist now to keep it out of the dive. His dusty orange tee lay folded on the dock above with his gloves. Sun burned his shoulders, water cooled his legs. Silt clouded around his calves in soft puffs.
