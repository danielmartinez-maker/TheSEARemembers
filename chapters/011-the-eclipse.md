## Chapter 11: The Eclipse

Eclipse morning arrived clear and cold.

Percy woke before the conch. Clarisse had already pulled on her boots; Silena stood beside the door with a bundle of clean towels and the expression she wore when someone was about to make a preventable mess.

"Infirmary supplies," she told him.

"I didn't ask."

"You looked like you would."

He sat up. Outside, counselors called campers toward breakfast. The ordinary sounds had acquired a hurried edge: doors banging, a bucket rolling off a porch, somebody asking for the third time whether looking through colored glass was safe.

Chiron had answered that question the evening before. He was apparently going to answer it again.

Percy dressed and followed his sisters out.

Annabeth waited by the Zeus cabin with her jacket buttoned and her notebook tucked beneath one arm. Thalia was checking the knot on the cord used to close the path to the Hall.

"No gaps," she said.

"You checked it twice," Annabeth replied.

"Then it should survive a third."

Percy looked toward the building beyond the cabins. Its roof shone in the morning sun. Whatever lay beneath the mosaic had not announced itself yet.

---

Near noon, the light began to change.

Chiron gathered the camp at the dining pavilion. He had arranged the benches by cabin so the counselors could count everyone without losing a small camper behind a large one. Mr. D stood near the fire with his arms folded and told anyone who asked that an eclipse was no excuse to burn lunch.

Percy sat with Clarisse and Silena. Across the aisle, Annabeth kept one hand on her notebook and watched Thalia speak with Zoe by the steps.

They were supposed to remain at the pavilion.

The plan had been clear: Zoe and Chiron would inspect the ward from outside the Hall, and the children would stay out of reach.

Then a sound came from the far end of camp.

Not thunder. A high metallic note, sustained until the bowls on the table began to tremble.

The water in Percy's cup rose against one side.

He caught it before it spilled.

Annabeth stood. Thalia was already moving down the steps.

"Stay here," Chiron called.

Thalia stopped, but the cord on the Hall path jerked taut. One post tore sideways out of the ground.

A white line flashed across the building's doorway.

Zoe put her hand over the mark on her diagram. "It isn't discharging."

Percy heard her despite the ringing. "It's drawing in."

---

Chiron sent the campers toward the Big House under their counselors. Clarisse took charge of the nearest group without being asked, pointing two younger children toward Silena and ordering the others to follow.

"Jackson," she said. "Don't make me count you missing."

"I'll stay with Chiron."

Clarisse looked at him, then toward Silena, who was guiding three younger campers past a fallen post. "You hear that, Silena? If I have to come looking for him, he's carrying all the sandbags afterward."

"I'll write it on the chore board," Silena called back.

Percy raised his hands. "I'm standing right here."

"Then stay there," Clarisse said, and ran to help move the fallen post. 

He joined Annabeth at the foot of the pavilion steps. Zoe was explaining something to Chiron, her words nearly lost beneath the metallic whine.

"The drain is blocked," Annabeth said.

Percy followed her pencil toward the map. The mark beneath the northern column sat over the channel that should have carried water and excess charge outside.

The Hall's open doors slammed against their stops.

Thalia looked at the doorway, then at the children still crossing the path beyond it.

"Get them farther back."

She ran before Annabeth could catch her sleeve.

Chiron called her name.

Thalia lifted her spear as she reached the Hall. A bright sheet of static spread between the columns, catching a burst that would have struck the path. Her boots slid on the threshold.

For one ugly moment Percy saw her framed by light, arms raised above a mosaic marked with hidden spellwork.

Every suspicion he had tried to put down returned.

Annabeth gripped his wrist.

"She's holding it away from them," she said.

He looked again.

Thalia was not standing at the mark. She was leaning against the doorframe, trying to keep the flare inside.

"I see it," he said.

They ran with Chiron and Zoe behind them.

---

Inside, the air tasted of hot pennies. A ceremonial cloth had fallen from a column and caught over Thalia's shoulder; she shook it free without taking her hand from the frame.

"Back," she said when she saw Annabeth. "I can hold it."

Another surge rolled through the room. Her knee buckled.

"For how long?" Annabeth asked.

Thalia did not answer.

The mosaic's northern edge glowed white. Between the tiles, something dark moved against the direction of the light.

Percy took a step toward it.

"Don't cross the center," Zoe said.

He stopped.

Chiron braced the doors open while Zoe crouched beside the marked column. She held her lantern near the floor without touching the tiles.

"Added over the drain," she said. "A second ward covering the first."

"Can you interrupt it?" Chiron asked.

"Only if we first isolate the active coupling," Zoe said. She turned toward the doorway. "Mara's portable grounding frame is outside. We tested the frame on an independent training mark. We haven't tested it on this."

"Then we proceed as though it may fail," Chiron said.

Annabeth looked at the diagram in her hands. Percy knew that look. She had found a weakness in the plan and was working out whether there was enough time to explain it.

"Say what you're thinking," he told her.

"The drain is flooded, and the western floor is covered with runoff. If anyone tries to use that water as a discharge path, the charge could spread unpredictably. Percy can move the water *away* from the exposed coupling. The electrical team needs a dry working area and a separate tested route to the grounding frame."

Percy nodded. "I can clear the puddles. I can't promise where lightning goes."

"Neither can I," Annabeth said. She flexed her bandaged hand. "The healer hasn't cleared me for electrical work. I'll watch the boundary and call the stop if the mark spreads."

Thalia was still braced against the frame, trying to keep the flare inside the Hall. Her breath came in hard pulls.

"You haven't done this together," Chiron said.

"No," Annabeth replied. "We haven't. So we stay with what each of us can do safely and let Zoe direct the ward work."

"Good," Chiron said. "No improvising with electrical discharge. Move the campers back another boundary. Mara brings the frame to the outside threshold. I call the stop."

---

Percy knelt beside the broken fountain, careful not to cross the line where the floor had begun to shine. Water had collected along the western wall, spreading toward the mosaic in a thin sheet. If it reached the exposed metal, anybody standing in the room could be caught by an uncontrolled discharge.

He could do something about the water. He did not intend to ask it to carry lightning.

"How much space?" he called to Zoe.

"Clear the western channel and the doorway. Keep the center untouched."

He drew the puddle back from the bright line, gathering it against the basin of the old fountain. The movement was slower than he'd expected; some of the water had mixed with grit and fragments of tile, and the floor sloped the wrong way. He resisted the urge to pull harder. A sudden surge might send water across the very surface he was trying to dry.

Annabeth crouched behind Chiron with the inspection diagram on a board. She followed the luminous line from the northern column to the doorway, marking where it had changed since their first observation.

"The third hook is brightening," she warned. "If it reaches the threshold, the grounding frame will be too close to the active edge."

Mara and another forge worker brought the frame to the outside path. Its bronze contacts were fixed within an insulated stone casing, carried on a sled so nobody had to grab the live metal. Zoe went to them, checked the connections without touching the active ward, and asked for the team to confirm the nearest escape route.

Percy held the water in the basin. His arms ached, but the western path remained dry.

Thalia's spear shook. The light she'd been keeping from the doorway had begun to climb the inside of the frame. She was no longer deflecting a single flare. The Hall's hidden ward was feeding pressure into the same point again and again.

"I can't hold this much longer," she said.

"Then tell us when you need to release," Chiron answered. "You do not have to wait until you fall."

"I need to release now."

"Then back three paces. Let the screen take the next flare."

She obeyed, though it cost her. The first flare struck the protective screen Zoe had placed against the inside of the wall. It rang like an enormous bell and threw a spray of sparks harmlessly into the enclosed portion of the ward. The grounding frame outside shuddered but did not move.

"Stop," Chiron called.

They stopped. Percy let the water settle into the fountain basin without trying to force it farther. Zoe studied the movement of the mark, counting the seconds between pulses. Annabeth checked her drawing against the live boundary.

"It's still drawing pressure toward the north column," she said. "The screen is holding at the threshold, but the mark itself hasn't changed."

"Then we have a moment to choose," Zoe replied.

She looked at the control notes she'd prepared, then at the fresh readings Mara held up through the doorway. The old western drainage connection was separate from the ward coupling. The safe repair procedure called for isolating the coupling before releasing the pressure it contained. They had not been able to make that separation during the storm.

"Chiron, the secondary contact has settled within the test range," Zoe said. "I can disengage the exposed fitting with the insulated tool. If the pressure rises, we stop and retreat."

Chiron checked Mara's signal and nodded.

Zoe moved to the edge of the northern mosaic. She kept one hand on the long insulated handle of the tool, the other free to drop it if the current jumped. Percy held the western floor dry, doing nothing with the charge itself. Annabeth watched the line's progress and counted aloud whenever its edge shifted.

Zoe eased the tip beneath the first hooked stroke. The metal resisted. She adjusted her angle and released a narrow retaining pin.

A black thread rose from the seam, moving against the direction of the light.

It reached toward Annabeth.

"Back," she said, already retreating. She did not try to meet it with lightning.

Thalia moved as though she meant to interpose herself, then stopped when Chiron shouted. She raised her spear but remained behind the screen.

The thread struck the edge of Zoe's containment lantern and curled along its inner surface. It did not enter the room. Zoe kept her grip steady while Mara checked the grounding frame from outside.

"Hold," Zoe said. "The ward is transferring. Don't touch anything."

Annabeth's injured fingers tightened on the board. Percy could see the effort it took her not to reach for power she could not yet control. She pointed instead.

"The circle has a break at the eastern edge. That's the part that wasn't on the original survey."

Zoe examined it without moving the tool. "Chiron, I can lift this coupling away from the active line. I cannot tell you what else was attached to it before today."

"Do only what the inspection permits."

She worked the tool beneath the loose metal. The second retaining pin gave with a brittle click. Pressure escaped into the isolated frame in a series of short pulses, each one quieter than the last. The bright line around the northern column dimmed. When the third pulse passed, the black thread folded in on itself and vanished into the seam.

"Clear," Mara called from outside. "The frame is stable."

"Do not touch the floor," Chiron replied. "Wait for a second reading."

They waited. Percy could hear water settling against the fountain's basin and the rough sound of Thalia trying to get her breath back. Annabeth's hand was shaking, but she held the map where Zoe could read it.

A second reading came. Then a third. The luminous edge retreated toward the exposed stone beneath the column.

Only when Zoe had confirmed the line was no longer active did Chiron allow anyone to move beyond the outer threshold.

Percy let the water drain slowly into the clear western channel, far from the isolated ward. The effort left him dizzy, and he sat down before his legs made the decision for him. Thalia lowered her spear, took one step toward her sister and nearly stumbled. Annabeth went to her, keeping both of them outside the damaged mosaic.

They had interrupted the surge. They had not learned who had installed the coupling, what the black thread had been meant to restrain, or why the Oracle had been silent. The object on the floor was now evidence, not an answer.


---

Light returned in a gray wash.

The Hall had not disappeared. Its columns still stood, the northern mosaic cracked around a circle of exposed stone. Someone had torn the ceremonial cloth. Water ran in ordinary rivulets toward the drain.

Thalia slid down the wall.

Annabeth knelt beside her. Percy could see a red mark across Thalia's palm where the spear had burned her.

"You said you could hold it," Annabeth said.

"I could."

"You were falling."

"I was about to revise my estimate."

The joke came out thin. Annabeth held her sister's wrist carefully, keeping her fingers away from the burn.

"You told me not to improvise alone."

Thalia looked at their joined hands.

"I saw the children on the path."

"I know. I saw them too."

For a moment neither spoke.

"Next time," Thalia said, "I call for help before I decide I'm the help."

Annabeth nodded.

"I can't lose you."

"I'm here."

Percy turned away long enough to bring a dry towel from the doorway. Clarisse had delivered the bundle after the last children reached the Big House; she stood outside the cord now, waiting for Chiron's signal.

Thalia took the towel.

"You looked at me," she said to Percy, "like you thought I knew what was happening."

He could not pretend he had only been checking the light.

"I did."

Her face closed a little.

"For a second. Then I saw what you were doing."

"What did you think before that second?"

He set the other towels down.

"That you might have wanted something different badly enough to ask a god to change things. You don't remember the same history I do. I let that turn into an accusation in my head."

Annabeth went still beside her.

Thalia looked at him for a long time.

"I don't know the life you're talking about," she said. "I know my sister. I know I want her safe. Those are the things I can answer for."

"I was wrong to treat your fear for Annabeth as proof against you," he said.

Thalia folded the towel carefully over her burned palm. "You don't have to like me, Jackson. But don't tell yourself you know my reasons better than I do."

"I won't."

"You don't get to decide that once and expect me to trust you forever."

Percy nodded. "Fair."

She stood, and for a moment he thought she'd walk away. Instead she helped Annabeth up, keeping her uninjured arm around her sister's shoulders. Percy followed them to the infirmary. They had fought on the same side today. That didn't erase the conversation.

---

Chiron made them visit the infirmary before he let them discuss the floor.

Thalia's palm needed dressing. Percy had a shallow burn beside his wrist and muscles that shook whenever he reached for something. Annabeth complained of a headache, then stopped complaining when the healer told her that counting the symptoms aloud was useful.

Silena brought them water.

Clarisse stood at the doorway and counted.

"Three," she said. "Good."

"Four with you," Percy answered.

"I wasn't in the stupid building."

"Five with Silena."

"Drink your water."

Annabeth leaned against Thalia while the dressing was finished. She looked exhausted and unmistakably herself. There was no return of a missing romance, no new childhood suddenly waiting behind her eyes.

When Thalia asked whether she remembered a similar storm from when they were small, Annabeth shook her head.

"I don't," Annabeth said. "I know that's hard to hear."

Thalia looked down at the fresh dressing on her palm. "It is."

Silena pretended to be occupied with arranging the water cups. Clarisse stayed at the doorway.

"If you ever want me to tell you about it again," Thalia said, "I will. You used to ask for the story."

"Maybe tomorrow," Annabeth said.

"Tomorrow's fine."

They did not try to remember it together. Thalia stayed until the healer told her she could leave, and Annabeth waited for her.

---

They met by the lake that evening, after Chiron had assured them that the Hall was closed and the fragment from the drain was locked away.

Zoe had not identified its maker. Breaking the mark had interrupted the surge; it had not explained the altered records, the changed families, or their return to twelve.

Percy sat beside Annabeth at the end of the dock.

"Same hands?" he asked.

She turned hers over in her lap.

"Still sparks if I get careless. Still Zeus."

"Still Grace."

"Still me."

He nodded.

Behind them, Thalia sat on the shore with Clarisse and Silena. Somebody had brought food from the pavilion. They were arguing about whether a sandwich rescued from a closed lunch counted as dinner.

Annabeth listened to her sister laugh.

"She remembers things I don't," she said. "And I remember things she doesn't. That hasn't changed."

"No."

"I don't want to make her love prove which history is right."

Percy looked toward his sisters. Silena was trying to pass Clarisse a second sandwich. Clarisse was protesting while taking it.

"Me neither."

Annabeth rested her head against his shoulder. He put an arm around her and felt the small shift as she settled comfortably, without having to work out who he was.

The lake smelled of damp reeds and cooling pine. A fish rose near the pilings, leaving a circle that widened beneath their feet.

"Tomorrow," she said, "we sleep late."

"An ambitious plan."

"No ancient seals before breakfast."

"Could put that on the cabin door."

She smiled.

They stayed until Thalia called them to eat. Then Annabeth took his hand and pulled him up, careful of his sore wrist. The unknown part of their lives was still unknown. When they reached the others, Clarisse handed Percy one sandwich and informed him that he'd earned exactly half of her forgiveness for missing his assigned place during the evacuation. He offered to carry the empty food basket back to the pavilion. She gave him the heavier one instead.

Silena made room for Annabeth beside Thalia. Annabeth took the place, shoulder touching her sister's, and nobody made a speech about it. There were five of them eating late on the shore. The Hall would still need repairs in the morning.
