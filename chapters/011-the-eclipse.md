## Chapter 11: The Eclipse

Eclipse morning arrived clear and cold.

Percy woke before the conch. Clarisse had already pulled on her boots; Silena stood beside the door with a bundle of clean towels and the expression she wore when someone was about to make a preventable mess.

"Infirmary supplies," she told him.

"I didn't ask."

"You looked like you would."

He sat up. Outside, counselors called campers toward breakfast. The ordinary sounds had acquired a hurried edge: doors banging, a bucket rolling off a porch, somebody asking for the third time whether looking through colored glass was safe.

Chiron had answered that question the evening before. He was apparently going to answer it again.

Percy dressed and followed his sisters out.

Annabeth waited by the Zeus cabin with her jacket buttoned and her notebook tucked beneath one arm. Thalia was checking the knot on the cord used to close the path to the Hall.

"No gaps," she said.

"You checked it twice," Annabeth replied.

"Then it should survive a third."

Percy looked toward the building beyond the cabins. Its roof shone in the morning sun. Whatever lay beneath the mosaic had not announced itself yet.

---

Near noon, the light began to change.

Chiron gathered the camp at the dining pavilion. He had arranged the benches by cabin so the counselors could count everyone without losing a small camper behind a large one. Mr. D stood near the fire with his arms folded and told anyone who asked that an eclipse was no excuse to burn lunch.

Percy sat with Clarisse and Silena. Across the aisle, Annabeth kept one hand on her notebook and watched Thalia speak with Zoe by the steps.

They were supposed to remain at the pavilion.

The plan had been clear: Zoe and Chiron would inspect the ward from outside the Hall, and the children would stay out of reach.

Then a sound came from the far end of camp.

Not thunder. A high metallic note, sustained until the bowls on the table began to tremble.

The water in Percy's cup rose against one side.

He caught it before it spilled.

Annabeth stood. Thalia was already moving down the steps.

"Stay here," Chiron called.

Thalia stopped, but the cord on the Hall path jerked taut. One post tore sideways out of the ground.

A white line flashed across the building's doorway.

Zoe put her hand over the mark on her diagram. "It isn't discharging."

Percy heard her despite the ringing. "It's drawing in."

---

Chiron sent the campers toward the Big House under their counselors. Clarisse took charge of the nearest group without being asked, pointing two younger children toward Silena and ordering the others to follow.

"Jackson," she said. "Don't make me count you missing."

"I'll stay with Chiron."

"Do that."

He joined Annabeth at the foot of the pavilion steps. Zoe was explaining something to Chiron, her words nearly lost beneath the metallic whine.

"The drain is blocked," Annabeth said.

Percy followed her pencil toward the map. The mark beneath the northern column sat over the channel that should have carried water and excess charge outside.

The Hall's open doors slammed against their stops.

Thalia looked at the doorway, then at the children still crossing the path beyond it.

"Get them farther back."

She ran before Annabeth could catch her sleeve.

Chiron called her name.

Thalia lifted her spear as she reached the Hall. A bright sheet of static spread between the columns, catching a burst that would have struck the path. Her boots slid on the threshold.

For one ugly moment Percy saw her framed by light, arms raised above a mosaic marked with hidden spellwork.

Every suspicion he had tried to put down returned.

Annabeth gripped his wrist.

"She's holding it away from them," she said.

He looked again.

Thalia was not standing at the mark. She was leaning against the doorframe, trying to keep the flare inside.

"I see it," he said.

They ran with Chiron and Zoe behind them.

---

Inside, the air tasted of hot pennies. A ceremonial cloth had fallen from a column and caught over Thalia's shoulder; she shook it free without taking her hand from the frame.

"Back," she said when she saw Annabeth. "I can hold it."

Another surge rolled through the room. Her knee buckled.

"For how long?" Annabeth asked.

Thalia did not answer.

The mosaic's northern edge glowed white. Between the tiles, something dark moved against the direction of the light.

Percy took a step toward it.

"Don't cross the center," Zoe said.

He stopped.

Chiron braced the doors open while Zoe crouched beside the marked column. She held her lantern near the floor without touching the tiles.

"Added over the drain," she said. "A second ward covering the first."

"Can you break it?" Chiron asked.

"I can interrupt this part. I need the charge out of it first."

Annabeth looked at Percy. He knew that look. He had followed it into worse places, and sometimes persuaded it to choose a better route.

"Say the plan," he told her.

"Water through the open drain. Not across the center. Thalia releases toward it in short bursts. I help steer the edge, if I can."

"If you can't?"

"I stop."

Thalia's breath came in hard pulls.

"You haven't done this together."

"We haven't," Annabeth said. "That's why we're saying what we'll do before we do it."

Chiron nodded once.

"Small bursts. I call the stop."

---

Percy knelt near the broken fountain. Water had pooled along the western wall, reflecting an eclipse-dark sky through the open door.

He drew it into a narrow stream.

The stream slid beside the tiles rather than over them. Its first turn faltered when his hand shook; he took a breath and tried again, remembering the channels he had practised in Tempest Hall.

"There," Annabeth said.

Zoe eased a loose tile away from the drain with the tip of a bronze tool.

Cold air breathed through the gap.

Thalia turned her spear toward it.

"Ready?"

Percy nodded.

The first release struck too hard. Water burst against the wall, and pain flashed through Thalia's grip.

"Stop," Chiron said.

They stopped. The room still rang. It mattered that they had stopped anyway.

Annabeth crouched beside Percy.

"Less water," she said. "A thinner path. I was trying to push the whole edge at once."

"So was I."

They reset. Thalia shook out her hand, jaw clenched.

"Second try," she said.

"Smaller," Annabeth reminded her.

This time the charge snapped toward the drain in one short flare. The white line around the column dimmed.

Zoe scraped through the first hooked stroke.

The black thread rose from the cut.

It reached toward Annabeth.

Thalia moved her spear between them, taking a sharp shock that drove her shoulder into the wall.

Annabeth caught her elbow.

"Don't put yourself in front of everything!"

"Then help me move it."

There was no time to decide which of them had sounded more frightened.

They faced the gap together.

Percy held the stream steady while Annabeth fed a narrow pulse into its edge. Thalia released the pressure she had been carrying a piece at a time, each burst short enough for Chiron to call out between them.

Zoe cut the second stroke.

The ringing broke into separate notes.

Outside, someone shouted that the sun was returning.

Inside, the last stroke split beneath Zoe's tool. The water rushed into the drain, taking a final scatter of blue sparks with it.

Percy lost the shape of the stream and sat back hard.

Nothing replaced it.

---

Light returned in a gray wash.

The Hall had not disappeared. Its columns still stood, the northern mosaic cracked around a circle of exposed stone. Someone had torn the ceremonial cloth. Water ran in ordinary rivulets toward the drain.

Thalia slid down the wall.

Annabeth knelt beside her. Percy could see a red mark across Thalia's palm where the spear had burned her.

"You said you could hold it," Annabeth said.

"I could."

"You were falling."

"I was about to revise my estimate."

The joke came out thin. Annabeth held her sister's wrist carefully, keeping her fingers away from the burn.

"You told me not to improvise alone."

Thalia looked at their joined hands.

"I saw the children on the path."

"I know. I saw them too."

For a moment neither spoke.

"Next time," Thalia said, "I call for help before I decide I'm the help."

Annabeth nodded.

"I can't lose you."

"I'm here."

Percy turned away long enough to bring a dry towel from the doorway. Clarisse had delivered the bundle after the last children reached the Big House; she stood outside the cord now, waiting for Chiron's signal.

Thalia took the towel.

"You looked at me," she said to Percy, "like you thought I knew what was happening."

He could not pretend he had only been checking the light.

"I did."

Her face closed a little.

"For a second. Then I saw what you were doing."

"What did you think before that second?"

He set the other towels down.

"That because you had something you lost in the history I remember, you might have wanted to change it."

Annabeth went still beside her.

Thalia looked at him for a long time.

"I don't know the life you're talking about," she said. "I know my sister. I know I want her safe. Those are the things I can answer for."

"I know. I'm sorry I made them evidence against you."

She lowered her eyes to the towel.

"Don't do it again."

"I won't."

---

Chiron made them visit the infirmary before he let them discuss the floor.

Thalia's palm needed dressing. Percy had a shallow burn beside his wrist and muscles that shook whenever he reached for something. Annabeth complained of a headache, then stopped complaining when the healer told her that counting the symptoms aloud was useful.

Silena brought them water.

Clarisse stood at the doorway and counted.

"Three," she said. "Good."

"Four with you," Percy answered.

"I wasn't in the stupid building."

"Five with Silena."

"Drink your water."

Annabeth leaned against Thalia while the dressing was finished. She looked exhausted and unmistakably herself. There was no return of a missing romance, no new childhood suddenly waiting behind her eyes.

When Thalia asked whether she remembered a similar storm when they were small, Annabeth shook her head.

"You can tell me later."

Thalia looked down at her.

"Later," she agreed.

---

They met by the lake that evening, after Chiron had assured them that the Hall was closed and the fragment from the drain was locked away.

Zoe had not identified its maker. Breaking the mark had interrupted the surge; it had not explained the altered records, the changed families, or their return to twelve.

Percy sat beside Annabeth at the end of the dock.

"Same hands?" he asked.

She turned hers over in her lap.

"Still sparks if I get careless. Still Zeus."

"Still Grace."

"Still me."

He nodded.

Behind them, Thalia sat on the shore with Clarisse and Silena. Somebody had brought food from the pavilion. They were arguing about whether a sandwich rescued from a closed lunch counted as dinner.

Annabeth listened to her sister laugh.

"She remembers things I don't," she said. "And I remember things she doesn't. That hasn't changed."

"No."

"I don't want to make her love prove which history is right."

Percy looked toward his sisters. Silena was trying to pass Clarisse a second sandwich. Clarisse was protesting while taking it.

"Me neither."

Annabeth rested her head against his shoulder. He put an arm around her and felt the small shift as she settled comfortably, without having to work out who he was.

The lake smelled of damp reeds and cooling pine. A fish rose near the pilings, leaving a circle that widened beneath their feet.

"Tomorrow," she said, "we sleep late."

"An ambitious plan."

"No ancient seals before breakfast."

"Could put that on the cabin door."

She smiled.

They stayed until Thalia called them to eat. Then Annabeth took his hand and pulled him up, careful of his sore wrist. The unknown part of their lives was still unknown. For tonight, there were five places on the shore, and someone had saved them two sandwiches.
