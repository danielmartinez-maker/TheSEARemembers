## Chapter 89: Belated Valentines (Camp-Style)

Their belated Valentine mail day dawned cold and bright in mid-June, with an unseasonable chill silvering the strawberry fields and smoke curling from the cabins in thin blue ribbons. The February attempt had washed out; now the notes had another chance.

By eight o'clock, the Aphrodite cabin had conquered the pavilion.

---

Silena Beauregard stood on a bench in a pink sweater three sizes too cozy, her dark hair pulled back with a red ribbon, directing her Aphrodite friends and volunteers like a general planning a siege.

"Okay, love is war and we are winning," she announced, clapping her hands. "Lacy, garlands. Mitchell, you are on sorting. Rosie, glitter is banned within ten feet of the food. Last year we had sparkly ambrosia for a month."

"I thought sparkly ambrosia was festive," Mitchell said, already up to his elbows in pink envelopes.

"It was festive until Chiron sneezed pink for a week," Silena said. "Post office opens at nine. Rules on the board. Everybody reads them."

On a big piece of poster board in looping Aphrodite-cabin handwriting, someone had written:

CAMP HALF-BLOOD BELATED VALENTINE POST OFFICE
RUN BY APHRODITE (obviously)
1. Send love. All kinds. Friends, crushes, siblings, cabinmates.
2. No curses, no hexes, no love potions. We check.
3. Anonymous allowed but be kind. Cowards who are mean get exposed.
4. Delivery at dinner by cherub express (Travis and Connor in wings).
5. Be brave.

Underneath, in smaller writing, Drew had added: *Cuteness is mandatory.*

Percy Jackson stood at the edge of the pavilion with his hands in the pockets of his Camp hoodie, watching the chaos with amusement as campers began streaming in with folded notes clutched in their mittens.

"This is new," he said to Annabeth Grace, who was beside him with a notebook already full of sketches for some new siege-engine-looking thing she claimed was a decorative arch. "We didn’t get to do this in February."

"We tried," Annabeth said. "It rained. The notes melted. Drew cried. Mitchell cried harder. Chiron called it off and gave everyone hot chocolate instead."

"So this is Valentine’s, round two. In June."

"Camp-style," Annabeth said, bumping his shoulder with hers. "Which means it will be sweet and completely insane."

Above them, Lacy strung paper hearts across the rafters while a son of Iris enchanted them to flutter faintly, like they were breathing. The Ares cabin had already tried to shoot one down and been threatened by Silena with a hairbrush, which somehow worked.

Chiron clopped in, his blanket draped over his horse half for warmth. "Ah," he said warmly, reading the board. "Love in all its forms. Friendship, familial devotion, young admiration. Remember, campers, the ancient Greeks had many words for love. Today we honor them all."

"Did the Greeks have Valentine's Day?" Percy asked.

"No," Chiron said. "But Aphrodite would have adored it, and Eros is already circling the lake looking for targets, so please keep your helmets on if you wish to remain unaffected."

Grover Underwood trotted up at that moment, his scarf crooked and his cheeks red from cold, with a stack of envelopes in his hands so thick he could barely hold them.

"Morning," he said cheerfully. "I come bearing mail and emotional vulnerability."

"Grover, are those all to send?" Annabeth asked, eyes wide.

"No, these are incoming. Well. Some are incoming. Some are outgoing. Some are incoming I am pretending are outgoing so I don't get nervous."

Percy peered at the top envelope. It was pink, sealed with a leaf sticker, and smelled faintly of juniper berries. "Dude. You already got mail and the post office isn't even open."

Grover blushed to the tips of his horns. "Juniper dropped one off at dawn before I woke up. She said the official system was too slow for urgent love."

"Urgent love," Percy repeated, grinning. "That's very satyr of you."

"It's very Juniper of her," Grover said fondly. "She once threw an acorn at my head from forty feet because I was late for lunch and she missed me."

Silena leaned over the sorting table. "Grover! Is that unsorted mail circumventing my system?"

"It's pre-approved love," Grover said quickly, hugging the envelopes. "Very official. Very dryad-sealed."

Silena pointed her sorting wand at him. "Everything goes through the post office. Even pre-approved love. Especially pre-approved love. Bring them here, you adorable goat boy."

Grover sighed happily and shuffled toward the table as more campers poured in.

The day had begun.

---

By nine fifteen, the pavilion was packed shoulder to shoulder.

The Aphrodite cabin had set up three boxes: PINK for romantic, GOLD for friendship, BLUE for family and siblings and cabin love. There was also a small black box in the corner labeled MYSTERY / ANONYMOUS / HARMLESSLY DRAMATIC that Drew guarded with her life.

Travis and Connor Stoll immediately tried to mail a live pigeon.

"No live animals," Mitchell said without looking up.

"It's a love pigeon," Travis protested. Connor was wearing cupid wings made of dryer sheets and pipe cleaners. "It's thematic."

"It's pooping on the mail," Mitchell said. "No."

Ella the harpy fluttered nervously near the edge of the crowd, her wings tucked tight and her red hair tangled with little bits of straw. She clutched a book of poetry to her chest like a shield and rocked back and forth on her talons, muttering under her breath.

"Love is complicated. Love is complicated. One Valentine's Day in 1978 saw three hundred and twelve paper cuts at P.S. 41. Ella does not want a paper cut. Paper cuts hurt."

Tyson, Percy's Cyclops half-brother, visiting on a day's leave from the undersea forges, ducked through the pavilion entrance sideways to fit his enormous shoulders. He wore a sweater Annabeth had knitted for him with a big yellow heart on it that said HUGS in wobbly letters. In his huge hands he held a single envelope, very carefully, as if it might break.

"Ella!" he boomed happily when he saw her. The whole pavilion turned. "I made you a card! It has feathers on it! Not yours! Other feathers! Chicken feathers!"

Ella blinked rapidly. "Chicken feathers are soft. Soft is good. Tyson is good. Ella is... Ella is nervous."

Tyson carefully offered her nothing yet, because Silena intercepted him.

"Ah ah ah," she said gently. "You have to send it through us, big guy. So we can deliver it at dinner. Makes it more exciting."

Tyson frowned, confused and a little heartbroken. "But I want to give it now."

"I know," Silena said, softening. She patted his massive arm. "But anticipation is part of love. Trust me. She'll love it more tonight when there's music and lights."

"Okay," Tyson said, slowly placing the lumpy envelope, which was bulging with extra feathers and what smelled like peanut butter, into the PINK box. "Do not squish it."

"We would never squish love," Mitchell promised solemnly.

Nearby, Clarisse La Rue stomped in wearing full armor except for her helmet, which she carried under her arm. The Ares cabin parted for her like water.

She slapped a plain brown envelope onto the table.

"Deliver this," she ordered.

Silena picked it up. It was addressed in Clarisse's blunt block letters: TO MY TRAINING TEAM. No names.

"Ooh, soft," Drew cooed.

Clarisse glared. "It's not soft. It's tactical morale support. Ares cabin morale was low after capture the flag. I wrote... nice things."

"You wrote nice things," Silena repeated, beaming.

"Shut up. And if anyone tells them I signed it with a heart, I'll kill you."

Mitchell peered at the envelope. Sure enough, in the corner, there was a tiny, furious heart.

"We will guard your heart with our lives," he said.

"Damn right," Clarisse muttered, stomping off. As she left, she nearly tripped over a small, unmarked package sitting on the bench by the door. It was wrapped in plain cloth and tied with twine. A tag read: FOR CLARISSE.

She stared at it. "What's this?"

"Anonymous delivery," Lacy said brightly. "Someone dropped it early. No name. We were going to deliver it tonight, but since you're here—"

Clarisse picked it up warily, as if it might explode. She untied the twine. Inside was a tin of high-quality spear polish, a new whetstone, and a spare leather grip wrap in deep red. Practical. Thoughtful. Exactly right.

Underneath was a note in careful, unfamiliar handwriting that had clearly been rewritten several times: *Your spear was dull on the left edge Tuesday. You fight like you don't need anyone. You don't. But your gear should keep up with you. — A.*

Clarisse stared.

"What the Hades," she said quietly.

"Secret admirer?" Drew whispered, delighted.

"I don't get admirers," Clarisse said flatly. "I get challengers and victims."

"Well, someone admires your spear maintenance," Mitchell said.

Clarisse re-wrapped the package with surprising care. Her ears were red. "If this is a Stoll prank and the polish is actually glue, I will mount your heads on my wall."

"It's not glue," Silena said, sniffing the tin. "That's real manticore-fat polish. Expensive. Someone... likes you. Or at least respects you a lot."

Clarisse grunted and tucked the package under her arm like it was suddenly precious. "Hmph," she said, and marched out, a little faster than usual.

Percy watched her go. "Any guesses?"

Annabeth was watching too, thoughtful. "Handwriting is disguised. A? Could be a lot of people. But manticore-fat polish? That's not from the camp store. Someone went off-campus or traded for that."

"You think it's Chris?" Percy asked quietly. Chris Rodriguez, the Hermes kid who had been on Luke's side and was still recovering, had been lingering around the edges of camp life, shy and ashamed.

Annabeth shrugged. "Maybe. Let's let it be a mystery for today. She looked... happy. Don't ruin it with investigating."

"Me? Investigate? I'm just a bystander in love's war."

"Uh huh," Annabeth said, smiling into her hot chocolate.

---

By noon, the chaos had fully bloomed.

Grover was the first casualty.

He came to lunch with a dazed expression and three pink envelopes stuck in his horns like bizarre antlers.

"Three," he told Percy and Annabeth as he slid onto the bench at the Poseidon/Zeus-adjacent table they always shared when cabins mingled. "I got three."

"Three what?" Percy asked, stealing a roll.

"Three valentines. Three separate admirers. This has never happened to me before. Usually I get one from Juniper and a coupon for free goat feed from the satyr council."

Annabeth laughed. "Let's see them."

Grover laid them out carefully.

The first was green, smelling of forest, sealed with a berry. Inside, in looping, impatient handwriting: *G-man — You are late for everything except being good. I love you anyway. Save me a dance tonight. — J.* There was a pressed juniper leaf inside.

"That's Juniper," Grover said, glowing. "She's my girlfriend. That's allowed. That's expected love."

The second was pink and glittery and clearly a joke. Inside, in Travis's terrible handwriting: *Grover, your fur is soft, your horns are pointy, please don't eat my valentine. Be mine? — Your Secret Admirer (definitely not Travis).* Underneath, in smaller writing: *(Connor helped but he denies it).*

Percy snorted milk through his nose. "You're blushing at that?"

"I'm blushing because I was briefly excited before I recognized the handwriting," Grover said with dignity. "My heart is tender today."

"And the third?" Annabeth asked.

Grover held up the third. It was small, made of bark paper, tied with grass. The handwriting was small and shy. *Dear Grover, you played panpipes by the woods last Tuesday and the saplings grew faster. Thank you for the music. You make growing things happy. — A friend.*

Grover's ears twitched. "It's from one of the young dryads. Maple, I think. She's only forty. That's like eight in human years. It's not romantic. It's... admiration. Pure admiration. And I don't know what to do with it because my heart is so full it's leaking."

"That's the point of today," Annabeth said gently. "All kinds of love. Juniper loves you romantically. Travis loves you as a friend in the most annoying way possible. Little Maple loves your music. That's not chaos. That's... wonderful."

Grover stared at her, then at the notes, then burst into tears.

"I'm leaking," he sobbed.

Juniper appeared out of nowhere, as dryads do when their boyfriends cry, and wrapped her arms around him from behind. "Oh, my goat," she said, kissing the top of his curly hair. "Are you crying over valentines again?"

"There are three," Grover wept. "Three loves."

"And you're my favorite love," Juniper said, plucking the Travis note off his horn and flicking it at Percy. "This one doesn't count."

"Hey!" Travis shouted from two tables over. "Love counts!"

Juniper sat down beside Grover and leaned her head on his shoulder. "Read me Maple's again," she said softly. "That's sweet."

Across the pavilion, Ella was having her own crisis.

She sat alone with her poetry book open but unread, muttering facts about Valentine's Day massacres and heart-shaped candy statistics. "In 2019, Americans spent twenty point seven billion dollars on Valentine's Day. Twenty point seven billion. Ella has zero dollars. Zero dollars cannot buy love."

Tyson sat down across from her with a thump that rattled the plates. He set his tray down and smiled at her with his one big eye shining.

"Hi Ella," he said.

"Hi Tyson," Ella whispered, not looking up. "Ella is not good at today. Too many feelings. Feelings are like too many books. They fall over."

Tyson nodded seriously. "I like books. I like you. I wrote you a poem. Well. I sent you a poem. Through the mail. Silena said to wait. Waiting is hard."

Ella's wings fluttered. "A poem. For Ella. Ella memorized six hundred and twelve love poems. None were for Ella. 'How do I love thee? Let me count the ways.' Elizabeth Barrett Browning. Sonnet 43. Ella always wondered. Would someone count for Ella?"

"I counted," Tyson said earnestly. "I counted to ten. For you."

Ella finally looked up. "Ten is a good number. Ten has closure."

"You will like it," Tyson promised. "It has rhymes. Annabeth helped with one rhyme because 'Ella' is hard to rhyme. We used 'umbrella.' Because you have wings like umbrellas."

Ella made a small sound, half laugh, half sob. "Umbrella. Ella-brella. That is... that is adequate. That is more than adequate. That is—" She buried her face in her book. "Ella will cry now. Do not be alarmed. Crying is saline."

Tyson reached across the table and very gently patted her hand with one huge finger. "Okay. I will guard your crying."

Percy watched from his table, his throat tight. He leaned toward Annabeth. "If that poem doesn't get delivered, I'm hand-delivering it myself."

"It'll get delivered," Annabeth said. "Silena may be dramatic, but she doesn't lose mail."

Percy was quiet for a moment, watching Tyson count on his fingers, probably rehearsing his rhymes. "He's a good brother," Percy said softly.

"He learned it from his brother," Annabeth said.

Percy looked at her. She was looking at him with that steady gray gaze that always saw too much.

"What?" he asked.

"Nothing," she said. "Just... filing that away for my letter."

"Your letter?"

"You'll see tonight," Annabeth said, standing up with her tray. "Come on. Archery's starting. And I heard Beckendorf's been hiding in the forge all morning. That's suspicious."

---

The forge was hot and loud despite the cool June morning.

Charles Beckendorf, huge and quiet, son of Hephaestus, stood at his workbench with a half-finished music box in front of him and a look of deep concentration. The music box was copper and silver, shaped like a dove. When he wound the key, it played a slightly tinny but sweet melody.

Jake Mason and Harley, his half-brothers, were watching from the doorway, snickering.

"Play it again," Harley begged. "Do the singing part."

"No," Beckendorf said flatly.

"Come on, man," Jake said. "If you don't practice, you'll choke tonight."

"I am not singing," Beckendorf said. "I am a blacksmith. Blacksmiths do not sing."

"Blacksmiths in love sing," Harley sang.

Beckendorf glared, but his ears were red. He carefully closed the music box and wrapped it in cloth.

Percy and Annabeth ducked in, drawn by the noise.

"Hey, Beckendorf," Percy said. "Whatcha making?"

"Nothing," Beckendorf said too quickly.

Annabeth spotted the cloth-wrapped box. "That looks like something. Is that for the post office?"

"It's for Silena," Beckendorf admitted after a pause, his deep voice quieter. "It's not much. Valentine's stuff isn't really... I don't do words good."

"You do work good," Percy said. "That's better."

Beckendorf nodded, turning the box over in his hands. "She likes songs. She sings while she works. I can't... I made her a song. On this. It's a tune my mom used to hum. I changed it a little. And I was going to... I wrote something to say when I give it. But it sounds stupid."

"Try us," Annabeth said gently.

Beckendorf cleared his throat, staring at the floor. "I was going to say... you make ugly things beautiful just by being near them. Like... like rust into shine. That's stupid, right?"

Percy and Annabeth exchanged a look.

"That's not stupid," Annabeth said firmly. "That's perfect. Say exactly that."

"She'll cry," Percy warned.

"Good crying or bad crying?" Beckendorf asked, alarmed.

"Good crying," Annabeth assured him. "There's a lot of that today."

Beckendorf carefully placed the music box into a box for the Aphrodite post office runners. "Don't tell her I was nervous."

"Your secret's safe," Percy said.

As they left the forge, Annabeth was quiet.

"What are you thinking?" Percy asked.

"About letters," she said. "About saying things right."

Percy bumped his hand against hers as they walked. "You always say things right. Even when you're bossy about it."

"I am not bossy," Annabeth said automatically, then smiled. "Okay, I'm a little bossy. It's part of my charm."

"It's my favorite part," Percy said, then flushed. "I mean—one of my favorite parts. Top ten. Top five. Uh—"

Annabeth laughed, but her cheeks were pink from more than cold. "Save it for the letter, Seaweed Brain."

"I will," Percy promised. "I've been working on it for a week."

---

Thalia Grace spent most of the belated mail day on the climbing wall, which was her version of hiding.

She told herself she was just training. That the pink hearts everywhere were annoying. That love was stupid and she was above it. Daughter of Zeus, camp scout, punk-rock big sister who had once punched a Laistrygonian giant in the nose — she did not do valentines.

But her spiky black hair was damp with sweat, her electric-blue eyes were stormy, and she kept missing handholds she could normally do blindfolded.

"You're distracted," said Luke Castellan from the ground, arms crossed, watching her. He wore a Camp Half-Blood shirt with a pink heart pinned to it that someone — probably Silena — had forced on him. He looked both amused and sympathetic.

"I'm focused," Thalia grunted, grabbing for the next hold. Lava bubbled below.

"You're thinking about the boxes," Luke said.

"I am not thinking about the boxes."

"You are thinking about whether anyone put anything in the BLUE box for you."

Thalia lost her grip and dropped, the safety ropes catching her. She rappelled down, landing hard on the damp ground.

"I don't need valentines," she snapped, yanking off her harness. "I'm on patrol. I don't— I don't have time for that. Aphrodite stuff."

Luke raised an eyebrow. "You signed up for patrol. You didn't give up family. Or friendship. Blue box is for that too, you know."

Thalia kicked at a muddy clod. "Yeah. I know."

Luke's expression softened. He had known Thalia since they were kids on the run together, before Camp, before everything. He knew her better than almost anyone.

"Did you send anything?" he asked.

Thalia was quiet. Then she muttered, "I sent something to Jason. Through the post office this morning before anyone was awake. Blue box."

Luke smiled. "He'll like that."

"He's in California," Thalia said. "With the Legion. He won't even be here for delivery. I just... Silena said they'd forward mail. I don't even know if he celebrates this Roman stuff. Probably they do something super Roman and disciplined like... push-ups for Venus."

Luke laughed. "Probably."

Thalia shoved her hands in her pockets. "Did you... send anything?"

"Yeah," Luke said. "One. Blue box."

Thalia glanced at him. "To who?"

"You'll see," Luke said mysteriously.

Thalia narrowed her eyes. "If it's to me and it's mushy, I'll kill you."

"If it's to you and it's mushy, you'll cry," Luke corrected.

"I do not cry."

"Last time you cried was when Jason sent you that drawing of you guarding a pine tree for Winter Solstice."

"I had pine sap in my eye," Thalia said fiercely. "I was decorating that tree for two hours. It was traumatic."

Luke just smiled and handed her a canteen. "Come on. Let's get hot chocolate. And stop pretending you don't hope someone sent you something."

Thalia followed him, grumbling, but her eyes kept drifting toward the pavilion where the Aphrodite cabin was sorting the afternoon haul into big canvas sacks labeled by cabin.

She told herself she didn't care.

She was lying.

---

By late afternoon, the whole camp buzzed with anticipation.

The Aphrodite post office closed at four. Silena emerged from the pavilion looking exhausted and triumphant, ink on her fingers and glitter in her hair despite the ban.

"Two hundred and fourteen pieces," she announced to her volunteer runners. "New record. Cherub express, suit up."

Travis and Connor donned their dryer-sheet wings.

"We are cherubs," Travis intoned solemnly. "We are adorable."

"We are terrifying," Connor corrected. "Let's deliver love."

Percy spent the last hour before the post office closed in his cabin, staring at a piece of paper.

He had started his letter to Annabeth six times.

The first draft was too jokey. The second was too serious. The third had a stain where he'd spilled seawater. The fourth was good but he'd spelled "architecture" wrong three different ways.

Now, on the fifth try, he finally wrote what he meant.

He wrote about the first time he saw her — not when she was pretty, though she was, but when she was brave, taking on monsters with a plan and a knife. He wrote about how she made him want to be smarter, not just stronger. He wrote about how she remembered everyone's birthday and how she talked in her sleep about buildings. He wrote that he liked who he was when he was with her, and that he didn't just mean crush stuff, though he felt that too, big and scary and real. He meant he admired her. He meant she was his best friend. He meant he wanted to be worthy of her trust.

He ended it simply: *Will you save me a dance tonight? Not a slow dance if you don't want. Just a dance. I like standing near you. — Percy*

He folded it, sealed it with a dab of wax Beckendorf had given him, and wrote TO ANNABETH in careful capitals. He added a small trident doodle. Then he ran to the pavilion just before closing and slipped it into the PINK box himself, his heart hammering.

Silena, locking up, saw him and smiled knowingly. "Pink box, huh?"

"Shut up," Percy said, but without heat.

"Romance looks good on you, Jackson," Silena said. "She's going to love it."

"I hope so," Percy muttered. "I didn't do poetry. Tyson did poetry. Mine's just... words."

"Just words are the best words," Silena said.

Across camp, at her desk in Cabin One, Annabeth was having her own crisis.

Her letter to Percy was three pages long, front and back, with diagrams.

She had crossed out half of it.

She wrote about how Percy was the first person who never made her feel weird for being smart. How he listened, really listened, even when she rambled about arches. How he was kind to everyone — to Tyson, to Grover, to Ella, to Nico when Nico was prickly, to Clarisse even when Clarisse was terrifying. How his loyalty wasn't just a fatal flaw the gods warned about, it was the best thing about him.

She wrote that she used to think love had to be grand and tragic like the old myths. But watching Percy share his dessert with Tyson, or carry Grover's pipes when Grover was tired, or sit with her in silence when she was sad about Luke and Thalia and everything — that felt bigger.

She wrote: *I know what we mean to each other, even when being young again feels strange. But I know I trust you with my weak spots, and I don't trust anyone with those. So... will you save me a dance? And maybe hold my hand during it, if that's okay? — Annabeth*

She drew a tiny owl and a tiny trident holding hands. Then she blushed furiously at her own drawing and almost erased it, but left it.

She delivered it herself, slipping it into the PINK box while Drew wasn't looking.

Drew saw anyway. "Ooh, Grace girl's got game."

"Deliver it or die," Annabeth said sweetly.

"Love you too," Drew said.

---

Dinner was transformed.

The dining pavilion glowed with a hundred floating candles conjured by the Hecate cabin. Paper hearts drifted down from the ceiling like snow. The Aphrodite cabin had set up a small stage with a borrowed Apollo lyre and a sign that read LOVE IS FOR EVERYONE.

Chiron stood to give the blessing. "Tonight, we celebrate philia, storge, eros, and agape — friendship, family, romance, and selfless love. May your hearts be full, and may your mail be kind."

"And may the cherubs not drop anything in the fire," Mr. D added, sipping Diet Coke. "February was a disaster."

Travis and Connor flapped in with the mail sacks, blowing kisses and dropping envelopes dramatically.

"Delivery!" they shouted. "Cherub express!"

Chaos erupted, warm and laughing.

Grover got his three letters re-delivered officially, and Juniper kissed his cheek while everyone aww'd. He turned the color of a tomato and hid behind his pipes.

"Speech!" Travis demanded.

Grover stood, trembling. "I just... I love music, and I love growing things, and I love Juniper, and I love my friends, and today I learned there is enough love for all of it. Thank you." He sat down to thunderous applause and Juniper hugging him so tight his horns squeaked.

Ella's moment came next.

Connor, trying to be gentle for once, knelt by her table and presented a lumpy envelope with chicken feathers sticking out. "Special delivery for Ella. Handle with care. It's fragile and rhymes."

The whole pavilion quieted. Tyson sat beside Ella, his hands clasped, his eye wide and hopeful.

Ella opened it with shaking talons.

Inside was a card covered in glued feathers, glitter (somehow), and a drawing of Ella and Tyson holding hands under an umbrella. Inside, in Tyson's big, careful printing with one word in Annabeth's neat handwriting where she'd helped with spelling, was the poem:

*Ella is smart, Ella is fast,
Ella remembers the past.
Your feathers are pretty, your voice is strong,
When you talk, I could listen long.
You are not scary, you are my friend,
I will like you until the end.
If it rains, I will share my umbrella,
Because you are wonderful, Ella.*

Ella read it once silently. Then again aloud, her voice shaking.

"'Because you are wonderful, Ella,'" she whispered. "Wonderful. Adjective. Inspiring delight. Ella has been called harpy. Monster. Pest. Never wonderful. Tyson calls Ella wonderful."

Tyson beamed. "You are wonderful."

Ella stood up suddenly, flapping her wings, scattering feathers everywhere. "Ella will keep this forever! Ella will memorize it! Line one: Ella is smart, Ella is fast! Ella accepts this valentine! Tyson is Ella's friend! Best friend! Ella-brella friend!"

She threw her arms — wings — around Tyson's neck and hugged him fiercely. Tyson hugged her back gently, as if she were made of glass, while the whole camp cheered and cried.

Percy wiped his eyes. "I'm not crying. There's glitter in my eye."

"There's no glitter," Annabeth said, crying herself. "It's banned near food, remember?"

"Then I'm crying," Percy admitted.

Next was Clarisse.

Travis approached her table cautiously, like one approaches a sleeping dragon. "Uh. Clarisse? Delivery. Anonymous package already delivered, but there's a note follow-up?"

Clarisse snatched the envelope. It was the same disguised handwriting: *Saw you got the kit. Hope the grip fits. If not, I can adjust it. I know leatherwork. — A.*

The Ares table went silent. Sherman Yang leaned over. "Who's A?"

"No idea," Clarisse said, but her voice was strange.

"Chris Rodriguez starts with C," Sherman pointed out unhelpfully. "But his last name... no. Hmm."

"Shut up," Clarisse said, but she carefully folded the note and put it in her armor pocket, over her heart. She glanced toward the Hermes table, where Chris sat at the edge, pretending not to look. When their eyes met, he flushed and looked down quickly at his plate.

Clarisse stared for a long moment. Then she stood, walked over to the Hermes table — the whole room holding its breath — and dropped a small chocolate wrapped in gold foil in front of Chris.

"Thanks for the... intel on spear maintenance," she said gruffly.

Chris stared at the chocolate like it was a holy relic. "You're welcome," he murmured. "Your left edge was dull."

"I fixed it," Clarisse said.

"Good," Chris said, smiling shyly. "You fight good."

Clarisse nodded once, ears burning, and marched back to her seat while the Ares cabin erupted in whoops and the Hermes cabin tackled Chris in a hug.

"Mystery solved?" Percy whispered.

"Mystery respected," Annabeth corrected. "Let them have it."

Then the lights dimmed.

Silena stepped onto the stage, glowing in the candlelight. She thought the deliveries were done for her — she had given love all day, which was its own reward.

Then Beckendorf stood up.

The huge son of Hephaestus walked to the front, carrying a small wrapped box and looking like he would rather face a Cyclops. Jake Mason gave him a thumbs up. Harley mouthed *sing!*

Beckendorf knelt — actually knelt — in front of the stage and held up the box.

"Silena," he said, his deep voice shaking just a little. "You make ugly things beautiful just by being near them. Like rust into shine. I made you this."

Silena opened the box. The copper-and-silver dove music box glinted. Beckendorf wound it. The sweet melody filled the pavilion — a lullaby, old and tender, made new.

Everyone was silent.

"You made me a song," Silena whispered, tears spilling over.

"I can't sing good," Beckendorf said. "So I built singing."

Silena launched herself off the stage into his arms. He caught her easily, laughing in surprise as she kissed his cheek a dozen times.

"It's perfect! It's the best valentine! Charlie, it's—" She wound it again and hugged it to her chest. "I'll keep it forever. I'll wind it every night."

Beckendorf held her close, his face buried in her hair, smiling like the sun had come out inside him. "Happy Valentine's, Silena."

The camp applauded until their hands hurt. Even Mr. D clapped once, then pretended he was swatting a fly.

Percy felt Annabeth's hand slip into his under the table.

He looked at her. She was looking at him, nervous and brave.

"Did you get something?" she asked softly.

"Did you?" he countered.

Connor appeared at their table with two pink envelopes, grinning like he knew everything. "Special delivery. One for Jackson. One for Grace. From... secret admirers. Very secret. Very obvious."

Percy and Annabeth stared at the envelopes.

"Read together?" Percy suggested, his voice cracking.

"On three," Annabeth said. "One... two... three."

They opened them.

The pavilion noise faded.

Percy read Annabeth's three pages with diagrams, his eyes moving faster and faster, his grin growing until it hurt. When he got to the part about trusting him with her weak spots, his breath caught. When he saw the little owl and trident holding hands, he laughed wetly.

Annabeth read Percy's letter, her hands trembling. When she got to *I like who I am when I'm with you*, she pressed her hand to her mouth. When she got to *Will you save me a dance?* she looked up, tears shining.

"He spelled architecture right," she whispered. "He must have worked so hard."

"He drew us holding hands," Percy whispered. "I'm keeping this forever."

They looked at each other across the table, surrounded by two hundred cheering campers, and for a moment it was just them.

"So?" Percy asked. "Will you?"

"Will I what?"

"Save me a dance? Hold hands? All of it?"

Annabeth smiled through tears, and slipped her hand more firmly into his, lacing their fingers. "Yes. To all of it. Will you?"

"Yes," Percy said immediately. "A thousand times yes."

They held hands under the table, shoulders touching, reading each other's letters again and again while the music played. When Annabeth looked up, Percy kissed her smiling cheek, and she stole another chocolate from his plate.

Around them, friends whooped. Grover cried. Tyson waved at them with Ella's poem clutched in his hand. Silena blew them a kiss from Beckendorf's lap where she sat sideways, winding her music box.

It was perfect.

---

Thalia had been quiet through all of it, clapping politely, smiling when others cried, pretending her own heart wasn't hammering.

When Connor came to her table last, with two envelopes — one small and blue, one larger and worn — her breath stopped.

"Thalia Grace?" Connor said gently, for once without a joke. "Forwarded mail. One came all the way from California. Express harpy post. And one... was in the Blue box this morning."

Thalia stared at the envelopes.

The first was in a child's careful handwriting she knew instantly, though it had matured. *TO THALIA — FROM JASON.* With a Roman stamp and a Legion seal.

The second was in Luke's familiar scrawl. *For Thalia. Blue Box.*

Her hands shook as she opened Jason's first.

*Dear Thalia,*

*Happy belated Valentine’s Day. Reyna told me about your June mail day. We did the February festival stuff at Camp Jupiter, which was weird and involved running. I didn’t run.*

*Reyna says Valentine's is for love in all forms, including family. So I'm writing to say I love you. Thank you for sending me the pinecone you painted like a hedgehog. It's on my bunk. Everyone thinks it's weird. I told them my sister is weird and awesome.*

*I miss you already after spring break. I know you’re on camp patrol now and you can’t visit much. But I think about you when I look at the sky during storms. That means you're thinking of me too, right?*

*Be safe. Don't get struck by lightning (ha ha). Save me some ambrosia.*

*Love, your little brother, Jason*

P.S. *I drew you a picture. It's you throwing lightning. You look cool.*

Inside was a crayon drawing of Thalia with spiky hair hurling lightning bolts while a small blond boy cheered from below.

Thalia stared at it, her lip trembling.

"You okay?" Luke asked quietly from beside her. He hadn't left her side all evening.

"Shut up," Thalia whispered, wiping her eyes furiously. "It's... it's stupid. He's my little brother. He drew me cool."

"He loves you," Luke said.

Thalia opened Luke's letter with even shakier hands.

*Thalia —*

*You said you don't do valentines. So I'm not sending you a valentine. I'm sending you a blue-box note, which is different and tougher and allowed.*

*You were my first family after I ran. Before Annabeth, before camp felt like home. You shared your food when you were starving. You stayed awake on watch so I could sleep. You told me I wasn't a failure when I felt like one.*

*I know things are complicated now. I know I've messed up and you're mad and you have every right to be. But you are still my sister in every way that counts. Not blood. Choice.*

*Jason got lucky. He got you for a sister by blood. I got you by luck and I thank the gods for it every day, even when I'm mad at them.*

*Happy Valentine's. Don't kill the messenger. Or me.*

*— Luke*

Thalia read it twice. Then a third time. Tears spilled over, hot and unstoppable.

"Damn it," she sobbed, laughing through it. "Damn it, Luke. This is mushy. This is so mushy. I said I'd kill you if it was mushy."

"Are you going to?" Luke asked, his own eyes bright.

Thalia threw her arms around him and hugged him fiercely, crying into his shoulder while he hugged her back just as fiercely. Brother and sister, not by blood but by survival, by choice.

"I hate you," she mumbled.

"I love you too," Luke said.

Jason's drawing fluttered to the table. Percy, from across the room, saw Thalia crying and started to stand, worried — but Annabeth pulled him back, shaking her head with a soft smile.

"Good crying," she said. "Let them have it."

Thalia pulled back, wiping her face on her sleeve. "Jason drew me cool," she told Luke, showing him the picture like it was treasure. "Look. Lightning. I'm awesome."

"You're awesome," Luke agreed. "Want to write him back together? We can send him Camp Half-Blood embarrassing stories."

Thalia laughed wetly and tucked both letters into her jacket, over her heart. "Yeah. Yeah, okay. And... thanks. For the blue-box-not-valentine."

"Anytime, little sister," Luke said.

"I am older than you," Thalia protested, but she leaned her head on his shoulder and stayed there while the music played, holding her brother's letter in one hand and her chosen brother's hand in the other.

The night softened into dancing.

The dance floor was just the cleared space in front of the stage, with Iris-magic lights swirling overhead in pink and gold.

Juniper dragged Grover up for a slow dance, and he stepped on her feet twice and apologized to her feet. Tyson danced with Ella, very carefully, while she recited the beats — "One-two-three, one-two-three, Tyson is surprisingly graceful for a Cyclops, fact" — and glowed.

Beckendorf danced with Silena, her music box tucked in his pocket, still playing faintly. She had her head on his chest and her eyes closed.

Clarisse did not dance. But she sat at the Ares table polishing her spear with her new polish, and when Chris walked by with a plate of cookies for his cabin, she offered him a cookie without a word. He took it. Their fingers brushed. Both turned red and pretended it didn't happen while both cabins silently screamed.

Thalia danced once with Luke, a goofy sibling dance full of spinning and almost falling, both laughing until they cried again. Then she danced with Annabeth, then with Percy, then with Grover, collecting family like flowers.

And Percy and Annabeth —

Percy stood and offered Annabeth his hand, formal and nervous like they were at a real ball instead of a camp dining pavilion with punch stains on the floor.

"May I have this dance?" he asked, echoing her letter.

Annabeth took his hand. "You may."

They danced slowly, awkwardly, Percy counting under his breath so he wouldn't step on her toes, Annabeth laughing softly and guiding him.

"I meant every word," Percy said quietly, so only she could hear over the music. "About admiring you. You're... you're amazing, Annabeth."

"I meant every word too," Annabeth said. "About trusting you. You're my favorite person."

"Even more than architecture?"

"Don't push it, Seaweed Brain."

They swayed, hands clasped, foreheads almost touching but not quite. Sweet and full of promise — not a grand tragic love, but a growing one, rooted in friendship and respect.

Around them, the camp they loved spun on — friends and siblings and crushes and chosen family, all kinds of love, all honored.

Silena caught Annabeth's eye from across the floor and mouthed *Pink box forever.* Annabeth mouthed back *Thank you.*

Chiron watched from his table, smiling into his punch. "Well done, Aphrodite cabin," he said to Silena as she passed, glowing. "Love, in all its forms, delivered."

"Two hundred and fourteen pieces," Silena said proudly, leaning against Beckendorf. "Zero lost. One mystery spear polish unsolved—"

"Partially solved," Mitchell called.

"—and a hundred percent happy crying."

Mr. D stood, raising his Diet Coke. "A toast," he said, surprising everyone into silence. "To valentines. To siblings who write from California. To Cyclopes who rhyme. To satyrs who leak love. To blacksmiths who build songs. To warriors who polish spears. And to young heroes who write letters." He glanced at Percy and Annabeth. "May your hearts remain... tolerable."

"To love!" the camp shouted.

"To love, camp-style!" Travis added.

The candles flared. The paper hearts fell like snow. Eros, invisible above the lake, finally put down his bow and just watched, satisfied.

Percy squeezed Annabeth's hand.

"Happy Valentine's, Wise Girl."

"Happy Valentine's, Seaweed Brain."

And the night, full of letters and songs and poems and polish and dances, held them all gently — a warm, laughing, loving ensemble — until the candles burned low.
