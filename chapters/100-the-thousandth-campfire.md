## Chapter 100: The Thousandth Campfire

The morning of the thousandth campfire dawned clear and gold over Camp Half-Blood, and Percy Jackson woke to someone hammering on the door of Cabin Three.

He sat up, hair sticking in every direction, heart thudding the way it always did when a loud noise woke him. For half a second the old instincts surfaced, the ones from dreams of being older, of wars and alarms and trumpet calls before dawn. Then he heard the voice.

"Up! Up! Thousandth campfire day! Everyone up!"

It was Connor Stoll, banging a wooden spoon against a pot as he ran down the cabin row, Travis right behind him with a conch shell he was blowing badly.

Percy groaned and flopped back onto his pillow. The fountain in the corner of the Poseidon cabin trickled. Morning light filtered blue-green through the shell windows. From the guest bunk across the room, Tyson, staying through the celebration on forge leave, snored on, blissfully immune to Hermes cabin wake-up calls.

The door cracked open and Clarisse La Rue stuck her head in, already in full armor except for her helmet, her camp necklace swinging.

"Tell Stoll if he hits my door with that spoon again I'm going to feed him the spoon," she said.

Percy grinned sleepily. "Morning to you too, sis."

Clarisse snorted and came in far enough to toss something onto his bed. A folded orange T-shirt. Freshly printed. Percy unfolded it.

Across the front in bold blue Greek letters it said 1000TH CAMPFIRE, and underneath in smaller print, Camp Half-Blood — Since Forever, Probably Before You Were Born. On the back was a list of cabin symbols circling a big bonfire.

"Silena made them," Clarisse said. "Aphrodite cabin stayed up half the night printing. She saved you a medium because she says you always steal larges and then swim in them."

"She knows me," Percy said, pulling the shirt over his head. It smelled like fresh ink and strawberries.

Clarisse leaned against the doorframe, arms crossed. She was trying to look annoyed, the way she always did when she was actually excited about something. It never worked on Percy. He had lived with her as a sister long enough to read the little tells, the way her mouth twitched at the corner when she was pleased.

"You know what this is about, right?" she said. "Chiron's calling it the thousandth campfire. Ella counted. She went through the camp records and the harpy memory chants and figured out tonight is number one thousand since the camp was refounded in its current spot."

Percy whistled. "A thousand?"

"A thousand," Clarisse said. "And the old satyrs say the fire remembers all of them. So tonight we remember back."

There was a softness in her voice under the gruffness. Percy heard it. He sat up straighter.

"Big party?" he asked.

"The biggest," Clarisse said. "Dinner feast. No training after lunch. Everybody decorates. Apollo music. Stories. Grover's got a speech he's been rewriting for a week and won't show anyone. And Ella and Rachel made some massive scroll thing they're keeping secret in the Big House attic. Silena's been crying about it already and she hasn't even seen it finished."

Percy laughed. "That sounds like Silena."

"It sounds like all of us," Clarisse said. She pushed off the doorframe. "Get up. Breakfast. Annabeth's probably already been up for two hours making lists."

She was right. When Percy got to the dining pavilion ten minutes later, hair still damp from a quick rinse in the fountain, Annabeth Grace was already there at the Zeus table with a wax tablet and three different colored styli, talking fast with Thalia while she ate toast with one hand.

Annabeth looked up when she saw him and her whole face lit. She waved him over with her stylus, and Percy felt that familiar warm swoop in his stomach, the same one he remembered from their quest journeys, when she would offer him half her cookie on the bus before unfolding another map.

He detoured to the Poseidon table to make his offering, scraping a bit of pancake into the brazier for his dad, then carried his plate to the edge of the Zeus table where Annabeth had saved him the end of the bench, the way she always did.

"Hi," she said.

"Hi," he said, bumping his shoulder against hers. "Heard you were making lists."

"I am making lists," Annabeth said solemnly. "Thousandth campfire lists. Very important. Chiron asked me to help coordinate the story circle. Everybody wants to tell their favorite."

Thalia Grace, sprawled across the bench on Annabeth's other side with her boots up on the table until Chiron gave her a look and she swung them down, grinned at Percy.

"Seaweed Brain," she said by way of greeting. "You ready to get all nostalgic tonight? Cry in front of everybody?"

"I'm hydrated and emotionally prepared," Percy said.

Annabeth laughed and stole a strawberry off his plate. "Good, because Silena's already made three playlists. One for dinner, one for stories, one for dancing."

Percy paused. "Dancing?"

Annabeth's cheeks went faintly pink, and her gray eyes flicked to his and held. "Yeah," she said softly. "Dancing."

Something in the way she said it took him straight back to when they were twelve, to dust and firelight and stepping on her toes while Grover played pipes. He squeezed her hand under the table.

"Good," he said quietly. "I was hoping."

---

By noon the whole camp smelled like paint and pine and sugar.

There was no training after the morning war games. Instead Chiron dismissed everyone with a wave of his hand and a rare, wide smile.

"Go," he said from his wheelchair at the head of the pavilion. "Prepare. Tonight we do not train for war. Tonight we remember why we fight to have a home at all."

The cheer that went up shook the grapes off the arbor.

Percy spent the afternoon with the Poseidon and Ares kids hauling logs to the amphitheater. A thousand campfires deserved a big one, and Beckendorf's old plans for a pyramid stack were pulled out of the Hephaestus files and followed with reverent care. Clarisse directed like a general, pointing with a spear.

"Higher! No, not that log, that one is crooked, are you blind? Tyson, that one there, yes, perfect, good job little brother."

Tyson beamed every time Clarisse called him little brother. He was taller than the stack already, but he still ducked his head shyly and placed each log as gently as if it were made of eggshells.

Percy wiped sweat off his forehead and grinned at his sister. "You're in bossy mode."

"I'm always in bossy mode," Clarisse said. "This is just bossy mode with a purpose."

Down by the Big House, the Aphrodite cabin had set up a shirt station and a flower crown station and a face paint station all in a row. Silena Beauregard moved between them like sunlight itself, her dark hair pulled back with a blue bandana, her new thousandth campfire shirt already smudged with gold paint.

When she saw Percy she ran to him and threw her arms around his neck, paint and all.

"Percy! Isn't it perfect? The weather, the fire, everything. I told Drew if it rains I'm holding her personally responsible."

Percy hugged her back, laughing. "You've done amazing, Si. The shirts are great."

Silena pulled back, eyes bright. She had that look she got when she was holding back tears and trying to turn them into organizing energy.

"I just keep thinking," she said. "A thousand campfires. How many kids sat around them? How many of them were scared on their first night and then somebody sang too loud and made them laugh? I was scared my first night. Do you remember?"

Percy could not remember Silena’s first night in the childhood this camp knew. But she had told him about crying quietly into her pillow in Cabin Three because she missed home and was afraid the harpies would get her if she snored. He remembered a different night, soon after his arrival here, bringing an extra blanket and telling her terrible jokes about Poseidon until she laughed.

"I remember our night with the extra blanket," he said softly. "You shared your animal crackers with me."

"And you told me the campfire would keep the scary dreams away if I sang along," Silena said, wiping her eyes and laughing. "You liar. It totally worked, though."

Clarisse came up behind them and slung an arm around Silena's shoulders without a word, squeezing. Silena leaned into her. Percy put his arm around both of them.

Siblings. Not by mortal blood, but by sea god blood, by the kitchen cabin table where they had all done homework together, by a thousand small rescues. His sisters.

"Save some paint for tonight," Clarisse muttered, but she did not let go.

---

By late afternoon the amphitheater had transformed.

Garlands of grapevine and olive branch hung from the stone steps. Lanterns made by the Hephaestus cabin, blue fire in bronze cages that would not burn anyone, lined the aisles. The Apollo cabin had set up a low platform with guitars and violins and drums and Grover's special stool left empty in the middle.

And at the top of the steps, under a white canopy to protect it from sun, lay something long and rolled, covered with a sheet. Two figures guarded it.

One was Rachel Elizabeth Dare, the Oracle, paint on her nose and a pencil behind her ear. The other was Ella, the harpy, her red feathers preened neat, her talons clutching a leather satchel full of scroll tags, muttering to herself.

"Ella is excited. Ella has counted and recounted. One thousand campfires. One thousand stories. Ella will remember them all so no one forgets."

Percy climbed up with Annabeth to say hi. Annabeth hugged Rachel, then crouched to be eye-level with Ella.

"Is it ready?" Annabeth asked gently.

Ella nodded furiously, her eyes bright. "Ella and Rachel made a timeline. A timeline scroll. From the first campfire to the thousandth. Every hero. Every song. Every time Percy fell in the lake and Annabeth laughed and Thalia said — Thalia said —"

"'That's my baby sister's boyfriend, be nice,'" Thalia supplied, appearing behind them with two lemonades. She handed one to Annabeth and one to Percy. "I did say that. Once. Under duress."

"You say it weekly," Annabeth said.

"I say it threateningly," Thalia corrected. She ruffled Ella's feathers lightly. "You did good, Ella. I saw the early draft. It's going to make everyone cry."

"Ella hopes for good crying. Not sad crying. Remember crying," Ella said. "Ella wrote down the slow dance too. Chapter forty-two. Ella remembers. Percy stepped on Annabeth's toes twelve times. Annabeth said artistic interpretation."

Percy choked on his lemonade. Annabeth went scarlet.

"Ella!" Annabeth said. "You remembered the number?"

"Ella remembers everything," Ella said proudly. "Ella is the memory. Twelve times. Percy said sorry fifteen times. Grover played pipes. Thalia and Clarisse watched from the steps and pretended not to care."

Thalia stared. "How did you — we didn't tell anyone that part."

"Ella sees. Ella was in the rafters. Ella always sees sisters watching. Sisters always pretend not to care. Then later they cry."

Clarisse, arriving with an armload of blankets, stopped dead. "I do not cry."

"You cried at the campfire when Percy danced," Ella said matter-of-factly. "Silent crying. One tear. Left eye."

The whole group burst out laughing. Clarisse went red to her ears.

"I had smoke in my eye!" she snapped, dumping the blankets. "Stupid harpy with stupid perfect memory. I'm confiscating that scroll."

"You will do no such thing," Silena said, arriving just in time to hear, wrapping an arm around Ella. "Ella's scroll is sacred. And you did cry. I saw. It was cute."

Clarisse looked like she wanted to argue, but Silena leaned her head against her shoulder and she deflated.

"Fine," she muttered. "Maybe one tear. It was dusty."

Thalia held up her lemonade. "To dusty amphitheaters."

Everyone clinked cups, even Ella with the tip of her wing.

---

At sunset the conch blew for the feast.

It was the best dinner anyone could remember. The tables groaned under platters from the kitchens and from Demeter's fields and from the nymphs who brought honey and berries as gifts. Mr. D, in a surprisingly festive Hawaiian shirt with flames on it, waved his hand and made the goblets refill with whatever each camper loved most. He even stood for a moment before the offerings, which he never did.

"A thousand campfires," he said, sounding almost sincere under the bored drawl. "I have been here for a great many of them. Most of you are insufferable. Some of you are tolerable. All of you are, annoyingly, family. Try not to burn the place down tonight. I would have to fill out paperwork."

"To Mr. D!" Travis shouted, and everyone cheered, and Mr. D pretended to be annoyed but sat a little straighter.

Percy sat with Annabeth between the Poseidon and Zeus tables, which everyone had pushed together for the night. Thalia sat across from them with Clarisse and Silena on either side, and Grover squeezed in next to Percy, his goat legs folded awkwardly under the bench, his reed pipes tucked in his pocket and his new button that said STAFF SATYR - ASK ME ABOUT NATURE gleaming on his shirt.

Amphitrite was there too, on a short surface visit cleared by Chiron, along with a few trusted mortal parents who knew. Triton would escort her back to Atlantis after the celebration. She sat near the Poseidon table, beaming at her sea son and his sisters and the two Grace sisters as if her heart might burst. Triton had his camera and was taking pictures of everything, and Beryl Grace was in quiet conversation with Chiron about the boundary wards. After her recent letter, the Grace girls had agreed to this day visit with Chiron nearby; it was a cautious step, not a promise that everything was healed.

After the offerings burned, Chiron rose. The pavilion fell silent.

"One thousand campfires," he said. His voice carried without shouting, old and warm as woodsmoke. "I lit the first of this count with my own hands, on this hill, when the camp was young again. I have watched a thousand fires catch, a thousand sparks rise. Each one the same and each one different, because each one had different children around it."

He looked around the pavilion, meeting eyes table by table.

"Tonight is not for training. Tonight is for remembering. We will go to the amphitheater. We will sing. We will tell stories. We will unroll the timeline our Oracle and our historian have made. And our oldest friend will speak."

He nodded to Grover. Grover went very still, his ears twitching nervously. Percy squeezed his shoulder hard.

"You will be great, G-man," Percy whispered.

Grover swallowed. "I rewrote it eleven times. Juniper says the eleventh is good but the ninth made her cry more."

"Go with eleven," Annabeth whispered. "Crying is good but breathing is also good."

Everyone laughed, and the procession to the amphitheater began, lanterns bobbing in the dark like fireflies, parents and campers and satyrs and nymphs all flowing downhill together.

---

The fire caught fast, the way it always did when it knew it was loved.

The Hephaestus kids lit it with a cheer, and flames climbed the pyramid stack, crackling and popping, sending a spiral of sparks straight up into the indigo sky where the first stars were already pricking through. The whole amphitheater glowed gold. A thousand faces turned golden. The smoke curled sweet and sharp.

Percy sat with his family halfway up the steps, on Silena's blue blanket, the same one from years ago, now faded and soft and embroidered with extra flowers. Annabeth tucked against his side, her head on his shoulder. Thalia sprawled on Annabeth's other side, boots crossed at the ankles, leather jacket over her camp shirt despite the warm evening. Clarisse and Silena sat just below them with Tyson and Grover and Juniper, passing popcorn.

It felt exactly like that night when they were twelve, and also completely different, because they were older now and the circle was wider and the fire was bigger.

Chiron raised his hands. "Who will start the remembering?"

Hands shot up. A little Hermes kid, maybe eight, stood and shouted, "The time Percy fought the Minotaur with no pants!"

Laughter exploded. Percy buried his face in Annabeth's hair.

"Hey!" he protested. "I had pants. They were just — torn. Strategically torn."

"You were nine!" the kid said. "And you still won!"

"Tell it right," Clarisse called, grinning. "Tell how his big sisters found him afterward and Silena gave him her jacket to cover up and I carried his sword because his arms were shaking too hard to hold it."

Silena nodded, eyes shining. "He was so brave and so embarrassed. He kept saying sorry for bleeding on my jacket."

"You kept that jacket," Percy said softly.

"Of course I kept it," Silena said. "It's in my trunk. First quest souvenir from my little brother."

The stories rolled like waves after that.

A Demeter girl told the story of the Grace sisters’ arrival, how Thalia had stood on Half-Blood Hill after getting them both past the monsters, blinking in the sun, and the first thing she said was, "Where’s my sister? Where’s Annabeth?" And Annabeth, only seven then, had broken through the crowd after the healer checked her and launched herself at Thalia so hard they both fell over. Annabeth held the image as a story told to her, a childhood she still could not recall.

Thalia's arm tightened around Annabeth as they listened. Annabeth reached up and laced her fingers with her sister's without looking.

An Apollo kid told the story of Grover playing pipes to stop a wildfire on the ridge, playing for six hours straight until his lips bled, until rain came. Grover ducked his head, ears red.

A Hephaestus boy told the story of Clarisse holding the drakon line alone for three minutes while the wounded were dragged back, roaring so loud the drakon hesitated. Clarisse stared at her popcorn.

"You never told us it was three minutes," Silena whispered. "You said it was a few seconds."

Clarisse shrugged, jaw tight. "Felt like seconds."

Silena took her hand and held it.

Then Katie Gardner stood and told the story of Annabeth sick with fever in Cabin Three’s guest bunk, how Percy and Thalia never left, how Clarisse and Silena ran cold spring water all day, how Percy held her hand through Tartarus dreams and did not let go even when she sparked. Annabeth had gone very still against Percy's side.

Katie finished softly. "Will says fevers fight harder when the body knows it's safe. I think that's what family is. People who make your body feel safe enough to heal."

There was hardly a dry eye in the amphitheater. Amphitrite was openly crying, Triton's arm around her. Thalia pressed her forehead to the top of Annabeth's head. Percy pressed his lips to Annabeth's temple.

Annabeth whispered, "You held on."

"I always hold on," Percy whispered back. "Always."

Between stories, the Apollo cabin played.

They played the old campfire songs, the silly ones about eating goblins and the ghost in the canoe, and everyone sang too loud and off-key on purpose. They played the brave ones, the hymns to the gods that made the fire burn bluer. Austin Lake, now tall and confident, played guitar. Kayla Knowles played violin. And in the middle, Grover played pipes.

His music threaded through everything like water through stones. Wild and old. Wind in tall grass and rain on leaves. Percy closed his eyes and listened, Annabeth's hand in his, and thought about how Grover had played for them when they were twelve and learning to dance, and how he was playing for them now, and how some things did not change, they only grew deeper.

After the songs, Chiron nodded to Grover.

It was time for the speech.

---

Grover Underwood stood up on legs that shook, and walked down to the front of the fire alone.

He was not a kid anymore, not really. He was Lord of the Wild now, bearer of Pan's spirit, with lines at the corners of his eyes and a circlet of grapevine in his curly hair that Juniper had woven for him that afternoon. But as he stood before a thousand faces and one great fire, clutching his pipes in one hand and a crumpled piece of paper in the other, Percy could still see the nervous young satyr who had once eaten tin cans and pretended to be brave on a bus to the Underworld.

Grover looked at the paper. He looked at the fire. He folded the paper and put it away.

A murmur rippled. Juniper, sitting with her hands clasped so tight her knuckles were white, smiled through tears and mouthed, Good. Eleven was the no-paper one.

Grover took a breath.

"Hi," he said.

Everyone laughed gently.

"I'm Grover," he said. "Which is stupid, because you all know that. But Pan, my teacher in the wild — he taught me that you should always start by saying who you are, because the wild doesn't care about titles. The wild cares if you show up."

He twisted his pipes in his hands.

"I've been to — I don't know how many of these campfires. Hundreds. I remember my first one. I was so scared I hid behind Chiron's wheelchair and only came out when somebody gave me a marshmallow. I burned it immediately. Chiron ate it anyway and said it was perfect."

Chiron smiled, eyes wet.

"I've played at funerals here," Grover said quietly. "Too many. Shrouds in the fire. Names on beads. Kids who should have had a thousand campfires and only got ten. I think about them every time I play. I play for them too."

Silence fell, deep and respectful. The fire popped.

"But mostly," Grover said, voice strengthening, "I think about what the fire is. It's not just wood. It's not just magic that makes it blue when the gods are happy. It's — it's a promise. Every night we light it and we sit in a circle, and the circle means nobody is outside. The new kid who just arrived and doesn't know anyone, the old kid who's leaving for college, the satyr who failed his searcher's license twice —" laughter — "the daughter of Zeus who keeps watch for her little sister, the son of Poseidon who just learned he has two sisters and doesn't know what to do with that — we all sit in the same circle. Same firelight on our faces. Same songs."

He looked straight at Percy, at Annabeth, at Thalia, at Clarisse, at Silena.

"I have best friends," Grover said simply. "Percy Jackson is my best friend. Annabeth Grace is my best friend. Thalia is my best friend when she's not threatening to zap me. Clarisse is my best friend when she's not threatening to punch me. Silena is my best friend who gives me cookies. That's a lot of best friends, and the fire gave them to me."

Everyone laughed through tears.

"People ask me what the Lord of the Wild does," Grover said. "I protect places. I grow things. But mostly, I think — I witness. I witness kids becoming heroes and heroes becoming siblings and siblings becoming family. I witness Percy holding Annabeth's hand through a fever. I witness Thalia braiding Annabeth's hair too tight on purpose. I witness Clarisse saving seats and pretending she didn't. I witness Silena remembering everyone's birthday and favorite song."

Clarisse wiped her eyes furiously. Silena openly wept on her shoulder.

"Ella counted a thousand campfires," Grover said. "That's a lot of witnessing. That's a lot of marshmallows." He paused. "I don't know how many more we'll get. I hope it's a thousand more. I hope it's ten thousand. But I know this — tonight, right now, we are here. The fire is lit. The circle is unbroken. Nobody is outside."

He raised his pipes.

"To the next hundred," he said. "To the next thousand. To showing up."

The cheer that went up shook sparks into the sky. Juniper was on her feet, clapping and crying. Percy was on his feet without realizing it, shouting Grover's name, Annabeth beside him whistling with two fingers the way Thalia taught her. Thalia had her fingers in her mouth whistling loud enough to startle the harpies in the rafters.

Grover bowed, ears scarlet, and hurried back to his seat where Juniper threw her arms around him and Percy pounded him on the back so hard he nearly fell over.

"You folded the paper!" Percy said. "You didn't even read it!"

"It was the eleventh draft," Grover said breathily. "Juniper said speak from the heart. So I did."

"You were perfect," Annabeth said, hugging him fiercely. "Absolutely perfect."

---

After the speech came the scroll.

Rachel and Ella carried it to the front together, Ella flying just above the ground to help unroll, Rachel walking backward with the other end. Campers gasped as it kept coming, foot after foot after foot, painted in Rachel's vivid colors and annotated in Ella's tiny perfect script with quotes and dates and cross-references.

It stretched across the entire front of the amphitheater, held up by volunteers from every cabin.

The Timeline of a Thousand Campfires.

Percy leaned forward, Annabeth perched on the edge of her seat, Thalia standing to see better, Clarisse and Silena craning around Tyson's broad shoulders.

There it was, all of it.

The first campfire, Chiron alone with three nervous demigods and one nervous satyr.

The arrival of the Big House. The first capture the flag. The first shroud.

Then faster, brighter. Thalia on the hill. Percy arriving. Annabeth running to meet him. Grover's first search. Clarisse's first chariot race win. Silena's first healing song that actually worked.

Sketches in the margins. A trident dripping with lake water. An owl with gray eyes. A boundary pine with two sisters beneath it, smiling. A spear crossed with a flower. A set of panpipes with musical notes turning into leaves.

Quotes Ella had memorized. "Seaweed Brain." "Wise Girl." "Thally." "Kelp Head." "For Pan." "For family." "I Got You." "Never Let Go."

Annabeth traced the line with her finger when they were allowed to come close during the intermission. She stopped at a small panel Rachel had painted with exquisite care.

Two stick figures in a dirt circle. Firelight. Musical notes. An arrow pointing to them that said us later? And underneath, in Ella's hand, Chapter 42 — First Slow Dance — Percy stepped on toes 12 times, said sorry 15 times, both perfect.

Annabeth made a small sound. Percy looked over her shoulder and felt his throat close.

"They remembered," Annabeth whispered.

"Ella remembers everything," Percy said softly. He took Annabeth's hand. "Do you want to — I mean — would you —"

Annabeth looked at him, gray eyes shining gold in the firelight. She was older now, but the look was the same. Steady. Patient. Willing to wait for him to get there.

"Are you asking me to dance, Seaweed Brain?" she asked.

Percy held out his hand, palm open, a little shaky, exactly the way he had when he was twelve.

"Annabeth Grace," he said, and his voice cracked just slightly on the middle of her name, exactly the way it had then, "do you want to dance with me?"

It came out too fast. Too loud. A kid from the Nemesis cabin two rows up turned to look, exactly the way someone had then.

Annabeth blinked. Then she smiled, wide and bright, that crinkled the corners of her eyes.

"Yeah," she said. "Yeah, I do."

She stood and slipped her hand into his. Her fingers were warm. Longer now, with calluses from pencils and climbing and dagger grips. Her palm fit against his like it had been measured for him.

Thalia saw them and immediately made a loud kissing noise. "Thalia!" Annabeth hissed, cheeks pink, and the whole amphitheater laughed because half of them had heard this story before and had been waiting for it.

"She will stab you if you step on her feet, Jackson!" Thalia called cheerfully.

"I will not!" Annabeth said over her shoulder.

"She will! I've seen her practice knives!"

Percy laughed, shaky but real, and led Annabeth down to the dirt circle. Austin, understanding without being asked, strummed a chord. It rang out clear and bright. Kayla joined on violin. And Grover, from his seat, lifted his pipes and joined in, eyes closed.

The slow song. The same progression, grown richer with years of playing.

Percy and Annabeth stepped into the circle together.

---

They were better now. A little.

Percy put one hand on Annabeth's waist without having to be told. Annabeth rested her hand on his shoulder without hesitation. Their other hands joined, fingers laced.

They swayed. Step, step, pause. Step, step, pause. Dust scuffed around their sneakers, small clouds in the firelight.

"You still look at my feet," Annabeth whispered, teasing.

"I'm checking for toes," Percy whispered back. "I've learned. I'm a toe-conscious dancer now."

Annabeth laughed, that same breathy laugh, and leaned her forehead gently against his.

The world narrowed to that small point of contact. Forehead to forehead. Breath to breath. Music wrapping around them like water.

Percy could hear Grover's pipes, clear and tender. He could smell woodsmoke and Annabeth's shampoo and the faint salt that always clung to his own skin. He could feel Annabeth's hand holding his like an anchor.

He thought about being twelve and terrified. He thought about remembering his older life while living young again. He thought about fevers and sparks and Tartarus and bacon saved from breakfast and cold washcloths and a blue blanket with flowers.

"I'm glad it's us," he whispered, the same words he had said at twelve, because they were still true. "I'm glad we get to do this. Without rushing. Just music and fire and —"

"And stepping on my feet?" Annabeth supplied.

"I haven't stepped on them yet!" Percy protested. "I'm doing great. This is growth."

"You stepped on my foot twice getting down the steps," Annabeth said solemnly. "I counted. Ella will want the number."

Percy groaned. "Tell Ella it was artistic interpretation."

"Already did," Annabeth said. She pulled back just enough to look at him, eyes bright, cheeks flushed. "We're doing perfect."

They danced through the whole song while the camp watched and smiled and pretended not to stare. Little kids whispered. Older campers leaned on each other. Triton filmed while Amphitrite watched with tears running down her face.

When the song ended, neither of them moved right away.

Applause broke out. Annabeth squeezed his hand.

"Next time?" Percy asked quietly, echoing his twelve-year-old self.

Annabeth squeezed back. "Every time."

They climbed the steps hand in hand, dazed and happy, and found their seats saved again.

A blanket spread across three steps. Two cups of water. A plate of cookies.

Clarisse saw them looking and scowled. "What? Silena saved those. Not me. I just — I was sitting here. Don't make it weird."

It was word for word what she had said at twelve, and Thalia burst out laughing so hard she nearly fell off the step.

"She saved you seats, she means," Thalia said, enunciating clearly through laughter. "She fought off three Hermes kids for this spot. It was violent. It was beautiful."

"I did not fight anybody," Clarisse snapped, ears pink as everyone laughed harder. "I told them to move and they moved because I am intimidating."

Annabeth looked at Clarisse, eyes soft. "Thanks, Clarisse."

Clarisse looked away. "Whatever. Sit down before I change my mind and take the blanket back."

Percy and Annabeth sat. The stone was warm. The blanket was soft. The water was cold. The cookies were chocolate chip, still a little warm.

Percy handed Annabeth a cup first. She bumped her shoulder against his in thanks.

Thalia leaned over Annabeth's head to smirk at Percy. "So. Jackson. How were the toes? Did my sister survive?"

"My toes survived," Annabeth said.

"Barely," Percy said. "I'm going to practice. So next time I don't —"

"Next time?" Thalia interrupted, eyebrows shooting up, playing her part to perfection. She looked at Annabeth. "Next time?"

Annabeth went scarlet. "Thalia!"

"What? I'm just repeating what he said. Next time." Thalia clutched her chest dramatically. "My baby sister is growing up. Having next times."

Everyone around them howled. Even Clarisse laughed, a short surprised bark she tried to turn into a cough. Silena leaned against her, giggling.

Percy put his arm carefully around Annabeth's shoulders. Annabeth settled against him like she belonged there, head tipped to rest lightly against his temple.

It was the same and it was different. The same blanket. The same joke. The same fire. But now Thalia's hair had a streak of gray from weathering the winter storms on her scouting trips. Now Clarisse had a scar on her chin from the drakon line. Now Silena had a healer's bead on her necklace. Now Grover had a circlet and Juniper's hand in his. Now Percy and Annabeth had years of holding on behind them.

---

Later, when the fire had burned low and the little kids had fallen asleep on blankets and the songs had gone soft and slow, Thalia stood and stretched.

She looked at Clarisse. She looked at Silena.

"Walk?" she said simply.

Clarisse frowned. "Where?"

"Just up. Top of the steps. Air."

Silena stood immediately, understanding something Percy did not yet understand. She held out a hand to Clarisse.

"Come on," Silena said softly.

Clarisse grumbled but stood, and the three of them climbed to the top of the amphitheater together, past the rolled edge of the timeline scroll, past the lanterns, to where the hill overlooked the Sound and the stars were thick overhead.

Percy watched them go, curious. Annabeth watched too, her head still on his shoulder.

"What are they doing?" Percy whispered.

"Being big sisters," Annabeth whispered back.

At the top of the steps, Thalia stopped and turned to face the other two. For a moment she just looked at them, tall and still in the starlight, her black hair lifting in the sea breeze, her electric blue eyes bright.

Then she said, very quietly, "Thank you."

Clarisse blinked. "For what?"

"For my sister," Thalia said. "For that fever day. For the cold water. For running. For guarding the door. For — for all of it. I was six when I first held her through a fever. I thought it was always going to be just me. Then camp. Then you. Then the cabin cots and the shared kitchen table and all of it. I never said — I never say it right. But thank you for loving her like sisters."

Silena's eyes filled instantly. "Oh, Thalia."

Clarisse looked away sharply, jaw working. "Gods. Don't get mushy, Grace."

"I mean it," Thalia said. Her voice shook just slightly. "Annabeth would have died in Tartarus without Percy. She would have died of a fever without all of you. She would have died of loneliness without — without people who saved her seats and brought her water and pretended not to cry when she danced. You're her sisters too. Camp siblings count."

Silena made a small sound and threw her arms around Thalia. Thalia caught her, surprised, then held on tight.

Clarisse stood stiffly for a second, arms at her sides, glaring at the stars like they had offended her. Then she muttered something that sounded like, "Oh, what the Hades," and stepped in and wrapped her arms around both of them.

The three of them stood there in a knot at the top of the hill, tall and strong and crying and laughing all at once.

Thalia, daughter of Zeus, who ran fast and hit hard and slept under open sky.

Clarisse, daughter of Poseidon, who held lines alone and saved seats and pretended not to care.

Silena, daughter of Poseidon, who remembered birthdays and healed with songs and loved so big it spilled over.

Big sisters. All of them.

Down below, Annabeth watched with tears on her cheeks. Percy put his arm tighter around her and felt her shake with quiet crying.

"They're hugging," Annabeth whispered. "Thalia never hugs in public. Clarisse never hugs at all."

"They're hugging for you," Percy whispered. "Because you're loved."

Annabeth turned and buried her face in his shoulder for a moment. When she looked up, her eyes were wet but smiling.

"I have the best sisters," she said. "And the best boyfriend."

Percy kissed the top of her head. "We have the best sisters. Plural. I'm sharing mine with you and you're sharing yours with me. That's the deal."

"Best deal," Annabeth said.

The fire crackled. Grover played one last soft tune, barely louder than breathing. Ella, perched in the rafters with Rachel, recited quietly to herself, "One thousand campfires. Sisters hugged. Percy did not step on toes this time. Growth. Ella will record growth."

Everyone laughed when they heard, even Clarisse from the top of the hill, who shouted, "I heard that, harpy!"

---

The party wound down the way campfires always wound down, slowly and reluctantly, with people drifting off in twos and threes, carrying sleeping little siblings, folding blankets, whispering.

Chiron banked the fire but did not put it out. The thousand-and-first fire would be lit from its coals in the morning, continuity unbroken.

Percy and Annabeth stayed.

They climbed past the sleeping forms and the stacked plates, past Grover dozing with his head in Juniper's lap and Tyson snoring with Ella nestled against his shoulder like a feathered football, past Thalia and Clarisse and Silena sitting shoulder to shoulder on the top step now, talking quietly with their heads together, past Amphitrite and Triton waving goodnight as they headed to the Big House guest room.

Percy and Annabeth went to their spot on Half-Blood Hill, just below the pine tree, where the grass was soft and the view opened to the Sound and the whole bowl of stars.

They lay back side by side in the grass, hands linked between them, sneakers touching.

The stars burned cold and bright overhead. The sea whispered. The banked campfire glowed faintly below, smoke rising straight up like a prayer.

Annabeth pointed. "There's Zeus. There's Heracles. There's the Huntress."

Percy pointed. "There's Bob. Ella added him to the scroll. Small star near the doors. She said everyone who holds the doors deserves a star."

Annabeth was quiet for a moment. Then she turned her head to look at him.

"A thousand campfires," she said.

"A thousand," Percy said.

"We were at — what, maybe a hundred of them?"

"Maybe more," Percy said. "Counting winters. Counting sick days in Cabin Three when we could see the glow through the window."

Annabeth laced her fingers tighter through his. "Grover said to the next hundred."

Percy looked at her, then up at the stars, then back at her. Firelight still clung to her hair. Grass in her curls. Marshmallow on her cheek, just like when she was twelve.

He brushed it off gently with his thumb.

"To the next hundred," he said.

Annabeth smiled, small and private, just for him.

"To the next hundred," she whispered. "To the next thousand. To showing up."

They lay there long after the lanterns went out, two children of gods who had been given a second chance to be children, a son of Poseidon and a daughter of Zeus, romantic partners bound by the life they remembered and every new fever watch and dance, looking at stars and holding hands while the thousandth fire burned low below and the future stretched bright and uncounted ahead.

Somewhere behind them, on the top step, three big sisters laughed about something and fell silent and laughed again.

Somewhere in the rafters, a harpy murmured in her sleep, "One thousand campfires. Remembered. All remembered."

The sparks rose.

The circle held.

Nobody was outside.
