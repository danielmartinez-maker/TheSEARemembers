## Chapter 88: Spear, Song, Sea

In the next week of June, the morning after the war council broke for rest, Percy Jackson woke to someone hammering on the Poseidon cabin door with the butt of a spear.

He knew, without opening his eyes, exactly who it was.

"Up," Clarisse La Rue barked through the wood. "You drool in your sleep, little brother. It's embarrassing for the bloodline."

Percy groaned into his pillow. In the bunk across from him, Silena Beauregard — his sister, whatever surnames they kept — laughed sleepily and threw a pillow at the door.

"Go away, Clarisse! It's barely dawn!"

"It's 0700," Clarisse said. "Barely dawn is for Aphrodite kids. Poseidon kids train at dawn. Dad would be ashamed."

Percy sat up, hair sticking in ten directions, sea-green eyes bleary. Silena was propped up on one elbow, dark hair in a braid coming undone, blue eyes bright with mischief even half-asleep. The Poseidon cabin was too big for one person and just right for three. Since Percy had joined his sisters at camp, Silena's trunk and Clarisse's spare armor stand had shared the room with his things. No one had argued. Even Mr. D had just poured another Diet Coke and muttered, "About time."

Percy padded to the door and opened it. Clarisse stood there in full training gear, spear over her shoulder, dark hair tied back, brown eyes fierce and fond all at once.

"Morning to you too, sis," Percy said.

Clarisse stared at him for half a second. Then she shoved past him into the cabin, spear thumping down.

"Don't call me that when I'm trying to be intimidating," she muttered, but her ears were red.

Silena sat up fully, grinning. "She likes it."

"I will stab you," Clarisse said without heat.

"You won't," Silena said. "You love us."

Clarisse looked between them — Percy barefoot in pajama pants with a trident on his shirt, Silena wrapped in a blanket covered in pegasi — and something in her face softened, just for a breath, before the La Rue scowl slammed back down.

"One week," she said. "We have one week before the next patrol rotation, before Chiron starts throwing quests at us again, before —" She didn't say *before Luke does something*. She didn't have to. "We keep fighting like three solo demigods who happen to share a dad, we're going to get each other killed. So. One week. Each of us teaches the other two our thing."

Percy blinked. "Our thing?"

"My thing is not getting stabbed," Clarisse said. "Your thing is fish magic. Silena's thing is —" She waved vaguely at Silena, who was unfairly pretty even with bedhead. "Pretty magic and not dying of poison."

"Charm and care," Silena corrected, tossing her braid over her shoulder. "Spear, song, sea. I like it."

Percy felt it then, that tug low in his gut that was never just his — the sea pulling, recognizing its own. Two heartbeats beside his, salt and iron and honey. Siblings.

"I'm in," he said.

Silena threw off her blanket. "I'm in. But if Clarisse makes me do push-ups in the sand, I'm telling Annabeth you cried during *The Little Mermaid*."

"I did not cry," Clarisse snapped. "I had seawater in my eyes. From my father. Shut up."

---

Clarisse taught first because Clarisse insisted, and no one won arguments with Clarisse before breakfast except Silena, and Silena was too busy braiding Percy's hair to argue.

"No," Clarisse said, staring at the two braids Silena had put in Percy's hair with little shells tied at the ends. "Absolutely not. Take those out before Ares sees them and I never live it down."

"You look cute," Silena told Percy, patting his head.

"I feel fast," Percy said solemnly. "Like aerodynamic."

Annabeth Grace arrived at the arena five minutes later with a notebook, Thalia Grace trailing behind her with a breakfast burrito and a look that said she would rather be electrocuted than be up this early. They settled on the bleachers as self-appointed judges.

"For moral support," Annabeth said, flipping her notebook open. She caught Percy's eye and smiled, just a quick bright thing that made his ears go hot. "And to document Clarisse committing war crimes against your footwork."

"Hey!" Percy said.

"She's not wrong," Thalia said around a mouthful of burrito. "Your footwork is tragic, Kelp Head."

Clarisse clapped her hands once, spear ringing against her shield. The sound snapped across the arena.

"Rule one," she said. "Forget everything Ares taught you about looking scary. Forget everything Athena taught you about twelve-step plans. Sparring forms are about living through the first thirty seconds."

She demonstrated. Percy had fought beside Clarisse half a dozen times and he'd never actually *watched* her move when she wasn't trying to kill something. Now, in the empty arena with morning light pooling gold on the sand, she was precise. Spear became an extension of her spine. Advance, turn, low guard, high guard, shield punch, spear sweep. Each motion economical, brutal, honest.

"Again," she ordered. "Copy me."

Percy tried. He was water — fluid, improvising, all instinct and current. Forms felt like trying to fold the ocean into a box. His spear wobbled. His shield dipped.

Silena was worse, and delighted about it. She spun when she should have stepped and nearly braided her spear into her hair.

"Stop dancing!" Clarisse barked.

"I am fighting gracefully!" Silena protested, laughing as she stumbled.

"No, you're flirting with the dummy and the dummy is winning!"

Annabeth was laughing so hard she dropped her pencil. Thalia was openly filming on a bronze Kodak-looking thing Leo had rigged.

What followed was two hours of glorious failure. Clarisse broke the forms into eights, the way an Ares instructor had once broken them for her when she was small and furious and too small for her spear. She counted aloud, voice carrying across the sand, and corrected with the flat of her spear — tap to Percy's calf when he overstepped, tap to Silena's elbow when she lifted too high. Never cruel. Always exact.

"You fight like water," she told Percy during a water break, wiping sweat from her brow with her forearm. "That's good in chaos. It's bad when you're tired, because water without a bank just spreads out and dies. Forms are your banks. They hold you when your brain quits."

Percy stared at her. "That was almost poetic."

"Shut up and drink your water."

Silena learned differently. She couldn't remember counts, but she could remember rhythm, so she set Form One to the chorus of the shanty she'd teach them later, humming under her breath with each turn. Step-turn-guard, step-turn-guard. Clarisse watched her, frowning, then nodded slowly.

"Again," Clarisse said. "With the humming. Louder. Percy, follow her tempo, not mine."

It worked. Percy stopped overthinking and followed Silena's voice, and suddenly his spear stopped wobbling. Advance on the downbeat, turn on the lift. Sand scuffed under their feet in matching arcs. Annabeth sat straight up on the bleachers, pencil flying, and muttered, "Oh, that's interesting," in her strategist voice that meant she'd seen a pattern worth stealing.

Thalia came down to the sand after an hour, bored of watching. "Give me a stick," she demanded. Clarisse tossed her a practice spear without looking. Thalia fell into the line beside Silena, taller and crackling faintly with static, and proceeded to be terrible at Form Two in a way that made Percy feel much better about himself.

"You're all leaning left," Annabeth called down, unable to help herself. "Clarisse, your back foot — Percy, I told you about your footwork — Silena, lower!"

"Stay out of this, Grace!" Clarisse barked, but she glanced at her back foot and fixed it.

By late afternoon they ran the full Form One together, four abreast if you counted Thalia, sweat sticking their hair to their foreheads. When they finished in something approaching unison, Clarisse stood silent for a long moment, spear grounded, chest heaving.

"Not awful," she allowed, which from her was a parade with fireworks.

Silena whooped and hugged her around the neck, and Clarisse, too tired to fight it, let herself be hugged.

"Okay, okay," Percy said, panting. "Show me again. Slower."

Clarisse exhaled through her nose. She stepped behind him, adjusted his grip with rough hands — not unkind, just certain. "Wrist like this, idiot. You're holding it like a mop. Elbow in. There. Feel that?"

He did. The spear settled.

She moved to Silena, and her voice dropped a fraction. "You lead with your shoulder every time because you're afraid of overextending your wrist from —" From the poison, from a recent patrol wound that still made her careful. She didn't say it. "Here. Let me wrap it."

She wrapped Silena's wrist with a practiced strip of linen. Silena watched her face the whole time, soft and steady.

"Better?" Clarisse asked gruffly.

"Much," Silena said. "My fierce big sister."

"I am literally younger than you," Clarisse muttered, but she didn't move away immediately.

By the end of the first day Percy could hold Form One without falling over. By day two he could transition to Form Two while Clarisse shouted counts and Silena chanted them back like a cheer routine until even Clarisse was laughing despite herself.

On the second afternoon Clarisse changed tactics. "Spar me," she said to Percy. "No powers. Just spear."

Percy swallowed. "You're going to kill me."

"I'm going to teach you," Clarisse corrected. "There's a difference. Small one."

They circled. Annabeth leaned forward on the bleachers, pencil forgotten. Thalia whistled.

Percy lasted forty seconds the first time before Clarisse swept his legs and put a blunted spear tip to his throat.

"Again," she said, offering a hand up.

He lasted a minute. Then two. He started to read her — not her plan, because Clarisse didn't plan like Annabeth, she *pressed* — but her rhythm. When she breathed in before a shield bash. When her weight shifted.

On the third day, sweating and bruised and grinning with sand in his hair, Percy caught her spear on his shield, turned it the way she'd drilled, and tapped her breastplate with his own spear butt.

The arena went silent.

Clarisse stared down at the spear touching her chest. Then, slowly, a grin split her face, fierce and proud.

"Good," she said. "Finally. You fight like one of us."

Silena cheered so loud the Apollo kids came to check if someone was dying. Annabeth was on her feet, clapping, eyes shining. Percy felt his face go red all the way to his ears.

What no one recorded in Annabeth's notebook, but all three of them remembered, was what happened after the crowd drifted off. Clarisse sat Percy and Silena down in the sand with two cups of water and made them walk through every mistake aloud, the Ares after-action ritual she claimed to hate.

"Percy, you drop your shield when you breathe in," she said bluntly. "You think no one notices because you're fast. I notice. Silena, you close your eyes when you thrust because you don't like seeing the hit land. Don't. Look at it. You owe the fight your eyes."

Silena swallowed and nodded, uncharacteristically solemn. Percy bumped his knee against hers.

"Your turn," Percy said to Clarisse. "Critique yourself, oh fearless leader."

Clarisse scowled. "I overcommit on the shield punch. If someone smaller than me slips it, I'm open for half a second. Happy?"

"Very," Silena said, and kissed her cheek loudly. Clarisse wiped it off with exaggerated disgust while Annabeth, still on the bleachers pretending not to eavesdrop, wrote *mutual critique = trust ritual* and underlined it twice.

Thalia dropped down beside Annabeth and stole her pencil. "They're good for her," she said quietly, nodding at Clarisse, who was now showing Silena how to fall without breaking her wrist, hands careful on Silena's shoulders as she guided her down. "La Rue's been carrying those Ares trainees through every drill. She needed people she doesn't have to be the wall for."

Annabeth watched Percy patiently let Silena practice the disarm on him for the twentieth time, hitting the sand hard and popping right back up with a grin. "They're good for each other," she said. "All three."

"Again!" Silena cried, jumping down from the stands to grab a practice spear. "Me! Teach me to do that!"

Clarisse rolled her eyes, but she spent the next hour drilling Silena on the disarm until Silena's arms shook and she finally landed it, shrieking with triumph and tackling Clarisse in a hug that nearly knocked them both into the sand.

"Careful," Clarisse grunted, but her arm came up around Silena's shoulders and stayed there.

That night Percy iced Silena's wrist and Clarisse iced Percy's shoulder, and Silena braided both their hair while Annabeth quizzed them on guard names from her notebook, and Thalia stole their dessert and said it was a supervision fee.

---

Silena taught second on the third evening, and Silena's classroom was the beach at sunset with a bonfire and a first-aid kit.

"No spears," she announced, spreading a blanket over the sand. "No getting hit. Today we sing."

Clarisse looked deeply suspicious. "This is a trap."

"It's healing," Silena said. "And history. Sit."

Percy sat immediately. Annabeth and Thalia, who had been invited on the strict condition they bring snacks, sat in a loose circle. The sun was melting into the sea, turning the waves to hammered copper. The fire crackled.

Silena pulled out an old, salt-warped lyre and a battered notebook covered in stickers.

"Sea shanties aren't just songs," she said, suddenly serious in a way that made Percy sit up straighter. "They're timing. They're breathing. They're how sailors rowed together without killing each other for three months. And for us — for Poseidon kids — they're focus. Rhythm holds you when the water gets loud."

She taught them *The Leaving of Liver-pool* first, then an older one, half-Greek, half-sailor cant, that her grandmother had sung. Her voice rose clear and sweet over the surf, and Percy felt the water lift slightly with the chorus, keeping time.

Clarisse refused to sing. For two full songs she sat with her arms crossed, mouth a flat line.

"Clarisse La Rue," Silena said sweetly, "if you don't sing, I'll tell everyone you harmonize in your sleep."

"I do not—"

"You do," Percy said. "You hum when you polish your spear. It's cute."

"I will murder both of you in this soft sand where no one will find the bodies."

But on the third chorus Clarisse's deep alto joined in, rough at first, then steady, anchoring them. Percy whooped and missed a verse from laughing. Annabeth leaned against his shoulder, humming the part she didn't know the words to, and Percy forgot the words too because her hair smelled like lemon and sun.

Thalia actually had a good voice, smoky and low, and when she and Silena harmonized on the last line, even the Nereids poked their heads up to listen.

Between songs Silena talked, the way she always taught — story first, lesson hidden inside it like honey in tea. She told them about the trireme crews who sang to keep stroke for eighteen hours, about the divers at Delos who hummed to slow their hearts, about a navigator in the 1800s who sang a whole crew through a hurricane by keeping the shanty steady when the mast cracked.

"That's what we're doing," she said, poking the fire with a stick, sparks spiraling up toward the first stars. "We're learning each other's tempo. Clarisse fights in eights. Percy breathes in tides. I sing in choruses. If we can hear each other underneath the noise, we don't have to look to know where the other is."

Clarisse was quiet for a long time, turning her spear calluses over in the firelight. "When I began training with the Ares campers," she said at last, voice low, "they taught me to fight alone. To be the strongest so no one could drag me down. It took me years to learn shield-wall. This —" She gestured at the circle, at Percy roasting a marshmallow into charcoal, at Annabeth leaning on his shoulder. "This is harder."

"It's better, though," Silena said gently. "Strongest alone still drowns. Together we float."

Percy, mouth full of burnt marshmallow, raised his stick like a trident. "Together we float!"

"Don't talk with your mouth full, little brother," both girls said at once, and then stared at each other and burst out laughing while Thalia groaned, "Oh gods, there's three of them now."

Annabeth caught Percy's eye over the fire and mouthed, *You okay?* He nodded, throat tight in a good way. In the life he remembered, he'd spent so many years being the only Poseidon kid, the odd one out, the prophecy boy. Now there were three heartbeats where there'd been one, and a sister who counted cadence and a sister who set the tune.

"Okay," Silena said, setting down the lyre as stars pricked out overhead. "Part two. Poultices."

Clarisse groaned. Percy perked up. Annabeth opened her notebook to a fresh page.

Silena opened the first-aid kit — not the camp-standard one, but hers, smelling of lavender and salt and honey. "When I — when I was hurt on patrol," she said carefully, touching her wrist, "the nectar wasn't enough. What helped was cold and pressure and the right herbs. Clarisse sat with me and changed the bandages every hour. She remembers." Percy remembered a different wound, a different ending, and steadied himself on the living voice beside him.

Clarisse looked away, jaw tight.

Silena laid out cheesecloth, dried yarrow, crushed seaweed, honey, salt water in a sealed jar.

"For burns, for cuts, for poison-draw," she said, hands moving with quiet competence. "Yarrow for bleeding. Honey for infection — yes, real honey, Annabeth, write that down. Seaweed for swelling and to keep it damp. Salt water to clean, *then* fresh water. Always clean first, Percy, I know you want to just slap water on it."

Percy, caught mid-reach for the seaweed, grinned sheepishly. Annabeth nudged him with her knee.

Silena made them each mix one. Percy's was lumpy and dripping. Clarisse's was military-perfect and too tight. Silena corrected them patiently, fingers over theirs, showing pressure, showing patience.

"Like this," she murmured, wrapping a practice bandage around Percy's forearm where a sparring bruise bloomed purple. "Snug, not cruel. You have to trust it to do its work."

Percy watched her face in the firelight — focused, kind, unafraid of hurt. He thought of all the times Silena had patched him up without comment after he'd done something stupid and brave.

"Teach me the song for this part," Clarisse said quietly. "The one you sang — before. When you were —"

Silena glanced up. Something passed between them, remembered fear and steady family.

"It's just a lullaby," Silena said softly. "My mom sang it. *Hush, love, the tide is turning...*" She sang a few bars, voice barely above the fire.

Clarisse learned it line by line, stumbling, repeating until she had it. Percy joined on the humming. By the third repetition Thalia had her eyes closed and Annabeth was wiping salt — just sea spray, obviously — from her cheek.

The next day Silena tested them. She faked a cut on her own arm with berry juice, groaned dramatically, and timed them with a water clock.

"Pressure! Clean! Honey! Wrap! Sing!" she ordered, cackling as Percy fumbled the cheesecloth into the fire and Clarisse threatened to use her spear as a splint.

By the end of the afternoon Percy could wrap a poultice one-handed and Clarisse could do it blindfolded while reciting symptoms, and both of them could sing the lullaby without prompting.

The real test came an hour later, unplanned. A young Demeter kid limped up from the strawberry fields with a sickle cut across his calf, crying and embarrassed, blood soaking through his jeans. Before Will Solace could even cross the green, Silena was already moving.

"Percy, clean water — not sea, fresh," she ordered, calm as a general. "Clarisse, pressure here, talk to him. What's your name, honey? Theo? Okay, Theo, you're doing great, look at me, not at it. Annabeth, kit."

Percy cupped his hands and drew moisture straight from the air, letting it fall in a clear stream over the wound. Clarisse knelt in the dirt in her good training pants without hesitation, one big hand steady on Theo's shoulder, talking him through breathing the way Percy had taught her — tide in, tide out. Silena cleaned, packed yarrow and honey with deft fingers, wrapped snug-not-cruel while humming the lullaby low.

Theo stopped crying before she tied it off.

"Whoa," he whispered, staring at the three of them. "Are you all doctors now?"

"We're siblings," Silena said simply, ruffling his hair. "Go show Will, get nectar. You're brave."

As Will led Theo away with praise for the field dressing, Clarisse stared at her blood-stained hands. Silena bumped her shoulder.

"See?" Silena murmured. "Gentle stuff matters too."

Clarisse didn't answer, but she didn't wipe the blood off until they'd washed the kit together, methodically, side by side.

"You know," Thalia said, watching Clarisse carefully tie off Silena's demonstration bandage with surprising gentleness, "you're good at this, La Rue. The gentle stuff."

Clarisse flushed. "Don't tell Ares."

"Your secret's safe with the scouts," Thalia said, and bumped her fist against Clarisse's shoulder — high praise, from her.

That night Percy woke from a nightmare — Luke's smile, cold water closing over his head — to find Silena humming the lullaby in the bunk beside his, half-asleep, and Clarisse's steady breathing from across the room keeping time like a shanty drum. He fell back asleep before the song ended.

---

Percy taught last on the fifth morning, and Percy’s classroom was *in* the sea.

"Absolutely not," Annabeth said, standing on the shore with her arms crossed while Percy waded backward into the surf, grinning. "Last time you said 'trust me, it's fine' we ended up in the Sirens' cove."

"That was one time," Percy said.

"Twice," Thalia said. "I counted."

"Today's safe," Percy promised. "No monsters. Just breathing."

Clarisse and Silena exchanged a look. They trusted Percy with water the way Percy trusted Annabeth with plans — completely, even when it scared them.

"Atlantean breathing," Percy said, once they were chest-deep, the waves lifting them easy as breath. Annabeth and Thalia in life jackets on surfboards nearby, ordered to observe only. "It's not holding your breath. It's — sharing breath. With the water."

He demonstrated. He sank beneath the surface, eyes open, hair drifting. Clarisse counted. Ten seconds. Thirty. A minute. Two minutes. Silena started to fret.

He surfaced calm, not gasping.

"How?" Clarisse demanded.

"You don't fight the pressure," Percy said. "You let it in. Think of your lungs as — as a tide pool. Small sea inside you. Dad's water recognizes it."

It sounded stupid when he said it out loud. Annabeth was scribbling furiously anyway, like she believed him.

He taught them the way his father had coached him in recent dreams: slow exhale through the nose, let the water take the bubbles, inhale *intention*, not air. Feel the current as an extension of blood.

Silena got it first. Of course she did — she sang to the sea every sunset. She slipped under, and Percy felt her heartbeat join the rhythm of the swell, steady and sweet. She stayed down ninety seconds and came up laughing, water streaming from her hair.

"I heard it!" she gasped. "Percy, I heard the song down there! It's like — like a choir!"

Clarisse struggled. She was iron and fire; surrender was not her language. She thrashed, surfaced coughing, cursed.

"Damn it — it's in my nose — I hate this —"

"Hey," Percy said, treading water beside her, voice low. "You're trying to conquer it. Don't. You're my sister. The sea already knows you. You don't have to prove anything."

Clarisse glared at him, salt stinging her eyes. "I don't — I'm not —"

"It's okay to be scared," he said. "I was scared too."

She stared at him. Then she took his hand — actually took it, calluses rough against his — and let him pull her under.

This time Percy stayed with her, forehead almost touching hers in the green gloom, breathing slow and visible, bubbles rising like prayers. *In. Out. Tide in. Tide out.* He projected calm the way he'd learned to project currents, wrapping her in still water.

Clarisse's eyes fluttered closed. Her heartbeat slowed. One minute. Two.

They surfaced together. Clarisse gasped, then laughed — a startled, real laugh — clinging to Percy's shoulder.

"Holy Hera," she breathed. "I did it."

"You did it," Percy said, grinning so wide his face hurt. Silena cheered and splashed them both. From the surfboards Annabeth was cheering too, fist in the air, and Thalia was whistling through her teeth.

"My little brother taught me fish magic," Clarisse said, shaking water from her hair, and ruffled Percy's hair hard enough to push him under.

The next day was currents. Percy stood in the surf with his sisters on either side and taught them to *feel*.

"Close your eyes," he said. "Tell me where the rip is."

Silena pointed left without hesitation. Clarisse frowned, then pointed right — correct.

"Good. Now — push."

Silena managed a ripple. Clarisse managed a splash that soaked Percy and made Annabeth fall off her surfboard laughing.

"Again," Percy said, patient as Annabeth with architecture. "Don't punch the water. Ask it."

By the sixth afternoon they could hold a calm circle in choppy surf for ten minutes. Before evening Silena could summon a small, singing whirlpool that lifted shells from the sand, and Clarisse could part a waist-high wave around herself without getting her spear wet — "FOR TACTICAL REASONS," she shouted when Thalia mocked her dry hair.

Percy saved the hardest lesson for last, on the night before the spar when the moon was high and the sea was black glass. He took them out past the buoys, just the three of them, no observers, and taught them the undertow call.

"If one of us goes down," he said quietly, floating on his back with his sisters' hands linked in his, "you don't dive blind. You feel for the pull first. The sea will tell you where it took us. Listen, then go."

He let go and sank like a stone, deliberately tangling his ankle in an old crab pot line he'd spotted that afternoon. He felt the panic flare bright in his chest and breathed through it, tide in, tide out, sending the current of his location outward like a song.

Silena found him first, following the hum he'd taught her to hear, knife flashing to cut the line. Clarisse was right behind her, hauling him up with iron arms, all three bursting into moonlit air gasping and laughing and furious.

"Don't you ever —" Clarisse snarled, shoving him, eyes bright with fear-anger. "Don't use yourself as bait, you idiot!"

"Had to know you could find me," Percy said, coughing water and grinning. "You did."

Silena hit him with a handful of seaweed and then hugged him so hard he nearly went under again. Overhead the stars wheeled, and below them the current they'd calmed together held steady, a circle of peace in dark water.

They floated back to shore linked elbow to elbow, and Percy felt the chord lock into place — spear, song, sea — three notes that would hold even in a storm.

Annabeth watched it all from the shore, notebook full of diagrams labeled *Atlantean respiration — hypothetical mechanism??* and *current manipulation — sibling resonance?* and, on the last page, circled twice: *He's a good teacher. He's patient. He listens.*

Earlier that sixth evening, before the night lesson, she had told him so, while they walked back from the beach with sand still between their toes. The others were ahead — Silena riding piggyback on a protesting Clarisse, Thalia pretending not to laugh.

"You were great with them," Annabeth said quietly. "With Clarisse, especially. She trusts you."

Percy shrugged, embarrassed and pleased. "She's my sister. They're — I don't know how to explain it, Annabeth. When we're in the water together now, it's like — three notes in a chord. I can feel where they are without looking."

Annabeth bumped her shoulder against his. "I'm glad you have that. You deserve —" She hesitated. "You deserve people who hear the same sea you do."

Percy stopped walking. The sunset was pink on the water. Annabeth's gray eyes were gold at the edges.

"You're my —" He fumbled. "You hear me, even when I'm being an idiot about plans."

"Especially then," Annabeth said, smiling. "Someone has to translate Seaweed Brain into strategy."

"Is that cheering?" Percy asked. "For tomorrow? Will you cheer for me?"

Annabeth rolled her eyes, but she stood on tiptoes and kissed his cheek, quick and warm. "I'll cheer for all of you, Seaweed Brain. Loudest for you. Don't tell the others."

Percy walked the rest of the way back to camp floating three inches off the ground, or so Thalia claimed.

---

The final spar was Thalia's idea. Of course it was.

"You three think you're so synchronized now," she said at breakfast on the seventh day, lightning crackling faintly in her hair. "Spear, song, sea, blah blah. Prove it. Sisters versus siblings."

Clarisse narrowed her eyes. "Define teams."

"You three," Thalia said, pointing her fork at Percy, Clarisse, and Silena, "Poseidon siblings. Versus me and Annabeth. Zeus sisters. Two versus three. We'll still win."

"You will eat sand," Clarisse said immediately.

"You're on," Silena said, eyes sparkling. "Losers do dishes for a week."

Percy looked at Annabeth. "You're really going to fight us?"

Annabeth smiled, sharp and bright. "Scared, Jackson?"

"Terrified," Percy said honestly. "You have plans."

"I have *so many* plans," Annabeth agreed.

By noon the whole camp knew. The arena filled. Chiron trotted to the judge's stand with a first-aid kit and a whistle. Mr. D showed up with popcorn. The Ares cabin painted half their faces red for Clarisse and the Aphrodite cabin painted the other half blue for Silena, which confused everyone. The Athena kids held up a banner reading TACTICS BEAT TRIDENTS. The Poseidon section had a hand-painted sign Annabeth hung before joining Thalia: GO SEAWEED (AND OTHER SEAWEEDS). Seeing it across the arena made Percy want to die of happiness on the spot.

"Rules," Chiron said. "Blunted weapons. First team to three touches or all opponents down wins. No lethal powers. Percy, that means no hurricanes. Thalia, that means no frying anyone. Clarisse —"

"I know," Clarisse grumbled. "Gentle."

"Have fun," Chiron said, and blew the whistle.

Annabeth and Thalia moved like they'd been fighting together for years — because they had. Thalia charged straight for Clarisse, sparks snapping, shield high. Annabeth went for Percy, dagger flashing, using his own lopsided footwork against him with infuriating precision.

For thirty seconds it was chaos. Clarisse met Thalia shield-to-shield with a clang that rang across the camp. Percy barely blocked Annabeth's first three strikes — she'd been taking notes all week, damn her — and Silena danced between them, deflecting Thalia's spear with her own, singing under her breath.

Percy heard it first — Silena's shanty rhythm, steady under the clash. *Row, breathe, turn.* He fell into it without thinking. Exhale. Step. Turn.

Clarisse heard it too. She stopped trying to overpower Thalia and shifted to Form Two — the patient form, the one she'd drilled until Percy's legs shook. Shield angle, spear feint, *wait*.

"Now!" Silena sang — literally sang the cue, bright and clear.

Percy drew a thin stream from the water barrel beside the arena gate and flicked it sideways, just enough to make Annabeth blink. Clarisse swept Thalia's legs the same instant, blunted spear tapping her breastplate.

"One!" Chiron called.

Thalia hit the sand laughing. "Okay! Okay, fish kids can coordinate!"

Annabeth reset, eyes narrowed, already replanning. She signaled Thalia — two fingers, flank. They split, forcing the siblings apart. Annabeth drove Percy back toward the wall with a flurry Athena would have been proud of, while Thalia kept Clarisse and Silena busy with wide, crackling arcs.

Percy was losing. Annabeth was better than him with no powers, always had been. She read his new forms as fast as Clarisse had taught them.

But then he heard Annabeth humming — humming Silena's lullaby without realizing it, the rhythm they'd all sung around the fire. And Percy, seaweed brain, loyal to the bone, grinned.

He started singing it out loud, breath steady in Atlantean rhythm. Loud enough for his sisters to hear.

Silena picked it up instantly, harmonizing as she parried Thalia. Clarisse, bleeding from a graze on her cheek that Silena would poultice later, laughed — actually laughed mid-fight — and joined on the *hush, love* line while she slammed her shield into Thalia's.

The three heartbeats synced. Percy felt it like a wave rising.

He dropped low as Clarisse went high, Silena spinning between them to catch Annabeth's dagger on her spear haft — a move Clarisse had drilled a hundred times — and Percy tapped Annabeth's ankle with his spear butt while Clarisse tapped Thalia's shoulder.

"Two!" Chiron called. The crowd roared.

"Hey!" Annabeth protested, sprawled in the sand. "You used music!"

"You taught me to use everything!" Percy said, offering her a hand up. She took it, but pulled him down beside her instead, both of them laughing.

The last round lasted five minutes and became camp legend. Thalia stopped holding back her speed. Annabeth stopped holding back her brilliance. They drove the siblings to the center, back to back to back, surrounded.

Percy looked left — Clarisse, bloody and grinning, spear steady. Looked right — Silena, hair coming loose, singing through gritted teeth, poultice-ready hands already checking his stance for injury even mid-battle.

"Sea," Percy whispered.

"Spear," Clarisse answered.

"Song," Silena said.

They moved as one. Not three fighters. One tide with three crests. Percy breathed for them — slow, Atlantean, calm in chaos. Silena sang for them — timing, courage, *together*. Clarisse led them — form, fury, *live through this*.

When the dust cleared, Percy's spear was at Thalia's throat, Clarisse's shield pinned Annabeth's dagger, Silena's spear touched them both lightly — and Annabeth's dagger was at Percy's ribs while Thalia's spear tapped Clarisse's helm.

Five points of contact. Utter stalemate.

Silence. Then Chiron laughed, deep and delighted.

"Draw!" he declared. "Five touches simultaneous! I have never — draw!"

The arena exploded. Ares kids chanting CLARISSE! Aphrodite kids screaming SILENA! Athena kids yelling REMATCH WITH DIAGRAMS! Thalia threw her head back and howled with laughter, pulling Clarisse into a headlock. Annabeth tackled Percy clean into the sand in a hug that knocked the wind out of him.

"You did it!" she said into his shoulder, fierce and proud. "You actually — you fought like one person! Percy, that was amazing!"

Percy's arms came around her automatically, sand and sweat and sunlight. "You cheered loudest for me, right?"

"Loudest," Annabeth promised, not letting go. "Don't tell the others."

"I heard that!" Silena sang, flopping down on top of them both. Clarisse followed, armor and all, and Thalia piled on with a war cry, until they were a heap of limbs and laughter in the middle of the arena while the whole camp cheered and Chiron pretended to look for his whistle and Mr. D ate popcorn and said, "About time these children learned teamwork."

Later, after Silena had poulticed every scrape with humming efficiency, after Clarisse had drilled them all — including Annabeth and Thalia — in the cool-down forms until they groaned, after Percy had led them all into the sea to float on their backs and breathe tide-in, tide-out while stars came out, the three siblings sat on the Poseidon cabin steps with salt drying on their skin.

"Spear," Clarisse said, bumping her shoulder against Silena's.

"Song," Silena said, leaning her head on Percy's shoulder.

"Sea," Percy said, leaning his head on Clarisse's pauldron, which was uncomfortable and perfect.

Inside, three bunks waited. Three trunks. One too-small bathroom they'd already fought over twice. A lyre, a spear, a notebook full of Annabeth's diagrams with *GO SEAWEED* doodled in the margin.

Family.

"Same time tomorrow?" Percy asked sleepily.

"0700," Clarisse said. "Don't be late, little brother."

"Wouldn't miss it, big sister," Percy said, and Silena, half-asleep between them, smiled like a song resolved.

Above them, far out over the Sound, the sea kept time.
