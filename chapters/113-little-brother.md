## Chapter 113: Little Brother

Clarisse hated being sick.

She hated the fever, the headache, the weakness in her legs. Most of all, she hated the way everyone kept telling her to stay in bed.

"I'm fine," she told Silena.

"You're shivering under three blankets."

"The cabin's cold."

"It's warm enough that Percy took his shirt off after training."

"Percy's weird."

Silena set a cup of tea on the bedside table. "Drink this."

Clarisse pushed it away. "I don't need—"

Percy appeared in the doorway, carrying a bowl. "I brought soup."

"I don't want soup."

"It's not poisoned."

"That's what someone who poisoned soup would say."

He put the bowl down anyway and sat on the edge of the bunk. "You don't have to eat it. Just smell it. It's good soup. Silena helped."

Clarisse narrowed her eyes. "She helped you?"

"She told me what to put in."

Silena raised an eyebrow. "And he followed approximately half the instructions."

Percy offered Clarisse the spoon. She took it to prove she could. The soup was warm, salty, and surprisingly good.

She took another spoonful.

Percy didn't comment. He just sat there, holding the bowl steady when her hand shook.

After a while, Clarisse said, "You don't have to stay."

"I know."

"You should be training."

"I did."

"You should be with Annabeth."

"I'll see her later."

Clarisse looked away. She wasn't used to being cared for. She knew what to do when someone needed a shield or a weapon. She didn't know what to do with Percy sitting beside her, patiently waiting for her to finish soup.

"You're a decent little brother," she said.

"Little?"

"You're the youngest. It's the law."

Silena pulled the blankets higher around Clarisse's shoulders. "Sleep. We'll be here."

Clarisse grumbled, but she closed her eyes.

---

When she woke, Percy was gone. Annabeth had taken his place with a book open on her knees.

"I don't need a guard," Clarisse said.

"I'm not guarding you. I'm reading."

Clarisse eyed the book. "Architecture?"

"The history of the Big House. It's interesting."

"You two are made for each other. You both think reading is a hobby."

Annabeth smiled. "He thinks it is too."

Clarisse studied her. "You love him?"

Annabeth looked up. "Yes."

"And he loves you?"

"Yes."

"Good." Clarisse settled back against the pillow. "He's a good kid. He deserves somebody who says what she means."

"I do try."

"Keep trying."

Annabeth reached over and touched Clarisse's wrist. "You deserve people who take care of you too."

Clarisse made a face. "Don't start."

"I'm not starting anything. I'm telling you the truth."

"Well, stop telling the truth so loudly. It gives me a headache."

Annabeth laughed and returned to her book.

---

Clarisse was back on her feet the next day, though Silena made her take it easy. Percy brought her a wooden practice spear and challenged her to a slow-motion duel in the cabin.

She beat him with one hand.

"You were sick yesterday," Percy protested.

"And you're still the baby."

He grinned. "I'm going to get you next time."

"You can try."

Silena watched them bicker and shook her head. Annabeth, beside her, slipped her hand into Percy's when the match ended. He turned his palm up and held hers.

Clarisse saw it. She didn't tease them.

She was beginning to understand that Percy could be her little brother and Annabeth's favorite person without either one taking anything away from her.

That was a lesson worth learning.
