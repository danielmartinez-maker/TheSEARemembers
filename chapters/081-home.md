## Chapter 81: Home

The last morning of the spring visitors' stay in late April smelled like cut grass and hot bronze and the pine resin baking off Half-Blood Hill.

Percy Jackson woke before the conch horn. He lay for a moment in Cabin Three with his hands behind his head, listening to the surf he could hear even when he was not near the water, watching dust move in the thin blue light through the shutters. Thirteen. The number still caught him off guard, and he still kept forgetting, still did the math wrong when Annabeth teased him about it. Thirteen felt the same as twelve except his shoes wore out faster and his voice cracked when he laughed too hard.

Today the buses would come. Today the cabins would empty. Today the Hermes kids would fight over who left what in the lost-and-found and the Apollo kids would sing too loud on purpose all the way down the hill.

He did not get up right away. He wanted to remember this exactly like this.

---

The mess hall was quieter without the rush. Not empty, just slower, like everyone had agreed without saying it to stretch the morning out.

Annabeth was already at the Zeus table with her hair pulled back, a half-finished packing list beside her plate, and a book she was not reading. When she saw Percy she smiled in the way she had smiled all through spring, steady and sure, and patted the bench beside her. It was not a secret that they sat together most mornings now. Nobody made a big deal of it. That was the best part.

"You're up," she said.

"I'm up," Percy said. He sat down close enough that their shoulders touched. "You sleep?"

"A little." She pushed a blueberry muffin toward him. "Silena packed you one? She packed you three?"

"Two," Percy said. "I ate one on the way over."

She laughed, and for a second he just watched her laugh because he could. Years at Camp in their lost life, and new seasons here. Capture the flag and monster drills and late-night planning sessions in the Big House attic. They had held hands crossing the creek when the current was high. They had fallen asleep shoulder to shoulder in the library during the winter planning weekend with Chiron's blanket over both of them. It was steady. It was simple and familiar. When he was thirteen and the world kept trying to make everything fast and sharp, steady felt like armor.

"You wrote your list?" she asked.

He pulled out a crumpled napkin. "Sword. Hoodie. Hoodie I stole from you. Toothbrush.Shell from Cabin Three step."

"That's not a packing list, that's an archaeological record."

"Says the archivist's apprentice."

Across the pavilion, Ella was indeed cataloging the spring visits. She sat with the Apollo table, a stack of index cards in front of her, her red feathers smoothed around her little reading glasses, muttering titles under her breath as she wrote. "Mess hall, departure breakfast, late April — eggs, slightly burned — laughter, high volume — home, pending." She looked up, caught Percy watching, and beamed. "Percy! Ella is making the record complete. Seventy-four capture-the-flag plays. Twelve new songs. One hundred and nine names memorized."

"You memorized everyone?" Annabeth asked.

"Everyone stays," Ella said firmly. "If I write them, they stay."

Chiron had made it official at the campfire two nights before. Ella, Junior Archivist of Camp Half-Blood, keeper of songs and lineages and small important facts like who takes honey in their tea. She had cried and then immediately asked for three more boxes and better pens.

Percy walked over and bumped her shoulder with his fist, gentle. "Best archivist ever."

"Only junior archivist ever," Ella corrected, but she glowed.

---

Thalia found them after breakfast by the forge, where the last repairs were being done before the armory was checked for the next training block.

She had her arm around Annabeth before either of them could say anything, pulling her sideways into a hug that nearly knocked Annabeth's cap off. Thalia Grace did not do small hellos.

"There are the Grace sisters," Clarisse said dryly from the doorway, but she was smiling.

Thalia stuck her tongue out at her. "Jealous."

"A little," Clarisse admitted, surprising everyone including herself. "You two make sisterhood look easy."

It had not been easy. Percy remembered the first year so clearly — Thalia all sharp edges and stormlight, Annabeth all walls and plans, circling each other like they were both afraid to be the first to need someone. Daughters of Zeus were not supposed to need. They were supposed to lead.

This spring had changed that. Late patrols together. Thalia teaching Annabeth how to throw a spear from horseback without flinching at the thunder. Annabeth teaching Thalia how to read a battle map without trying to punch through it. The night the barrier flickered during the storm and they had held it together, back to back, shouting coordinates until their voices went raw.

Now Thalia ruffled Annabeth's hair the way an older sister was legally required to do, and Annabeth leaned into it the way a younger sister did when she finally stopped pretending it annoyed her.

"Packing?" Thalia asked.

"Almost," Annabeth said. "You?"

"I live here, Wise Girl. Scouts don't pack. We dramatically depart."

"You dramatically left your boots beside my bed in Cabin One," Annabeth said.

"Those are ceremonial boots."

Percy laughed. Thalia turned to him and punched his arm, not hard.

"Seaweed Brain. Walk with me later? Before the hill tonight. Just us kids who keep watch on the hill."

"Always," Percy said, and he meant it. Thalia had been on the hill when he arrived, guarding Annabeth and watching him with suspicion. She had become one of the reasons he understood that Camp could keep you even when you could not keep yourself. They were not siblings by blood, but by everything that counted in the months since — the letters she wrote, the mornings she checked in — she was family.

---

Silena found Percy by the cabins at noon, sitting on the steps of Cabin Three with Clarisse, passing a canteen back and forth like they had done a hundred times.

For a long time Clarisse La Rue had been the person Percy measured himself against by trying not to get killed. Then she had been his rival. Then, somewhere in their time here, without a big speech or a quest oath, he had begun to trust the sister this history had given him.

Not metaphorical. Real: they shared Poseidon, though Percy did not remember growing up with her. The night he had stayed up with her before her fear trial, sharpening her spear in silence because she did not want advice, just company. The morning she had stayed up with him after his birthday, when Amphitrite could not visit until the weekend and he pretended it did not bother him and Clarisse saw straight through it and stole him blue pancakes and told him about her dad and how Poseidon showed love differently to his children. The look on her face when he called her his big sister by accident in front of the whole cabin and she had frozen and then said, "Damn right," too gruff to hide that her eyes were wet.

Silena was the soft to Clarisse's hard, and she had welcomed their brother just as completely. Daughter of Poseidon, big sister to half the camp, keeper of spare blankets and hair ties and secrets. She braided Annabeth's hair when Annabeth was stressed. She braided Percy's friendship bracelet back together when the string frayed and refused to make him a new one because, she said, this one has history.

"You boys moping?" Silena asked now, sitting down on the step below them, her dress tucked around her knees.

"Reflecting," Clarisse corrected. "Manly reflection."

"Moping," Silena translated to Percy, and he grinned.

"We're going to miss this," Percy said honestly. "Even the latrine duty roster."

"Speak for yourself," Clarisse said. "I'm going to miss you two idiots. The latrines can burn."

Silena leaned her head back against Clarisse's knee. Clarisse rested her hand absently on Silena's hair, sister-easy. Percy watched them and felt something settle warm behind his ribs.

"You know," Silena said, "when I first got here, I thought siblings meant competition. Who's Dad's favorite. Who gets claimed first. Who's prettiest, strongest." She glanced up at Clarisse. "Then this one threatened to break a boy's nose for stealing my scarf, and I realized, oh. It's this. It's just showing up."

Clarisse snorted. "It was a nice scarf."

"It was," Silena agreed. She looked at Percy. "And then you came along with your stupid barnacle hair and your big save-everybody heart, and suddenly I had a little brother who tracks sand into my cabin and eats my lip balm thinking it's candy."

"It was cherry!" Percy protested. "It smelled like candy!"

"See?" Silena said to Clarisse. "Little brother."

Clarisse bumped Percy's shoulder with hers. Hard enough to bruise. Perfect.

"Little brother," Clarisse confirmed. "Annoying. Ours."

Percy had memories of a mortal mom he loved more than anything, Amphitrite here, and a godly dad who was trying. He knew what family was. This was family too. Not replacing. Adding. Camp did that — it added rooms to your heart without asking permission.

"Come here," Silena said, and opened her arms. Percy let himself be pulled into a hug that smelled like sunscreen and roses. Clarisse ruffled his hair hard enough to mess it up completely.

"Ow."

"Love you too," Clarisse said.

---

Grover was by the lake in the afternoon, his pack already half-packed, his pipes in his lap, staring at the water like he was memorizing it.

Percy sat down beside him without asking. They had stopped asking long ago, in the life Percy remembered.

"Searcher," Percy said.

"Searcher," Grover agreed. His voice wobbled. Then steadied. "Official. Council vote yesterday. Chiron told me this morning. I leave in three days. First assignment — Pacific Northwest. Rumors of a wild grove. And — and Pan—" He stopped. Breathed. "You know."

Percy knew. Every satyr knew. Pan, lost. The Wild, fading. Grover Underwood, who cried at sad songs and ate tin cans when he was nervous, had decided he was going to walk into the wild and listen until it answered.

"I'm proud of you," Percy said. His throat felt tight. "I'm also mad at you for leaving."

"I'm mad at me too," Grover said, sniffling a laugh. "Juniper says she's proud. She's also mad. She threw a pinecone at me."

"Love pinecone?"

"Love pinecone," Grover confirmed. He leaned his head against Percy's shoulder. Best friends since sixth grade. Since the bus to the museum. Since Grover limped and pretended it was an injury and Percy pretended to believe him. "You think I'll be okay out there?"

"I think the Wild's been waiting for you," Percy said honestly. "I think you're the only one dumb enough and brave enough to go hear it."

Grover laughed wetly. "Thanks?"

"You're welcome." Percy bumped his forehead lightly against Grover's. "You send letters. Ella's making you a special archive box."

"I heard. She already labeled it G-R-O-V-E-R in capital letters so I would feel important."

"You are important, man."

They sat there while the lake lapped and the pegasi circled far overhead. No monsters. No prophecy. Just two boys who had grown up a little and were about to grow up a little more apart.

"You'll watch the hill with us tonight?" Percy asked.

"Wouldn't miss it," Grover said. "Last watch before the visitors go. Gotta do it right."

---

Zoe Nightshade was holding spring combat assessments on the green, which mostly meant she was letting the younger kids win just enough to feel brave and then correcting their footwork so precisely they did not even realize they had been taught.

Percy watched her for a while from the fence. In Percy's original life, Zoe had been a Hunter for longer than he had been alive. Here the daughter of Hecate was a camper and a teacher. She had been cold when he first met her here, private and difficult to read. In their time here she had thawed in places she would never admit. She taught. That was the surprise. Chiron had asked her to stay through the spring visits to run archery intensives, and she had stayed, and then she had stayed extra hours, and then Ella had started calling her Teacher Zoe and Zoe had pretended to hate it while keeping the drawing Ella made her of a bow labeled BEST TEACHER.

"You stare, Perseus Jackson, and I will put thee on drills," Zoe called without looking.

"Just admiring," Percy said, hopping the fence. "Teacher."

She rolled her eyes, but her mouth twitched. "Thou hast improved. Thy stance no longer offends me greatly."

"High praise."

Annabeth joined them, bow over her shoulder. Thalia fell in on Percy's other side. For a moment the four of them stood there — teacher and daughters and son — watching the little kids shoot arrows that went gloriously everywhere except the target, and laughing when one stuck quivering in the grass two feet in front of them.

"You staying through summer?" Annabeth asked Zoe quietly.

Zoe nodded. "My mother approves. Chiron requests. I —" She hesitated, which was rare for her. "I find I enjoy seeing progress. To plant and to watch grow. It is quiet work, but it is not unworthy."

"You're good at it," Thalia said. She and Zoe had history, complicated and old. Thalia bumped Zoe's arm. "They listen to you."

"They fear me," Zoe corrected.

"Same thing at that age," Annabeth said, and Zoe actually laughed.

Percy felt Annabeth's hand slip into his, brief and warm. Chaste, easy, thirteen and unafraid. Zoe saw and did not comment except to look away with something almost soft in her expression, giving them privacy without making it a thing.

That was Camp now. People let you love each other in your own way.

---

Annabeth's survey team had left a gift for Percy on the Athena table: a hand-drawn map of Camp, every path inked, every shortcut marked, with little notes in Annabeth's precise handwriting.

Here he had fallen off the climbing wall and Clarisse had laughed so hard she fell off too. Here Thalia had taught him to listen for thunder before the lightning. Here Grover had played pipes to calm the strawberry fields during the drought scare last July. Here Silena had set up the first-aid station during war games and handed out juice boxes like a general. Here Ella had hidden during capture the flag because, she said, the librarian must remain neutral, and then shouted directions to both teams because neutrality was boring. Here Zoe had corrected his archery until his arms shook and then said, quiet, thou endures well, and Percy had known it was the highest compliment she gave.

Here Annabeth had kissed his cheek on his thirteenth birthday, quick and brave, in front of everyone, and the whole pavilion had cheered and Percy had turned so red Clarisse said he matched the strawberries.

He traced that spot on the map with his thumb.

"You keep that," Annabeth said, coming up behind him. She wrapped her arms briefly around his shoulders from behind, chin on top of his head, comfortable and familiar. She bumped her forehead against his when he looked up, then straightened the crooked collar of his shirt. He caught her hand before she could fuss with his hair too. There would be time to grow up. This spring they had learned how to stay.

"I'll keep it forever," Percy said. "Even when I'm old and gray and you have to explain maps to me again."

"I will always explain maps to you," Annabeth promised. "It's my girlfriend duty."

"Is that official?"

"Thalia approved it. As your older sister in law-ish."

Percy laughed. "Thalia would."

They walked the camp together before dinner, doing the last loop. Not patrolling. Remembering.

At the canoe lake they found Clarisse teaching the youngest Ares kids how to tie a proper splint, her voice low and patient in a way she never used with older campers. Percy hung back and watched. This was the second-year Clarisse, the one who had learned that strength was not just breaking things. After the winter quest, after she and Percy had bled side by side in the tunnels under the hill and she had dragged him out by his ankle when his leg gave out, something had shifted. She still called him Prissy and Seaweed Brain. She also left extra ambrosia outside his cabin when he was hurt and denied it completely.

"You gonna stand there all day, Jackson?" Clarisse called without looking up.

"Maybe," Percy said. "Learning by observation."

"Learn faster," Clarisse said, but she waved him over. "Hold this."

He held the bandage while she tied. The little kid stared at Percy with wide eyes. "Are you the brother?"

Clarisse's ears went pink. "What did Silena tell you?"

"She said you're his sister now and we have to be nice to him or else," the kid recited faithfully.

Silena, arriving with fresh towels, clapped her hands over her mouth. "I said be nice because he's nice!"

"You said or else," the kid insisted.

Percy grinned at Clarisse. "Or else."

"Or else I'll drown you in the lake," Clarisse muttered, but she was smiling. She finished the splint and sent the kids off with a stern lecture about hydration. Then she looked at Percy, suddenly serious. "You good? For the study block? Your mom—"

"Good," Percy said. Amphitrite was waiting in Atlantis with blue sweets and a room with wards Poseidon had personally blessed. School. Normal-ish, for undersea life. Iris messages every Sunday. Chiron had already agreed to discuss supervised camp visits for architecture weekends with Annabeth. It would not be Camp, but it would be okay because Camp came with him now. "You?"

"Home for two weeks, then back for year-round," Clarisse said. "Dad's—" She shrugged. Poseidon could be hard to pin down. "Mom's good. Silena's staying too. We're gonna get the cabins ready for the summer crowd. You better write."

"Every week," Percy promised. "Little brother's oath."

Silena linked her arm through both of theirs. "Siblings solid?"

"Siblings solid," Percy and Clarisse said together, and Silena beamed like Aphrodite herself had blessed it.

---

Nico and Bianca di Angelo were on the Big House porch at sunset, sharing a blanket despite the heat, playing a card game Bianca was clearly winning.

It still stopped Percy's heart sometimes to see them like that. Alive. Together. Home.

After everything last year — the ghosts, the guilt, Bianca almost choosing to stay in the Underworld because she thought Nico would be safer without her — seeing them argue over Uno was a miracle so ordinary it hurt.

"Nico cheats," Bianca announced as Percy and Annabeth came up the steps.

"I strategize," Nico said.

"You hid a Draw Four under your leg."

"Strategic concealment."

Bianca stole his cards and kissed the top of his head. Little sister, big sister — it flipped depending on the day. They had decided, with Hades' reluctant blessing and Persephone's fierce one, to stay at Camp year-round. Not because they had nowhere else, but because here they could just be kids learning to be siblings again instead of symbols.

"We're staying," Bianca told Annabeth for the hundredth time that week, like she still needed to say it to believe it. "Full year. School here. Iris-message tutors."

"You're stuck with us," Nico said to Percy, trying to sound annoyed and failing completely. He was smiling. Real smiling, not the thin ghost-smile from before.

"Good," Percy said. He sat down on the steps. Annabeth sat beside him. "Somebody's gotta teach you how to swim without summoning skeletons."

"I did it once!"

"Twice."

Bianca dealt them both in. They played three rounds as the sky went pink and then gold. Nico won once and celebrated like he had defeated a Titan. Bianca let him. Percy saw her let him, and saw Nico see that she let him, and saw them both decide to pretend otherwise. Siblings.

Chiron came out with lemonade. Mr. D, in his Hawaiian shirt, pretended not to care and then stayed for two whole games and accused Nico of cheating with actual death magic.

"Home," Bianca whispered later, leaning against the porch rail, watching the hill light up. "It feels like home now. Not just safe. Home."

Percy nodded. He understood exactly.

---

Dinner was loud. Last-night loud. The tables were pushed a little closer. People kept getting up to hug people at other tables. The Apollo kids sang the camp song Ella had archived, all seventy-four verses of inside jokes, and everyone groaned and sang along anyway.

Percy sat with Annabeth, Thalia across from them, Clarisse and Silena squished in on the end, Grover and Juniper holding hands under the table, Ella taking notes between bites, Zoe pretending not to sing while mouthing the words, Nico and Bianca stealing each other's dessert.

No speeches from the gods. No prophecy smoke. Just Chiron standing at the end, raising his glass.

"To spring," he said. "To those who return, and those who remain, and those who carry Camp with them."

"To home," Silena added softly, lifting her cup, and everyone echoed it.

Percy looked around the pavilion and memorized it the way Ella memorized — not the grand things, but the small ones. The chip in Annabeth's cup. The way Thalia laughed with her whole body. Clarisse wiping whipped cream off Silena's nose. Grover's reed pipes tucked safe in his pack. Zoe adjusting a new camper's grip one last time. Nico leaning his head on Bianca's shoulder, unafraid to be tired.

He could spend the whole study block describing this dinner and still miss something, he thought. For now he would keep it simple: we showed up. We stayed.

---

The campfire had burned low and gold, and Chiron had let it burn without adding more logs, letting the dark gather close the way it did when stories mattered.

Thalia stood with Annabeth at the edge of the firelight, sisters shoulder to shoulder, watching the sparks rise. They did not need to talk the whole time. That was the new thing. When Percy and Annabeth first returned, every conversation had been a test. Who was smarter. Who was braver. Which daughter their father praised more, which was a terrible game because gods loved badly and everyone knew it. This spring they had finally stopped playing.

"You remember the first night we shared watch after you and Percy woke up changed?" Thalia asked quietly.

Annabeth nodded. "You said I overthink like the Athena kids."

"You said Zeus kids over-lightning."

"You do."

"You do overthink."

They grinned at each other. Annabeth leaned her head briefly against Thalia's shoulder. Older sister, younger sister — sisters by blood, learning to choose each other with a childhood only Thalia could remember. Annabeth believed her when she told those stories. She was still learning how to live beside them.

Across the fire, Percy caught Annabeth's eye and smiled. Thalia saw it and rolled her eyes fondly.

"Go on," she told Annabeth, nudging her forward. "Go sit with Steady. I'll hold your spot. Sisters solid?"

"Sisters solid," Annabeth said, squeezing Thalia's hand hard before she went. Solid as the rock of the Acropolis, solid as the root of the pine on the hill. Nothing would shake it now — not scouts and campers, not separate duties, not school blocks apart.

Percy made room for Annabeth on the log without a word, lifting his arm so she could tuck in against his side. Easy, familiar, and known to everyone. Silena, passing with blankets, dropped one over both their knees and winked. Clarisse, on Percy's other side, pretended to gag and then leaned her weight against him anyway so Percy sat braced between his girlfriend and his sister like that was the easiest arrangement in the world. Grover's pipes joined the Apollo lyres for the last song. Ella sang every word, off-key and perfect, her archive complete for now. Zoe kept time with her bow against her boot, teacher-proud. Nico laughed at something Bianca whispered, head thrown back, unafraid, home.

Chiron watched them all with eyes that had watched a hundred rounds of departures. He caught Percy's gaze and nodded once — you did well — and Percy felt his chest go tight and full at the same time.

This was what he wanted to come home to, he thought. The music, the blanket, the people leaning on him.

---

The hill watch was tradition. Before a round of departures, anyone who wanted climbed Half-Blood Hill after curfew with blankets and flashlights Chiron pretended not to see, and watched the stars come out over the barrier.

They went up in a long straggling line. Thalia led with her patrol lantern. Zoe walked with the younger kids, pointing out constellations in Greek and in the old mythic names. Ella narrated the whole climb for the record, breathless. Grover helped Nico, who was still a little afraid of heights at night and would never admit it. Clarisse carried Silena's bag without being asked. Annabeth walked beside Percy, their fingers laced, swinging slightly between them.

At the top, the boundary pine — the tree beside their usual watch post — rustled in the warm wind. The boundary wards glowed faint among the branches, keeping the barrier humming soft as a lullaby.

They spread blankets. They lay back. No one talked for a while.

Percy lay with Annabeth's head on his shoulder, Thalia on his other side close enough to be family, Clarisse's boots touching his boots, Silena's laugh somewhere to his left, Grover's quiet breathing to his right. Ella whispering star names. Zoe humming low. Nico and Bianca counting satellites and arguing fondly about whether gods counted as satellites.

The sky turned ink and then velvet. Stars pricked through.

"I used to be scared of this hill," Thalia said quietly, so only Percy and Annabeth heard. "When we first arrived. I thought everyone would just see a guard for Annabeth. Not me."

Annabeth reached over Percy to squeeze Thalia's hand. Sister to sister, solid as stone. "We see you."

"I know," Thalia said. Her voice was rough. "That's why I can leave it now. It'll be here. You'll be here. I'm not — I'm not stuck anymore. I'm just... from here."

Percy swallowed hard. "Me too."

Annabeth turned her face into his shoulder. Her cap was off. Her hair smelled like sunscreen and library dust. "Steady," she murmured, their private word all spring for we are okay. "Steady?"

"Steady," Percy whispered back. Their hands were held under a blanket, warm against the cooling night, while their friends argued softly about the stars. He pressed his forehead briefly to hers. "Always steady."

Clarisse, eavesdropping shamelessly, tossed a pinecone at him. "Sap."

"Says my sister," Percy tossed back, and Clarisse grinned fierce in the starlight.

Silena sighed happily. "Our little brother's growing up."

"Don't," Clarisse and Percy said together, and everyone laughed.

Grover sat up suddenly. "Shh. Listen."

They listened. Crickets. Wind in the pine. The distant wash of Long Island Sound. An owl. Somewhere down the hill, a naiad singing the last campers to sleep.

"Wild's still here," Grover whispered. "Even here. Even now."

Juniper kissed his cheek. "Bring it with you."

"I will," Grover promised. Searcher-bound, reed pipes ready, heart huge. "I'll find more."

Ella sat up and wrote by flashlight: HILL WATCH, FINAL NIGHT — ALL PRESENT — STARS, MANY — HOME, ACHIEVED. She capped her pen with a click that sounded like a period at the end of a very long sentence.

Zoe stood and stretched, teacher-tired and content. "Sleep, little ones," she told the campers near her, pulling blankets up. "Thou hast done well this spring. I am —" She paused. "I am proud to teach thee."

Thalia met her eyes over the blankets and nodded, old hurt forgiven into something new. Sisters in arms. Family by choice.

Nico had fallen asleep with his head in Bianca's lap. Bianca looked up at Percy and mouthed thank you. Percy shook his head — no thanks needed — but Bianca mouthed it again anyway, because some debts you just kept saying, and Percy nodded because some thanks you just kept receiving.

Home was not a place that kept you from leaving, Percy thought, watching them all. Home was the place that stayed in you when you left.

---

Dawn came slow and pink over the Sound.

No one had meant to stay all night, but they had. Blankets damp with dew. Flashlights dead. Ella asleep on her index cards. Grover snoring softly against Juniper's shoulder. Zoe awake on watch, as always, nodding to Thalia who had woken to share it without being asked. Clarisse with her arm over Silena's shoulders, both out cold. Nico curled into Bianca's side, Bianca's chin on top of his head.

Percy woke first with Annabeth still tucked against him. He watched the light climb.

"Hey," he whispered. "Sunrise."

Annabeth blinked awake. Thalia stirred. One by one, like a wave, they sat up, rubbing eyes, pulling blankets around shoulders, turning east.

The sun rose over Half-Blood Hill the way it had risen a thousand times before and would rise a thousand times after. Gold spilled across the strawberry fields. The ward-light caught fire among the branches. The barrier shimmered, thin as soap, strong as love. The pine stood tall.

Nobody spoke. There was nothing left that needed proving.

Thalia reached for Annabeth's hand. Annabeth took it. Sisters, solid.

Clarisse shoved Percy's foot with hers. Silena waved sleepily. Siblings, solid.

Annabeth leaned her temple against Percy's. Couple, steady.

Grover lifted his pipes and played one soft note to greet the sun. Ella woke just enough to write SUNRISE, FINAL — COLOR, GOLD — FEELING, HOME. Zoe smiled. Nico and Bianca watched hand in hand, home at last.

Percy Jackson, thirteen, son of Poseidon, brother, friend, boyfriend, camper, breathed in cut grass and pine and salt, and smiled at the new day with everyone he loved around him.

They were home, and morning had come.

The buses would honk. The trunks would drag. Goodbyes would be said with too much laughing so nobody cried, and then cried anyway. May would come with school and cities and quests yet unknown. But Camp would hold — pine and wards and hearth — and they would return, always, to this hill, to each other, to home that no distance could unmake.
