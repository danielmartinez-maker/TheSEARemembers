## Chapter 66: Jason's Letter

The morning mail at Camp Half-Blood never came the normal way.

There were no mailboxes. If you wanted to send something, you trusted Hermes cabin and accepted it might arrive opened and three weeks late, or you used Iris-messages and hoped nobody ran through the mist mid-sentence.

So when the eagle came, everybody noticed.

Percy noticed first because he was down at the canoe lake skipping stones before breakfast, Annabeth beside him with her notebook open on her knees. The shadow crossed the water, huge and straight, wings locked, and Percy squinted up.

"That's not a pegasus," he said.

Annabeth looked up, pencil stopping mid-equation. "No. That's— Percy, that's a Roman eagle."

The bird — no, not a bird, an actual eagle, golden and massive with eyes like polished coins — circled once over the lake, screamed, and dove straight for the Big House. Campers stumbled out of cabins in pajamas to stare. From the cabins near the armory someone shouted, "Is that an attack?" From the Ares cabin someone else shouted, "Dibs on the feathers!"

Percy was already on his feet, pulling Annabeth up with him. "Come on."

They ran up the hill together, Annabeth's hand tight in his, her notebook abandoned on the dock. By the time they reached the Big House porch, a small crowd had gathered. Chiron stood there in his wheelchair form, trotting out in his human disguise only half-fastened, Mr. D in a bathrobe that said I AM THE PARTY, and in the middle of it all, Thalia Grace with her arm extended to the sky.

The eagle landed on her wrist like it weighed nothing, though Thalia's knees buckled. She was in her black camp clothes against the April chill, hair spiked up. Tied to the eagle's leg with red and gold cord was a leather tube stamped SPQR.

Thalia went very still.

"Whoa," Clarisse said, pushing forward with Silena right behind her. Silena had a face mask on and cucumber slices in a baggie, clearly interrupted mid-morning routine. "Pinecone Face has a Roman pen pal?"

"Shut up, Clarisse," Thalia said automatically, but her voice shook. She untied the tube with fingers that fumbled twice. The eagle nipped her sleeve affectionately, then launched itself back into the sky with a scream that rattled the porch windows. Thalia stared at the tube.

Annabeth got there first. She put a hand on Thalia's shoulder. "Thalia? You okay?"

"It's his handwriting," Thalia whispered. She turned the leather over. Burned into the cap in careful block letters: THALIA — FROM JASON. DO NOT LET PERCY EAT THIS. "It's from Jason."

Percy blinked. "Your Jason? Your little brother Jason?"

"My little brother who is Roman and insufferable, yes, that Jason," Thalia said, and laughed a little wetly. "Gods. He actually wrote."

Chiron smiled kindly. "Why don't you take that somewhere quiet, my dear? It looks thick."

"It is thick," Clarisse observed. "What, did he send you a toga catalog?"

"Clarisse," Silena scolded, but she was leaning in too. "Is it really from Camp Jupiter? Do you think he mentions— you know, what it's like there?"

Thalia looked down at the tube, then at Percy and Annabeth. The porch was too crowded, too loud. Mr. D was already trying to go back to bed. The Stolls were whispering about how much Roman postage would be worth on eBay.

"Come on," Annabeth said softly. "Let's go to our spot."

Their spot was the little overlook behind Cabin One, under the oak where Percy and Annabeth had spent long afternoons last summer. Thalia followed them without argument, which told Percy how nervous she was.

They sat in the grass. Thalia sat cross-legged, Percy sprawled on his stomach, Annabeth tucked against his side with her chin on his shoulder. Clarisse and Silena invited themselves and sat a little way off, pretending they weren't eavesdropping and failing completely.

Thalia pulled the cap off.

Out slid a roll of paper so thick it unspooled across her lap and onto the grass. Page after page, covered front and back in neat, squared-off handwriting, with little sketches in the margins — an eagle, a shield, a temple, a very bad drawing of what was labeled ME IN A TOGA (HELP). A dried California poppy fell out and landed on Percy's knee.

"Holy gods," Percy said. "He wrote a novel."

"He wrote a letter," Thalia corrected fiercely, carefully smoothing the first page. Her hands were trembling. "He— okay. Okay. I'm just gonna read it. Out loud. You guys good with that?"

Annabeth squeezed her arm. "Of course."

"Yeah," Percy said. "Read, Thals."

Thalia cleared her throat, glanced at the first line, laughed, wiped her eyes already, and began.

---

*Dear Thalia,*

*If Connor Stoll is reading this over your shoulder, Connor, I will find you and I will push you into the Tiber. Thalia told me about you in her last letter. Don't think I won't.*

*If Percy Jackson is eating this letter, Percy, stop. I wrote DO NOT EAT on the outside for a reason. Reyna says Greeks will eat anything if you put enough cheese on it. Please prove her wrong.*

*Okay. Now that that's handled. Hi.*

*Hi, Thalia.*

*I rewrote that first part like six times. Sorry. I've been writing to you from Camp Jupiter for more than a year now and I still don't know how to start letters. Octavian says a proper Roman letter should start with formal greetings and your full lineage and the current omens. So: Hail, Thalia Grace, daughter of Zeus, sister of Jason Grace, son of Jupiter, the eagles fly east and the liver was mostly normal today. There. That's Roman.*

*I miss you.*

*I wanted to get that down before I start showing off, because I'm going to show off a lot in this letter and I don't want you to think I forgot the important part. I miss you. I think about you every day. Not in a sad way all the time. Just — I see stuff here and I think, Thalia would hate this, or Thalia would love this, or Thalia would set this on fire, and then I have to write it down or I'll forget.*

*Dakota keeps trying to steal my notebook to see what I'm writing. I told him it's tactical observations. It's mostly you.*

*So. Camp Jupiter.*

*Where do I even start? It's nothing like Camp Half-Blood and it's everything like it at the same time. That's the weirdest part. Like meeting a cousin you never knew you had who has your nose but talks funny.*

*First: the place itself. It's in California, in the hills near San Francisco, hidden by magic like ours. But instead of strawberry fields and woods and cabins scattered everywhere, it's a valley with a little city in it. A real city. New Rome. With houses and streets and a forum and temples and shops. And a university! Actual Roman demigods go to college here. They have majors. Frank wants to major in military history and also snacks. That's a direct quote. I wrote it down.*

*The camp itself is on a hill overlooking the city — the Field of Mars, they call it, for war games. The legion lives in barracks, not cabins. Everyone's mixed together by cohort, not by godly parent. There are five cohorts. I'm in the Fifth, which is the losers cohort, which Reyna says is perfect for me because I have loser energy. I think she likes me, though. It's hard to tell. She's scary in the way you were scary when you were fourteen and Dad first— sorry. You know what I mean. Competent scary. Good scary.*

*Second: the Romans. They are SO Roman, Thalia. You would lose your mind. Everything is formations and discipline and honor and tradition. They march everywhere. They MARCH to breakfast. I tried skipping once and a centurion named Gwen physically blocked me with her shield and said, "We do not skip in the legion, Grace." I have not skipped since. I march now. I march in my sleep. Last week Hazel said I was marking time in my bunk.*

*Hazel! I have to tell you about Hazel and Frank. They're my — well, they're my best friends here, I guess. Like Percy and Annabeth are yours? Is that okay to say? I hope that's okay to say. Hazel Levesque, daughter of Pluto. She's thirteen, small, curly hair, gold eyes, rides a horse named Arion who is literally the fastest horse in the world and will bite anyone except her. She grew up in the 1940s and then died and then came back — it's a long story, I'll let her write it herself sometime, she says hi, by the way, she made me promise to say HAZEL SAYS HI THALIA in all caps. So: HAZEL SAYS HI THALIA. Frank Zhang, son of Mars. Sixteen, big, soft-spoken, can turn into animals — like, any animal, he turned into an elephant last week during war games and sat on three guys from the First Cohort and they had to forfeit. He's sweet and nervous and braver than anyone I know. You'll like them. I keep saving seats for you at meals even though you're three thousand miles away. Frank just eats your portion. Sorry.*

*Third: daily life. Okay, so a normal day. Reveille before dawn — yes, before dawn, Thalia, I know, I can hear you yelling from here. Then drills. Sword drills, shield wall drills, javelin, formation running. Then breakfast in the mess hall, sitting by cohort. Then classes — yes, classes, like Latin (I'm good at it, it's weird, it just clicks), Roman history (which is just Greek history but they insist they did it better), engineering, augury (don't get me started on Octavian), and survival. Then more drills. Then dinner. Then free time in New Rome if you have privileges. Then taps. Lights out. Every day. It's exhausting and I kind of love it? Is that weird? It's different from the stories you tell about traveling, always moving. Here I know where my bunk is. I know where my shield goes. There's something nice about that. Don't tell Octavian I said something nice about routine.*

*But I miss you two too. I miss the stars over the lake and the way Thalia keeps pretending she isn't counting heads every ten minutes. Do you still braid Annabeth's hair the same way before a patrol? Do you still sing off-key when you think no one's listening? Chiron mentioned it in his letter. He writes better letters than you, by the way. Shorter. Less threatening.*

*Tell me about the archery visitors. Tell me about everyone. Is Atalanta still beating everyone at the targets? Did Naomi ever get her wolf to stop chewing boots? Do Artemis's guests ever ask about me? You don't have to answer that if it's weird. I just wonder.*

*Okay, I need to tell you about Reyna properly because she's kind of my big sister here and I feel guilty about that, like I'm cheating on you. Reyna Avila Ramirez-Arellano, daughter of Bellona, praetor of the legion. She's seventeen, has this black horse Scipio and two gold dogs that are actually automaton guard dogs, and she can make people listen just by raising one eyebrow. She reminds me of you and Annabeth combined, which is terrifying. She runs the whole camp with this other guy, Octavian — no, he's not praetor, he's augur, he reads guts and is creepy and everyone knows he's power-hungry but he's good at paperwork so they keep him around? Roman politics are weird. Anyway, Reyna's been showing me the ropes. She says I fight like a Greek — meaning messy and showy — and she's trying to fix me. I told her my sister would kill her if she tried to fix me too much. She said she'd like to meet you and then decide who would win. Please never meet. California can't handle it.*

*Reyna asked about you. She asks about my family a lot. I told her about you — about how you ran away with Annabeth and kept her safe on the road and how you finally reached camp (she thought some of the monster stories were jokes, I had to promise they weren't) and how you zapped the crap out of everything at the Hoover Dam. She was quiet for a long time and then she said, "You miss her." Just like that. Not a question. I said yeah. She said, "Good. Romans who forget their sisters don't stay Roman long." So I'm staying Roman, I guess, by missing you.*

*She also asked if I had other sisters. I told her about Annabeth, of course, and then about Percy too. He's not blood, but he's a friend I want to see again. Is that okay? I hope I didn't overstep. The way you write about them makes me feel closer than a short visit ever could. Can you tell them that? Tell Percy I said thanks for looking out for you when I couldn't. Tell Annabeth I said thanks for being the sister Thalia actually listens to. (Thalia, don't kill me for saying that. It's true. Your handwriting gets softer when you write about her.)*

*Are they good? Are they happy? You said in your last letter that they were going steady and that Percy is still an idiot but a sweet idiot and Annabeth is still scary-smart but lets Percy get away with everything. Is that still true? Do they still sit on that hill behind the Athena cabin? You drew a map. I memorized it. Tell them I said hi. Tell them — ugh, this sounds dumb — tell them I hope someone writes them letters like this someday. Everyone deserves a Thalia letter.*

*I have so many questions. Okay, rapid-fire, answer all of these in your next letter or I will die of curiosity and then Reyna will have to do a whole funeral with eagles and it will be a hassle:*

*1. How is Camp Half-Blood? Is the lava wall still trying to kill people? Do the cabins still sing those stupid songs? What's new?*
*2. How are Clarisse and Silena? You mentioned them. Clarisse sounds terrifying. Silena sounds nice. Are they nice to you? If Clarisse is mean to Percy, tell her I will come to New York and challenge her to single combat. Roman style. With witnesses.*
*3. Is Percy still doing that thing where he talks to horses? Frank does that too, kind of, except he IS the horse sometimes. Do you think they'd get along? I think they'd get along.*
*4. Is Annabeth still drawing? You said she draws battle plans in the margins of her notebooks. I draw in my margins too. Do you think she'd like my drawings? Be honest. Don't show her the toga one. Actually, show her. She'll laugh. I need someone to laugh.*
*5. Do you think — okay, this is a real one. Do you think you'd ever come visit? There's a temple to Diana here. Small, on the edge of New Rome. Greek guests need permission in the legion — long story, old grudges — but family is family. Reyna says a family visit may be possible during the next school break when the legion's schedule allows. Would you ask Chiron about it? I want you and Annabeth there, if you'd both like to come. Percy could come another time if the adults agree. I want you to see my bunk. I want you to meet Hazel and Frank and Arion (from a safe distance, he bites). I want to show you the Little Tiber and the forum and the baths (the baths are AMAZING, Thalia, they're hot and there's mosaics and no monsters, I fell asleep in one and Dakota drew on my face). I just want my sister to see where I live.*

*Sorry. That got serious. Let's lighten up.*

*Funny story: last week we had war games — Fifth Cohort plus some others vs. First and Second, capture the flag but Roman, meaning with siege weapons and actual fort-building. I was on a scouting team with Hazel. We got chased by giant eagles (not friendly mail eagles like this one — this is Windy, she's Reyna's personal messenger, be nice to her, give her bacon if you have any). Anyway, Hazel and I hid in a ditch. A MUDDY ditch. Full of mud and old shield straps. Hazel looked at me and said, very seriously, "Praetor Reyna will never know." And we stayed there for an hour while Frank, as a giant badger, tunneled under the enemy fort and stole their banner. We won! Fifth Cohort never wins! Everyone carried Frank around and he turned bright red and then turned into a squirrel by accident from stress and fell into someone's helmet. I laughed so hard I cried. I wished you were there to laugh with me. You have that loud donkey laugh. I missed it.*

*Another funny story: Octavian tried to predict the chariot races by cutting open a pillow because he couldn't find a chicken — feathers everywhere, Dakota ate three and went to the infirmary. Justice.*

*Okay. I should wrap this up. Windy is getting impatient and I've used like ten pages. Sorry for the length. Sorry for the handwriting. Sorry for the toga drawing. Sorry for everything, basically. That's very Roman of me — we apologize by building aqueducts. I'll build you an aqueduct.*

*Thalia. Thank you for writing. Thank you for finding me. Thank you for being my big sister even when the gods tried to make us forget each other. Hera took me when I was so little that whole pieces of home are missing. I can't remember all the things you write about, but you write anyway. You sent that first letter through Hermes express with three pages of threats and one line at the end that said "You're still my brother, idiot." I keep it under my pillow. The Fifth Cohort calls you my terrifying pen pal. I let them. They'll understand the terrifying part when you visit. You're Thalia.*

*Come visit. Please. And write back. Long. With pictures. Tell Percy and Annabeth to add something. I want to hear from all of you.*

*Your little (growing again) brother,*

*Jason*

*P.S. Thalia, are you happy? With school and camp? Really happy? You always sound happy in your letters but you also sound tired. You don't have to be the tough one with me. I'm Roman now. I can handle heavy stuff. I lift shields all day.*

*P.P.S. Percy — if you're still reading — is it true you fought Ares when you were twelve? Frank doesn't believe me. Please confirm so I can win five denarii.*

*P.P.P.S. Annabeth — Thalia says you're the best architect at Camp and that you want to build things that last. New Rome lasts. You should see it. You'd love the temples. The Temple of Jupiter is huge. I go there sometimes just to think. It makes me feel small in a good way. Like family is bigger than me. Anyway. Thalia says you draw comics. Will you draw me one? Anything. Greeks vs. Romans. I'll show Hazel and Frank. Please?*

*P.P.P.P.S. Okay, last one. Tell Thalia she's allowed to cry at letters. Reyna says strong people cry. I cried writing this. Don't tell Octavian.*

Thalia stopped reading.

For a moment nobody said anything. The oak leaves rattled overhead. Down the hill, someone blew the conch for breakfast, long and low, but none of them moved.

Thalia was staring at the last page, at the little sketch of Jason in a toga with HELP written under it, her shoulders shaking. She pressed the heel of her hand hard against her eyes.

"Thals?" Percy said quietly.

"I'm fine," Thalia said thickly. "Shut up. I'm fine. It's just— gods, he's such a dork. A pillow. He—"

And then she was crying, silent at first and then with a hitched breath. Annabeth moved instantly, wrapping both arms around her. Percy leaned in from the other side, his hand on her back.

"He's okay," Thalia whispered into Annabeth's hair. "He's really okay. He's got friends. He's got— did you hear that? He saves me a seat. He's such an idiot. He's such a good kid."

"He sounds great," Annabeth said, her own eyes bright. "He sounds like you."

"He's grown again," Thalia sobbed. "He said he's growing. When did that happen? I missed it. I missed—"

"You didn't miss it," Percy said firmly. "You wrote him. You found him. That's why he wrote this, Thalia. Because you didn't let go."

Thalia pulled back, wiping her nose on her sleeve. "I am NOT crying. Grace sisters don't cry. It's allergies."

"Yes," Silena said gently from a few feet away, tears running openly down her own face. "Terrible allergies. In April."

Clarisse cleared her throat loudly, staring very hard at a tree root. "Stupid Roman sap," she muttered, swiping at her eyes with her wrist guard. "Making Thalia cry. I'm gonna kill him. In single combat. With witnesses."

Thalia laughed through tears, a real laugh this time, loud and donkey-like just as Jason had described. "Gods, I love him. I love that idiot so much."

Percy picked up the dried poppy from his knee and tucked it carefully behind Thalia's ear. "Then let's write him back. Right now. Long. With pictures. Like he asked."

Annabeth was already flipping to a blank page in the back of her notebook, pencil poised, eyes shining with that particular architect-artist light Percy loved. "He asked for a comic. Greeks vs. Romans. I'm on it."

"You don't have to—" Thalia started.

"Are you kidding?" Annabeth said. "He requested Annabeth original art? He's getting Annabeth original art. Percy, give me details. What should Jason know about Camp Half-Blood?"

Percy grinned, settling back on his elbows. "Tell him the lava wall still tries to kill people. Tell him the cabins still sing stupid songs. Tell him Clarisse still threatens everyone and Silena still keeps her human."

"Hey!" Clarisse said, but she was smiling.

Silena scooted closer, pulling her knees up. "Can I ask something? About Romans? Like, actually? I've always wondered. Do they really— do they really march everywhere? Even to breakfast?"

Thalia sniffled, folding Jason's letter with reverent care and tucking it back into the tube. "Apparently. My brother marches in his sleep now. Hera save us all."

"And they live in a city?" Silena pressed. "With shops? And a university? That's so different. We just have cabins and— and camp. Do they get to grow up there? Stay?"

Percy nodded slowly, thinking. "Jason said demigods go to college. Live there. Have families. That's... that's kind of amazing, actually. We just— we train and then we go out there and hope. They get a place."

"They get structure," Annabeth said, sketching as she talked, quick light lines forming panels. "Legion, cohorts, ranks. It's very Roman. Very stable. Very—" She glanced up. "Very lonely, in a way? If you don't fit? Jason said Fifth Cohort is the losers cohort. That sounds like us, honestly. The misfits finding each other."

Clarisse snorted. "Misfits win wars, Grace. Look at us."

"Exactly," Annabeth said, smiling. "That's what I'll draw. Greeks vs. Romans — but not fighting. Racing. Or building. Or eating. Percy, what's the funniest Greek thing we do that would confuse a Roman?"

Percy thought for a second, then lit up. "Capture the flag in pajamas. Tell him about last month when the Stolls greased the flagpole with peanut butter and Travis got stuck halfway up in his boxers with a Spider-Man cape."

Thalia barked a laugh. "Yes! Include that! Jason needs to know Greeks have no decorum whatsoever. Reyna said decorum. Tell her sister says decorum is overrated."

Annabeth's pencil flew. In the first panel, a tall blond boy in a toga labeled JASON (?) stood at attention with a shield, while a boy with black hair and a sea-green shirt labeled PERCY (DEFINITELY) slid down a peanut-buttered pole in his pajamas shouting WHEEE. In the second panel, a girl with curly blond hair and a pencil behind her ear labeled ANNABETH (GENIUS) built a tiny model city while a small girl with gold eyes and a huge horse labeled HAZEL (?) waved. In the third, a big boy turning into a squirrel labeled FRANK (?) fell into a helmet while Thalia, drawn with spiky hair and lightning bolts, laughed her donkey laugh.

"Oh my gods," Silena breathed, leaning over Annabeth's shoulder. "That's so good. Annabeth, that's— Hazel's horse is huge!"

"Arion is the fastest horse in the world," Percy said knowledgeably. "I should know. I speak horse. Tell Jason I said Blackie— I mean Arion — and I would get along. Tell Frank I can also turn into a fish? No, wait. Tell him I can't turn into anything but I can breathe underwater, which is almost as cool as turning into an elephant."

"Almost," Thalia said dryly, but she was smiling, watching Annabeth draw with her head bent close to Percy's, their temples almost touching. Percy kept handing her details — "give Jason a little laurel crown but crooked" — and Annabeth kept bumping his shoulder with hers when he made her laugh.

Clarisse watched them too, then looked away, then looked at Silena. Lowering her voice so only Silena and Thalia could hear, she said, "Okay, real question. Romans vs. Greeks. Who'd win? Honestly."

Thalia stretched her legs out, crossing her boots. She looked calmer now, pink-eyed but grounded, the letter tube hugged to her chest like armor. "Honestly? Romans would win a set battle. Discipline, shields, formations. Greeks would win a messed-up, no-plan, monsters-everywhere quest. Chaos. Improvisation."

"So us," Clarisse said with satisfaction.

"So us," Thalia agreed. "Jason fights like a Greek, Reyna says. Messy and showy. Good. Means he's learned plenty since Hera took him. I only got to coach him on the visit."

Silena chewed her lip. "Do you think— do you think they'd let us visit? During the next approved break, like he said? I've never left Camp except on quests. A whole city of demigods... shopping in New Rome..."

"You just want to see the shops," Clarisse accused, but fondly.

"And the temples," Silena said. "And the baths with mosaics. And Hazel and Frank. I want to meet Hazel. She sounds lovely."

"She sounds thirteen and raising a flesh-eating horse," Clarisse said. "But yeah. It'd be cool. To see Roman life. To see if their war games are tougher than ours."

Percy sat up. "We should go. All of us. Thalia, Annabeth, me, you guys. Road trip to California. Iris-message ahead: WARNING, GREEKS INCOMING, HIDE THE PILLOWS."

Everyone laughed. Thalia's laugh faded into something softer. She looked down at the tube in her arms. "I'd like that. To see his bunk. To meet Reyna and tell her hands off my brother. To—" She swallowed. "To see that temple to Diana. To know he's got gods watching him too."

Annabeth paused in her drawing, looking up. "He asked if you were happy, you know. At the end. If you're really happy with school and camp."

Thalia was quiet. A jay screamed in the woods. The breakfast conch blew again, more insistent.

"Are you?" Percy asked gently. Not teasing. Just brotherly.

Thalia picked at a thread on her sleeve. "Yeah. I am. I love learning something that isn't about monsters, and having a purpose that isn't just waiting for Zeus to throw another lightning bolt at my family. But..." She glanced at Percy and Annabeth, curled together so naturally, at Clarisse and Silena shoulder to shoulder. "Sometimes I miss camp when I'm at school. Staying. Having people who know where my bunk is. Jason has that now. I'm glad. I'm jealous too. Is that awful?"

"No," Annabeth said immediately. "That's human. That's sister."

"You're allowed to miss more than one home," Percy added.

Thalia smiled crookedly. "Hearts are stretchy. Did Annabeth teach you that?"

"Nope. That was all me. I'm profound."

"You profounded all over my sleeve," Thalia said, but she leaned her head briefly against his shoulder. "Thanks, Kelp Head."

Annabeth had gone back to drawing, but her ears were pink. She added a fourth panel: Thalia on a hill under stars, with two small figures waving from far away — one with a shield, one with SPQR — and a speech bubble from Thalia: STILL MY BROTHER, IDIOT. Underneath, she wrote in neat capitals: COME VISIT SOON. BRING BACON FOR WINDY. — P.S. FROM ANNABETH: YOUR TOGA DRAWING WAS TERRIBLE. I'VE REDRAWN YOUR ARMOR PROPERLY. YOU'RE WELCOME.

Percy howled. "Let me add something! Let me add something!"

He took the pencil, tongue sticking out in concentration, and in the margin drew a wobbly fish with a laurel wreath and a speech bubble: HI JASON! I'M A ROMAN FISH NOW! I MARCH IN FORMATION! BLUB BLUB! — PERCY (CONFIRMED NOT EATEN).

"It's beautiful," Thalia said solemnly. "It's the worst thing I've ever seen."

"It's perfect," Annabeth corrected, taking the pencil back and adding little motion lines to the fish so it looked like it was marching. "He asked for a sea joke. He got a sea joke."

"He asked if you fought Ares at twelve," Thalia remembered. "For five denarii."

Percy grinned wickedly. "Tell Frank: yes. And I won. And Ares cried. Okay, he didn't cry, but he bled. Tell Frank five denarii please, payable in bacon for Windy."

"I'll write the actual letter," Thalia said, pulling a folded piece of her own stationery — dark blue with a lightning watermark — from her jacket. "You guys do pictures. I'll do words. But—" She hesitated. "Will you guys stay? While I write? I don't— I want you here."

Annabeth didn't even hesitate. She shifted so Thalia could lean against the oak between her and Percy, all three in a row. Clarisse and Silena settled in the grass in front of them, Silena braiding grass into a crown, Clarisse sharpening a dagger but listening with her whole body.

"Always," Annabeth said.

Thalia uncapped her pen. For a while there was only the scratch of pens and pencils, the wind, the distant clatter of breakfast. Percy lay back with his hands behind his head, watching clouds, occasionally suggesting additions — "Tell him Clarisse says hi and also she could beat Reyna in arm wrestling" — "I NEVER SAID THAT" — "You were thinking it" — and Annabeth shading her comic with careful crosshatching, Silena offering color commentary — "Give Hazel bigger curls, she's cuter that way" — and Thalia writing steadily, pausing sometimes to press her sleeve to her eyes and keep going.

What did Thalia write? Percy peeked once and saw fragments:

*Hey Dirtbag —*

*Got your brick. Cried like a baby. Deny it and die...*

*Camp's good. Percy's here. Annabeth's here. They're disgusting. They share desserts. They hold hands during war councils. I want to throw up and also I get it now...*

*I'm proud of you. So proud it hurts...*

*Yes, I'm happy. Don't worry about me. Worry about your shield drills...*

*Reyna sounds cool. Tell her if she makes you march in your sleep again I'll come west and... actually tell her thanks. For looking out for my brother when I couldn't...*

*Come home? No — you ARE home. I'll come to you. When the adults agree on a date, I'll ask you when you're free. No surprise trip. I'll bring Annabeth if you want her there...*

*You're still my brother, idiot. Love — T.*

Percy pretended not to have read the last line, but his throat got tight. He rolled onto his side and bumped Thalia's boot with his head.

"You're a good big sister, you know," he said quietly.

Thalia snorted. "Annabeth's his big sister too. Apparently he already knows which of us gives better instructions."

"No, I mean it," Percy said. He glanced at Annabeth, who had stopped drawing to listen, pencil hovering. "You wrote him even when he forgot you. You didn't give up. That's— that's the job. Showing up."

Thalia looked at him, really looked, her electric-blue eyes bright and wet and fierce. "You'd do the same."

"Yeah," Percy said. "I learned from the best. You and Annabeth. You're both—" He fumbled, suddenly shy under Annabeth's gaze. "You're both good at that. Looking out. Being family."

Annabeth's cheeks went pink. She ducked her head, but Percy caught her hand, lacing their fingers in the grass where Thalia could see and Clarisse pretended not to see and Silena smiled into her grass crown.

"You are a good big sister too," Percy told Annabeth softly. "You know that, right? With Jason. Even without the memories everyone expects you to have. With everyone. You're the person people listen to."

Annabeth stared at him. "Percy—"

"I mean it," he said. He was blushing furiously now, ears red, but he didn't look away. "You're brilliant and you're kind and you draw reply comics for homesick Roman brothers and you make people feel— safe. Like they have a place. That's big-sister stuff. That's— that's mom stuff, someday, if you want. Far someday. Like, way far. College and jobs and— but you'd be amazing."

Silence. Even Clarisse stopped sharpening.

Annabeth's eyes filled. She laughed, a little overwhelmed, a little watery, and pressed her forehead to his. "You can't just say stuff like that before breakfast, Seaweed Brain. I'm dehydrated. I'm vulnerable."

"Sorry," Percy whispered, grinning. "It's true, though."

"It's true," Thalia echoed gruffly, clearing her throat hard. "Annabeth, you're the little sister I actually listen to. Jason's right. Don't let it go to your head."

"It is going to my head," Annabeth said, laughing through tears, hugging Percy tight with one arm and reaching out to squeeze Thalia's hand with the other. "It's going straight to my head and staying there."

"Gross," Clarisse announced to the sky. "Romance before nine a.m. should be illegal. Silena, back me up."

Silena was crying again, happily. "Nope. It's perfect. It's family fluff. Let them have it."

"Family fluff?" Clarisse said. "What does that even—"

"It means," Silena said, standing and brushing grass off her pajama shorts, "that we're going to breakfast, we're bringing Thalia's letter-tube as our honored guest, we're telling everyone that Romans march to breakfast and Greeks slide down peanut-butter poles in Spider-Man capes, and we're going to plan a trip to California for spring break. And Clarisse is going to admit she's excited."

"I am not—" Clarisse started, then saw Thalia's face, Percy's hopeful grin, Annabeth's shining eyes. She sighed theatrically. "Fine. I'm excited. But if any Roman tries to make me march, I'm stabbing them. With witnesses. Roman style."

Thalia stood, tucking Jason's letter safe against her heart, Annabeth's comic carefully rolled on top. Windy's feather she tucked behind her other ear to match the poppy.

"Come on, family," she said. "Breakfast. I'm starving. And somebody's gotta tell the Stolls that Jason will push them into the Tiber."

Percy jumped up, pulling Annabeth with him, keeping her hand. Annabeth tucked her notebook under her other arm, leaning into him as they walked. Clarisse fell in beside Thalia, bumping shoulders in rough affection. Silena looped arms with both of them, grass crown perched crookedly on Clarisse's head before Clarisse could protest.

Behind them, the oak leaves whispered. Ahead, the dining pavilion buzzed, bacon scent drifting up the hill.

Thalia looked west, toward California, where her brother was probably marching to breakfast right now.

"See you when we both have a date we can keep, Dirtbag," she whispered, and smiled through happy tears, and walked down to breakfast surrounded by family.
