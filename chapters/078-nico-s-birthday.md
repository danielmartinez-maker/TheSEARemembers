## Chapter 78: Nico's Birthday

Bianca di Angelo knew two things for certain on a Monday morning in April.

The first was that her little brother’s birthday was on Friday. The second was that no one at Camp Half-Blood could know that she had almost forgotten.

It was not that she did not care. She cared so much it sat like a stone behind her ribs every time she looked at him. It was that time moved strangely at camp. Days bled together in a wash of sword practice and dishes and capture the flag and late-night whispers in cabins that smelled like cedar and old blankets. The recent weeks had been monsters and new campers and the Hermes cabin setting off a stink bomb in the Big House plumbing. April had turned mild and bright, and somehow, without her noticing, the little marks she had been scratching on the inside of her trunk — four, then five, then six days — had become only a few days left.

Nico had not said anything. That was the worst part. He had not said, My birthday is coming. He had not hinted or begged or left drawings of cakes on her pillow the way he used to when they lived in the Lotus Hotel and time had not meant anything at all. He had just gotten quieter, watchful in the way he got when he was trying not to want something too much in case it did not happen.

Bianca had seen him that morning at breakfast, sitting at the end of the Hades table with his feet not quite touching the ground, swinging them a little, carefully cutting his pancakes into exact squares. He had Mythomagic cards in his pocket — she could see the corners sticking out — and a smudge of dirt on his cheek from where he had been playing with the skeleton flowers behind Cabin Thirteen that only bloomed for him.

He caught her looking and grinned at her, quick and missing one tooth on the side, and mouthed, Hi.

Her heart cracked clean in half.

She waited until he ran off to morning activities with the Apollo kids — they were teaching the younger campers how to shoot without closing both eyes — and then she went to find Chiron.

He was on the Big House porch, grading archery scores and drinking something that smelled like hay and apples. When she told him, in a rush, that Nico’s birthday was on Friday and she had no present and no plan and she was a terrible sister, he listened the way he always did, like he had all the time in the world and she was the only war council that mattered.

"You are not a terrible sister, Bianca," he said firmly. "You are a sister who noticed in time. That counts for a great deal."

"I want to do something good," she said, twisting the hem of her jacket. "Not just — not just cake in the mess hall. Everyone gets that. He deserves — he had years where we didn't celebrate because we didn't know what day it was. In the Lotus, I mean. And then after we came out, everything was scary and running. I want him to feel like — like here is different. Like here is home."

Chiron set down his clipboard. His wise old eyes were very kind.

"Then let us make it so," he said. "And, my dear, you do not have to do it alone. This camp loves nothing more than a secret project."

By lunch, it was not a secret project. It was a conspiracy.

---

Bianca had meant to ask one person. She asked Percy first because he was sitting nearest to her at lunch and because Nico worshipped him in the quiet, sideways way little brothers worship big brothers they will not admit to worshipping.

Percy Jackson was sun-browned and perpetually damp around the edges, as if the lake had claimed him and only lent him back for meals. He was shoveling spaghetti into his mouth when Bianca slid onto the bench across from him and whispered, "Can you help me with something for Nico?"

He stopped mid-bite. "Yeah. Of course. Anything. Is he okay?"

"He's fine. It's his birthday Friday. I want to throw him a surprise party."

Percy's whole face lit up. For a moment he looked like a little kid himself.

"A surprise party! Yes! What do you need? Do you need me to steal something? I am great at stealing things for good reasons. Annabeth tells me I can't say that anymore, but it's true."

Bianca laughed before she could stop herself. Percy had that effect. He was not her brother, not by blood, but camp did funny things to the word sibling. He and his girlfriend Annabeth were the closest thing to a fixed point the camp had — bickering, inseparable, always watching each other's backs without having to be asked.

"I need water decorations," Bianca said. "Nico loves the lake, but he's scared of swimming where it's deep. I thought — could you do something with the water? Like something only you could do?"

Percy leaned forward, spaghetti forgotten. "Oh. Oh, I got you. I can do floating lanterns. No fire, so it's safe. Just water shapes holding light. Like jellyfish lamps. Or little boats. I did it once for Annabeth when she was sick in winter and couldn't go outside — I made snow melt into fish and swim around her bunk. She pretended she hated it and then made me do it for an hour."

From two seats down, Annabeth Grace, who had been pretending not to eavesdrop while reading a book on siege engineering, said without looking up, "I did hate it. It dripped on my notes."

"You loved it," Percy said.

"I tolerated it."

"You kept the little fish for three days in a bowl."

Annabeth turned a page. "That was for scientific observation."

Percy winked at Bianca. "She loved it. I'll make Nico a whole fleet. What does he like?"

"Skeletons," Bianca said promptly. "Ghosts. Shadows. Mythomagic. He likes knights, too. And — he likes stars. He tells me the stars are different here than he remembers."

Percy nodded solemnly, as if taking royal orders. "Ghost ships and starfish and shadow jellyfish. Got it. I'll practice in the canoe lake where no one can see and pretend I'm just doing extra training if anyone asks."

"You are the best," Bianca said, and meant it.

"I'm the second best," Percy corrected, jerking his thumb at his girlfriend. "She's going to want in, and whatever she builds will be better than mine and she will never let me forget it."

Annabeth finally looked up, gray eyes sharp and amused. Her hair was pulled back in a messy ponytail with a pencil stuck through it, and there was sawdust on her shirt from the workshop.

"I heard my name and the words surprise party," she said. "Bianca, tell me everything."

---

Annabeth listened the way she always listened, chin in her hand, eyes focused, asking three questions for every answer until the vague shape of an idea became a plan with dimensions.

"Where?" she asked.

"I was thinking behind the Hades cabin," Bianca said. "There's that little clearing in the trees. Nico likes it because it's shady. He says bright sun gives him a headache sometimes. And he can — you know. He can do his shadow stuff there without people staring."

Annabeth nodded. She understood. Nico was still learning what it meant to be a son of Hades at a camp that, for all its talk of family, still got quiet when shadows moved wrong.

"Okay," she said. "I'll build him a fort."

Bianca blinked. "A fort?"

"A shadow-friendly fort," Annabeth corrected, already sketching on a napkin with the pencil from her hair. "Not a bunker. Not a tent. A real fort. Low walls so adults can see in but it still feels secret. A roof with slats so sunlight comes through in stripes but there's always shade to slip into. A loft for Mythomagic battles. A hidden compartment under the floorboards — every good fort needs one. And I'll line the posts with bronze so monsters can't just walk in. It'll be his and yours. Somewhere that's his even when the rest of camp feels too loud."

Percy peered at the sketch. "That looks structurally unsound."

"It is a napkin sketch, Seaweed Brain."

"It looks like a pancake with legs."

Annabeth flicked spaghetti at him, and he caught it in his mouth to Bianca's delight.

"I can have Malcolm and the Athena kids help haul lumber," Annabeth went on. "And I'll ask the Hephaestus cabin for brackets. But the design is mine. And yours," she added to Bianca. "You tell me what he needs. I build it."

Bianca felt her throat get tight. "He needs somewhere no one tells him to be less creepy," she said quietly.

Annabeth reached across the table and squeezed her wrist, brief and fierce. As the girlfriend of the most infamous troublemaker at camp and Jason's older sister, she knew exactly what it was to love someone the world decided was too much.

"Then that's what he'll get," she said.

Across the pavilion, as if summoned by the words fort and secret, a tray clattered. Clarisse La Rue stood with her hands on her hips, glaring at a new Ares kid who had dropped his plate. She was broad-shouldered, with a permanent scowl that had softened exactly two degrees since last summer — which at camp counted as a personality transplant.

Nico adored her. Utterly. Hopelessly. Bianca had never told anyone, but she had seen his drawings: Clarisse with her spear, ten feet tall, lightning behind her, labeled in wobbly Greek THE BEST WARRIOR.

"I have another idea," Bianca said slowly. "But she's going to say no."

Percy followed her gaze and grinned. "Nah. She'll say yes. You just have to ask her right."

"And what's right?"

"Tell her Nico thinks she's awesome and wants to be like her when he grows up. Then run before she gets emotional and tries to kill you to cover it up."

Annabeth snorted. "That is actually accurate sibling advice. I'm horrified."

---

Asking Clarisse was exactly as terrifying as Bianca expected.

She found her after lunch at the training arena, running sword drills with the Ares campers she trained. The air rang with wood on wood and thudding shields. Dust hung gold in the light. Clarisse was in the center, helmet under her arm, demonstrating a disarm with brisk, economical movements that made it look easy and were absolutely not easy.

Bianca waited until there was a water break, twisting her hands together, and then stepped forward before she could lose her nerve.

"Clarisse? Can I ask you something?"

Clarisse wiped sweat from her forehead. "If it's about your brother putting plastic skeletons in my bunk again, tell him I kept the big one. It's got good form."

Bianca flushed. "He — sorry. He thought you'd think it was funny."

"I did think it was funny. Don't tell anyone." Clarisse took a long drink. "What do you need, di Angelo?"

"It's Nico's birthday Friday. I'm doing a surprise party and — he really looks up to you. Like, a lot. He has drawings. Would you — would you spar with him? As a present? Not a real fight. Just — show him a move? Let him win a little? He loves watching you fight."

For a moment Clarisse's face did something complicated. The scowl slipped. Underneath was surprise, and something softer and almost panicked, like she had been handed something fragile.

"He’s little," Clarisse said gruffly. "He should be playing tag, not sparring."

"He plays tag," Bianca said. "He's terrible at it. He hides so well no one can find him and then he gets lonely in the hiding spot."

Clarisse snorted, then sighed. She looked over at the dummies, at the trainees wrestling in the dirt, at nothing in particular.

"Poseidon never made much of my birthdays," she said abruptly. "Ares cabin does, you know? I used to train with them on those days. They do cake and punch the birthday kid in the arm for each year and then we punch them once extra for luck. But before camp — nah. I got a new knife when I was seven and told not to cry when I cut myself. That's it."

Bianca did not know what to say, so she just waited.

Clarisse rolled her shoulders, uncomfortable. "Yeah. I'll do it. I'll teach him the La Rue Special. It's a shield fake into a low sweep. Totally unfair. He'll love it. I'll bring a spare helmet so he doesn't crack his head open and make you kill me."

"Really?" Bianca breathed.

"Yeah. But if he cries, I'm blaming you."

"He won't cry. He'll probably try to summon a skeleton to be his sparring partner."

"Good kid," Clarisse said with approval. "Friday, huh? I'll be there. And — tell Jackson to stay out of my way. Last time he 'helped' with a birthday he flooded the arena."

From the edge of the arena, Percy, who had been hovering to provide moral support, called, "It was one time! And the cake was still edible! It was just a little moist!"

"It was soup, Jackson!"

"It was cake soup! That's innovative!"

Clarisse threw a practice sword at him. He caught it. Bianca laughed so hard her stomach hurt, and for the first time since Monday morning, the stone behind her ribs felt a little lighter.

---

Silena Beauregard cried when Bianca asked her.

Not big dramatic tears — just immediate, shining eyes and a hand pressed to her chest. "A baby's birthday! Oh, of course! Oh, honey, come here," she said, pulling Bianca into a hug that smelled like vanilla and sunshine. Behind her, the Aphrodite cabin's front porch was draped with drying flowers and ribbons, and someone was playing a lyre badly but enthusiastically inside.

Nico had once told Bianca, very seriously, that Silena was a princess from a fairy tale who had gotten lost and decided to live at camp. Bianca had not corrected him.

"What kind of cake does he like?" Silena asked, already pulling out a notebook covered in stickers. "Chocolate? Vanilla? Red velvet? Does he have a favorite color? Favorite animal? Favorite godly parent — no, don't answer that, it's obviously you."

"He likes chocolate," Bianca said. "And black. And — he likes blue. Like the lake. And he likes those little candy eyeballs? For Halloween? He saves them."

Silena clapped her hands. "A midnight chocolate cake with blue frosting like the night sky! And candy stars! And those eyeballs on little ghost meringues! Oh, this is going to be adorable and slightly disturbing, which is perfect for him."

"I don't want to make it too girly," Bianca said anxiously. "He's at that age where —"

"Honey," Silena said gently, "pretty is not girly. Pretty is powerful. I am going to make that boy a cake so beautiful he will feel beautiful eating it. There's a difference."

She recruited two Aphrodite friends for frosting duty, one for meringue ghosts, and herself to the cake layers. She also, to Bianca's astonishment, insisted on making a crown.

"Not a princess crown," she clarified, seeing Bianca's face. "A king's crown. Dark silver, with little skulls. He can wear it all day and no one is allowed to make fun of him. I'll hex anyone who tries. Lovingly."

Bianca left the Aphrodite cabin with a list of ingredients and a ribbon tied around her wrist for luck.

She was rounding the corner toward the Big House when the sky cracked open.

It was not a big crack. Just a snap and a sizzle, like static on a wool blanket. A bolt of lightning no thicker than her finger struck the grass ten feet away, fizzled, and left behind a perfect smoking circle and the smell of ozone.

Thalia Grace stood in the middle of the path with her hands on her hips, frowning at the sky.

"No! Smaller!" she shouted at it. Or at herself. "I said tiny!"

Bianca stared. Thalia's black hair was standing up slightly at the ends. Her blue eyes were narrowed in concentration. Sparks crawled over her knuckles.

"Sorry!" Thalia said when she saw Bianca. "Sorry. I'm practicing. Don't walk there, it's tingly."

"Practicing what?"

"Birthday fireworks." Thalia grinned, sheepish and proud. "Annabeth told me. Look, I can't do big stuff — last time I tried party tricks I set a table on fire. But Chiron said if I can keep it small and controlled, I can do a finale. Small pops, no boom. Like sparklers but lightning. Safe lightning. Which sounds fake, but —"

She held out her hand, breathed out slow, and let a thread of electricity dance across her palm. It split into three, then five tiny sparks that hung in the air like fireflies, white-blue and humming faintly, then faded.

Bianca gasped. "That's amazing."

"It's nothing," Thalia muttered, but she was pleased. Her whole posture loosened. Thalia, daughter of Zeus, big sister, professional tough kid, had a soft spot for little brothers the size of the moon. She had once carried Nico on her shoulders for an entire capture-the-flag game because he had twisted his ankle and refused to go to the infirmary.

"Nothing is going to be too loud?" Bianca asked. "He doesn't like loud booms. Thunderstorms scare him."

Thalia's expression turned serious. "Then no booms. I swear on the Styx. Just light."

She held out her pinky. Bianca linked it with hers, solemn as an oath.

"Styx is listening, you know," Bianca whispered.

"Good," Thalia said. "Let her hear I'm good for something besides breaking things."

---

For three days, Camp Half-Blood kept a secret, which was a miracle on par with the Labyrinth.

Percy practiced at dawn in the canoe lake while mist still hung low. At first his shapes collapsed — a ghost ship that melted into a blob, a jellyfish that looked, in Annabeth's words, like a haunted jelly donut.

"You're overthinking it," Annabeth told him Wednesday morning. "Water wants to be round. Don't force corners. Suggest them."

He tried again, softer. The water rose and settled into a small translucent boat with a skull on its sail, glowing with bottled daylight from the Apollo kids.

By Thursday he had a fleet: six ghost ships, a dozen pulsing jellyfish lanterns, and glowing starfish. He hid them in a cove under Mist with a sign that said in wobbly Greek DO NOT TOUCH — SCIENCE.

Annabeth built like a woman possessed. She and Malcolm and two Hephaestus kids — Beckendorf and Nyssa — hauled cedar planks to the clearing behind Cabin Thirteen. They worked in the afternoons when Nico was at archery or in the library with his Mythomagic club, sawing and hammering while Juniper and a few dryads wove living ivy through the rails to soften the edges.

The fort rose quickly because Annabeth had designed it to. Four low walls, corner posts wrapped in Stygian iron, a slatted roof that dappled the interior with light and shadow. A loft platform just big enough for two kids and an army of plastic figures. And under the main floor, a trapdoor leading to a box lined with velvet — Bianca's idea — for treasures.

"This is good work," Beckendorf said on Thursday evening, wiping sweat from his brow and admiring the joints. "You sure you're Zeus and not Hephaestus?"

"I am sure I am better than Hephaestus," Annabeth said, but she smiled. She ran her hand along the smooth rail and imagined Nico's face. She thought about being twelve and new and certain everyone was watching her fail. She thought about Percy teaching her to skip stones when she was frustrated, his endless patience. Siblings, she had learned, were not just people you lived with. They were people you built shelters for.

She carved two names into the lintel over the entrance, small and neat: BIANCA + NICO. Underneath, in smaller letters: EST. CAMP.

Percy found her there after dinner, staring at it.

"You did good," he said, bumping his shoulder to hers.

"You think he'll like it?" she asked, suddenly nervous. Annabeth Grace was rarely nervous about buildings. People were harder.

"He's going to lose his mind," Percy said.

She laughed, leaned her head briefly against his shoulder.

"Thanks for helping with the Mist," he said.

"Thanks for not flooding the fort," she said.

"I made no promises."

---

Nico knew something was happening. Of course he knew. He was young, not stupid.

On Tuesday, Clarisse winked at him. Clarisse never winked. He spent an entire hour theorizing with his best friend Cecil from the Hermes cabin that she had something in her eye.

On Wednesday, Silena asked him, very casually, what his favorite cake flavor was, and when he said chocolate with extra frosting, she wrote it down like it was battle plans and then hugged him so hard his ribs creaked.

On Thursday, Percy kept smiling at him in a shifty way and asking weird questions like, "If you were a ghost pirate, would you prefer a hat or a bandana?" When Nico said, "Hat. With a skull. Duh," Percy nodded gravely and said, "Noted," and walked into the lake fully clothed.

But nobody said birthday. Nobody said party. And by Thursday night, as Bianca tucked his blanket around him in the Hades cabin — their cabin, with its black walls covered in his drawings and her stars charts, with two beds and a rug Mrs. O'Leary the hellhound had chewed and then slept on as apology — Nico had decided he had imagined it.

Maybe, he thought as Bianca hummed an old Italian lullaby their mother used to sing, maybe camp did not do birthdays for kids like him. Maybe sons of Hades did not get parties. Maybe it was okay. He had Bianca. He had his cards. He had a hellhound who drooled on his pillow.

He fell asleep holding a plastic skeleton warrior and trying not to hope.

Bianca watched him sleep for a long time, his mouth open, his hair sticking up, one hand curled around the toy. Her chest ached with love so fierce it felt like anger — at the Lotus, at the Titans, at every year they had lost.

"Just wait, piccolino," she whispered. "Just wait."

---

Friday dawned bright and blue and perfect, the kind of mild April day that felt like spring showing off between showers.

Bianca woke Nico the way she always did, by jumping on his bed and shouting in Italian until he shrieked and tried to smother her with a pillow. They ate breakfast — pancakes cut into squares — and she gave him his first present, alone, before the day could start: a small bronze ring with a skull on it, sized for his finger, that she had traded three weeks of dish duty to get Beckendorf to make.

"For protection," she said as she slipped it on. "And so you always have a skeleton with you."

Nico stared at it, turning his hand this way and that so it caught the light. "It's cool," he whispered, which from him meant I love it so much I might cry and I will die if you tell anyone.

Then he looked up, hopeful and trying to hide it. "Did you — is there —?"

Bianca's face was a perfect mask of sisterly innocence, honed by years of practice. "Is there what?"

"Nothing," he mumbled, swinging his feet.

"Good. Because Clarisse wants you at the arena at ten. She said something about footwork. Better not be late."

His head snapped up. "Clarisse? Really?"

"Really. Go brush your teeth. You have dirt on your face again."

He ran. Bianca exhaled. Phase one complete.

At ten o'clock sharp, half the camp found excuses to be near the training arena.

Clarisse was waiting, arms crossed, with two wooden swords and two helmets that looked comically large and small next to each other. Nico skidded to a stop in front of her, helmet already half on backwards, eyes huge.

"You wanted to learn to fight?" Clarisse barked.

"Yes, ma'am!" Nico squeaked.

"Good. Rule one: Don't die. Rule two: Look cool doing rule one. Rule three: If Jackson tells you to do something heroic, think twice."

Percy, lurking behind a dummy with Annabeth, whispered, "Hey!"

"Rule four," Clarisse continued, ignoring him, "is the La Rue Special. Watch."

What followed was fifteen minutes of pure joy. Clarisse taught the way she fought — direct, no nonsense, patient in spite of herself. She showed Nico how to hold the sword without strangling it, how to fake high with the shield and sweep low, how to roll without landing on his head. Nico, who was small and quick and utterly fearless when he was not thinking about being scared, picked it up fast. He stuck his tongue out when he concentrated. He shouted "HA!" every time he scored a touch, which Clarisse allowed exactly three times before she swept his legs and put him gently in the dirt.

"Again," she ordered.

He got up, grinning with a mouth full of dust. "Again!"

On the fourth try, he faked her — a tiny, perfect shield lift — and tapped her knee. The arena went silent. Clarisse looked down at the wooden blade against her greave, then at him.

Then she threw back her head and laughed, loud and startled.

"Good!" she barked, hauling him to his feet and ruffling his hair so hard his helmet spun. "Good! You got me! Remember that when you're telling stories — you beat Clarisse La Rue fair and square on your birthday!"

Nico froze. "My — you knew?"

Clarisse, realizing her mistake, went red to the ears. "No. I — shut up. Happy birthday, kid. Here."

She shoved a package into his hands — clumsily wrapped in red cloth. Inside was a leather bracer, sized small, with a boar's head stamped on it and his initials burned into the edge.

"So you don't bruise your arm like an idiot," she muttered. "And so you look like a real fighter."

Nico held it to his chest like it was gold. "Thank you," he whispered. "I'll wear it always. Even sleeping. Even bathing."

"Don't wear it bathing, it'll stink," Clarisse said, but her voice was thick. She cleared her throat, turned away, and shouted at the trainees to get back to drills before they all turned to stone. As Nico ran off to show Bianca, she watched him go with an expression Percy caught and would tease her about for months.

"You're soft," Percy mouthed.

"You're soup," Clarisse mouthed back, but she was smiling.

---

"Where are we going?" Nico demanded for the fifth time as Bianca led him, blindfolded with her own jacket, through the woods behind their cabin that afternoon. "Is it the infirmary? Did Mrs. O'Leary eat something again?"

"Nope."

"Is it lessons? I already did archery. I hit the target twice. Well, I hit the target once and Cecil once—"

"Nope."

"Bianca—"

"Shh. Walk."

She stopped him at the edge of the clearing. Behind her, she could hear the held breath of thirty campers crammed behind trees and the fort walls. She could hear Percy whispering, "Everybody ready? On three—" and Annabeth hissing, "Percy, that's the surprise ruined—" and Thalia crackling faintly like a bug zapper.

Bianca pulled the jacket away. "Surprise!"

"HAPPY BIRTHDAY, NICO!"

Nico stumbled back a step, hands flying to his face. His eyes were huge and dark and shining.

The clearing was transformed.

From the trees hung paper stars and black ribbons and little skull lanterns Silena had made. The fort stood in the center — his fort, with his and Bianca's names over the door — draped with a banner that read in wobbly, loving letters: HAPPY 9TH NICO!! WE LOVE YOU!! The loft was already full of Mythomagic figures arranged in battle formation. The hidden compartment, Bianca whispered, had candy in it.

And the water — Nico gasped at the water.

Percy stood by a shallow basin he had filled that morning, hands raised, grinning like an idiot. As Nico watched, six tiny ghost ships rose from the surface, sails glowing blue, and sailed in a circle in the air before settling to float just above the grass. Jellyfish lanterns pulsed softly around the fort's posts. Starfish glowed on the rails.

"Ghost pirates!" Percy shouted. "With hats! With skulls! As requested!"

Nico ran to him and stopped short, staring up. "You did this? For me?"

"Of course, man. Happy birthday. Want to drive one?"

Nico nodded, speechless, and Percy knelt and let him put his hands over his, guiding the water together. The ship wobbled, steadied, spun. Nico laughed — a high, startled, delighted sound Bianca had not heard in months.

Annabeth crouched beside the fort entrance. "Want to see inside, birthday boy?"

Nico crawled in, Bianca after him. Inside it was cool and dim and perfect, striped with light. The floor was smooth. The velvet box was full of chocolate coins. On the loft wall, Annabeth had burned a tiny owl and a trident and a skull, all three together.

"It's shadow-friendly," Annabeth explained, suddenly shy. "So you can practice and no one bothers you. And it's strong. It'll hold you and Bianca and about ten skeletons. I calculated."

Nico threw his arms around her neck, sudden and fierce. Annabeth, who was not a hugger with most people, caught him and held on tight.

"Thank you," he mumbled into her shoulder. "It's the coolest thing anyone ever made."

"You're welcome, kiddo," she said softly, meeting Bianca's eyes over his head. Sisters, both of them, understanding each other perfectly.

Then Silena arrived with the cake, carried high like a torch, her siblings trailing with sparklers, and everyone sang — off-key, too loud, wonderful. The cake was midnight chocolate with blue frosting and candy stars and four little meringue ghosts with candy eyeballs that Nico immediately declared his favorites and refused to eat because they were too cool, then ate because they were too delicious.

He wore the dark silver crown and the leather bracer and the bronze ring, and he sat in the middle of the fort with crumbs on his face while campers he barely knew gave him presents: a new pack of Mythomagic cards from Cecil, a carved bone whistle from Grover, a sketch of Mrs. O'Leary wearing a party hat from Piper, a tiny shield from the Ares cabin with NICO'S BIRTHDAY MASSACRE — JUST KIDDING scratched on the back.

He opened each one like it was treasure. He thanked each person by name, even when he had to ask Bianca to whisper it first. He kept looking around like he was afraid it would vanish if he blinked.

Bianca watched from the fort doorway with her hands pressed to her mouth, crying and laughing at the same time. Percy appeared beside her and handed her a handkerchief that was soaking wet.

"Sorry," he said. "It's all I have. I was in the lake."

She took it anyway. "Thank you. For all of this."

"Hey. That's what big brothers are for. Even borrowed ones." He bumped her shoulder gently. "You did good, Bianca. He's happy."

"He thinks everyone forgot," she whispered. "Last night — he didn't say, but I could tell."

Percy looked at Nico, who was currently trying to teach Clarisse how to play Mythomagic while Clarisse pretended not to be interested and was clearly taking notes.

"He knows now," Percy said. "Trust me. Kids remember this stuff. I still remember every birthday Annabeth made me a card with a trap in it."

"That was one time!" Annabeth called from inside the fort, where she was helping Nico arrange his new cards. "And it was a pop-up, not a trap."

"It popped up and hit me in the eye!"

"It built character!"

Bianca laughed through tears. This — this bickering, this warmth — this was what she had wanted for Nico. Not just a party. A family so big and loud he could never doubt his place in it again.

---

As dusk fell, Thalia took Nico's hand and led him to the center of the clearing.

"Close your eyes," she told him. "No, it's not scary. Count to five."

Nico closed his eyes tight. Thalia looked at Bianca, who nodded. She looked at Chiron, standing at the edge of the trees with a blanket around his shoulders, who nodded too.

Thalia breathed in, raised both hands, and let go.

Not lightning as a weapon. Lightning as lace.

Tiny threads of light bloomed above the fort, splitting and rejoining, hanging for a heartbeat like glowing spiderwebs before dissolving into sparks that drifted down slow as snow. Blue-white, gold-white, soft violet. They popped faintly, like distant popcorn, no louder than leaves rustling. They reflected in the jellyfish lanterns, in Percy's ghost ships, in Nico's wide eyes.

"Whoa," Nico breathed. "Whoa, whoa, whoa—"

The other kids ooh-ed and ah-ed. Mrs. O'Leary, who had arrived with a bow tied around her neck, bayed at the lights and tried to catch them, sending sparks scattering harmlessly off her nose.

Thalia kept it going for three minutes — her personal best for control — then let the last threads fade, breathing hard and grinning. Sweat stood out on her forehead. Her hands trembled slightly.

Nico ran to her and hugged her legs. "That was better than fireworks! Can you do it every year?"

Thalia picked him up — he was getting big, but she managed — and settled him on her hip. "Every year, little man. As long as you keep wanting rides. Even when you get too tall for my shoulders. I'll keep practicing."

"Promise?"

"Styx promise."

Bianca joined them, and Annabeth, and Percy, and soon it was a pile — Silena and Clarisse and Grover and Beckendorf and all of them, laughing in the striped shadows of the fort while the water lanterns glowed and the cake crumbs stuck to everyone's shoes.

Chiron watched from the trees, smiling. He had seen many birthdays at camp. Some grand, some quiet. This one, he thought, would be remembered. Not for the magic — though there had been plenty — but for the look on a small boy's face when he realized the whole world had conspired to say: you belong.

Later, much later, after the cake was gone and the lanterns dimmed and the younger campers were herded yawning to their cabins, Nico sat in the loft of his fort with Bianca, both of them in pajamas, sharing the last chocolate coin.

"Bianca?" he said sleepily, leaning against her side, crown crooked on his head.

"Yeah, piccolino?"

"Did you plan all this?"

She kissed the top of his head. "With help. A lot of help."

"I love it," he said. "I love the fort and the ships and Clarisse teaching me and the cake and Thalia's lights. But — my favorite was the ring. Because it's from you."

Bianca held him tighter, blinking fast. Above them, through the slats, stars showed in stripes. Below, Percy and Annabeth were still awake by the basin, Percy making tiny water stars swim while Annabeth named constellations for him in a low, fond voice: "That's Cassiopeia. No, Seaweed Brain, that's your hand. Move your hand."

Nico yawned. "I'm glad we came to camp."

"Me too," Bianca whispered. "Happy birthday, Nico."

"Happy birthday to me," he mumbled, already half asleep, plastic skeleton in one hand, new bracer still strapped on, ring glinting faintly in the dark.

He slept. The camp slept. The fort stood guard, striped with starlight, names carved over the door.
