## Chapter 108: Three Things We Keep

Clarisse said the Poseidon cabin needed a rulebook.

Silena said it already had one.

Clarisse asked where it was.

Silena pointed at the sign beside the door: *Don't Track Sand Inside. Don't Touch Clarisse's Spear. If Percy Is Asleep, Wake Him Gently.*

"That's not a rulebook," Clarisse said. "That's a list of things that annoy me."

"Most rules are."

Percy sat on the floor between them, sorting a bucket of polished shells into piles for Silena's jewelry work. "What do you think the cabin needs a rulebook for?"

"Training," Clarisse said. "We should have forms everyone knows. Signals. A way to move as a unit. We all use water differently, and that makes us messy in a fight."

"You want to write down the drills we use together?" Percy asked.

"We already do. We improvise too much." She tossed him a shell. "So we do it properly. I teach you stance and timing. You teach us whatever Atlantean nonsense you learned. Silena teaches us how to patch the cuts afterward."

Silena looked up from her necklace. "I can write down the poultices. The songs too, if you want."

"The song counts?" Percy asked.

"Sea songs. They help you keep time when you're moving through currents."

Clarisse crossed her arms. "I’m not writing down my singing."

"You hum when you think nobody can hear."

"I do not."

"You hummed the entire western patrol last week."

"That was a tactical rhythm."

Percy laughed. Clarisse tossed a shell at his head.

"Fine," she said. "Training tomorrow. Dawn. Tempest Hall. Don't be late."

"I won't."

"If you're late, you're running laps."

"Got it."

Silena tied off the necklace and held it up. A small blue shell hung from a braided cord. "This is for you, Percy. You can wear it when we train so we can see you in the pool."

"It looks like a tiny clam."

"It's a cowrie. It means safe passage."

Percy took it carefully. "Thanks."

Clarisse watched them. Her expression was difficult to read, but she didn't make a joke. That was how Percy knew she liked the moment.

---

The next round of training began at dawn.

Clarisse started by checking their stances. She had Percy and Silena stand on the tile floor of Tempest Hall, feet shoulder-width apart, knees loose, arms ready. She walked around them, correcting their posture with sharp taps of her spear butt.

"You're leaning."

"I am not," Percy said.

She tapped his shoulder. He wobbled.

"You are."

Silena tried to mimic Clarisse and nearly fell over. Clarisse caught her by the elbow before she hit the floor.

"See? This is why we practice."

"I thought I was doing well."

"You were. Then you tried to copy me instead of standing in a way that works for you." Clarisse helped her settle again. "Your balance is different. Use it."

Silena adjusted her stance. This time she stood steady.

Clarisse nodded. "Good."

Percy watched them. The tenderness in Clarisse's hands was brief, almost hidden, but it was there. She was fierce because she cared. She was careful because she knew what happened when people got hurt.

Then Clarisse turned to Percy. "You. Stop thinking about Atlantis and look at me."

"I was looking at you."

"You were thinking at me. It's different."

He tried again.

Clarisse showed them a basic pivot. She had learned it from a counselor in the Ares cabin years ago, but she'd adapted it for a trident: one step to invite the strike, a turn to let it pass, then a counter from the side.

Percy knew a similar move, though the Atlantean version used currents instead of footwork. He demonstrated it slowly, keeping his weight low and his hands relaxed.

Clarisse frowned. "That won't work on land."

"I know. But it works in the pool."

"Show me."

They moved to the water. Percy waded in first, then held out his hand to Clarisse. She took it and stepped down. Silena followed, clutching the safety rail.

The current in the pool was mild, enough to move their clothes and stir their hair. Percy showed them how to stop fighting it, how to feel the direction and use it to turn rather than resist.

Clarisse learned fast. Silena learned carefully. When she slipped, Clarisse caught her. When Percy over-rotated, Silena steadied him.

By the end of the morning, they had managed a clumsy three-person turn that left them bumping shoulders and laughing in the shallow water.

"Again," Clarisse said.

"We got it," Silena protested.

"We got it once. Do it again."

They did.

---

Silena’s review came after breakfast. She brought a basket of herbs to the infirmary and showed her siblings how to clean a cut, bind a sprain, and make a poultice from yarrow and crushed kelp.

"The salt helps reduce swelling," she explained, mixing the paste in a wooden bowl. "But not too much, or it stings."

Clarisse held out her hand. There was a shallow scrape across her palm from the practice trident.

Silena cleaned it. Clarisse tried not to wince.

"You can say it hurts," Silena said.

"It doesn't."

"You're squeezing your eyes shut."

"The sun's bright."

They were inside.

Percy laughed, and Clarisse flicked a drop of water at him. Silena smoothed the poultice over the scrape and wrapped it with a clean strip of cloth.

"There. You'll live."

"Good. I had plans."

Silena handed Percy the bowl. "Your turn."

"I don't have a cut."

"You have that bruise on your elbow."

"That's nothing."

"Then this won't hurt."

It stung. Percy didn't say so.

Silena gave him a look. "You and Clarisse are going to be impossible together."

"We already are," Clarisse said. "We're practicing."

---

That afternoon, Annabeth and Thalia found them in Tempest Hall.

"Are you training without us?" Annabeth asked.

"We started in the morning," Clarisse said. "You were asleep."

"That was on purpose."

Thalia picked up a practice spear. "What's the lesson?"

"Water movement," Percy said. "Clarisse's adapting the ground forms. Silena is teaching first aid."

Annabeth's eyes lit up. "Can we try?"

"You're a Zeus kid," Clarisse said. "You can try anything. Just don't electrocute the pool."

"I can control it. Mostly."

"That is not reassuring."

Thalia stepped into the pool first. She let the water rise to her waist and felt the current with her hands. She followed it more quickly than Percy expected. The sky and sea were not the same; this was practice and attention, not power over water. She watched the ripples and let Percy show her where the pull changed.

Annabeth was less comfortable. She stayed near the steps, watching the others try the turn.

Percy came to stand beside her. "You don't have to do it."

"I want to. I just don't want to lose control."

"Then start small. You can keep one hand on the rail."

She nodded and stepped in. The water lapped at her knees. She held the rail with one hand and reached out with the other. A faint crackle of static moved across her fingers.

"Easy," Thalia said. "Don't push it. Let the current move first."

Annabeth breathed in. The sparks faded.

She felt the current, or the pressure, or whatever her body interpreted as water. Percy guided her through the turn with a hand at her elbow. She stumbled once, laughed, and tried again.

The second time, she moved with them.

Clarisse gave an approving nod. "Good."

They practiced in pairs, then in a group. Percy and Clarisse made the anchor. Silena circled to keep the current moving. Thalia directed the turn. Annabeth used her power to release a small pulse of static that signaled the shift without calling lightning.

The move wasn't perfect, but it worked. Together, they could turn the current into a kind of shield.

"Again," Clarisse said.

They did it again.

When they finally stopped, everyone was wet and tired. Annabeth had a new streak of static in her hair, and Percy had taken another practice bruise on his shoulder.

Thalia tossed him a towel. "You make a good anchor."

"I thought I was a prince."

"You're allowed to be more than one thing."

Percy looked at his sisters. Clarisse's arm was draped across Silena's shoulders. Thalia stood beside Annabeth, still close enough to catch her if she lost balance. The five of them were damp, exhausted, and grinning.

For a moment, it felt like a family.

---

That evening, Clarisse asked Percy to walk with her.

They went to the beach, where she carried two wooden practice spears. She handed him one.

"I want to try the current move on sand," she said. "See if it works without the water."

They practiced under the last light. The move changed on land. It lost its fluidity, but the idea remained: yield, turn, answer. Clarisse corrected his feet. Percy reminded her to keep her shoulder loose. They argued about the best angle and tried the motion again.

Finally, Clarisse lowered her spear. "You don't have to be better than me at everything."

"I'm not."

"Good. It would be annoying."

She sat in the sand. Percy sat beside her.

"I used to think having a brother would mean having someone to compete with," Clarisse said. "Someone Dad would compare me to. Someone who'd get what I never did."

"Does it feel like that?"

"Sometimes." She dug the spear point into the sand. "Then I remember you're not the reason he wasn't there. And you're not him. You're just Percy."

"I'm glad."

"You should be. You're annoying, but you're ours."

Percy's throat tightened. "You're mine too."

Clarisse bumped her shoulder against his. "Yeah. I know."

They watched the tide turn. After a while, Silena and the others came down with the lantern and the leftover poultice herbs. Annabeth sat beside Percy, and their knees touched.

Silena set the small wooden figures from the earlier cabin gathering in the sand: ship, panther, sea horse, owl. They made a crooked little circle around the lantern.

"We should add the new move to our training list," she said. "The one with the current."

"What should we call it?" Thalia asked.

"The Anchor Turn," Annabeth said.

Clarisse tested the phrase. "That works."

Percy looked at the circle of figures, at his sisters and the girl he loved, at Thalia and the people who had become part of the same orbit.

"The Anchor Turn," he repeated.

The name fit.

---

That night, Percy returned to the cabin with Clarisse and Silena. They were still discussing the move, arguing about whether the third step should be a pivot or a slide.

"You need the pivot," Clarisse said.

"The slide is more stable," Silena replied.

"The pivot is faster."

"The slide gives you time to adjust."

Percy listened to them debate as he changed into dry clothes. In his lonelier old life, he might have looked at the argument and seen conflict. Now he heard the affection under it. Clarisse wanted the move to work. Silena wanted everyone safe. They were both trying to protect the others in the way they knew best.

When they finally settled on testing both versions tomorrow, Clarisse tossed Percy a clean shirt.

"You'll be here?"

"Of course."

"Good." She turned away too quickly. "Someone has to keep count."

Silena smiled at him over Clarisse's shoulder. Percy smiled back.

He was not the oldest. He was not their only source of strength. He wasn't the one in charge.

He was the little brother who got handed a dry shirt and expected to show up tomorrow.

For once, that felt like exactly the right place to be.
