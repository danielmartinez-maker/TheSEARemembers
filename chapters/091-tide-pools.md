## Chapter 91: Tide Pools

The morning smelled like low tide and hot sand before Percy even opened his eyes.

He knew the sound of the Poseidon cabin at seven in the morning, the particular way the sea breeze pushed through the cracked window and made the fishing net curtains sway, the way the floorboards creaked near the door where the salt had gotten into the wood. Clarisse was already up. That was not unusual. Clarisse was always already up, lacing her sneakers with sharp tugs, braiding her dark hair back so tight it had to hurt, humming under her breath like she wanted someone to ask what song it was so she could pretend she did not care.

Silena was sitting cross-legged on her bunk with a sketchbook balanced on her knees, a pencil tucked behind her ear and another in her hand. Her hair was still messy from sleep, pale blond tangled around her shoulders, and she smiled at Percy when he rolled over.

"Morning, barnacle," she said.

Percy grinned and threw his pillow at her. She caught it without looking up from her page.

Clarisse tossed a folded piece of paper onto his blanket. "Chiron approved it. Sibling day. Tide pools past the north border. We leave at nine. Bring sunscreen and do not embarrass us."

Percy sat up and unfolded the paper. It was a camp excursion slip in Chiron's neat handwriting, listing the three of them by name, the destination, the return time, and the supervisor signature at the bottom. Grover, with a little hoof print doodled beside it.

"Sibling day?" Percy said.

"Yes," Silena said, turning her sketchbook so he could see. She had drawn the three of them as different kinds of waves. Clarisse was a breaker, all white foam and sharp lines. Silena had drawn herself as a long rolling swell. Percy was a curl of water reaching toward a small starfish in the corner. "Just us. Poseidon kids. No quests, no training, no capture the flag. We go look at tide pools all day."

Clarisse shrugged like it was not a big deal, but she had already packed a bag and lined up three water bottles by the door. "Silena wanted to sketch. I wanted to get out of camp. You were the excuse."

Percy felt his chest go warm. He had been at camp for months now, and he still sometimes woke up braced for someone to tell him he did not belong. Having sisters who claimed him on paper, who packed an extra water bottle without making a speech about it, settled something restless in him.

"Best excuse ever," he said.

"Eat breakfast," Clarisse ordered. "And wear shoes you can get wet."

---

The path north beyond the border was not a path most campers took.

Percy walked between his sisters, his backpack bouncing, his sneakers already damp from morning grass. The air turned cooler and sharper beyond the strawberry fields, tasting of salt. Gulls cried overhead, and the trees thinned until pale sand showed through in patches.

Grover trotted a little ahead, his baseball cap pulled low and his fake feet squeaking faintly. He kept stopping to sniff the wind and glance back with an easy grin.

"All clear," he called. "No monsters, no tourists before ten. You three are good."

"Before ten?" Clarisse said.

"After ten, no promises," Grover said cheerfully.

Silena laughed and climbed onto a flat boulder to look toward the water. The sea spread blue and glittering to the horizon, and closer in, a dark shelf of rock jutted into the surf, pocked with hollows that caught the tide.

Percy could hear the pools before he saw them, the soft slap of water in stone cups, the hiss as a wave pulled back through mussel beds.

His blood hummed near the ocean, like hearing a familiar voice in another room.

Clarisse led the way down the slope, sure-footed as a goat, her bag slung over one shoulder. Silena followed more carefully, holding her sketchbook case high against her chest. Percy went last, tasting salt on his lips, watching the light catch on wet stone.

The tide pools opened below them like a scattered mirror.

There were dozens, some no bigger than a dinner plate, some wide enough to sit in, linked by narrow channels. The rock was black and rust-red with algae, studded with barnacles. In the clear water, colors bloomed — orange starfish, green anemones, purple urchins, small silver fish darting in schools that flashed when they turned.

Percy stopped at the edge and breathed in deep.

"Okay," Clarisse said, dropping her bag onto dry rock. "Rules. Stay where Grover can see us. Do not drink seawater. Do not pick up anything with blue rings. Do not fall in."

"Very big-sisterly," Silena said, setting down her own bag and pulling out a wide-brimmed hat. She settled it on her head and smiled at Percy. "What do you want to see first?"

"Everything," Percy said honestly.

Grover found himself a sunny slab a little above the pools, spread out a blanket, pulled out a reed pipe and an apple, and announced he was officially supervising from a horizontal position.

"Shout if anything tries to eat you," he said. "Otherwise, have fun."

---

Percy crouched beside the first pool and went still in the way his father had taught him without teaching, the way the water itself seemed to ask for.

The pool was shallow, ringed with pink coralline algae, and in the center a cluster of hermit crabs was making slow progress across the sand. One had a shell banded in brown and cream. Another had claimed a periwinkle shell far too big for it and kept tipping sideways.

"That one is Sir Clanksalot," Percy announced.

Clarisse, who had been testing the slipperiness of a nearby rock with the toe of her shoe, glanced over. "You are naming them?"

"Somebody has to," Percy said. "He has a noble face."

Silena settled on a rock with her sketchbook open, already drawing the outline of the pool. "What about the big one?"

Percy studied the oversized shell crab as it righted itself and waved its claws in the air. "Professor Wobble."

Clarisse snorted. "Terrible names."

"Great names," Percy corrected. "Look, that anemone is opening. That is Lady Velvet."

The green anemone in the corner did look velvety, its tentacles swaying gently, the tips a paler green that glowed when the sun hit. A tiny shrimp, almost transparent, picked its way around the base.

"And that is her bodyguard," Percy said, pointing. "Sergeant Shrimp."

Silena laughed so hard her pencil slipped. "Draw them," she said, flipping to a fresh page. "No, seriously. Hold still, crabs. Percy, keep talking. I like how you see them."

Percy moved from pool to pool on his knees, his shorts getting soaked, his hands pruned, completely happy. He named a starfish the size of his palm Captain Orange, with five lieutenants for each arm. He named a sculpin hiding under a ledge Baron Grumpface because it stared at him with huge eyes and puffed its cheeks when he wiggled his fingers. He found a cluster of barnacles that opened and closed their feathery feet with the surge and declared them the Chorus.

"They sing when the water comes in," he told Silena. "You cannot hear it unless you listen close."

Silena paused in her sketching and leaned closer, her hair falling forward, her expression serious in a way that made Percy sit up straighter. She listened. The water lapped. A gull cried. The barnacles waved their tiny fans.

"I hear it," she said softly.

Clarisse rolled her eyes, but she came to look anyway, crouching beside Percy with her forearms on her knees. For a moment the three of them were quiet around the small pool, watching the Chorus breathe.

The sea moved in Percy like a second heartbeat. He could feel each pool as a distinct cool spot, each channel as a thin line of flow. It would have been easy to push the water, to lift a little arc into the air or coax a fish closer. He did not need to. It was enough to sit inside the feeling and let it sit inside him.

"You always do that," Clarisse said after a while, not unkindly.

"Do what?" Percy asked.

"Go quiet when you are near the water. Like you are listening to someone else."

Percy shrugged, flicking a droplet off his fingertip. "Maybe I am. It says hi."

Silena smiled and sketched his hand hovering over the water, the reflection underneath broken into ripples. "Tell it we said hi back."

---

By midmorning the sun had burned off the haze and the rocks were warm underfoot where they were dry.

Clarisse had claimed a long, flat pool as a racetrack. She had collected four hermit crabs of similar size, set them at one end on a line drawn in wet sand, and marked a finish line with two small pebbles.

"Come on," she called to Percy. "You named them. You get to be commentator."

Percy scrambled over, dripping. Silena followed with her sketchbook, settling where she could see both the race and the sea beyond.

"Okay, contestants," Percy said, crouching at the side. He pointed with a stick. "In lane one, Sir Clanksalot, defending champion of doing nothing. In lane two, Professor Wobble, known for style, not speed. Lane three is new arrival Turbo Shell, very confident, very small. And lane four is Duchess Pinch, who looks like she will cut someone."

Clarisse adjusted Turbo Shell, who had immediately turned around and started walking the wrong way. "On your marks. No touching after start. No blowing on them. No— Percy, no water powers."

"I was not going to," Percy protested, grinning. "Maybe a little current of encouragement."

"No currents," Clarisse said firmly, but she was smiling, her eyes bright in a way Percy rarely saw when they were in camp doing drills. Out here she looked younger, or older, he could not tell. Lighter.

"Ready?" she said. "Go!"

She lifted her hand. The crabs did nothing for three full seconds.

Then Duchess Pinch lurched forward half an inch. Professor Wobble tipped over. Sir Clanksalot tucked deeper into his shell. Turbo Shell reversed directly into the starting line and fell asleep, as far as Percy could tell.

The four of them stared.

"This is thrilling," Silena whispered.

"Shh," Clarisse whispered back, completely serious. "Duchess is making a move."

Duchess Pinch took another heroic step, her tiny legs working, her shell rocking. A trickle of water from the incoming surge lifted the sand around her and she surfed forward a full inch, claws raised.

Percy grabbed his stick and held it like a microphone. "And Duchess Pinch takes the lead by a claw! Professor Wobble is down! I repeat, Professor Wobble is down! Sir Clanksalot refuses to participate in a system that ranks his worth by speed! Turbo Shell is going the wrong way and loving it!"

Silena was laughing so hard she had to press her hand to her mouth to keep quiet. Clarisse was biting her lip, leaning forward, her braid slipping over her shoulder.

"Come on, Wobble," she muttered. "Come on."

As if he heard her, Professor Wobble righted himself with a wobble that lived up to his name and took three quick steps forward, overtaking Sir Clanksalot, who had not moved.

"Oh! A comeback!" Percy shouted. "Wobble passes Clanksalot! The crowd goes wild!"

A gull overhead screamed, perfectly timed. Silena clapped. Grover, from his sunny slab, lifted his pipes and played a quick trill.

The surge came in, spilling over the lip of the pool in a thin sheet, lifting all four crabs and swirling them gently. When the water settled, Duchess Pinch was across the pebble line, her claws waving victoriously. Turbo Shell was facing the finish line for the first time. Professor Wobble was half-buried in sand. Sir Clanksalot had not moved at all.

"Duchess wins!" Percy declared. "By a tide! What a race!"

Clarisse pumped her fist. "Yes! I knew she had it. Look at those legs."

She picked up Duchess Pinch carefully, holding her flat on her palm while the little crab peered out. "Good girl," she said quietly, and her voice was gentler than Percy had ever heard it. "You go back to your rock now."

She set each crab back where she had found it, checking the shells for cracks, making sure they were right-side up near food. Percy helped, placing Sir Clanksalot beside a bit of algae.

"You are good with them," Percy said.

Clarisse shrugged, but color touched her cheeks. "They are small. People step on small things without looking. Someone should pay attention."

Silena caught Percy's eye over Clarisse's head and smiled, soft and proud. She turned to a new page and began to sketch fast, her pencil flying — Clarisse with a crab on her palm, the sea behind her, the wind lifting the end of her braid.

Percy watched his oldest sister draw his other sister and felt something lift and settle in his chest at the same time. He did not have words for it. He did not need words. The pools glittered. The gulls called. The water kept time against stone.

---

Silena's corner of the rocks slowly turned into a studio.

She had a tin of pencils, watercolors, brushes, and a jar for seawater. She worked with her hat tipped back, her feet tucked under her.

Percy flopped onto his stomach nearby to watch. She did not just copy what she saw. She found the shape inside it first, then built outward.

She drew the starfish pool in wide washes, orange against teal. She drew Grover asleep with his cap over his face, which made Percy snort.

"Do me," Percy said. "Draw me handsome."

"You are handsome," Silena said absently, shading a ripple. "Hold still and look at the water like you love it."

Percy tried to hold still, which was easy because he loved the water. He stared into a deep pool where silversides hung suspended. Below them, a purple urchin bristled in a crack.

Silena's pencil scratched. Clarisse came to peer over her shoulder and went quiet.

The drawing showed Percy from the side, one hand trailing in the water, his reflection looking back up, clearer and steadier. Around him Silena had left white space, so he seemed to be sitting in light.

Percy stared. "That does not even look like me. It looks better."

"It looks like you," Clarisse said. "You just do not see it."

Silena tore the page carefully from the book along the perforation and handed it to him. "For your cabin wall. So you remember."

Percy held the paper like it might tear in the wind, which it might. He folded it gently and slipped it into the front pocket of his backpack, between his towel and his sunscreen, where it would stay flat.

"Thank you," he said, and his voice came out rough.

Silena bumped her shoulder against his. "Anytime, barnacle."

Clarisse sat down on his other side and stole a grape from his trail mix without asking, which was basically a hug from Clarisse. The three of them sat in a row with their feet toward the pools, watching the light move.

A hermit crab the size of a thumbnail picked its way across Percy's sneaker and he held his breath until it passed, delighted.

"I want to remember this," Silena said quietly, flipping back through her pages. Tide pools, crab races, Grover snoring, Percy naming the Chorus. She had caught it all in lines and washes. "Winter gets long. I want to be able to open this and smell salt."

"You will," Percy said. "You draw smells."

Clarisse stretched out and leaned back on her hands, her face tipped to the sun. "Mom would like this spot," she said.

Percy turned. They rarely talked about their mother.

"She would sit right there," Clarisse said, nodding toward a flat rock. "And tell us not to splash her while we splashed her."

Silena smiled, her eyes bright. "She would know all the names. Better than Percy."

"Nothing is better than Sir Clanksalot," Percy said loyally, and both his sisters laughed.

Grover's pipes sounded from above, a slow wandering tune that fit the water and the wind. Percy closed his eyes and listened, and the sea listened with him.

---

The picnic arrived just after noon with laughter and the smell of warm bread.

Percy heard them before he saw them — Thalia's boots on rock, quick and confident, and Annabeth's lighter step beside her, and the rustle of a canvas tote.

"Permission to invade sibling day?" Thalia called from the top of the slope.

Percy was on his feet, grinning. Annabeth stood beside her sister with her hair in a ponytail, gray eyes bright behind sunglasses, wearing shorts and a Camp Half-Blood t-shirt. She carried a tote that smelled of strawberries. Thalia carried a cooler.

"Annabeth!" Percy said.

"Hi, Seaweed Brain," Annabeth said, and her smile did something fizzy to his stomach.

Thalia set the cooler down with a thump and spread her arms. "Zeus sisters, reporting for picnic duty. We come bearing sandwiches and gossip."

Clarisse stood and high-fived Thalia, the two older girls sharing a look of mutual respect forged in countless sparring matches. "You are late."

"We are exactly on time," Thalia said. "Lunch is at noon. It is noon."

Silena hugged Annabeth, careful of her sketchbook, then hugged Thalia. Percy hung back for half a second, suddenly shy in front of his sisters, then Annabeth turned to him and held out her hand, palm up, simple and sure.

He took it. Her fingers were warm and slightly sticky from strawberry juice, her grip firm. They did not need to announce anything. They stood hand in hand while Thalia unpacked the cooler with a flourish, and that was announcement enough.

"Chiron said you were mapping pools," Annabeth said. "We brought field rations."

"Peanut butter and jelly," Thalia said, lifting sandwiches wrapped in wax paper. "Turkey and cheese. Chips. Apples. Lemonade. And cookies the size of your head, courtesy of the kitchen nymphs who love me."

Grover sat up at the word cookies, his cap falling off. "Did someone say supervision snacks?"

Everyone laughed. They spread towels on the flattest dry rocks, weighing corners with shoes and bags. Thalia and Clarisse argued about chips. Silena showed Annabeth her sketches, and Annabeth asked sharp questions that made Silena flip pages faster.

Percy sat beside Annabeth, shoulders touching, trying not to grin like an idiot. He failed.

Annabeth nudged him with her elbow. "What?"

"Nothing," Percy said. "You came."

"Of course I came," Annabeth said simply. "Thalia said your sisters stole you to the tide pools. I was not missing that."

Thalia overheard and pointed a sandwich at them. "For the record, Annabeth has been talking about tide pools since Tuesday. She has a list."

"I do not have a list," Annabeth said quickly.

"She has a list," Thalia told Percy solemnly. " laminated, probably."

Annabeth's ears turned pink. "It is not laminated. It is just — there are zonation patterns. Upper intertidal, middle, lower. Different creatures at each level. I wanted to see if this shore matches the diagrams."

Percy loved this about her, the way her mind built frameworks for everything, the way she got excited about patterns. "Show me," he said. "We will check your list against real life."

Annabeth pulled a folded notebook page from her pocket, a little soft from being carried. Sure enough, there was a neat column in her handwriting: barnacles high, mussels middle, starfish low, with little checkboxes.

Percy laughed with delight. "You really did bring a list."

"It is a hypothesis," Annabeth said with dignity, but she was laughing too.

Thalia flopped back on the blanket with her sandwich. "Nerds," she said fondly. "My sister, the nerd, and her nerd boyfriend."

"We are not—" Annabeth started, then glanced at Percy, then stopped. They had agreed on words already, back at camp under the stars, quiet and careful in their younger bodies. Boyfriend and girlfriend sounded too small and too big at once, but they were the words for the relationship they had kept through the impossible change. They were figuring out how to live it here one hand-hold at a time, with promises to keep it kind and clean and honest.

Percy squeezed her hand once, quick. "We are picnic nerds," he said. "Eat your sandwich, Thalia."

Thalia saluted with her sandwich. Clarisse threw a chip at her. Silena poured lemonade into paper cups, passing them around. Grover told a story about a reed pipe competition with a flock of seagulls that ended with him owing them a french fry.

The food tasted better for salt air. Percy ate two sandwiches and a cookie as big as his palm. Annabeth gave him her crusts, and he gave her his extra strawberries.

After lunch, Thalia and Clarisse compared sparring scars and debated trident versus spear. Silena sketched with Annabeth watching each line appear.

"Can I try?" Annabeth asked quietly.

Silena blinked, then smiled wide and handed over a pencil and a blank page. "Of course. Draw what you see, not what you think you should see."

Annabeth bit her lip and drew a limpet with careful lines, measuring, erasing, retrying. Silena praised the observation and showed her how to loosen her wrist for shadow.

Percy watched them, his heart full. Grover snored softly with cookie crumbs on his shirt. The sea glittered beyond.

He did not want to be anywhere else.

---

Annabeth's list led them on an expedition after lunch.

She stood at the edge of the rocks with her notebook page and her ponytail whipping in the breeze, pointing like a general.

"Okay," she said. "Upper zone should be barnacles and limpets. Middle should be mussels and anemones. Lower should be starfish and urchins. Let's test it."

Percy loved that invitation. He fell in beside her, still holding her hand when the rocks allowed, letting go when they needed both hands to climb, finding her again on flat stretches.

Thalia followed with Clarisse, the two of them placing bets on whether Annabeth would be right.

"She is always right," Thalia said. "It is annoying."

"She is not always right," Clarisse said. "She lost to me in sparring last week."

"She let you win," Thalia said.

"I did not let her win," Annabeth called over her shoulder without turning around. "She won fair. Her footwork was better."

Clarisse looked smug. Thalia looked delighted. Percy squeezed Annabeth's hand, proud of how easily she gave credit.

They started high, where the rocks were mostly dry and white with salt. Sure enough, tiny barnacles clustered in cracks, and limpets held tight in neat rows.

"Check," Annabeth said, marking her page with a pencil stub. "Percy, lift that rock? No, that one. Careful."

Percy lifted. Beneath, a small crab scuttled sideways, and Annabeth crouched to observe, her eyes wide.

"Shore crab," she whispered. "See the square shell? And the way it moves?"

Percy crouched beside her, his knee brushing hers. "That is Kevin."

Annabeth laughed. "Kevin?"

"He looks like a Kevin," Percy said solemnly. "Kevin of the Upper Rocks, eater of crumbs, avoider of seagulls."

Annabeth wrote Kevin in parentheses beside shore crab on her list, shaking her head but smiling. "Your taxonomy needs work."

"My taxonomy is perfect," Percy said.

They moved down to the middle zone, where black mussels carpeted the stone, water squirting between them with each wave. Green anemones dotted the edges, and dog whelks inched along.

Annabeth checked them off fast. Silena sketched the mussel beds, pausing to show how light caught the wet shells. Thalia and Clarisse waded in a shallow channel, shrieking at the cold, daring each other to stand still while minnows nibbled their toes.

In the lower zone the pools deepened. Starfish in orange and purple curled around mussels, urchins waved their spines, and a bright green crab glowed like sea glass.

"Captain Orange was right," Percy breathed, lying flat on his stomach to peer into the clear water. "Look at them all."

Annabeth lay down beside him, her cheek nearly touching his shoulder, her ponytail spilling onto the rock. Together they watched a starfish slowly, slowly pry at a mussel, tube feet working.

"It is eating," Annabeth whispered, awed. "Really eating. The diagram did not do it justice."

Percy turned his head to look at her. Water droplets clung to her eyelashes. Her gray eyes reflected the pool, green and blue and bright. She felt his look and turned too, and for a moment they just stared at each other, close enough to count freckles.

Thalia cleared her throat loudly from three pools away. "Keep it chaste, lovebirds. There are minors present."

"We are minors," Percy said without looking away, and everyone laughed, breaking the spell gently.

Annabeth sat up, her cheeks pink, and marked big stars beside starfish and urchins on her list. "Hypothesis confirmed," she announced. "This shore matches the pattern."

Percy helped her to her feet, holding both her hands to pull her up. She came up laughing, stumbling a little on the slick rock, catching herself against his chest for half a second before stepping back with a shy smile.

"Thank you," she said.

"Anytime," Percy said.

Silena caught his eye and winked. Clarisse pretended to gag. Thalia gave him a thumbs up behind Annabeth's back.

Percy felt his ears burn, but he did not mind. Being teased by sisters meant belonging.

---

The perfect shell appeared when Percy was not looking for it.

They had split up to explore — Thalia and Clarisse seeing who could find the strangest creature, Silena settled with her watercolors above a quiet pool, Grover dozing again, Annabeth crouched with her list, checking off algae types.

Percy wandered alone for a little while, not far, always within sight of Grover's sunny slab, letting his feet take him where the water pulled. The surf was louder here, the rocks darker, the pools deeper and more secret. He liked the feeling of being small beside something large and old.

He slipped his hands into a shallow channel to steady himself and felt something smooth under his palm.

He lifted it, shaking water from his fingers.

It was a scallop shell, no bigger than his thumb, but whole, which was rare. Most shells on this shore were broken by surf and stone. This one was intact, fan-shaped, ridged delicately from hinge to edge. The outside was pale cream blushed with rose, the inside glossy, catching light in pinks and lavenders and a hint of gold near the center, like sunrise trapped in miniature.

Percy turned it over and over, his breath catching.

It was beautiful. It was also, instantly, Annabeth's.

Not because she liked pretty things, though she did in her precise way, favoring clean lines and true colors. But because the inside shimmered like the inside of a nautilus he had once shown her in a book, and she had stared at the spiral for a long time and said it looked like math you could hold. This shell looked like math you could hold, each ridge evenly spaced, each color blending in order.

He rubbed the sand from the hinge with his thumb and held it to his ear, listening for the sea. The sea was already all around him, but inside the shell it sounded closer, private.

He closed his fist around it and went to find her.

Annabeth was sitting cross-legged beside a pool full of coralline algae, her notebook in her lap, her pencil behind her ear, watching a tiny blenny dart between stones. She looked up when his shadow fell over her and smiled, squinting into the sun.

"Find another Kevin?" she asked.

Percy sat down beside her, close but not touching, his hands in his lap. His heart was beating fast in a way that felt silly for just a shell, but it mattered and he wanted to get it right.

"Close your eyes," he said.

Annabeth raised an eyebrow. "Why?"

"Trust me?"

She studied him for a second, then closed her eyes, her lashes dark against her cheeks, her mouth curved in a curious smile. Percy gently placed the shell in her palm and curled her fingers around it.

"Okay," he said. "Open."

Annabeth opened her eyes and then her hand. She went still.

"Oh," she breathed.

She held the shell up to the light, turning it slowly. The colors shifted, rose to gold to pale violet. Her eyes widened, gray going soft and bright.

"Percy," she said. "Where did you find this?"

"In the channel by the big rock," Percy said. "It was just sitting there. I thought — it reminded me of you. The way the colors go in order. Like you would like the pattern."

Annabeth stared at him, then back at the shell, then at him again. She pressed her lips together like she was trying not to smile too big and failing.

"I love it," she said quietly. "It is perfect."

Percy felt relief rush through him, warm as sunlight. "Really?"

"Really." Annabeth ran her fingertip along the ridges, counting without realizing she was counting. "Look how even they are. And the inside — it is like mother-of-pearl but warmer. Do you think it is a calico scallop? The blush on the outside—"

"You can keep it in your pocket," Percy said. "For luck. Or for science."

Annabeth laughed, a little wet around the edges. She slipped the shell into the front pocket of her shorts, patting it flat. It made a small round outline against the fabric.

"I will keep it always," she said. "In my pocket on quests. When I need to think. I will hold it and remember tide pools."

Percy swallowed hard. "Good," he managed. "That is good."

Annabeth leaned sideways and rested her head briefly against his shoulder, just for a second, her hair brushing his cheek. It was the bravest, sweetest thing. Percy sat very still, afraid to breathe and break it, his shoulder warm where she had touched.

"Thank you, Percy," she murmured.

"Anytime, Wise Girl," he whispered back.

From across the rocks, Thalia whooped. "Is that a shell? Did Seaweed Brain just do something romantic? Someone write down the date!"

"Shut up, Thalia!" Annabeth called back, laughing, her hand flying to her pocket to make sure the shell was safe. Percy waved the attention away, grinning, his face hot.

Silena looked up from her painting and smiled, soft and knowing. Clarisse gave Percy a nod of approval, sharp and brief, which from her was practically a hug.

Grover played a little flourish on his pipes.

Annabeth kept touching her pocket every few minutes after that, just to check, just to feel the shape through the fabric. Each time she did, Percy caught her eye and smiled, and she smiled back, secret and happy.

---

The afternoon stretched golden and slow.

They regrouped in the shade to cool off, sharing lemonade and trading finds. Thalia had found a sea hare the size of her hand. Clarisse had found a crab molting and spoke about it quietly, turning the empty shell gently before setting it back.

Silena showed her watercolors, still damp. There was Percy with his hand in the water, Clarisse with Duchess Pinch, Grover with cookie crumbs, Annabeth drawing her limpet.

Annabeth touched the edge of the painting of herself and said, "Can I keep that one? Please?"

Silena tore it out and gave it to her. Annabeth folded it carefully and put it in her tote beside her notebook, opposite the pocket with the shell, balancing her treasures.

Thalia braided Annabeth's hair to keep it out of her face while Annabeth leaned back with eyes closed. Clarisse re-braided her own hair and offered to do Silena's, and soon they were trading hair ties and sea-glass beads.

Percy lay back on the warm rock, watching clouds, listening to his sisters talk about camp, archery, pranks, songs on the radio.

Thalia spoke about her mother with humor that made Annabeth laugh until she cried. Clarisse spoke about the lighthouse foghorn on cold mornings. Silena spoke about jasmine and planting sea kale near the cabin.

Annabeth spoke about the home she remembered, books, and walking with Frederick, her father in their original life. Percy listened to every word.

When there was a lull, Silena said, "Tell us something about your mom, Percy."

Percy stared at the sky. He thought about blue pancakes, stories about the sea, being small and safe.

"Sally made blue food on purpose, in the life I remember," he said. "Blue pancakes, blue cookies. She told stories about the sea like she missed it."

Clarisse reached over and squeezed his ankle once. Silena smiled with her eyes shiny. Annabeth found his hand and held it, her thumb tracing the shell-smooth skin of his palm.

"She sounds great," Thalia said quietly. "I wish I could have met her."

"She would have fed you blue cookies and asked you a hundred questions," Percy said. "Amphitrite says she will try another batch from the recipe I wrote down."

"Then we will have cookies while you tell us more," Thalia promised.

The wind shifted, cooler now, bringing the smell of deeper water. Grover sat up and sniffed, then checked the sun.

"Two more hours of good light," he announced. "Then we head back for dinner. Use them well."

---

Percy and Annabeth took the last exploration together, just the two of them, within sight but out of earshot.

They walked the lower edge where waves broke white against stone. Percy pointed out urchins and the blenny. Annabeth pointed out erosion and the algae high-tide line.

At the farthest pool they stopped. The water was so still Percy could see every pebble, every frond, a small octopus tucked in a crevice, its skin shifting from rust to sand as it breathed.

"Look," Percy whispered, crouching and pointing.

Annabeth crouched beside him, her knee against his, her hand on his arm for balance. She gasped softly when she saw the eye.

"It is camouflaging," she breathed. "Oh, Percy, look at that."

They watched for a long time, barely moving, while the octopus breathed and rippled and finally jetted backward into shadow in a puff of sand, gone.

Annabeth sat back on her heels, her eyes shining. "That was amazing. Thank you for showing me."

"You saw it too," Percy said. "We saw it together."

Annabeth touched her pocket, feeling the shell. "I will remember this day when I am old. I will be like, ninety, and I will still have this shell and I will tell everyone about Kevin and Sir Clanksalot and Duchess Pinch."

Percy laughed. "You will be a cool ninety-year-old."

"You will be a cool ninety-year-old too," Annabeth said. "With a beard full of barnacles."

"Gross," Percy said, delighted.

Annabeth leaned her head against his shoulder again, longer this time, and Percy leaned his cheek lightly against her hair. The sun warmed their backs. The sea moved below them, endless and patient.

They stayed like that until Thalia shouted that Silena needed models for a group sketch and Grover needed help eating the last cookies.

They stood, shook sand from their clothes, and walked back hand in hand, swinging their joined hands just a little.

---

Silena arranged them like a photographer.

"Okay," she said, flipping to a fresh double page. "I want everyone. Sit where I tell you. Look natural, which means do not pose, Clarisse."

"I do not pose," Clarisse said, posing.

Thalia laughed and tackled her sideways, and Clarisse shoved her back, and Percy ended up squished between Annabeth and Silena with Thalia's boots in his lap and Clarisse's elbow in his ribs and Grover's pipes poking his ear, and it was perfect.

Silena sketched fast, her eyes flicking up and down, her hand sure. Annabeth watched over her shoulder, asking about proportions. Percy watched Annabeth watching, memorizing the line of her concentration, the way she tucked her hair behind her ear and immediately had to tuck it again.

"Hold still," Silena ordered. "Percy, stop looking at Annabeth and look at the sea."

Everyone laughed. Percy looked at the sea, his face burning, while Annabeth bumped his knee with hers under the blanket.

When Silena turned the book around, they all leaned in.

There they were in pencil and pale wash — Thalia grinning with windblown hair, Clarisse mid-eye-roll but smiling, Silena in the middle with her hat, Grover with his apple core, Percy with sun-pink cheeks, Annabeth with her hand in her pocket where the shell made a small bump, her head tilted toward Percy just slightly.

It looked like happiness felt.

"Wow," Thalia said softly.

Clarisse cleared her throat. "You make us look good."

"You are good," Silena said simply.

She let the pages flutter in the breeze to dry. Annabeth asked if she could add the date in the corner in her neat handwriting, and Silena said yes, and Annabeth wrote it carefully, with little waves around the numbers.

Percy touched the edge of the page with one fingertip, careful not to smudge. "Can you copy it? For my mom? So she can see?"

Silena's face softened. "I will make a copy tonight. In color. You can send it home with your letter."

Percy nodded, his throat tight. He imagined Amphitrite opening the letter in her blue sitting room in Atlantis, seeing him with his sisters and Annabeth and the sea behind them, knowing he was okay, knowing he was held.

"Thank you," he said.

---

The walk back was slow and sandy and happy-tired.

They packed up towels stiff with salt, sketchbooks full, cooler empty except for crumbs. Grover led the way, playing a walking tune that matched their steps. Thalia and Clarisse ranged ahead, talking spears and shields and daring each other to race to the border marker. Silena walked with Annabeth, heads bent over the sketchbook, planning the copy for Percy's mom, discussing which blue to use for the water.

Percy brought up the rear, his backpack heavy with wet towels and his sisters' water bottles because he had insisted on carrying them. His sneakers squelched. His shoulders were pink with sun. His hair was stiff with salt. He felt wonderful.

Annabeth dropped back to walk with him, her tote bumping her hip, her hand finding his without looking.

"Tired?" she asked.

"Good tired," Percy said.

She swung their hands once. "Today was perfect."

"Even with Turbo Shell going the wrong way?"

"Especially with Turbo Shell going the wrong way," Annabeth said. "It was real. I like real."

Percy thought about that. Real meant sunburn and sand in his shoes and crab races that barely moved and sandwiches with grit in them and his sisters teasing him and Annabeth's head on his shoulder and a small shell that fit in a palm like it had been made for it.

Real was good.

"Me too," he said.

They climbed the last rise toward camp, where the strawberry fields glowed green in late light and the cabins clustered below, smoke rising from the dining pavilion chimneys. The border marker gleamed. The breeze smelled like dinner and pine now, salt underneath.

At the top, Percy stopped and looked back toward the sea. The tide pools were hidden from here, tucked below rock, but he could feel them, cool and bright in his mind, each one holding its small world. He lifted his hand in a small wave.

Annabeth followed his gaze, her hand in her pocket around the shell. "Saying goodbye?"

"Saying see you later," Percy said. "The sea remembers. It will keep them for us."

Annabeth smiled, her gray eyes warm. "Then we will come back. All of us. Next low tide."

"Promise?" Percy said.

"Promise," Annabeth said, and linked her pinky with his, solemn as an oath on the Styx, sweet as strawberries.

Clarisse shouted from ahead, "Come on, slowpokes! Dinner!"

Thalia cupped her hands around her mouth. "Move it, lovebirds!"

Silena turned and walked backward, grinning, holding up her sketchbook. "Last one back has to model for my next painting!"

Percy looked at Annabeth. Annabeth looked at Percy. Together they ran, hand in hand at first, then letting go to sprint, laughing, sandals slapping, hair flying, racing toward their sisters and supper and home.

Annabeth's pocket bounced with the shell safe inside. Percy's backpack bounced with the sketch for his wall. The sun tipped toward evening, gold on water, gold on rock, gold on the tops of their heads.

Behind them, the tide turned, filling the pools anew.
