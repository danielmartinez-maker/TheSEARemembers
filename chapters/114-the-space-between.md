## Chapter 114: The Space Between

The first time Percy and Annabeth disagreed about a familiar sound, it was about a song.

Annabeth hummed it while they walked back from the library. Percy stopped so abruptly she nearly walked into him.

"Where did you learn that?"

She paused. "I don't know. It was in my head."

"It's an Atlantis song. Triton sang it on my last visit. He says he used to sing it when I was little."

"Maybe I heard it from you."

"I never taught it to you."

Annabeth's face closed. "I meant I could have heard somebody humming it here."

"Silena knows sea songs. You could have heard her. I just don't remember her singing this one."

"I don't remember both childhoods, Percy. A tune sounding familiar doesn't change that."

Percy heard the edge in her voice and realized he'd pushed too hard. "Sorry. I didn't mean—"

"You asked where I got it. I answered."

"I know. I just got surprised."

She continued walking. Percy followed, feeling foolish. He wanted to understand the childhood he'd been told was his, but he couldn't turn every familiar sound into a test of what Annabeth knew.

At the lake, Annabeth sat on the dock and looked out over the water. Percy sat a few feet away, giving her room.

"I don't like feeling as if I need to prove where every little thing came from," she said.

"You shouldn't have to."

"Sometimes you ask me questions like you want me to be the older me. And sometimes you say you don't expect that. I don't know which version of you I'm supposed to answer."

Percy took in her words. He didn't want to defend himself. He wanted to understand what he'd done.

"You're right," he said. "I don't want you to prove anything. I got caught up in the song because it reminded me of home. I should've said that instead of asking where you got it."

Annabeth's shoulders eased slightly. "I don't know where I got it. Maybe it was from somebody here and I wasn't paying attention. Maybe my fingers know the tune better than I do. It doesn't give me back a childhood."

"It can just be a song you like."

She looked at him. "That's allowed?"

"I think so."

Annabeth smiled faintly. "Then I'm keeping it."

"Good."

He sat beside her, leaving a little space between their shoulders. She looked at that space, then at him.

"You can come closer."

Percy shifted nearer, slowly enough that she could change her mind. Annabeth rested her head against his shoulder.

"Thanks for listening," she said.

"Thanks for telling me."

They watched the water until the song came back to her. This time she hummed it softly, and Percy listened without trying to claim it.

---

The next day, Percy asked if she wanted to hear the full song.

Annabeth considered. "Yes. But tell me what it means to you. Not what it means for me."

He sat beside her in the shade and sang it in Atlantean. His voice was rough and untrained. The melody rose and fell like a current moving through a narrow channel. The words were about a small fish that left the reef, learned the deep sea, and returned with a different name.

When he finished, Annabeth was quiet.

"That's beautiful," she said. "And sad."

"It's supposed to be both."

"I think I like the part about returning."

"Me too."

She took his hand. "I remember us. I don't need the childhood everyone else remembers to be here with you."

"I know."

"But I might ask questions. I might get things wrong."

"I'll tell you what I remember. You can decide what to do with it."

Annabeth leaned over and kissed him, a small, deliberate kiss. "That's what I want."

---

That evening, they walked back to the cabins together. They passed the Zeus cabin first. Thalia was on the porch, sharpening her spear.

"Everything okay?" she asked.

"We had a disagreement," Annabeth said. "We talked about it."

Thalia nodded. "Good."

She looked at Percy. "You listened?"

"I did."

"Good." Thalia went back to her spear. "Keep doing that."

Annabeth squeezed Percy's hand and continued toward the Poseidon cabin with him. At the path split, she leaned in and kissed his cheek.

"See you tomorrow."

"Tomorrow."

She walked back to Thalia, who shifted over to make room on the porch. Percy watched them sit together, shoulders touching, then headed home to his own cabin.

The song stayed with him. It no longer felt like something he'd lost or something Annabeth had to explain finding. It was a song they shared now, with room for both of them inside it.
