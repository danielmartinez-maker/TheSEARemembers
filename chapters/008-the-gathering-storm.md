## Chapter 8: The Gathering Storm

Percy discovered that the fastest way to lose an argument with Silena was to insist that he was capable of doing two things at once.

"You can carry the letters or you can carry the toolbox," she told him. "If you drop the bag in the creek, the post doesn't get to tell Amphitrite that you meant well."

"The creek is about a foot deep."

"Then your mother's going to get a letter that smells like a foot-deep creek. Which do you think she'll like better?"

Percy looked at the canvas mailbag she'd placed on the bench. His sealed letter to Amphitrite was inside, along with several others that needed to go out with the satyr messenger before noon. The letter had occupied most of the previous evening, and he'd spent enough time thinking about it that even the possibility of its getting wet made him uneasy.

He handed the toolbox to Clarisse.

"Fine. You win."

"I didn't realize we were competing," Silena said, counting the envelopes a second time.

"We weren't," Clarisse told him. "You invented that part."

It had been two days since the storm damaged the Hall's already vulnerable ward. The paths around the building were still cordoned off. Chiron had tightened curfew, assigned extra border patrols and warned campers against guessing which god might be responsible for the trouble. Percy understood the warning. He also knew that a rule against speculation was not going to stop the speculation.

He helped Silena take the mailbag to the messenger's collection point. On the way they passed two Hephaestus campers carrying fresh wooden panels toward the Hall. A third camper stood by the path, making a list of everyone going in and out of the work zone. She was taking the job seriously enough that a senior counselor had to explain why his name was missing from the roster.

Silena watched the exchange with approval.

"At least somebody's recording who goes into the building now."

"You don't think they did before?"

"I think they did sometimes. Then people stopped thinking it mattered because they knew one another. That's the problem with a place where everybody knows which drawer the keys are in. Eventually nobody remembers who last opened it."

Percy thought of the attic window in the Big House, which had been left loose long enough that he and Annabeth already knew how to use it. He looked away before Silena could notice the expression.

"You found anything new about the delivery signature?" he asked.

"Not yet. The stores keeper remembers the crate because it was heavier than the invoice said it ought to be. That's something we can check. He doesn't remember the person who signed for it, and I'm not going to make up an answer because Nico wants the ledger finished."

"Have you told Chiron?"

"Of course. He asked for the original invoice to be sealed while we compare the copies. It's still in the stores office, and this time somebody's actually signed the borrowing register."

She paused at the messenger's desk and deposited the mailbag. Percy watched the satyr open it, count the envelopes and place Amphitrite's letter in a separate pouch for sea delivery. The sight gave him an odd mixture of relief and dread.

"She'll write back," Silena said.

"I know."

"You look as though you're not sure you want that."

He thought about the childhood the letter had mentioned without describing. He hadn't lied, exactly. He had chosen what he could tell.

"I want to hear from her," he said. "I'm just not always sure I know what to say when she writes about things from before camp."

Silena looked at him for a moment. "Then ask her about the things you want to know. You don't have to pretend you remember every detail before you're allowed to talk to her."

Percy nodded. He'd been given that advice before. It still hadn't become easier to follow.

At breakfast, Annabeth claimed the end of the Poseidon table for their notebook. She had kept the bandage on two fingers, which made her pencil grip awkward, and had arranged a small stack of loose pages beside her bowl. Percy brought two plates and sat opposite her.

"You know Chiron's going to put you back on an early bedtime if he finds out you skipped breakfast to make another chart."

"I didn't skip breakfast. I'm eating right now."

"That's an empty bowl."

"I had an apple before you got here."

"That's the same defense you used yesterday, and Thalia ate the apple."

Annabeth looked at the plate he pushed toward her. "You brought bread?"

"And eggs. I have been informed that they're good for keeping a person alive."

She took the plate and began eating, though she kept one hand on the notebook so the morning breeze wouldn't lift the pages.

They had agreed to keep two sets of notes. The first contained what they remembered from their erased life: the wars they had survived, the families they had known, the version of camp where Annabeth was Athena's daughter and Clarisse belonged to Ares. The second contained things anybody in this camp could check, such as the Hall's construction dates, the purchase orders, the damaged wards and the conflicting prophecy records.

They could not afford to mix the two.

"The problem isn't that we're getting nowhere," Annabeth said, using the side of her thumb to turn a page. "It's that we're asking different questions without realizing it. We keep acting as though proving the Hall had a bad installation would prove why our lives changed. It wouldn't."

"Agreed. We could have a badly installed ward and somebody rewriting time. Camp could be unlucky enough to get both."

"And the Oracle's silence might be another separate problem."

Percy looked toward the Big House. "Or they might be connected."

"Exactly. But if we're going to test the connection, we need a reason other than both things frightening us."

He leaned over the notebook. She had drawn three boxes and connected them with dotted lines.

*Hall maintenance.*

*Prophecy transcripts.*

*Oracle silence.*

Under the first she'd written *physical evidence and witnesses*. Under the second, *dated copies, origin uncertain*. Under the third, *Chiron confirms no new prophecy for three weeks*.

"Where do our memories go?" Percy asked.

"On the other page. We know those memories exist, Percy. We don't have to prove them to each other. But Nico can't inspect what we remember. If we ask him for help, we have to give him something he can actually examine."

He looked at the margin. There she'd written a question about the cabin layout, then crossed it out and written *not independent proof*.

She had been doing that more often. A detail from their old lives would occur to her, and she would reach for the easy explanation before remembering that the camp around them held a very different set of records. Percy knew why it frustrated her. She had spent years solving problems by comparing what she knew against what the world told her. Now even that comparison required an argument about which world had been real.

"You're going to wear a hole through the paper," he said, pointing to the place where she'd been pressing her pencil.

"I've noticed."

"Want me to write for a while?"

She hesitated, looking at the bandages, then handed him the pencil.

He wrote *check original dated copies* beneath the prophecy box.

Annabeth nodded approvingly. "Good. And we need to ask about the Oracle without making it sound as though we've decided somebody gagged her. Chiron knows she's been silent, but he hasn't said why."

"Do you think he'll let us speak with her?"

"I think he'll tell us that he's still investigating the records and the ward damage. But I want to ask properly before we do anything else."

Percy lifted an eyebrow. "You mean before we do something he tells us not to do."

"Preferably before we even decide whether we're going to do that," she replied, reaching for her bread. "I'm trying to improve my methods."

"You could start by asking your boyfriend whether he's finished his own repairs before volunteering him for a burglary."

"I haven't volunteered you for anything."

"Yet."

Annabeth smiled faintly. It had been an exhausting week, and he was glad she still found him funny.

They took the notebook to the Athena cabin to compare two public maps. The older one showed a path that the Hall's builders had rerouted three years earlier. Annabeth pointed out where the new foundation had overlapped the previous supply road. Percy marked the difference in pencil, then asked why there were so many crossed-out measurements along the southern edge.

"The ground was probably unstable," she said. "Or the survey team changed the design. There's a note here about drainage work, and this area gets water from the hill after heavy rain."

"So yesterday's flooding wasn't exactly a surprise."

"No, but the amount was worse than the plans expected. That's something the repair crew can use, even if it has nothing to do with the prophecy."

He could see the distinction she was making. The mystery was not the only reason to ask a good question. Sometimes a drainage map mattered because somebody might slip in a flooded entrance during the next storm.

At midday they took their public notes to the Big House and requested an appointment. The secretary at the front desk was a harried satyr with a stack of forms taller than his horns.

"Lord Chiron is handling the border patrol reports," he said. "If you have information about the Hall, leave it with the maintenance team. If it's about the Oracle, put your request in writing."

Annabeth produced a page from her satchel.

The satyr examined it. "You've already written it."

"I had a feeling you might ask."

"I can send it upstairs. That doesn't mean he'll say yes."

"We understand," Percy said.

They waited on the porch while the satyr carried the request away. Annabeth ran her thumb over the edge of the notebook, catching herself when it pressed against a healing burn. Percy wanted to tell her to stop, but he'd heard enough people telling her what to do with her hands this week. He shifted closer so she could rest the book on his knee instead.

Chiron returned their request that afternoon with a handwritten note: the attic remained restricted because its protections were under review, and no one was permitted to question the Oracle until he and Mr. D determined that it was safe. He would consider a supervised meeting when the inspection was complete.

That was a refusal, but not an unreasonable one.

Annabeth read the note twice.

"How long is the inspection going to take?"

"It doesn't say."

"He should at least tell us what he's checking. We're the ones who found the conflicting copies."

"Maybe the next step is to ask for the dates and stop trying to go into the attic," Percy said.

She looked at him sharply. "I don't want to stop. Something is wrong with the Oracle, and if we wait for everybody to finish repairing every bracket in camp, we could be waiting until the next disaster."

"That's possible," he said. "But there are people who can help us without climbing through a window. Nico knows how to trace the records. Zoe knows the Hall's ward marks. Thalia can ask about Olympus's purchase orders. We could spend one day listening to what they find."

Annabeth looked away toward the Hall. The new canvas was tied down tightly, and a worker on a ladder was measuring the distance between two mounting points.

"You want to wait," she said.

"I want us to have a reason to break the rules that isn't just being frightened somebody will get there first. I've broken plenty of them. I don't want to do it automatically because we're worried."

She turned back, a little surprised by his answer.

"I hate that you're making sense," she said.

"It's upsetting me too."

They agreed to ask the others what they knew before making another decision. For once, the notebook went back into Annabeth's bag without a new midnight plan written in the margin.
---
Nico had taken over one end of the Hall workshop without asking anyone's permission.

There were three ledgers spread across the table when Percy and Annabeth arrived, each opened to a different year. Beside them lay a folder of copied receipts and a narrow wooden case holding the damaged fitting from the storm. The fitting was sealed beneath a glass lid. Nico had written a note on the case: *Not to be handled until the ward team finishes testing.*

Percy read it aloud. "You sound like Silena."

"Silena made me write it. Somebody tried to pick the thing up with his bare hands yesterday."

"Was it you?"

"It was a counselor who ought to know better."

Nico moved his notebook out of the way so Annabeth could sit. She had brought the public section of their investigation, with her more personal notes left in Cabin One. That distinction mattered more to Percy now that he'd watched how seriously Nico treated the provenance of even a damaged invoice.

"We asked Chiron for an interview with the Oracle," she said. "He won't let us go into the attic until the ward inspections are finished. Have you found anything about the old transcripts that could justify an earlier review?"

Nico opened the oldest ledger.

"Not a reason to go in without permission. But I did find something odd about the way the transcripts are stored. They used to be copied for the camp archives as soon as Chiron approved them. After the Hall was built, the records show a second copy being sent to the Hall's custodian."

"Why?" Percy asked.

"Probably for the official displays. The Hall has an exhibit on the history of prophecy. A custodian would need source copies to check the inscriptions."

Annabeth leaned forward. "Who signed the transfer?"

"That's the odd part. The first two years have signatures from a known assistant. The last one has the custodian's seal but no name on the receipt."

"Does that mean the copy was stolen?"

"It means the receipt is incomplete. I'd like to ask the custodian before I make the story bigger."

Percy looked down at the year. It matched the period when the prophecy records had begun disagreeing.

"Could we compare the Hall's display copy with the original in the Big House?"

"Only if Chiron allows it. Right now the Hall copy is in conservation because the mosaic room got flooded. The original is still with the archivist." Nico hesitated. "I did ask to see the earlier catalog. Chiron said I could check the public index but not the restricted transcripts."

Annabeth made a note.

"Then the public index should tell us whether there's a dated copy earlier than the one we found."

"Maybe. But a catalog entry doesn't always describe what's actually in the folder. I used to think it did." Nico looked rather offended by his own discovery. "Apparently archivists make mistakes like everyone else."

Zoe arrived carrying a rolled diagram, which she laid over the end of the table without disturbing Nico's ledgers. She was younger than Thalia but had the weary expression of somebody who'd been answering the same questions for two days.

"I heard somebody wanted to compare the ward marks," she said.

Annabeth opened her notebook to the drawing she'd made after the storm.

"We saw these marks near the northern column. Are they from Hecate's protections?"

"Some of them. That one isn't." Zoe pointed to three hooked strokes meeting at a circle. "The Hall borrowed a common motif from older boundary inscriptions. It means containment in several different magical traditions. You can't identify the caster from it."

"Could the same symbol be used in the attic?" Percy asked.

Zoe thought for a moment.

"Possibly. The Oracle has protective inscriptions of her own, and the Big House is older than the Hall. You'd have to compare an actual inscription, not just a sketch, before you could say the marks were the same."

Nico looked up. "We have old survey drawings from before the Hall was built. We could check whether the motif was copied into the new design."

"Those surveys weren't complete," Zoe warned. "The builders changed several sections during construction. I can tell you what the motif was meant to do in the plan, but I can't promise the finished building matches it."

Annabeth nodded. "That's still useful. We can compare what the design intended with what the repair team found."

Percy watched them work through the drawings. Annabeth asked about line thickness and orientation. Zoe answered with a careful explanation of how protective inscriptions could look similar without being magically identical. Nico kept interrupting to ask where every measurement had been recorded and whether they could cite the actual page.

It was the sort of conversation Percy normally enjoyed only when he could be somewhere else. Today he listened. They were showing him how to build an answer from things another person could verify.

At the end of an hour, Nico had drawn a smaller diagram of the records chain. Zoe had identified three common ward patterns that might be mistaken for one another, and Annabeth had produced a list of questions she wanted to put to the archivist.

None of it told them who had rewritten their lives.

"It doesn't mean we wasted the morning," Annabeth said, as though Percy had complained.

"I didn't say that."

"You looked like you were going to."

"I was thinking that if the Hall borrowed its symbols from the Big House, the old protection could be the useful starting point. Not because it's suspicious. Because it's older."

Zoe glanced at him with new interest.

"That's actually a reasonable observation."

"Thank you. I'll remember this day."

She ignored the joke. "If you want to learn why a symbol was copied, check the architect's design notes. But don't assume the person who copied it knew how the original enchantment worked. People have been decorating sacred buildings with symbols they no longer understand for centuries."

Nico tapped the ledger. "The Hall custodian might have those notes."

"Then I'll ask," Annabeth said.

"You'll ask politely, right?" Percy said.

She gave him a flat look. "You are enjoying this far too much."

They took their questions to the Hall office. The custodian was unavailable, but the assistant promised to forward their request and showed them which catalog numbers referred to the original construction survey. It was a small success. The assistant also explained that the older transcripts were being checked for water damage and that access would be restricted until the work was finished.

Percy wrote down the dates.

When they left, Annabeth was walking faster than he was.

"You're upset," he said.

"I'm frustrated. There are answers in those files, and every person we ask has another reason why we can't see them."

"Some of those reasons are good. The archive isn't a puzzle box somebody left for us to solve. There are people trying to keep it from falling apart."

"I know. I hate that I know. But I still don't like waiting."

He didn't tell her to be patient. She'd heard more than enough of that word since the fever, and he wasn't going to turn it into an argument about whether she had a right to want answers.

They walked back toward the cabins with an appointment request, two new names and a list of catalog entries. Percy counted those as real progress. Annabeth looked at the Hall's sealed doors as though she was measuring the distance between the information they needed and the time somebody would finally let them touch it.
---


Past midnight, they climbed for the attic again.

The Big House slept. Pine sap and old wood met them at the same loose window, the same groaning stair, the same attic door swollen with summer humidity.

Inside, the Oracle's mist had thickened. It banked in the corners and threw back iridescent light that stung Percy's eyes. Ozone prickled his nose. The floorboards seemed to vibrate, the way the dock did before lightning struck the Sound. Somewhere under the wax and dust lay a sour trace, like old pennies left in rainwater, and every breath felt slightly too thick to finish.

Annabeth knelt beside the archive chest and sorted the loose pages by their dates. Percy held the shaded lamp; neither of them touched the Oracle's chair.

"What are you looking for?" he whispered.

"A copy made before the theft."

"You think someone left it in here?"

"I think whoever changed one page might have missed a boring one."

Quiet settled, broken by the tick of cooling shingles and paper rasping beneath her fingers. Then she paused over a sheet clipped to a patrol report.

"Percy."

He brought the lamp closer. The margin held four lines in Chiron's cramped hand, dated before the transcript they had found. A question mark followed the last one.

Annabeth copied them into her notebook before she read them aloud:

*The oath shall break, the king shall fall,*
*The son of Poseidon shall face the call.*
*One throne shaken, one sister bound,*
*In darkness and fear, the truth is found.*

Annabeth set the page back under its clip. Her hand shook slightly as she underlined the differences in her notebook.

"An earlier version," she whispered. "'One throne' and 'one sister' — not 'three thrones' and 'two sisters.'"

"So somebody upsized it."

"Or this is a bad copy." She swallowed. "We need to know which. What if someone is *building* toward the altered wording?"

Cold spread from his sternum outward. "You mean they might be answering a prophecy."

"Might. A date and four lines aren't proof of who changed our lives."

"Who does that?"

"Someone who profits when it happens."

They stared at each other while dust sifted through the mist around them.

Percy exhaled. "Then we decode the altered version. 'Three thrones shaken, two sisters bound.' Which thrones? Which sisters?"

"No idea." Annabeth pushed upright, wincing. "But I doubt we'll have to wait long to be introduced."

Floorboards creaked below — footsteps, voices. Both of them went rigid.

"Someone's coming," Percy breathed.

He hauled Annabeth upright and towed her behind a tower of sheet-draped furniture. They wedged into the shadows, chests barely moving, as the attic door swung inward and lamplight spilled across the floor.

"— telling you, something's wrong." Chiron's voice, low and urgent. "Three weeks silent. That has never happened."

"Oracles are theatrical." Mr. D sounded sharp, irritated, a glass clinking. "They sulk. They return. They spout riddles. It's the job description."

"This isn't temperament. Her last prophecy — the stolen one — broke every pattern in her record. Now silence." A pause, hooves shifting on boards. "That's not drama. That's a gag."

"Placed by whom?"

"I don't know. But I mean to learn."

The voices thinned. The door shut. Darkness resettled, smelling of dust and lemon polish.

Percy counted his own heartbeats to five hundred before he moved.

"Silent," Annabeth murmured. "No new prophecies. We don't know whether she can't or won't."

"Or won't. Maybe she knows something she can't afford to say aloud."

"There might be a connection between the records, the silent Oracle, and the monsters at the border. But we still don't know if one cause explains all three."

"And we're sitting in the middle of the web."

"Yes."

They exited the way they entered — window, ivy, damp grass — and held their silence until the Big House windows had shrunk behind pines and the lake air replaced attic dust in their lungs.

Near the water, Annabeth stopped. "We need to speak with her. Directly."

"The Oracle who hasn't spoken in three weeks?"

"The only witness who was in the room when it happened."

Percy weighed exposure against ignorance: the watcher they hadn't identified, the questions that would mark them. Annabeth was right anyway. She usually was.

"Okay." He bumped his shoulder against hers. "Careful. And not solo."

"Not solo," she agreed.

They walked back toward the cabins without talking, the decision settling damp and heavy as lake fog on their shoulders.

Far off, thunder worked its way along the hills.

Rain was gathering.


---

By morning, Clarisse found Percy helping replace a weapon rack at Tempest Hall. She waited until the instructor walked away before speaking.

"You smell like attic dust," she said.

"Good morning to you too."

"Chiron doubled curfew. Somebody's been leaving footprints under the Big House windows anyway."

Percy set down the bracket. "You watching the windows?"

"Somebody has to. Nico's watching the records. Silena's finding out who got paid to fix the Hall. I'm watching our idiot little brother so he doesn't get himself expelled before he's learned where the clean towels go."

"I haven't been expelled."

"That is not an achievement yet."

He turned the bracket over in his hands. He wanted to tell her why the prophecy mattered so much. If he did, he'd have to explain the memories he and Annabeth had already agreed to keep between themselves. Clarisse had been his sister for three weeks. Her concern was genuine. It wasn't a reason to hand her something that could frighten her for the rest of her life.

"What do you know about Zoe?" he asked instead.

Clarisse frowned. "Why?"

"She was the first person who told me about the missing prophecy."

"She knows the Hall's wards better than most counselors. Hecate kids study that kind of thing. She's spent a lot of time around the Hall since it opened, and she's been quiet since the break-in."

"You think she's responsible?"

"I think she has a reason to care. That isn't the same thing." Clarisse took the bracket from him and held it against the post. "Hold that steady."

Percy obeyed.

"If you're going to ask questions about her, ask Nico what he's found," she added. "And maybe ask Zoe herself. You're good at guessing motives from somebody's face. Doesn't mean you're right."

Percy started fastening the bracket. "Did Silena tell you to say that?"

"She said I should use fewer threats."

"Did it work?"

"I'm trying." Clarisse took the wrench back before he could strip the bolt. "Now get out of the way."

---

At dusk, Annabeth found Percy on the dock reviewing their notes. The night before, the Oracle's mist had gathered around the chair without producing a new prophecy. They had found a dated copy in Chiron's handwriting, not an explanation.

"I spoke to him," she said.

"To Chiron?"

"He confirmed the Oracle has been silent for three weeks. He wouldn't say whether he thinks that's connected to the stolen prophecy from three years ago."

"That's not quite the same as knowing someone's stopped her from speaking."

"I know. I hate it." Annabeth sat beside him. "We have two things now: an older copy and a pattern of silence. Both matter. Neither tells us the rest."

Percy closed the notebook. "So what now?"

"We try asking her directly, with Chiron there if we can persuade him. If that fails, we'll decide whether going back at night is worth it."

"That sounds suspiciously sensible."

"You make me sound ill."

He nudged her shoe with his. "I like you sensible. I also like you when you're sure everyone else is wrong."

"That's most of the time."

"I noticed."

She leaned her shoulder against his, just for a moment. Then she stood and took the notebook.

"Come on. Curfew. We can get arrested tomorrow."

They made it back before the horn. Percy counted that as an achievement.
